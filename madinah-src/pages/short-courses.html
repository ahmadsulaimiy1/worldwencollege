What a short-course certificate says

A short course of four to eight weeks carries between 24 and 60 notional hours of study, stated on its syllabus and divided into instruction, independent work and examination. Every course is examined; none is certificated on attendance alone.

Where a course concludes with an examination the candidate has passed, the instrument is a Certificate of Attainment and names the competences examined. Where a candidate completes the course without sitting or passing its examination, the instrument is a Certificate of Attendance, which states on its face that it attests attendance and submission and makes no claim of attainment. The College issues both, and does not blur them.

Admission to a short course is by application to the Registry. No placement assessment is required except for Applied Tajwīd, which assumes a sound reading. A short course carries no credit toward a Certificate, Diploma or Higher Diploma of the College unless its syllabus says so, and the syllabi that do say so name the programme and the exemption exactly.

The Ramaḍān programme.

The general timetable of the College is suspended for the month, and the following programme is offered in its place.

Accelerated memorisation

Daily classes at increased portion for students already admitted to the Diploma in Qur’ānic Memorisation.

Khatm circles

Completion of the Qur’ān within the month, recited in class under correction.

Nightly exegesis

A daily half-hour upon the portion appointed for that night’s reading.

Preparation for Tarāwīḥ

For those appointed to lead, in the fortnight preceding the month.

Memorisation schedules

Schedules of memorisation.

The Faculty of Qur’ānic Studies issues four graduated schedules for the memorisation of the Qur’ān, each incorporating a programme of revision from the first week. They are supplied without charge, and without condition of enrolment.

The measured pace

المتيسِّر — for a student holding a full timetable elsewhere.

The regular pace

المنتظم — presupposes a sound reading and a cleared hour each day.

The intensive pace

المكثَّف — for those already holding a substantial portion.

Full-time

المتفرِّغ — study as the principal occupation of the day.

On the daily portion and the time to completion

Both are set out in the schedule itself rather than here. The College published two different sets of figures for these four schedules — one in each language — and they did not agree. Rather than choose between them, or average them, the College has withdrawn both and referred the question to the Faculty of Qur’ānic Studies. A single approved schedule will be published in both languages together.

Nothing about the offer has changed. The schedules are issued on request, free, without condition of enrolment, and each carries the Five Returns (Reg. 3.3) from the first week — because a portion without revision is a count of what has passed, not a record of what remains.
