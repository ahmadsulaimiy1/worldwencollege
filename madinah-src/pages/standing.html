Disclosure

Standing, Recognition and Disclosure

What the College is, what its awards are and are not, what it has not yet obtained, and how it states a figure. An unstated limitation is a misrepresentation, and a prospective student is entitled to read the limitations before the prospectus.

What is claimed

The claims of the College, and the claims it does not make.

Awards are conferred on the College’s own authority

The Certificate, the Diploma and the Higher Diploma are awards of Al-Madinah International College, conferred by its Academic Council against published syllabi and published criteria. They are what they say they are and nothing further.

The College does not award degrees

It confers no bachelor’s, master’s or doctoral award, and describes none of its programmes as degree-equivalent. The words university, degree and faculty in the legal sense do not appear on any instrument it issues.

It holds no state accreditation

The College is not accredited by the National Universities Commission, by any Nigerian state ministry of education, or by any foreign accrediting body, and does not represent otherwise. Where a student needs a recognised award for employment or for further study, the Registry says so before admission rather than after graduation.

Ijāzah is not a College award in the ordinary sense

An ijāzah is conferred by a teacher who himself holds a connected chain, and its authority is the chain, not the institution. The College records, publishes and verifies it; it does not manufacture it. The conditions are at Awards, conditions of ijāzah.

A distance is a distance

The College does not claim that instruction at a distance is the equivalent of sitting before a teacher. It claims to be reachable, examined and recorded, which is both a large claim and a true one.

The faculty is published as it is appointed

No member of faculty is listed before appointment, and no chain is entered in the Register of Asānīd before the Academic Council has seen the instrument that carries it. A programme with no appointed teacher is shown as forthcoming, with the semester in which it opens.

The graded certificates

Two routes, named differently, and why.

Ibtidā’iyyah, I’dādiyyah and Thānawiyyah do not name only a body of knowledge. Across Nigeria and the Arabic-Islamic educational world they name school stages, and a school-leaving certificate makes a claim about a period of formation and not only about knowledge held.

In Nigeria the body which examines and awards at those stages is the National Board for Arabic and Islamic Studies. Bodies of that kind characteristically prescribe a minimum period of attendance, precisely because of what a school-leaving certificate asserts. The College therefore separates what it may truthfully award now from what depends on another body’s rules, and gives the two different names.

What this means for an applicant today

A candidate admitted to the graded certificates today is admitted to the Academic Route, and the certificate they receive is an award of this College. It is not a certificate of the National Board for Arabic and Islamic Studies and is not represented as one.

The Certificated School Route will be opened only when the Board’s current requirements have been confirmed with the Board in writing and the College satisfies them. Until then it is not offered, not advertised and not sold. A candidate whose purpose is the external certificate is told this at the point of enquiry and, where the College cannot yet serve it, is directed to a centre that can.

External examination

A limitation the College states rather than waits to be asked about.

Every summative gate is moderated by a second qualified examiner under Reg. 5. That moderation is presently internal: the second examiner is a member of this College.

No serious framework of evaluation anywhere treats self-moderation as sufficient, and the College will not describe its examination as though it did. Until an External Examiner is appointed in a faculty, every surface of the College which describes examination in that faculty says exactly this.

What is being built toward

Each faculty is to appoint an External Examiner — a qualified scholar with no other relationship to the College — who annually reviews a sample of gate examinations, the record of moderation, and a sample of the instruments issued against the criteria published for them, and reports in writing to the Academic Council.

Those reports are retained permanently. They are the evidence on which any future application for recognition would rest, and they are of no value unless they begin before the application does.

Continuity

The register of instruments must outlive the College.

A graduate’s award must not stop being verifiable because the institution that issued it stopped existing. A promise of permanence with no mechanism is a promise broken by the first hosting invoice nobody pays.

An open format

The register of record is kept as plain files in a documented, text-based format — not a proprietary database and not a supplier’s export. It must be readable in thirty years by someone with no access to our software and no knowledge of us.

Provision 1

Rebuildable from one archive

The whole register must be reconstructable from a single archive file, and that property is tested rather than assumed: a restoration is performed and checked on a schedule, and a failure is treated as a fault in the register itself.

Provision 2

Three copies, two custodians

The live register, an independent mirror, and an offline archival copy held by a second party who is not an officer of the College.

Provision 3

A named successor

The instrument constituting the College names the custodian who receives the register if the College ceases, with the duty of keeping verification answering.

Provision 4

Verification is switched off last

Should the College ever wind up, the order is fixed: publicity first, then the website, then the Riwāq, and the register last of all.

Provision 5

Verification is free to every enquirer, for ever, requires no account, and returns only whether an instrument is valid, what it attests, to whom, when it was issued and whether it has been revoked. Nothing else. A verification service that returns the whole record is a disclosure of a student’s data with a friendly name on it.

Information held

What the College keeps about a student.

The College collects what the teaching, the examination and the record require, and not more: identity and contact, the account of prior study, the programme and mode, attempts and outcomes with the examiner named, notional hours, attendance at live classes, recordings of sessions, adjustments granted, fees and any supported place, and every change to the record with the person who made it and the date.

Recordings of classes are made for revision and, where a minor is present, for the guardian. They are retained for twelve months and are not used for any other purpose, are not published, and are not used to advertise the College.

  • A student may ask for a copy of their record, without charge
  • A student may have an error in it corrected
  • The record is never sold, never rented, and never given to a third party except where a student asks or the law of the student’s country requires it
  • Information about a student under eighteen is held to the minimum and is used for no analysis of any kind
  • Where a student leaves, the record is retained under the College’s retention schedule; the entry in the register of instruments is permanent, because an award that can be deleted cannot be verified

The College serves students in several jurisdictions with different laws on personal information. Where the law of a student’s own country grants them a right this page does not name, that right stands and the Registry will honour it.

How figures are stated

Every number on this website is one of three things.

A recorded fact

Counted from the College’s own records, and stated with the period it covers. The enrolment figures of the predecessor programme are of this kind: they are counts from that programme’s registers between 2020 and 2022, not projections and not a present enrolment.

A published rule

A fee, a class size, a duration, a number of hours, a pass standard. These are decisions of the College, and they bind it. Where one changes, the change carries a date and does not operate backwards against a registered student.

A stated estimate

A range of semesters, an indicative number of hours for a portion of the Qur’ān, a weekly commitment. These are described as estimates where they appear and are revised against the College’s own completion data as it accumulates.

Nothing is quantified until it has been counted and dated. The College publishes no figure it cannot show the working for, states the standing of its predecessor programme rather than absorbing its students into a present claim, and would rather print a smaller true number than a larger arguable one.
