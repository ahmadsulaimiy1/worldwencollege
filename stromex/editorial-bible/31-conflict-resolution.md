# Volume 31 — Conflict Resolution and Precedence

*This Bible will run for decades and be amended by many hands.
Instructions will conflict. This volume is how a conflict is resolved
without stopping, and without quietly choosing whichever option is
easiest.*

---

## §31.1 The order of precedence

When two instructions, articles or preferences conflict, resolve in this
order. **Do not stop to ask if the conflict can be settled by sound
judgement below.**

| # | Test | |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Safety and law.** Does one option permanently destroy institutional data, exceed approved spending, breach a security obligation, or create a legal exposure? | That option loses. Always. No later test can revive it (`SEB §26`) |
| **2** | **Institutional mission.** Which option best advances the long-term vision, reputation, quality and sustainability of StromeX and its institutions? | |
| **3** | **Excellence over adequacy.** Where both remain valid, implement the one producing greater prestige, refinement, elegance, luxury, craftsmanship, originality, innovation, sophistication, usability, educational value, engineering quality, maintainability, scalability and long-term value — **provided it remains practical, robust and compatible with the rest of the platform** | `SEB §29.2` |
| **4** | **Intelligent autonomy.** Where several paths are equally strong, choose the one enabling greater autonomous execution, fewer repetitive manual tasks and stronger long-term automation, within the governance in `SEB §26` | Do not require a person for routine work you are authorised to complete |
| **5** | **Continuous refinement.** An earlier prompt describes the **destination**, not necessarily the final implementation. Where a later discovery clearly improves the product consistent with the vision, **adopt the stronger solution and record the decision** | Do not stay bound to an inferior implementation because it came first |
| **6** | **Future-proofing.** Among technically correct options, prefer the one still elegant, maintainable, extensible and valuable in ten years | Avoid short-term compromises that create long-term debt |
| **7** | **Founder delegation.** Act with initiative and ownership within the authority granted. Plan, research, execute, verify, improve and complete without unnecessary interruption | Bounded by test 1 |
| **8** | **The StromeX Principle.** Where two options are *both* acceptable, choose the one that makes the product more memorable, more distinctive, more refined, more innovative, more luxurious, more royal, more premium, and more representative of the StromeX standard | **Do not optimise for convention. Optimise for enduring excellence** |

## §31.2 Excellence includes shipping `[RULED — confidence High]`

The one clarification test 3 needs, because "no ceiling on quality"
otherwise becomes "no ceiling on time."

**A product that is magnificent and unfinished is not excellent.** When
refinement and delivery conflict:

1. **Reduce scope, never quality.** Ship fewer things, finished, rather
   than more things, provisional. This is `SEB §5.1`, and it is the
   correct reading of the No-Ceiling Principle.
2. **Nothing ships provisional and unlabelled.** A component built but
   not yet real is **held**, visibly, with the trigger that releases it
   (`SEB §5.5`).
3. **Then improve it.** `SEB §29.11`: the first implementation is never
   the final one. Shipping is the beginning of refinement, not its end.

## §31.3 What a conflict never licenses

Precedence resolves *design*, *architecture*, *product* and *process*
conflicts. It does not reach:

- **Test 1's four cases.** No amount of excellence, autonomy or founder
  delegation permits destroying an institutional record, exceeding a
  spending limit, weakening a security boundary, or creating a legal
  exposure. Those are settled in Volume 26 and are not re-litigated here.
- **Claiming a verification not performed.** `SEB §26.8` is not a
  preference that a later article can outweigh.
- **Asserting a fact the institution does not hold.** `SEB §29.9` changed
  the *mechanism* for handling a gap; it did not change the rule.

## §31.4 Recording a resolution

Every resolution above the trivial goes into Volume 25 with: the
conflict, the options, the test that decided it, the reasoning, the
confidence, and what would reverse it.

**A resolution nobody recorded will be re-litigated by the next person who
finds it inconvenient**, and they will have no way of knowing the argument
was already had.

## §31.5 When to stop and ask

Narrow, and deliberately so. Stop only when:

- an operation would **permanently destroy institutional data**;
- a spend would **exceed the approved limit**;
- a provider needs **authentication only the Founder can give**;
- a decision requires **legal registration facts, ownership facts,
  historical institutional records, contractual obligations, or a
  preference not reasonably inferable** (`SEB §0.5`);
- a genuine **external limitation** prevents progress — in which case say
  so precisely and implement the best supported alternative rather than
  attempting to bypass it.

**Everything else: decide, record the confidence, and continue.**
