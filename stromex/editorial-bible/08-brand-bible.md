## §8.2 The naming veto gate `[OBSERVED]`

`AMC-EB §8.4` codifies this as "a permanent rule binding every future
name," adopted after it **caught two failures the scoring matrix had
ranked as winners.** It is the single most reusable naming artefact in the
estate and it applies to institutions, products, sub-brands, repositories
and services alike.

A candidate name is vetoed — regardless of score — if:

| Veto | Test |
|---|---|
| **V1 · Unearned identity** | Does the name claim a status the institution does not hold? *Dār al-Qurrāʾ* ("House of the Reciters") scored highest of 36 candidates and was vetoed because the college issues no ijāzah and appoints no muqriʾīn. **The matrix winner, killed by the honesty rule.** |
| **V2 · Appropriated honorific** | Does it apply to itself a term reserved, in scripture or scholarship, for a class of people? *Al-Rāsikhūn* was vetoed on this ground. |
| **V3 · Borrowed authority** | Does it trade on a place, a lineage or an institution whose standing is not ours? Where a name legitimately carries such an association, a **public non-affiliation statement** is mandatory (`AMC-EB §8.5`). |
| **V4 · Prior mark** | Is there a claimed or registered mark in an overlapping field? *Sankoré*, the strongest African-positioning candidate, was vetoed on this. |
| **V5 · Saturation** | Is the namespace already crowded? The research finding that reframed the whole study: the evocative-Arabic-word namespace in online Qur'an and Arabic education is *comprehensively* saturated — Itqān (5 incumbents), Suffa (6, including a chartered university), Riwāq (5), Bayān, Qalam, Manārah. **"A beautiful Arabic word" feels distinctive and is the single most crowded strategy available.** |

**A veto has a standing review trigger, not a permanent death.** `AMC`
records: re-open *Dār al-Qurrāʾ* if and when muqriʾīn are appointed and
ijāzah is issued — the V1 veto would fall away and it would then be the
strongest name in the study.

**The honest-characterisation rule.** `AMC-D D-02` records its own result
as "a weak-form win": the briefed name survived not because it was strong
but because every distinctive alternative failed on trademark,
saturation, propriety or pronounceability. A naming study that cannot say
that about itself is a marketing document.

## §8.3 One transliteration, forever `[OBSERVED]`

`AMC-EB §8.2`. One spelling of a name is chosen and is then invariant
across every surface, language, file name, database row, certificate,
domain and email address. A second spelling is not a variant — it is a
second entity, and the estate has already paid for that: **two offices
recorded under two spellings of one person's name were read as two
people, and a documented two-person control silently ceased to exist for
an entire institution** (`SHRS role-permission-matrix §3`).

**Binding.** Every institution maintains a transliteration standard as an
appendix to its own bible, and identity is resolved to an **id**, never to
a rendered name (`SEB §20.5`).

## §8.4 Distinctness is engineered, not assumed `[OBSERVED]`

When two institutions in one group approach the same palette, the
proximity is **stated plainly and then engineered away**, not pretended
about. `AMC-EB §14.1a` is the model: adopting blue and gold moved it
toward a palette its own preamble had rejected, so it recorded the
collision and then separated the two by hue (226 ultramarine against 220
grey-navy), by markedly higher chroma, by a different third colour
(turquoise instead of oxblood), by a blue-black ink, and by restricting
gold to hairline and mark where the other institution uses gradients.

And the honest note: **"The gold hue cannot meaningfully move; that is
stated rather than disguised."**

**Binding.** Two institutions in one group are separated on at least three
independent axes — hue *and* chroma *and* a distinct third colour, or type
system, or ornament grammar. The separation is written down, with the
axis that could not move named.

## §8.5 The brand asset library `[OBSERVED]`

`AMC-EB §44.2`. **One source of truth**: logos, typefaces, colour tokens,
icons, ornament, templates, photography, certificate masters — versioned,
licensed and access-controlled.

- **Nothing is recreated.** A mark redrawn by hand for one application is
  a second mark.
- **Nothing is used from a personal file.**
- **Nothing is sourced from a search engine.**
- Every asset carries its licence in a register, entered **before it
  ships**.

## §8.6 Third parties `[OBSERVED]`

`AMC-EB §44.3`, adopted whole: every external designer, developer, agency,
translator, calligrapher and photographer receives the relevant bible,
works to it, and **assigns all rights in writing before work begins**.
Non-compliant work is not paid for. **This clause goes in the contract.**

## §8.7 The removal test `[OBSERVED]`

`AMC-DX §14` gate G4: cover the mark, the seal and the name. **Do seven of
ten readers still identify the institution?** An identity that depends on
its logo being visible is a logo, not an identity — the type, the colour
proportion, the component canon and the ornament grammar should carry it
alone.

## §8.8 Institutional marks and seals `[OBSERVED]`

- A **crest or seal** is a ceremonial object, used at ceremonial moments —
  on a certificate, a closing plate, a watermark once per page — not as a
  header logo repeated on every screen.
- The **operational mark** is a separate, lighter drawing: legible at
  34×40px in a way a detailed raster crest is not (`WEC-EB Part III`).
- A raster crest is not composited with a blend mode to hide its own
  background. That trick works over one ground and fails over every other;
  the mark is redrawn in vector (`WEC-EB Part III·b`).
- Certificate and seal artwork is governed by the certificate constitution
  (Volume 12), not by this volume, because its requirements are security
  requirements before they are aesthetic ones.

## §8.9 Brand governance `[OBSERVED]`

`AMC-EB §44.1`: **no asset, page, price, certificate or campaign is
published without the relevant authority's sign-off, recorded.** The
authorities are at `SEB §0.7`.

## §8.10 What each institution owns, and what it may not change `[RULED — confidence High]`

An institution owns its **palette, its typefaces, its ornament, its
photography direction, its voice within the estate's register, and its
own bible's institution-specific volumes.**

An institution may **not** change: the component canon's *purpose*
(`SEB §7.3` — it may extend the canon, argued and recorded), the
accessibility floor, the Mobile Covenant, the honesty protocol, the
rejected-conventions register, or anything in Volume 26.

The reason is `SEB §0.3` rule 1: exceptions are how institutions lose
their character, and a group of institutions loses its character one
justified exception at a time.
