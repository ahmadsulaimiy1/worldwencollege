Support · Safeguarding

Safeguarding, written for a College without a building.

A safeguarding policy is worth what it can reach. This one says what the College can do from where it is — and what it does in the hours when that is the thing that matters.

The College teaches adults online, across many jurisdictions, often one to one and at hours nobody else is awake. This instrument sets out who is protected, the boundaries every instructor works inside, what follows a disclosure, and the office that answers.

Enrolment
Adults, 18 and over
A report

“Say what you can reach, and reach it.”

I The Commitment

Three duties the College takes on, each with the measure that tells a learner it was kept.

What the College undertakes.

Safeguarding here means one thing: a person’s safety and dignity outrank the College’s convenience, its reputation and its timetable, every time the two meet.

One

To act on what it is told

A report of harm is acknowledged within one working day and reaches the Registrar the same day. Protective measures follow within three, because a person at risk needs a decision rather than a quorum.

Two

To separate the two questions

Whether somebody is safe and whether somebody is at fault run on different clocks. Safety is settled first, in days. Fault is settled afterwards, under the code of conduct, with the right of reply it guarantees.

Three

To protect the person who speaks

Raising a concern in good faith is entered on no conduct record, weighed in no mark and reflected in no reference. A report that proves mistaken is still one made in good faith.

What this instrument is

A rule of the College, adopted by the Executive and binding on everyone who teaches, marks, administers or represents it. It sits beside the law of the place where a learner lives and displaces none of it: a learner keeps every right their own jurisdiction gives them.

II Who Is Covered

Everyone the College teaches, and the rule that decides whom it may teach at all.

Adults, and everyone the College deals with.

The College admits adults. That is a decision about what it can supervise rather than a judgement about young learners, and it is stated here so that a family reading before applying has the answer at once.

The rule

Eighteen on the day you enrol

Enrolment is open to a person aged eighteen or over on the day their first level opens. The application asks directly, enrolment completes once it is answered, and the College keeps the answer rather than a birth date.

The reason

Why the line is drawn there

Teaching a young person safely at a distance takes a verified guardian, a supervised room and a local authority to refer to. An institution admitting a minor it cannot supervise has borrowed a duty it cannot discharge.

If it turns out otherwise

What happens the same day

Where the College learns a registered learner is under eighteen, one-to-one contact stops that day and moves to a written channel a guardian can see. The Registrar writes within three working days, and an unbegun level is refunded under the refund policy.

Everyone else

Beyond the enrolled learner

This instrument covers applicants, enrolled learners, award-holders, anyone attending a residency, and every member of staff. Somebody outside all of those reporting harm from a College activity is answered on the same clock.

A child in the room

Adults study at kitchen tables, and children walk past them. Where a member of staff sees or hears something suggesting a child is being harmed, it goes to the Registrar the same day. The College acts by the one route open to it from a distance: it tells the adult learner what was seen, gives them the child-protection number for their country, and records what it did.

III Boundaries

What a member of staff may and may not do, in a tutorial and in writing.

The boundaries every instructor works inside.

Most harm in education is done by somebody the learner trusts, in private, a little at a time. The defence is structure rather than vigilance: a channel that keeps a record, a rule about privacy, and a difference in authority its holder must name.

The channel

Teaching happens where the College can see it

Tutorials, feedback and every exchange about a learner’s work run on College channels. Staff give no personal number, add no learner on a personal account, and move no teaching into a private application.

One to one

A tutorial is private, and it is not secret

One-to-one teaching is how this College works. It requires that the tutorial be scheduled through the College, that a short note of it go on the record, and that a learner may ask for a second member of staff without reason.

In writing

Feedback addresses the work

Written comment addresses the English and the task. It does not address a learner’s body, dress, faith or household, and carries no endearment and no remark they would be uncomfortable showing a relative.

Money and favours

Nothing passes between staff and learner

Staff take no payment, gift, loan or service from a learner, and offer none. Private tuition arranged outside the College with a learner the instructor marks is refused. An instructor close to a learner declares it and is moved off their marking.

  • A learner may ask for a different instructor, in writing, and gives no reason.
  • A learner may ask for a second member of staff in any one-to-one conversation.
  • Every tutorial is scheduled through the College and noted on the record.
  • A breach of these boundaries is misconduct, whether or not a learner objected.
At a residency, in person

A learner who takes the residency in the United Kingdom meets staff in a room rather than on a screen, and three rules are added. Sessions run at a published venue during published hours. No member of staff is alone with a learner behind a closed door. Travel and social arrangements stay separate from the College’s.

IV A Disclosure

What a member of staff does when somebody says they are being harmed, and what they may never promise.

What happens when you tell somebody.

Disclosures here rarely arrive as a report. They arrive inside a piece of writing, in the reason a deadline was missed, or in a sentence at the end of a tutorial. Staff treat all three the same way.

Said before you speak, not after

No member of staff may promise to keep a disclosure of harm secret. What they can promise is this: it goes to the Registrar and nobody else inside the College; you are told what will be passed on and to whom before it goes; and it leaves the College only with your agreement. The one exception is an immediate risk to a life or to a child.

The emergency contact

At registration every learner is invited to give one emergency contact: a name and a way of reaching them. It is used where there is an immediate risk to life, and on no other occasion. Giving one is a choice, it can be withdrawn at any time, and a learner who declines is not asked again.

V Across Borders

What reaches across a border, and the procedure that stands where reach ends.

What the College can reach, and what it does where it cannot.

A learner in difficulty is entitled to know the shape of the help before they need it — said here, in the calm, rather than discovered in the crisis.

The plain sentence

The College cannot summon an ambulance, the police or a welfare service in another country. Emergency services answer the person in front of them, in their own jurisdiction, on their own number. What the College can do is put that number in a learner’s hand within seconds, stay with them while they use it, and reach the person they nominated. The procedure below is made of those three things.

Held in advance

The numbers, before they are needed

The College holds the emergency number and crisis line for every country a current learner gives as their place of study, and checks the list at the start of each cohort. An instructor has it in front of them before a hard conversation goes further.

During the session

A learner in crisis, step by step

The instructor stops the lesson and stays. They ask whether the learner is safe now and whether anyone is with them. They give the local number and offer to stay on the call while it is dialled. They ask whether to reach the nominated contact, and act on that answer.

Afterwards

The day after, and the month after

The Registrar writes the next working day with what was agreed and what happens to the learner’s study. Deadlines are suspended for as long as it takes. The place is held, and the interruption carries no fee.

Study, and care, are different things

This College teaches English. Care for illness, injury or a mental-health crisis comes from a clinician where a learner lives, and the College’s part is to help somebody reach one and hold their place while they do. Saying it plainly is itself the safeguarding act: a learner who thought a college was a clinic is one who delayed calling somebody who could help.

VI Between Learners

Harassment through a connection the College made, and the measures that stand meanwhile.

Harassment between learners, including where it moves.

Learners meet one another through this College — in a shared exercise, at a residency, in a channel it operates. Where that connection is used to pursue, threaten or sexually pressure somebody, it remains a matter for the College even after the messages move somewhere private.

The reach rule

The connection was made here

Conduct that began through a College connection falls within this instrument wherever it continues. A learner who obtained an address or a name here and used it to harass another learner answers to the College for it.

Within 2 working days

Separation first, decision later

The Registrar may separate two learners while a matter is decided: different sessions, a restricted channel, a different instructor. It is protective and temporary, is neither finding nor sanction, and is reviewed every twenty working days.

Never asked

You are not sent to sort it out

Nobody who reports harassment is asked to raise it with the other person, to meet them, or to accept mediation as a condition of being heard. Where both agree to a facilitated conversation it is arranged, and refusing carries no consequence.

Where the finding is made

Protection is decided here; fault is decided under the code of conduct, which sets the standard of proof, the right of reply and the published tariff. This instrument adds one rule: a learner who reported harm may give their account in writing, and may bring one person to any conversation that does take place.

VII Raising A Concern

Where a report goes, who reads it, what happens next and by when.

One address, and what follows it.

Safeguarding concerns go to the Registrar, who holds this instrument for the Executive. A report may be made by the person affected, by somebody on their behalf, or by a member of staff — and about anybody, including an officer of the College.

Stage one · 1 working day

Received and acknowledged

Write to info@worldwencollege.co.uk with the word Safeguarding in the subject line. A person answers within one working day, naming the office handling it and what follows.

Stage two · 3 working days

Protective measures decided

The Registrar decides what protects the person now: a change of instructor, a restricted channel, suspended deadlines, a separation. It is written with its reason and stands whether or not anybody is later found at fault.

Stage three · 20 working days

Outcome, in writing

Within twenty working days the person who reported receives a written outcome: what was found, what the College did, and what remains in place. Where a matter runs longer they are told why every twenty working days until it closes.

  • A report about the Registrar goes to the Provost and is held outside that office entirely.
  • A report about an officer goes to the Governor for Ethics and Institutional Values.
  • Nobody who is the subject of a report takes any part in deciding it.
  • An outcome you disagree with is appealable under the academic regulations.
How the College checks itself

The Registrar reports to the Executive every six months on reports received, the median working days to a protective decision, and the number closed within twenty working days. The Student Experience Committee reviews the same figures annually under the quality architecture. Counting how fast a College answers is how it learns that it stopped.
