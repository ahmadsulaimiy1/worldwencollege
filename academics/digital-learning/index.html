Academics · Policy

The digital learning environment.

The College teaches entirely online, so the platform is the building. This document states what the building is guaranteed to do, and what you are owed on a day it does not.

Availability, and the remedy for losing it. What the platform records about your study, and what that record is for. How long a recording of your voice is kept. How to get a copy of everything held about you. The device you need, what works offline, and access for a learner who cannot hear.

The Digital Campus Your Academic Record
I The Standard

Four commitments about the platform, each one measured rather than asserted.

What the platform is guaranteed to do.

A promise with no figure attached cannot be broken, which is why most of them carry none. These four carry figures.

Availability

99.5% of every quarter

Measured on the parts a learner signs into: lessons, the Listening Lab, submission and the academic record. The allowance is about eleven hours a quarter. Announced maintenance sits outside the measure; everything else counts, including a fault affecting one country.

Planned work

Seven days’ notice, and never before an examination

Maintenance that takes the platform down is announced at least seven days ahead, scheduled between 01:00 and 05:00 UTC, and never inside the 72 hours before a scheduled examination. Work that costs no downtime is not announced, because it is not felt.

Unplanned loss

A notice in 30 minutes, an account in five days

An outage lasting more than half an hour is posted on the sign-in page while it is happening. Within five working days the College writes to affected learners with what failed, how long it lasted, and what was done. A fault nobody explains happens twice.

Judgement

Nothing on the platform decides anything about you

Software records attempts, opens the next step and stores your files. A person confirms a level is finished, marks the work and writes the feedback — see the academic regulations and the policy on artificial intelligence.

II If It Goes Down

What happens to a deadline, an attempt in progress, and a level you have paid for.

Lost access is returned as access.

Every remedy below applies without an application. A learner should not have to write in and argue for something the College already knows happened.

Deadlines

Any deadline in the window moves a working day

A deadline falling during an outage, or in the twelve hours after it ends, moves by one full working day for each day the fault ran. It moves for everybody affected, at once, without anybody asking.

An attempt in progress

An interrupted attempt is void

An assessment cut short by a fault does not stand, does not count as an attempt, and does not count against your two resits. You sit it again on a fresh task where the assessment requires one, and the interrupted attempt leaves no mark on the record.

Access

Time lost is added back

Where access is lost for more than three consecutive days, the level’s access period extends by the days lost. Beyond twenty consecutive days you may withdraw from the level under the refund terms instead, and the choice is yours.

What an outage never does

It never removes work already submitted, because a submission is written to storage before you are told it succeeded. It never shortens a level, and it never becomes a reason to mark you down. Time lost is replaced with time.

III What Is Measured

The whole of what the platform records about how you study, and the single question it exists to answer.

One total per module, and no history behind it.

Most learning platforms record everything and decide later what it was for. This one holds the least that answers a single question: how long does a module actually take?

Time on task

One row for you and a module, holding a total

While you are working, the page says only that you are still there. The server decides what that is worth from its own clock, crediting at most 150 seconds per interval and stopping at 40 hours for a module. A tab left open overnight earns one interval.

Deliberately absent

No clickstream, no page log, no session history

The platform does not keep a record of what you opened, in what order, or on which evening. Nothing is retained that could reconstruct a study session, because nothing about the question being asked requires it.

What it is used for

Publishing how long a level really takes

The College publishes the median hours a level takes, and only once at least thirty learners have completed it. The median is reported rather than the mean, because one learner who left a page open for a week moves a mean and does not move a median.

What it is never used for

It is not a mark and no marker sees it

Study time carries no academic weight. It is not shown to the person marking your work, it is not evidence in any decision about you, and a learner who reaches the standard in half the hours has reached the standard.

IV Recordings

Your recorded voice is coursework, assessment evidence and personal data at once. Three retention terms, set here.

How long a recording of your voice is kept.

Speaking is assessed by recording you speaking, so this College holds more of its learners’ voices than most institutions ever would. The terms are stated rather than left to practice.

WhatKept forWhy that period
A recording assessed for an awardSix years from the date the award is conferred A certificate resting on assessed speech has to show that the audio marked is the audio held. Six years covers the period in which an employer or a university is likely to check.
A recording assessed with no award followingSix years from the assessment The same evidential reason, counted from the only date there is.
A practice takeTwelve months from the day you made it It is your working material. Long enough to hear your own progress across a level, short enough that the College is not holding your voice for no purpose.
The assessment record itselfPermanently The mark, the feedback and a checksum of the audio survive the audio. A register that loses entries cannot be trusted about the ones it keeps.
The rule that protects you

A recording keeps the terms it was made under

The retention period is stamped on each recording at the moment it completes. Changing this policy applies to recordings made afterwards, so a later decision can never reach back and shorten — or extend — the terms you already recorded under.

What you control

Delete a practice take whenever you like

Practice takes are yours to remove at any time, and removing one affects nothing else. A recording already submitted for assessment stays until its term expires, for the same reason a submitted script does.

How it is held

Never on a public address

Storage is encrypted at rest, and no recording has a link that works without a session. Every playback is authorised at the moment it is requested, so access ends when a person’s relationship with the College ends.

V Your Data, Out

How to get a copy of everything the College holds about you, and what a copy contains.

Ask, and it is sent to you.

Data you cannot get out of a system is data you have lent rather than owned. One request, one address, one deadline.

How

One email, no form and no reason required

Write to the College asking for a copy of your record. You are never asked why you want it, and asking has no effect on your standing, your marks or anything else.

When

Within 20 working days, at no charge

As often as you ask. Where a request is unusually large the College writes inside those 20 days to say what is coming and when.

What arrives

Readable by you and by a machine

A structured data file for everything held as records, your recordings as the original audio files, and your transcript as a document you can hand to somebody. Three forms, because the three are wanted for different reasons.

What a copy contains

  • Your enrolment record, the levels you hold, and the dates of each.
  • Every attempt, every mark by skill, and every piece of feedback written to you.
  • Every recording still within its retention term, as the file you made.
  • Your measured study time, by module.
  • Your transcript, with the honours and the average printed on it.

One thing a request cannot remove: an award conferred on you stays on the academic register, because a register that quietly loses entries cannot be trusted about the ones it keeps. What a student controls is set out under Your academic record.

VI Device & Offline

The minimum you need, and the parts of the Lab that keep working when the connection stops.

What you need, and what survives a dropped connection.

The College admits learners in dozens of countries, many of them on metered or intermittent connections. The platform was built for that reader rather than adapted to them afterwards.

The device

A phone is enough, and so is an old laptop

Any computer, tablet or phone with a browser you keep updated. The interface is composed down to 360 pixels of width, so a small phone gets the whole programme rather than a reduced version of it. A built-in microphone is all the Listening Lab asks for.

The connection

No video is served, so audio is the heaviest thing here

The programme is taught self-paced and serves no video at any point, which removes the requirement that excludes most learners on a slow line. Audio is the only heavy asset, and it is never fetched until you ask for it.

Offline

Reading, marking up and recording all work with no connection

Transcripts, cue navigation, bookmarks, private notes and the voice recorder keep working when the network stops, and the page says plainly that you are offline. Notes stay on your device and are never sent to the College at all.

Uploads

A dropped upload resumes rather than restarts

A recording is sent in parts, and the parts already received are kept. When the connection returns the upload carries on from where it stopped. A take may run to 100 MB, which is far beyond any spoken answer the programme asks for.

Audio is downloaded only when you say so

Nothing is cached onto your device behind your back. Each listening carries a control that keeps it offline and one that removes it again, so a learner paying for data by the megabyte decides what to spend it on. Signing in, submitting and reading marks are the parts that need the network.

VII Access

What was built for a learner who cannot hear the audio, cannot see the screen, or cannot use a mouse.

The learning environment itself.

An accessible website in front of an inaccessible classroom helps nobody. What follows is about the classroom.

Hearing

The script is the text, not a substitute for it

Every listening in the programme is published as a complete script, and the Lab is designed around the script rather than around the audio. A learner who cannot hear the recording has the whole of the material, the comprehension questions and the notes, in text.

Sight and motion

The position in the transcript is announced

As playback moves from line to line, the line reached is announced to assistive technology, so a learner using a screen reader can follow where the audio has got to. Where a device asks for reduced motion, the animation stops and the finished state is what remains.

Keyboard

The transport runs from the keyboard

Play and pause on the space bar, and five seconds back or forward on the arrow keys, with no pointer involved. Every control carries a real label rather than an icon alone, in English and in Arabic.

If something here is unusable for you

Write to the College describing what you were doing and what happened. Adjustments — extra time, a different format, an audio-only arrangement — are made by agreement, and what the College can and cannot support is described under Accessibility. A report of a real barrier moves faster here than any policy sentence.
