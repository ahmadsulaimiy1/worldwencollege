Academics

How the College teaches.

Every lesson in the programme is planned before it is taught, and every planned lesson carries a record of what a teacher will need at each point in it. This page describes the method, the record, and the part of the record that is empty.

The Method

Four commitments that shape every lesson.

Not a philosophy of language teaching in the abstract — these are the four decisions that visibly determine what a AIPC lesson looks like.

One

Taught in English, from Level I

Including at A1, where it is done through restricted language, repetition, visual support and a teacher who slows down instead of translating. Teaching a language through the language is a method, and Level I was written on that assumption rather than adapted to it afterwards.

Two

The assessment is written first

Each assessment exists before the teaching it tests, so that the lesson is built toward a standard rather than the standard assembled afterwards from whatever was taught.

Three

Speaking is assessed by speaking

Recorded, marked by a person, with feedback written against a named pronunciation target. There is no automated pronunciation score, and none is claimed.

Four

Every lesson plans for the learner who does not follow

A second explanation, the mistakes that point provokes, and what causes them, are written into the plan in advance — not improvised when someone looks lost.

The Record

What is written down.

The programme is not a syllabus with lesson titles. It is 60 modules, 414 planned items, and a teaching support record of 530 authored entries across 17 fields.

60Modules
414Planned items
17Support fields per lesson
530Authored support entries

The authored support layer is complete for Level I; three further record fields hold no entries yet and are listed as not yet evidenced. The figures above are what exists, not what is planned.

The Empty Column

Nothing here has been observed in a classroom.

This is the most important sentence in the section, so it is not in a footnote.

Why it is empty

The College has taught no one

Every entry in the support record declares where its knowledge came from, and one of the four possible sources is a real classroom. That column stands at zero and will stand at zero until a teacher teaches a cohort and writes down what happened.

Why it is a column at all

So that it cannot be quietly filled by something else

The easy version of this record would mark everything simply "our teaching experience". Keeping observation separate means the College can say precisely which of its claims a first term would confirm, correct or overturn — and cannot pass off design for evidence.

The Anatomy

What every lesson carries.

Before

A named prerequisite

What the learner must already hold for this lesson to work. Stating it makes the sequence checkable: a lesson whose prerequisite is not taught anywhere earlier is a defect, and it can be found automatically rather than discovered by a confused class.

During

Stages with declared minutes

Each stage has a purpose and a duration. The durations sum to the lesson, and the sum is checked. A lesson plan whose stages do not add up is a plan nobody has actually run through.

After

A named outcome and its assessment

What the learner can now do, and the assessment that would show it. Both are written before the lesson is delivered, which is the only order in which the lesson can be built toward the standard.

Planned For

The learner who does not follow the first explanation.

Most lesson plans describe the successful path. These describe the unsuccessful one too, in advance, because that is where teaching actually happens.

Written in advance

A second explanation

A different route to the same point, for the learner the first route did not reach. Improvising this at the moment of confusion is what experienced teachers do; writing it down is what lets a less experienced teacher do it too.

Written in advance

The mistakes this point provokes

Named specifically, for this lesson, rather than as general advice. A mistake you were expecting is a teaching opportunity; the same mistake unexpected is an interruption.

Written in advance

Why those mistakes happen

The cause, not just the symptom — interference from a first language, an over-applied rule, an arbitrary feature of English that simply has to be learned. A teacher who knows the cause can address it; one who only knows the error can only correct it.

Written in advance

What to do with the learner who finishes early

An extension that is genuinely harder rather than merely longer. Otherwise the strongest learner in the room spends the lesson waiting.

Checked, Not Trusted

The plan is verified against itself.

A programme this size cannot be kept consistent by care alone. Several properties are checked automatically and fail the build when broken.

Sequence

Every prerequisite is taught earlier

A lesson that depends on something the programme never teaches, or teaches later, is a defect that no amount of proofreading reliably finds.

Timing

Stage minutes sum to the lesson

Checked arithmetically. It is the cheapest possible signal that a plan has been thought through rather than assembled.

Coverage

Every module carries its assessments

Quiz, assignment and rubric, to one published policy, across all 60 modules. Consistency at that scale is enforced or it does not exist.

Four Kinds of Knowledge

Not all teaching knowledge is the same kind of thing.

Blurring these is how a designed judgement comes to be cited as evidence. Each entry in the record is marked with exactly one.

Derived

359 entries

Read off the programme itself: the prerequisite a lesson names, the minutes its stages declare, the confusion its own self-check was written to trap. Not an opinion — a fact about the curriculum, and checkable against it.

Established

19 entries

Attested in the international teaching of English and not particular to this College — that the third-person -s is among the most persistent errors for learners of every first language, that countability in English is arbitrary and must be memorised. Synthesis, and marked as synthesis.

Designed

152 entries

An authored judgement by the people who wrote the curriculum: how else to explain this, which analogy holds, how to stretch a learner who has finished early. Defensible, and improvable by anyone who teaches it and finds better.

Observed

0 entries

What actually happened in a room with real learners. This is empty, and it is empty because the College has taught nobody. It is not filled with the other three.

Why this record was rebuilt

It began with three states and most of it empty, on the reasoning that a teacher’s knowledge comes from teaching and this College has taught no one. That was right about one kind of knowledge and wrong about three others — and the conflation left the Teacher’s Companion unwritable for a reason that did not actually apply to most of what it would contain.

Coverage

Complete for Level I, and honest about the rest.

Level I

19 teaching lessons, supported in depth

The eight authored support fields are written for every Level I teaching lesson — the level a beginner meets first and the level where a teacher has least room to improvise. Three of the seventeen record fields hold no entries yet, and the record lists them as not yet evidenced rather than hiding them.

Levels II&ndash;VI

Partially written

The fields readable from the curriculum are populated across the programme; the authored fields are being written level by level. What exists is what is published; nothing is projected.

The whole record

Openly countable

530 authored entries today. The figure moves as the work proceeds and is generated from the record rather than typed into this page.

The Volume

What it contains.

Every lesson

All 19 teaching lessons of Level I

Each with its stages and their declared minutes in the running head, so a teacher can see the shape of the lesson without turning back to the plan.

Every panel

Marked with its provenance

DERIVED, ESTABLISHED or DESIGNED, printed on the panel itself rather than explained once in a preface. A teacher disagreeing with a panel can see immediately whether they are disagreeing with the curriculum, with the field, or with the authors.

One mark absent

OBSERVED

The fourth mark does not appear anywhere in the book, and the front matter says why. A companion that quietly dropped the category would be claiming a kind of authority it does not have.

How It Is Made

Generated from the record, not written alongside it.

One source

The book cannot disagree with the curriculum

It is composed directly from the same record the platform teaches from. A companion maintained by hand drifts from the programme within one revision, and every teacher who trusts it then teaches something the College no longer says.

Counted, not asserted

The figures in the front matter are measured

The number of panels of each kind is counted from the pages as composed, not from the database that fed them — because some entries render in a running head rather than as a panel, and a front matter that said otherwise would be wrong in a way nobody would ever check.

Availability

How to obtain a copy.

Not a download on this site

The Companion is a typeset volume produced by AIPC Press and is not published for download here. Teachers, reviewers and anyone assessing the College’s academic work can request a copy from info@worldwencollege.co.uk. It is offered particularly to anyone willing to review it — every volume the College has produced is currently unreviewed by anyone who did not write it.

The Position

Stated at the top, not at the bottom.

What is true today

No lesson has been observed. No continuing professional development has been recorded for anyone — the record that would hold it contains 0 entries. No teacher has taught a AIPC cohort, because there has been no cohort. Any page describing teacher development at this College as a running programme would be describing something that does not exist.

Why It Matters More Than It Sounds

One term of teaching is worth more than another year of writing.

This is the College’s own assessment of where it stands, and it is uncomfortable enough to be worth publishing.

What writing produced

A complete, inspectable programme

60 modules, 414 planned items, every assessment written with published criteria, and 530 entries of teaching support. All of it can be examined by anyone.

What writing cannot produce

Any of the fourth kind of knowledge

What a real class does with a lesson, where the plan breaks, which explanation works with which learner, how long a stage actually takes. None of it can be reasoned out. It can only be observed.

The consequence

The single most valuable appointment is a practising teacher

One teacher, one cohort, one term generates evidence the College cannot obtain any other way — and would immediately improve material that is currently designed rather than tested. It is named as a priority on Careers.

Built and Waiting

What the first term will be written into.

The observation state

A field on every support entry

When a lesson is observed, what was learned is recorded against the specific entry it revises, marked as observed rather than designed. The revision is visible as a revision instead of replacing the original silently.

The development record

Per member of staff

A record of continuing professional development exists per person and stands empty. It will be filled by what is actually undertaken, not by what is intended.

The instructor workspace

Where marking and feedback happen

Built and tested, and it has marked nothing, because there is nothing to mark yet.

The commitment attached to this

When observation begins, what it overturns will be published as an overturning. A record in which the designed judgement always turns out to have been right is a record nobody is actually checking.
