Admissions · WorldWide English College

Studying from your own country.

A learner outside Britain is owed the plain version before they spend anything. The College teaches online, sponsors no immigration application, and confers an award that is a record of English proficiency — and everything below follows from those three facts.

Guidance for learners applying from outside the United Kingdom. It states what the College is and what its award does at a border. It then sets out what genuinely differs between countries: the clock, assessment, paying under currency controls, how a name is printed, and the terms for a short educational visit to Britain.

Where you study
Wherever you live
Immigration documents
Issued by governments alone
Deadlines

Educational visits
Optional, and separate

“The award is a record of English. A border is a matter for a government.”

I The Position

What the College is, what its award is for, and who answers a question about a border.

Said before anything else on the page.

A reader here may be weighing tuition against the cost of an application to a government. That reader is owed the position in the first screen.

The position

WorldWide English College teaches online. It sponsors no immigration application, issues no document that any government accepts as the basis of permission to enter or remain, and gives no immigration advice. That holds for every level, every fee arrangement and every nationality. It is stated in full at the visa position, and nothing here qualifies it.

What it is

A college that teaches at a distance

Every level is taught, marked and examined online. There is no campus to attend and no country a learner must be standing in. Lagos, Jakarta and Riyadh receive the same teaching at the same price.

What the award is

An academic record of English

An award states the CEFR level a learner reached and carries the transcript that earned it. It is an academic document and reads as one. Where an institution or a government names a particular test by title, that test is what they mean.

Where advice comes from

The government issuing the permission

Immigration rules are written and applied by governments, and they change. The dependable statement of them is the official government website of the country concerned, and, where a case is difficult, an adviser regulated there.

Anyone selling a College visa, or entry to Britain through this College, is offering what the College could not provide for itself. The warning and the address to report it are printed on the Admissions pillar.

II Any Country

One programme, every longitude. What changes with a time zone, and what does not.

Nothing here is set to a London hour.

The programme is taught asynchronously. No lesson, reading or recording asks a learner to be awake at an hour chosen somewhere else, and an international cohort rests on that.

The clock

The only clock you meet is a deadline

Lessons, readings, recordings and the Listening Lab open when you open them. There is no attendance to keep, because attendance measures the wrong thing in a programme designed to be studied at any hour.

Your time zone

A deadline falls at 23:59 where you are

Every deadline resolves against the time zone recorded on your account, so a calendar day is worth the same everywhere. The setting is yours to change. A change applies to work you have not opened; an assessment already running keeps its own clock.

Tutorials

Booked inside your own daylight

A tutorial is asked for rather than timetabled, and placed in a window you name. The College asks no learner to accept an appointment between 22:00 and 07:00 in their own time zone. The tutor keeps the learner’s clock.

The College’s clocks

Published in UTC, converted once

Where the College states an hour of its own — a marking return, a maintenance window — it publishes it in UTC. One conversion done once beats a local time meaning a different moment in every country reading it.

III Assessment

Where a candidate sits changes the logistics. It never changes the standard.

Assessment that rewards no particular longitude.

An examination released at one fixed hour hands an advantage to whoever lives beside that hour. The College sets assessment so that a candidate’s address is invisible to it.

Windows

A window, rather than an hour

An examination is released as a window measured in days, and the clock starts when a candidate opens the paper. Nobody sits down at 03:00 to start level with everybody else.

Marking

Marked against a rubric, not a region

Every summative is marked against criteria published before the work was set, and second-marked. A marker sees the script and the rubric. The country it came from is not shown and is not part of the judgement.

Holidays

An extension that asks for no reason

A national holiday, a religious observance and a family obligation fall on different days in every country. Rather than judge between them, the College grants the standing extension to anyone who asks before the deadline, once per module, with no document.

The same discipline, pointed inward

The College holds itself to clocks a learner can hold it to. A marked script comes back by a stated UTC deadline. Where the platform is unavailable, every deadline the outage touched moves for everybody affected at once.

  • Deadlines resolve in the time zone recorded on your account, and the setting is yours.
  • One extension of up to ten working days per module, asked for before the deadline.
  • An outage moves the deadlines it touched, for everyone affected, on the College’s initiative.
  • An appeal goes to somebody who took no part in the decision — see the appeals procedure.
IV Paying

One price, one currency, and the routes money takes to reach it.

Paying from a country that makes paying difficult.

The College charges one fee to everybody, in one currency. What differs is the route the money travels, and the College treats that route as its own problem rather than the learner’s.

What you are facingWhat the College does
Your bank converts at its own rateThe published price is in US dollars and does not move. The conversion is between you and your bank, and the College publishes no local figure a rate could falsify.
Your jurisdiction restricts outbound paymentA third party may pay on your behalf. Paying for a person buys a seat and nothing else: the record, the marks and every decision in them stay the student’s.
A transfer is held by a bank or a regulatorSend the Registrar the bank’s own notice. Your place is held 30 days beyond the payment date, once per level, at no charge and with the instalment plan intact.
A transfer arrives short after intermediary chargesThe College credits what arrived and carries the shortfall to your next instalment. An enrolment is never reversed over a correspondent bank’s fee.
Payment cannot lawfully be accepted from your jurisdictionThe College says so at the point of application rather than taking money it must return. The ground is published in the admissions policy.
Routes

Several routes, offered in a published order

The platform holds routing preferences for the countries it has mapped, and offers the route likeliest to succeed where you are. That order is published, because a payment page failing in silence reads as a refusal.

Receipts

A receipt a ministry will accept

Every payment produces a receipt number held against your record. Where an employer, a sponsor or a ministry needs it on College letterhead, Admissions issues it against that number.

The fee schedule, the currency position and the refund windows are set out once, on the tuition page. A figure published twice will eventually disagree with itself, so this leaf carries only what paying from abroad adds.

V Names

How a name is taken, held, sorted and printed across four regions.

A name is not a form field.

The College’s learners come from regions that order names differently and write them in different scripts. A register built on one convention mis-files much of the cohort and prints the wrong thing on a certificate.

Two fields

The name you print, and the name we file by

The record holds your full name exactly as it should appear, and separately the element the register files on. A certificate prints the first, an index uses the second, and neither may overwrite the other. That is where most systems go wrong.

Script

Your own script, on the same document

Where your name is written in Arabic, Persian, Urdu, Cyrillic, Chinese or another script, the record holds that form beside the Latin one. On request a printed certificate carries both, and the verification service resolves a code from either.

Spelling

The transliteration is yours to give

The College never transliterates a name on a learner’s behalf. It asks once, at registration, for the Latin spelling you use on your own documents or choose for yourself, and then uses it everywhere without amending it to a house style.

Corrections

Corrected on the day, at no charge

A misspelt, mis-ordered or changed name is corrected by writing to the Registrar, and digital certificates and transcripts are reissued free for life. A change is recorded with its date, and the earlier form kept, so an old certificate still verifies.

Why this is written down at all

A name printed wrongly is not a clerical matter to the person holding the certificate. It is the document they hand an employer, and a name set in an order they do not use argues with their passport. Getting it right is the cheapest respect an institution can pay.

VI Visits to Britain

A short educational visit, on standing terms, and entirely apart from the qualification.

A short visit to Britain, on terms published in advance.

The qualification is taught, examined and awarded without a learner leaving their own country. Where somebody nonetheless wants time in an English-speaking city, these are the standing terms — published so a traveller reads them before paying anything.

What it is

A short educational visit, measured in days

Language practice, seminars and cultural visits, on a written schedule published before any money is asked for. It is arranged apart from enrolment, paid apart from tuition, and offered only once its dates are settled.

What it changes

The award is settled without it

A visit carries no assessment, no credit and no mark, and appears nowhere on the academic record. A learner who never travels holds precisely the award a traveller holds, marked to the same standard by the same people.

How you travel

The visitor route, applied for by you

A visit of this kind is made on the ordinary visitor route. The traveller applies directly to the government, on its published criteria, and the decision belongs to that government alone. This is not a study route, and it permits no work and no stay.

The standing terms

  1. 1Booked at least 90 days before it runs. An application to a government takes time, and a later booking leaves a traveller applying against a clock. Bookings close at 90 days rather than let somebody gamble on the post.
  2. 2Documented with facts, and only facts. On request the College issues a letter stating that you are enrolled, which level you study, the dates and content of the visit, and who is paying. It asserts nothing else.
  3. 3Refunded in full if permission is refused. Tell the Registrar and the visit fee returns within 20 working days. Your place is untouched, your level continues, and the refusal is recorded nowhere in your academic record.
  4. 4Separate from the qualification, in both directions. Declining a visit costs a learner nothing academically, and taking one earns a learner nothing academically. That symmetry keeps the offer honest.
  5. 5Adults, on the same conduct standard. The College admits adults, so a visit is for adults. It runs on the conduct rules that govern the programme, and a place may be withdrawn on those rules alone.
What the College will not do, said plainly

It will not sponsor an application, promise an outcome, or assist a visit presented as a route to work or settlement in the United Kingdom. A traveller who describes it that way is asking for what the College refuses any applicant.

A residency in Britain is a different arrangement: an assessed component of a level, which may equally be completed in your own city under supervision for the same award. Its terms are at the residency in Britain.

VII Answered Plainly

The five questions international applicants ask, in the words the College would use.

Asked directly, answered directly.

No. Studying here grants no permission to enter, remain or work anywhere. The College teaches online and sponsors no immigration application. Permission to travel is granted by the government of the country you travel to, on its own criteria, and by nobody else.
It is a record of English proficiency and reads as one. Where a university or a government names IELTS, TOEFL or a Cambridge examination by title, that test is what satisfies them. The upper levels prepare for those tests directly, so plan on sitting the one they name.
It can state facts: that you are enrolled, which level you study, and the dates of any educational visit you have booked. It states them and stops. The College gives no immigration advice and makes no representation to any government on anybody’s behalf.
Write to Admissions with your country and what the gateway said. The platform offers alternatives in a published order, and your place is held while a route is found. A payment problem is the College’s to solve, not a reason to lose a seat.
The order you give. The record keeps the printed form and the filing element apart, so a certificate matches your own papers without breaking the register. If it is already printed wrongly, write to the Registrar; correction and reissue both cost nothing.

Read the position, then decide.

Everything a learner outside Britain needs to weigh this programme is published before an application is made, including the parts it profits the College nothing to print.
