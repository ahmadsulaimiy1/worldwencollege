Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools · Internal architecture

The Royal Standard

مدارس السلطان حنفي الملكية

A master blueprint for the SHRS Learning & Academic Management Platform — the architecture settled before a line of it is built. Not a copy of Canvas, Moodle or Blackboard: an institution-shaped system that can hold secular secondary education, the classical Islamic sciences, and complete Qur'ān memorisation inside one coherent ecosystem, and still be recognisably SHRS on every screen.

Phase 2 of 3 — architecture Status Draft for approval Precedes all implementation
00

The mandate

The instruction was to stop, study the field, and design the whole thing before building any of it. This document is that design. It is written to be argued with — every structural decision below is stated plainly enough to be rejected.

Three standing constraints govern everything that follows, and they are the reason this is not simply a feature list.

  • Nothing may feel added afterwards. A module that has to be bolted on later is a module the model got wrong. The test applied throughout: could a programme SHRS has not yet invented be launched without a schema migration or a redesign?
  • The institution is the product, not the software. A student should feel they are studying inside a great institution, not operating an application. That is a design constraint with engineering consequences — chiefly that the credential, the record and the seal are first-class objects, not PDF exports.
  • Excellence over delivery speed. Where a shortcut exists, it is named and rejected in writing, so the decision survives the people who made it.
On honesty of claims

A platform that issues credentials must never overstate what it has verified. Throughout this design, uploading a document is distinguished from verifying it; completing a programme is distinguished from being accredited by an external body. Every screen that could be misread as an accreditation claim says plainly what it is and is not. This is not timidity — it is what makes the seal worth something.

01

What the study found

The brief asked for a study of more than a hundred leading institutions. The efficient route to that coverage is not a hundred homepages — it is the platforms those institutions actually run, plus the standards bodies that bind them together.

By enrolment share in North America, Canvas holds roughly 50%, Brightspace about 20%, Blackboard about 12% and Moodle about 9% — so four platform families account for around nine in ten institutions in scope, from Harvard and MIT (Open edX) through state systems to secondary schools. Studying those families, the enterprise student-record systems behind them (Ellucian Banner, Workday Student), the credential standards they must speak, and the specialist Qur'ān platforms that none of them can accommodate, covers the field far better than a survey of marketing sites.

What the leaders genuinely do well

Canvas
Open integration surface

Its advantage is not features but LTI openness — the institution can adopt a specialist tool without waiting for the vendor. Worth taking: never let a module become the only way to do something.

Brightspace
Outcomes as evidence

Targets WCAG 2.2 AAA and EN 301 549, and treats learning outcomes as accreditation evidence rather than decoration. Worth taking: outcomes must be queryable, not narrative.

Moodle
Source-code sovereignty

Twenty years of accreditor recognition and 1,800+ plugins, because the institution owns the code. Worth taking: SHRS must own its data model outright.

Open edX
Content as components

The xBlock architecture lets new interaction types be added without touching the core. Worth taking — but not its operational weight: 20+ containers in a default install.

Where all of them fail SHRS

Every mainstream LMS encodes one shape of learning: course → module → item → completion, assessed once, marked, and closed. That model is a good fit for a semester of Chemistry. It cannot express hifdh at all.

  • Memorisation is a stock, not a course. A ḥāfiẓ does not complete Juz' 30 — they hold it, and holding decays without revision. A completion checkbox is the wrong instrument in principle, not in detail.
  • Oral assessment is second-class. Mainstream platforms treat recitation as a file upload with a comment. The classical error taxonomy — laḥn jalī against laḥn khafī — has no representation anywhere in them.
  • Transmission has no model. An ijāzah carries a sanad: an ordered chain of named teachers. No LMS on earth models a credential whose value lies in its chain.
  • The registrar is an afterthought. Banner is Oracle/PL-SQL with a data model largely unchanged beneath a newer web skin; upgrades are disruptive and customisations accumulate as debt. Credential production is bolted to third parties.

The standards SHRS will speak

Owning the model does not mean inventing the interfaces. These are settled, and adopting them costs little now and saves everything later.

02

Three worlds, one institution

This is the intellectual centre of the design. SHRS is unusual — it runs three educational traditions that have genuinely different shapes, and the platform's whole architecture turns on refusing to flatten them.

TraditionUnit of progressAssessmentEnds?
Secular secondary (NERDC → WAEC / NECO / JAMB)Term and cohort; a fixed syllabus moving at one paceContinuous assessment plus a terminal written exam, increasingly computer-basedYes — at the certificate
Islamic sciences (Arabic, fiqh, Ḥanafī)Text: a matn, then its sharḥ, under a named teacherOral examination and the teacher's authorisation to transmitYes — at the ijāzah
Qur'ān hifdhPortion of a fixed 604-page, 6,236-āyah space, individually pacedOral recitation, error-classified, before a teacherNo — retention is maintained for life

A student at SHRS is inside all three at once. On one Tuesday they sit a Chemistry continuous-assessment test, recite their sabaq to a teacher, and read a passage of matn. One timetable, one attendance record, one parent, one fee, one report.

The decision this forces

Most platforms would model this as three products stitched together — and it would feel stitched, forever. The alternative is to find the abstraction that all three are special cases of, and make the differences data rather than code. That is what §03 does, and it is the single decision the rest of the platform hangs from.

03

The Academic Object Model

Four primitives. Everything the institution teaches, assesses, records and certifies is expressed in them — and the behavioural differences between traditions live in one field.

Why the Attainment Record is append-only

The record is the institution's memory, and memory that can be quietly rewritten is worthless at exactly the moment it matters — a disputed grade, an audit, a graduate's employer calling in 2043. Corrections are therefore new records that supersede, never edits. The current mark is a computed view over the chain; the chain itself is immutable and carries who changed what, when, and on whose authority.

This costs a little storage and buys the thing no LMS gives you back once it is lost: an academic history that can be defended.

04

The nine pillars

The domain divided into nine bounded contexts. Each owns its data and exposes it through a defined contract; none reaches into another's tables. This is what allows the platform to be built in phases without the later phases forcing rewrites of the earlier ones.

Pillar I
Identity & People

One person, many roles, held over time — a student becomes an alumnus, a parent is also a teacher. Roles are dated grants, so history stays truthful.

Pillar II
Admissions

Enquiry through offer to enrolment, as a resumable multi-step application with optional document upload — never a single form that loses work.

Pillar III
Curriculum

Programmes, study units, modalities, outcomes. The authored spine — versioned, so a syllabus change never rewrites last year's records.

Pillar IV
Delivery

Timetable, attendance, halaqah and class sessions, lesson materials, live sessions. Where the school day actually happens.

Pillar V
Assessment

Items, banks, sittings, submissions, oral recitations, marking, moderation. Written and oral treated as equals, not one as a special case of the other.

Pillar VI
The Register

Attainment records, progression, awards, transcripts, ijāzāt. The append-only spine and the institution's legal memory.

Pillar VII
Finance

Fees, instalments, scholarships, receipts, reconciliation — multi-currency and multi-provider from day one, given the Nigerian payments landscape.

Pillar VIII
Communication

Announcements, teacher–parent correspondence, results release, notifications. Logged, because a school is judged on what it told whom and when.

Pillar IX
Insight

Event stream, dashboards, early support signals. Reads from every pillar; writes to none.

Reuse already available

A substantial part of Pillars I, II, VI and VII is already engineered in the sibling codebase on Cloudflare — roughly 130 tables covering enrolment, a four-provider payment router, signing keys, credential signatures, issued documents with verification codes, and an evidence register. Pillar V's oral machinery is largely there too: learner_recordings, recording_upload_parts, pronunciation_targets and private R2 audio storage with authorised playback. That is most of the hifdh recitation pipeline, already built and tested.

05

The Hifdh Engine

This is where SHRS beats every platform in the world, because none of them attempt it. The crest names it — Complete Qur'ān Memorisation — and the platform should make that claim technically real.

The Qur'ān is a fixed, fully addressable space: 114 sūrah, 30 juz', 6,236 āyāt, 604 pages of the standard muṣḥaf. Every portion of it can be given a stable identifier, and every learner holds a state against every portion. That is the model — not a course, a holding.

The error taxonomy is the differentiator

When a student recites, the teacher marks errors against the portion — and the taxonomy is the classical one, because it is the one that carries meaning:

  • Laḥn jalī — clear error: a wrong word, a wrong ending, a changed meaning. Blocks progression on that portion.
  • Laḥn khafī — subtle error: a tajwīd rule mis-applied. Tracked and coached, does not block.
  • Each error is tagged to a rule — ikhfā', idghām, iqlāb, qalqalah, madd and its measures — and to an āyah position.

Tagging at that grain produces something no ordinary gradebook can: a tajwīd weakness profile per student, per class, per teacher. A teacher can see that eleven of their thirty students consistently miss madd munfaṣil length, and teach it on Monday. That is the difference between recording marks and improving recitation.

Recitations are captured as audio against the portion — the existing recording pipeline already handles multipart upload, private storage and authorised playback — so a mark is never an unevidenced assertion, and a disputed assessment can be re-heard.

Sanad and ijāzah as a modelled chain

An ijāzah is not a certificate of attendance; its worth is the sanad, the named chain of transmission behind it. SHRS should model that chain explicitly — an ordered list of named authorities with the riwāyah (Ḥafṣ, Warsh, and others) recorded — and issue the ijāzah as a signed verifiable credential carrying the chain inside it.

Why this matters beyond SHRS

No learning platform anywhere issues a cryptographically verifiable credential whose payload is a chain of transmission. Doing it correctly — Open Badges 3.0 over the W3C Verifiable Credentials model, with the sanad as structured data rather than prose on a certificate — would be a genuine first, and it is achievable with the signing infrastructure already built.

06

The Registrar's Works

The brief asked that the Registrar operate something closer to a credential manufactory than a set of forms. That is the right instinct, and it is mostly an architectural question: documents must be produced from the record, never typed.

What the Registrar's console must do

  • Issue in batch. A cohort's certificates produced in one authorised action, serialised, sealed and logged — not one form at a time.
  • Reissue without rewriting history. A replacement carries a new serial and a visible link to the original; the original is never deleted.
  • Answer verifications. An employer or university scans the QR and gets a plain, unambiguous statement of what SHRS certifies — and nothing it does not.
  • Rotate keys safely. Signatures record the key that made them, so a rotated key does not invalidate a decade of past awards.
  • Show the audit trail. Every conferral, correction and reissue attributable to a named officer at a timestamp.

Transcripts follow the same discipline: rendered from the register on demand, in an SHRS typographic standard, with a verification code — and structured as a Comprehensive Learner Record so that a student's secular results and their ijāzāt can sit in one document without either pretending to be the other.

07

Quiet intelligence

The brief was exact: AI throughout, but quietly — assist, never distract. That is a governance rule as much as a design one, so it is written as constraints first.

  • No AI mark is ever final. Machine assessment proposes; a teacher disposes. On a credential-bearing judgement a human is always the author of record.
  • No chat bubble in the corner. Intelligence appears where the work is — a suggested revision plan inside the plan, a flagged submission inside the marking queue.
  • Everything suggested is attributable and reversible. If the system drafted it, the record says so.
  • No AI in the sacred path without scholarly sign-off. Automated tajwīd feedback is a coaching aid shown to the student and teacher; it never issues an ijāzah, never certifies a portion, and its limits are stated on screen.

08

Instrumentation

Dashboards at every level — institution, department, programme, class, teacher, student, registrar, finance — all reading one event stream rather than each querying the tables their own way. One definition of "attendance", computed once.

The research on early-alert systems is genuinely useful here, and part of it is a warning. Systems that predict course failure from engagement, missed work and declining scores do work — but two failure modes are well documented and both are designed out here rather than discovered later.

  • Target leakage. A model that quietly uses information unavailable at prediction time scores beautifully in testing and fails in use. Every SHRS signal must be computable from data that existed before the moment it claims to predict — enforced in how features are built, not checked afterwards.
  • Alerts that harm. A flag with no agreed response, or one that follows a student as a label, does damage. So: every signal names an intervention and an owner, signals expire, and no student is shown a prediction about themselves as a verdict.

What a student sees of their own data is progress and effort, honestly presented — including for hifdh, where the meaningful number is not pages memorised but retention held.

09

The Royal Standard

The design language, taken from the crest rather than invented alongside it. This page is drawn in it, so it can be judged rather than described.

Palette

The crest is a gold armorial shield on an obsidian field, bordered in bright gold, set within a pearl eight-point star. That is the palette; nothing needs adding to it.

TokenValueRole
Obsidian#0B0C0FThe ground. Everything sits on it.
Antique gold#C7A560Structure — rules, small caps, the shield itself.
Bright gold#F0C244The single accent. Used rarely enough to still mean something.
Pearl#EDE9DFReading text on dark.
Warm grey#8C8677A grey biased toward the gold, so it reads as chosen.

The eight-point star

The khātim on the crest is two squares, one rotated forty-five degrees. That construction is the platform's structural motif — section markers, focus states, loading states, the grid itself. It appears throughout this document. It is used as geometry, never as ornament pasted onto a surface.

Typography

An all-serif document, because SHRS is a school of classical scholarship and a charter reads better than a dashboard. A humanist display face with inscriptional weight for headings, a transitional serif for reading, a monospace for codes, serials and identifiers — and أميري for Arabic, set at a size that respects it rather than shrinking it to fit an English line.

Bilingual is structural, not a translation layer

Arabic is not an afterthought at SHRS, so the platform is built with a full right-to-left mirror of every surface — direction, layout, numerals and typography — rather than a language toggle over an English skeleton. The sibling codebase already carries this discipline with a parallel /ar tree and RTL partials, which is a pattern worth inheriting wholesale.

Motion

Weight, not bounce. Everything moves like a heavy, well-machined object: 200–400ms, eased, never springy, never playful. Motion confirms an action or reveals structure; it does not decorate. And it respects prefers-reduced-motion — as this page does.

Mobile

Desktop is the flagship; mobile is not a reduction of it. Different layouts, same standard — which in practice means the phone gets the things a phone is for (today's plan, this week's recitation, a fee notice, a message from a teacher) at full quality, rather than a shrunken registrar console nobody can use.

10

Platform & security

The recommendation is to build on the stack already proven in the sibling codebase, because it is a genuinely good fit for a Nigerian institution and it is already working.

Edge
Cloudflare Pages + Functions

Served from points of presence close to Nigerian users, with no server to maintain. Bandwidth and cold-start behaviour matter more here than in Europe or North America.

Data
D1, with a defined exit

SQLite at the edge — right for the first phase. §11 sets the threshold at which this must be revisited, so the decision is dated rather than assumed permanent.

Objects
Private R2

Recitations and identity documents. Never a public or signed URL — every read passes an authorisation check, as the existing buckets already enforce.

Delivery
Server-rendered, progressive

No heavy client framework for pages that must work on a mid-range Android over patchy 3G. Interactivity where it earns its weight.

Security

  • Permissions are fine-grained and role-dated. A teacher sees their classes; a form teacher sees their form; the registrar sees the register. Every grant is an event with a start and an end.
  • Everything consequential is audited — who saw a record, who changed a mark, who issued a document, on whose authority.
  • Academic history is immutable by construction, per §03.
  • Credential integrity by signature, not by database trust, per §06.

Nigerian compliance is not optional

In February 2026 the Nigeria Data Protection Commission issued a 21-day compliance notice to educational institutions, requiring evidence of Compliance Audit Returns, a designated Data Protection Officer, and registration as a Data Controller of Major Importance where applicable — with administrative penalties for those who cannot demonstrate adequate practice. SHRS processes children's data, which is exactly the high-risk category the Commission is targeting.

So the platform is built to make compliance demonstrable rather than asserted:

  • A data inventory that is generated from the schema, not maintained by hand in a document nobody updates.
  • Retention periods declared per data class — recitation audio in particular — and enforced by a scheduled purge, which the existing codebase already implements.
  • Consent and guardian authority modelled explicitly for students under 18.
  • Breach detection and a reporting path, because the Act requires notification.
  • A DPIA prepared for the high-risk processing before it goes live, not after.
11

The scale path

The brief asks for an architecture that could serve ten schools, a hundred, or a million learners without redesign. That is achievable, but only if the tenancy decision is made now — retrofitting it is the single most expensive mistake available here.

So: every row that belongs to a school carries a school identifier from the first migration, even while there is exactly one school. It costs almost nothing today and it is the difference between opening a second campus in a week and rebuilding for a year.

StageShapeWhat changes
One schoolSingle D1 database, tenant column present and always setNothing — this is the starting point
A few campusesSame database, tenant-scoped queries and permissionsNothing structural; administration gains a school switcher
Many schoolsDatabase per tenant, one routing layerOnly the connection resolver — because every query was already tenant-scoped
Very largeRegional data stores; heavy reads moved off the write pathStorage engine, not the domain model

The honest caveat: D1 is right for stage one and probably stage two, and it is not where a million learners will live. The design keeps the domain model independent of the store, so that migration is an infrastructure project rather than a rewrite — and the trigger for it is named in advance rather than discovered during an outage.

13

Decisions I need

Everything above I can settle on the evidence. These five I should not decide alone — each changes what gets built, and a wrong assumption here is expensive later.

Where does this live?

The shroyalschools repository holds only photographs today. The sibling college codebase holds ~130 tables of directly reusable engineering. My recommendation is a fresh SHRS repository that ports the proven components, rather than either starting from nothing or branching a codebase carrying another institution's identity throughout.

Recommended — new repository, port the good parts

Which levels does SHRS actually run today?

The crest and signage say secular, Islamic/Arabic and complete Qur'ān memorisation, and the campus has real science laboratories. But the brief also names diplomas, degrees, research and executive education. I need to know which exist now, which are planned, and which are aspiration — the model accommodates all of them, but Phase II builds only what has real students.

Which examination tracks must be supported first?

WAEC, NECO, JAMB/UTME, BECE — and with the Federal Government moving school candidates' May/June examinations toward full computer-based testing, does SHRS want internal CBT practice built as part of Phase IV?

Do parents get their own portal in Phase II, or a view inside the student's?

A separate parent portal is significantly more work — separate identity, separate permissions, guardian relationships modelled properly. It is also, in my view, where a school of this kind wins or loses its reputation. My recommendation is to model guardianship properly in Phase I and ship the parent surface in Phase II.

Recommended — model in I, ship in II

How many students, teachers and campuses, now and in three years?

This is the number that decides whether D1 is right for stage two, how the murāja'ah scheduler is built, and how much of §11 is real rather than theoretical.
