## 2. Method

The Board directed: think first, research first, benchmark first, reason
first, and only then determine figures. This paper follows that order,
and the figures appear in section 7.

**What was benchmarked.** Real published prices, retrieved 18 August
2026. Where a figure could not be retrieved it is absent rather than
estimated; two premium comparators (The London School of English, ILSC)
are missing because the network refused those hosts, and the Board should
treat their absence as a gap in this paper rather than as evidence.

**What was measured internally.** The College's own record — lesson
counts, content volume, assignment counts and rubric complexity per
qualification — because the cost of delivering the Mastery Stage is a
fact about this institution, not about the market.

**What was not done.** No willingness-to-pay research, no applicant
interviews, no elasticity modelling. The College has no applicants to
ask. Every demand statement in this paper is therefore a judgement, and
is labelled as one.

## 3. The benchmark evidence

| Comparator | Published price | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| ASU Global Launch, self-paced online English | **$350 per 8-week course** | University-affiliated, online, self-paced |
| British Council / FutureLearn self-study | **$25–30 per month** | Online, self-study, subscription |
| British Council tutored, India | **≈472 INR per hour** | Live tutored, regional pricing |
| British Council tutored, Kuwait | **≈7.2 KWD per hour** | Live tutored, regional pricing |
| US in-person intensive ESL | **$1,200–$2,500 per month** | Campus, live, immersive |
| Accredited online ESL degree study | **$300–$600 per credit hour** | Accredited, credit-bearing |
| Cambridge B2 First / C1 / C2 | **€167–€228**; **$450** (US) | Examination only, internationally recognised |

Two things follow immediately.

**The self-paced online market is cheap.** ASU — a real university —
sells a self-paced eight-week online English course for $350. The
British Council sells self-study for the price of a streaming
subscription. Any self-paced online offer is priced against these,
whatever else it contains.

**Recognised examination is the expensive part, and we do not have it.**
A Cambridge certificate costs €167–€228 because it is recognised. That
recognition is the product. The College holds none.

---

## 4. The finding the Board must confront first

**The current price is $19,000.02, and it is not a price. It is
$19,000 divided by six.**

`programme_levels` carries $3,166.67 six times. Nobody costed a
qualification; somebody chose a round total and divided. The two cent
remainder is the proof.

That would matter less if the number were defensible. It is not:

- **It is 54× the closest true comparator.** ASU sells eight weeks of
  self-paced online English for $350. The College sells four months for
  $3,166.67.
- **It prices campus immersion without a campus.** $19,000 is roughly
  eight to fifteen months of US in-person intensive ESL — live teaching,
  a building, a visa, a city. The College delivers none of those and says
  so: there are no live classes, no attendance tracking and no video.
- **It prices accreditation the College does not hold.** Credit-bearing
  accredited study runs $300–$600 per credit hour *because the credits
  are recognised*. Ours are not recognised by anyone.
- **It prices conferral the College cannot perform.** No External
  Examiner is appointed. Nothing has been conferred on anyone. At
  $19,000 the College would be charging a year's salary in much of its
  target market for a qualification it currently cannot award.

The Finance Committee's position is that $19,000 is not a premium
position but an unsustainable one: the first informed applicant who
compares it to ASU does not negotiate, they leave, and they tell people
why.

**Correcting it is not a discount.** It is the removal of a number that
was never reasoned.

---

## 5. What the College actually sells

Between a $30 subscription and a $19,000 immersion there is a real
position, and it is the one the College occupies.

**More than self-study.** Ten rubric-graded assignments per
qualification, marked by a person against published criteria. A scripted
listening set and a pronunciation laboratory in every module. An
examination with a defined pass standard, two resits and a published
appeals route. Self-study platforms mark nothing and examine nothing.

**Less than an accredited programme.** No accreditation. No External
Examiner. No conferred award. No credit that transfers anywhere. No
campus, no live class, no cohort.

The honest description of the product is therefore: **a designed,
human-marked, examined English curriculum with published qualification
descriptors, awarded by an institution whose awards no external body has
yet assessed.** It should be priced as that, and as nothing else.

### The cost that actually varies

Content is a sunk cost: the curriculum is written. What varies per
learner, per qualification, is marking — and it is measurable.

| Qualification | Content volume | Signature rubric criteria | Marker standard needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation · ECIC | ~9k words | 1 | Trained marker |
| Development · HCIC | ~11k words | 1 | Trained marker |
| Application · CAEC | ~13k words | 2 | Experienced teacher |
| Professional · HCAEC | ~14k words | 3 | Experienced teacher |
| Advanced · ACEC | ~13k words | 3 | Senior, C1+ |
| Mastery · WEPC | ~19k words | 4 | Senior, C2, subject-expert |

Measured from the record, not asserted. Content roughly doubles and
rubric complexity quadruples across the framework. A Mastery assignment
is longer, is judged against four signature criteria rather than one, and
cannot be marked by someone who has not themselves reached C2.

**This is the basis for progressive pricing, and it is a real one.** The
Board asked for no mechanical increments. The increments below are not
mechanical because they are not increments: each figure is set against
the marking load and marker seniority that qualification requires.

---

## 6. Principles the Finance Committee applied

1. **Every fee corresponds to something a person does.** Marking, examining,
   supporting. Where nothing is done, nothing is charged — which is why
   there is no certification fee.
2. **The learner who commits is rewarded; the learner who does not is
   not punished.** Single-qualification pricing is a fair price, not a
   penalty rate designed to force commitment.
3. **No price ends in 99.** The College is not a retailer. Figures end in
   0 and are chosen, not computed.
4. **Nothing is charged twice.** The examination is part of the
   qualification, so the first attempt is in the tuition.
5. **No fee may fall on a learner for being poor.** No late fee, no
   reinstatement fee, no payment-plan surcharge.
6. **Price honestly against what we lack.** Until an External Examiner is
   appointed, the College charges for teaching and assessment, not for a
   credential's standing.

## 7. The architecture

### 7.1 Tuition

| Stage | Qualification | Single | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation | ECIC | **$690** | Lightest marking load, trained markers. Set above ASU's $350 because ten assignments are marked by a person and an examination is set; set below four figures because a beginner qualification that costs four figures excludes exactly the learner it is for. |
| Development | HCIC | **$740** | Marking load barely above Foundation, so the price barely moves. A larger step here would be arithmetic rather than cost. |
| Application | CAEC | **$880** | The register changes: academic English begins, rubric criteria double, an entry diagnostic is set and revisited. First genuine step up. |
| Professional | HCAEC | **$1,140** | The heaviest step in the framework, and the record says so. Three signature criteria, experienced markers, the stage most often repeated. |
| Advanced | ACEC | **$1,380** | Senior markers at C1+, rhetorical-effectiveness judgement. Fewer people can mark this. |
| Mastery | WEPC | **$1,650** | Longest content, four signature criteria, a capstone examination, and a marker who must themselves be at C2. The College's own name is on this certificate. |
| | **Six taken singly** | **$6,480** | |

### 7.2 The committed pathway

**All six, committed at entry: $4,980.**

The saving is $1,500 — more than the Foundation and Development
qualifications cost together ($1,430). The Committee chose the figure so
that sentence would be true and sayable: *commit to the framework and the
first two qualifications are, in effect, not charged.*

Partial commitment is rewarded proportionately but not equally, because
partial commitment is worth less:

| Committed together | Reduction from single prices |
|---|---|
| Two consecutive qualifications | **less $180** |
| Three consecutive | **less $520** |
| Four consecutive | **less $960** |
| All six | **less $1,500** ($4,980) |

**Alumni progression.** A holder returning for the next qualification
pays the difference between what they have paid and the pathway rate, not
the single price. A learner who completes ECIC at $690 and returns is not
charged as a stranger.

### 7.3 Examination, certification and records

| Item | Fee | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| First examination attempt | **Included** | It is part of the qualification. |
| First resit | **$95** | A second marking event by a person. Set far below Cambridge's €167–228 because this is an internal examination, not a recognised certificate — pricing near Cambridge would imply a standing we do not hold. |
| Second resit | **$95** | Same work, same fee. |
| Certificate on conferral | **No fee** | A College that charges to hand over what it has already assessed is selling the certificate rather than the education. |
| Graduation | **No fee** | There is no ceremony to cost. If one is ever held, it comes back to this Board. |
| Digital verification | **No fee, permanently** | Verification exists so a stranger can check a credential. Charging for it would make the credential less trustworthy, which is the opposite of its purpose. |
| Replacement certificate | **$40** | Reissue, re-signature and re-registration. Real administrative work. |
| Transcript | **No fee** | The learner's own record. |

### 7.4 Payment

No plan carries interest, a surcharge or a late fee. A learner in
difficulty is a learner to keep, not a margin to harvest.

| Plan | Terms |
|---|---|
| In full, single qualification | As table 7.1 |
| Single qualification, four instalments | Equal payments across the four months of study, first at enrolment |
| Pathway, twelve instalments | **$415 a month** for twelve months |
| Pathway, eighteen instalments | **$277 a month** — with the last payment $271, because the total is $4,980 and not a number chosen to divide neatly |
| Employer or sponsor invoiced | 30 days, single invoice, on a purchase order |

The eighteen-month plan brings the College's whole framework within
$277 a month. That is the number the Committee considers most
strategically important in this paper: it is below a month of British
Council tutored hours in Kuwait, and it is the figure that decides
whether a salaried adult in the target market can enrol at all.

### 7.5 Independent learner route — RECOMMENDED, DEFERRED

Assessment without teaching, for a candidate who already has the English
and wants the qualification: **$430 per qualification.**

The Committee recommends this route and recommends **not opening it
yet**. Selling assessment alone to someone who never studied with us
puts the entire weight of the price on the credential's standing — and
the credential has no external standing until an External Examiner is
appointed. Open it the term after that appointment, not before.

### 7.6 Organisations

| Route | Terms |
|---|---|
| Corporate, 5–19 seats | Pathway rate **less $300 a seat** |
| Corporate, 20–99 seats | Pathway rate **less $600 a seat** |
| Corporate, 100+ | By agreement, with a floor of **$3,600 a seat** |
| Government or ministry cohort | Minimum 25 seats, **$3,900 a seat**, single invoice, cohort reporting |
| Institutional partnership | Annual licence plus per-seat, negotiated, minimum three years |

The floor exists so that volume cannot argue the price below the cost of
marking. A hundred-seat contract still requires a thousand assignments to
be read by a person.

### 7.7 Scholarships and bursaries

The Committee will not recommend a scholarship scheme with an invented
budget. Instead the scheme is **funded by construction**:

- **One full pathway scholarship for every forty paid pathway
  enrolments.** Self-funding, visibly proportionate, and it cannot
  overcommit the College because it cannot exist before the revenue that
  pays for it does.
- **Bursary fund: one tenth of examination resit income.** A fund built
  from the learners who struggled, spent on the learners who cannot pay.
- **Hardship deferral, at no cost.** Any learner may pause a payment plan
  once per qualification without penalty, on request, without
  documentation. Requiring proof of poverty costs more in dignity than
  it saves in revenue.

No scholarship may be awarded before the Academic Senate has members: an
award of merit decided by nobody is a marketing device.

### 7.8 Referral and commission — RECOMMENDED AGAINST

International education runs on agent commission, typically 10–20% of
first-year tuition. **The Committee recommends the College pay none, and
recommends the Board record why.**

An agent paid by commission is paid to enrol, not to advise. Our
qualification is not accredited, is not recognised, and cannot yet be
conferred. A commissioned intermediary explaining that honestly earns
nothing; one explaining it dishonestly earns $500. The structure selects
for the second, and the College would be the last to find out.

Instead: **an alumni introduction credit of $120** against a returning
holder's next qualification when someone they introduced enrols and
completes their first month. Paid to a learner, not a salesperson;
payable only after the newcomer has stayed; and capped at two per person
per year.

Revisit after accreditation. The objection is to commission before
standing, not to commission for ever.

## 8. What this does to the College's finances

**Per pathway learner, taking all six:** $4,980, against $19,000 today.

**Break-even is a marking question, not a pricing one.** The one cost
that scales is human marking: sixty assignments and six examinations
across the framework. At $4,980 the College retains roughly $75 per
marked assessment before platform and support costs. Whether that is
sufficient depends on marker rates the College has not yet set, and the
Committee flags this as the single largest unknown in the paper. **The
Board should not adopt these figures without also setting a marker rate**,
because the two decisions are the same decision.

**Volume assumption: none.** No forecast appears here because the College
has no applicants, no conversion history and no marketing spend. A
revenue projection would be a fabrication with a chart on it.

## 9. Risks

**The price falls and the perceived value falls with it.** Real, and the
Committee's answer is that $19,000 buys no perception at all if nobody
pays it. Prestige comes from the qualification descriptors, the published
assessment standard, the free verification and the honesty of the
institutional-status page — not from a number nobody tested.

**A future accreditation makes these prices look low.** Likely, and it is
a good problem. The architecture is built to be repriced upward on a
governance event: accreditation, or the appointment of an External
Examiner, is the moment to return to this Board. Prices set here are
explicitly not permanent.

**Early enrolments at a low price become a precedent.** Mitigated: every
learner is enrolled under the tuition published on the day they enrol,
and the College honours it for the qualification they bought. It does not
honour it for qualifications they have not yet paid for.

**Instalment default.** A learner on the eighteen-month plan may stop
paying. The policy is that access pauses and the record persists;
nothing already earned is withdrawn for non-payment. Withdrawing an
assessed result over money would make the academic record a debt
collection instrument.

**Currency.** Only USD is settled. Every figure here is USD, and a
learner in Cairo or Jakarta meets a dollar price. The FX architecture
exists but no second currency has been activated, and until one is, the
$277 monthly figure is less accessible than it appears.

---

## 10. Alternatives considered and rejected

**Keep $19,000 and discount heavily.** Rejected. A list price nobody pays
is a lie with a strikethrough on it, and it teaches applicants to wait
for a sale.

**Flat price per qualification, as today.** Rejected. It charges the same
for marking a beginner's paragraph and a C2 capstone, which is not a
simplification but an inaccuracy.

**Subscription, monthly, cancel any time.** Rejected on academic grounds
rather than commercial ones. A subscription prices access to content; the
College's product is an assessed qualification with a conferral event. A
learner who cancels in month three of a four-month qualification has
bought nothing, and a structure that makes that common is badly designed.

**Free tuition, paid assessment.** Genuinely attractive and rejected for
now: it makes the credential the whole product at exactly the moment the
credential has no external standing. Reconsider after accreditation.

**Price by hour, like the tutored comparators.** Rejected. The College
does not sell hours — it sells a qualification with a fixed designed
extent — and hourly pricing would invite a comparison to live tuition it
would lose.

---

## 11. Recommendation

The Committee recommends the Board adopt **section 7 in full**, with two
conditions:

1. **Set a marker rate in the same resolution.** These prices are not
   safe to adopt without one (section 8).
2. **Keep the independent learner route closed** until an External
   Examiner is appointed (section 7.5).

And notes for the record that the Board is being asked to reduce
published tuition by roughly three-quarters, on the reasoning at section
4: the existing figure was never costed, and the College should not
defend a number simply because it is already on the page.

**On adoption, and not before,** these figures replace
`programme_levels.price_usd_cents`, the tuition page, the admissions
pages, the prospectus and the Arabic editions in a single change, with a
test binding every published figure to the record so the site and the
resolution cannot drift.
