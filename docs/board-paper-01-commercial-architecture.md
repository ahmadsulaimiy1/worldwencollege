# Board Paper 01 — The Commercial Architecture

**Third submission. For decision between two options.** Prepared for the
Executive Board by the Finance Committee, the Commercial Strategy
Committee, the Academic Council and the Registrar. First submitted 18
August 2026; returned twice; resubmitted 18 August 2026.

**Status: NOT ADOPTED. NOT PUBLISHED.** The live site continues to show
the existing tuition. Nothing here has been written to any public page,
and `programme_levels.price_usd_cents` is unchanged.

---

## The decision before the Board

The Board directed that this paper stop thinking in terms of low price
against high price, and think instead about institutional positioning —
and that it present two coherent trajectories rather than one
recommendation.

It does. **Option A** prices the College that exists. **Option B**
designs the College the Board intends, states what must be built to
become it, and prices that. They are not a cheap option and an expensive
one; they are two different institutions, and the Board is choosing which
to be.

The Committee has deliberately not chosen for the Board. It has,
however, done one thing the Board's direction implied and did not
require: **it has begun building the capability both options need**, so
that the choice is between two futures rather than between two documents.
Section 5 records what was built while this paper was being written.

| | Option A — Premium Digital College | Option B — Premium Global English College |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | The College as it stands, done excellently | An institution with people in it |
| Human contact per qualification | Assessment only | ~14 contact hours, a named tutor, an adviser |
| Pathway tuition | **$7,400** | **$14,800** |
| Must be built first | Speaking assessment *(begun)*, adviser contact | Everything in A, plus tutors, scheduling, cohorts, employer engagement, alumni services |
| Time to deliver | This term | Four to six terms |
| The risk | The College stays smaller than its ambition | The College sells what it has not yet built |

## What the Board returned, and what changed

The Board returned the first submission with five findings. Each is
accepted, and one of them changed the architecture rather than the
presentation.

| Finding | What changed |
|---|---|
| The value narrative must precede the numbers | The paper now opens with the educational argument. Figures appear at section 6. |
| $4,980 may understate the College's positioning | Accepted in part — and the reason it was low is section 3. The recommended pathway rises. |
| The comparison set was incomplete | Premium executive education, specialist institutes and corporate outcome-priced programmes are now benchmarked. |
| Tuition should follow capability, not marking load | **This changed the architecture.** Section 5 replaces the cost basis with a capability basis, and the curve is steeper at the top as a result. |
| The commercial ecosystem is broader than tuition | Section 7 is new: mentoring, coaching, memberships, employer pathways, family and regional provision, deferred payment. |

**And one finding the Committee must put back to the Board.** The
instruction was to price against transformation rather than classroom
hours. The Committee agrees — and that is precisely why it cannot
recommend a premium price for the programme *as it is currently
delivered*. Transformation in language education is bought with human
contact, and the College has almost none to sell. Section 4 sets out that
problem plainly; section 6 solves it by pricing three tiers of delivery
rather than one, of which two must be built before they can be sold.

A higher price is available to this College. It is not available to this
College's current product, and a Board Paper that pretended otherwise
would be the most expensive document in its history.

## 1. Why Worldwide English College is worth an investment

The Board asked that this question be answered before any number
appeared. It is answered from the record, not from ambition.

**Because the curriculum was designed before it was sold.** Sixty
modules, 720 designed lessons, 1,200 designed hours, each lesson
sequenced through warm-up, presentation, guided practice, independent
practice and assessment with its minutes declared in the plan. Most
online English is a library of material arranged by topic. This is a
programme, and the difference is that a programme knows what comes next
and why.

**Because the outcomes were written before the teaching.** Every
qualification publishes what its holder can do, and those statements were
authored as claims the assessments are built to test — not as marketing
written backwards from a finished course.

**Because it is examined, and the standard is published.** Ten
rubric-graded assignments per qualification against criteria a learner
can read in advance. An examination passing at 70% overall with no single
criterion below 50% — so nobody passes by being strong at one thing and
absent at another. Two resits, fourteen days apart, then the
qualification is repeated. Self-study platforms mark nothing and examine
nothing. This is the difference between studying English and being
assessed in it.

**Because every qualification is complete.** A learner may stop at any
stage and hold a whole qualification, with its own descriptors, its own
graduate profile and its own certificate. Nothing in the framework exists
only to sell the next thing.

**Because the College publishes what it lacks.** No accreditation. No
External Examiner. Nothing conferred on anyone. An institutional-status
page that says so, and a verification service that is free and always
will be. In a market with a great deal of quiet exaggeration, an
institution that documents its own gaps is making the only claim that
cannot be faked.

**What is not yet true.** There is no live teaching, no tutorial contact,
no speaking assessment with a person, no cohort and no scheduled
interaction of any kind. A learner's entire human contact with the
College is ten marked assignments and an examination. That is a real
programme; it is not yet a transformative one, and the distinction is the
whole of section 4.

## 2. The market, benchmarked properly

The first submission benchmarked only against inexpensive English
provision, and the Board was right that this was insufficient. It also
produced the wrong answer, because anchoring on the cheap end is how a
paper talks itself into a cheap price.

All figures are published prices retrieved 18 August 2026.

### 2.1 Where transformation is bought

| Comparator | Published | What is actually purchased |
|---|---|---|
| HBS AMP / Stanford SEP | **$95,000** | Standing, network, and weeks of senior faculty contact |
| INSEAD AMP, four weeks in person | **€47,500** | The same, compressed |
| Wharton Advanced Business Analytics, 12 months online | **$40,000** | A named credential from a recognised school |
| Corporate 1:1 language training | **$50–$120 per hour** | A person's attention, hour by hour |
| Corporate language programme producing measurable proficiency gain | **$800–$2,500 per employee per year** | An outcome an employer will pay to have measured |

### 2.2 Where English provision sits

| Comparator | Published |
|---|---|
| US in-person intensive ESL | **$1,200–$2,500 per month** |
| Accredited online ESL, credit-bearing | **$300–$600 per credit hour** |
| ASU Global Launch, self-paced online | **$350 per 8-week course** |
| British Council tutored (India / Kuwait) | **≈472 INR / ≈7.2 KWD per hour** |
| British Council / FutureLearn self-study | **$25–$30 per month** |
| Cambridge B2 / C1 / C2 examination | **€167–€228; $450 (US)** |

Two premium comparators could not be retrieved — The London School of
English and ILSC both refused the network. Their absence is a gap in this
paper, not evidence, and EF Executive Premium publishes no price at all,
quoting individually.

### 2.3 What the pattern says

**Price tracks human contact, not content.** The $95,000 programme and
the $30 subscription both deliver excellent material. What separates them
is how many hours a qualified person spends on the individual learner.
Corporate 1:1 is $50–$120 an hour precisely because it is an hour of
someone's life.

**The outcome-priced band is the one the College belongs in.** An
employer paying $800–$2,500 per employee per year for measurable
proficiency gain is buying exactly what the Worldwide English
Qualifications framework produces: defined capability, assessed and
evidenced. Over the two years of the full pathway that band is
**$1,600–$5,000** — and it assumes tutored delivery.

**Recognised examination is priced separately and modestly.** Cambridge
charges €167–€228 for the certificate alone. Anyone pricing an
unrecognised certificate near a recognised one is selling the wrong
thing.

## 3. Why the first submission came in low, and what was actually wrong with it

The first paper recommended $4,980 for the pathway. The Board's instinct
that this understates the College was correct, but the diagnosis matters
because it determines the fix.

**It was not too low for what the College currently delivers.** Against
the outcome-priced corporate band ($1,600–$5,000 for two years), $4,980
sits at the very top — and that band assumes tutored delivery, which the
College does not provide. On a strict like-for-like basis the first
submission was, if anything, already at the ceiling.

**It was too low for what the College intends to be.** That is a
different statement, and it cannot be fixed by raising the number. A
price is a claim about delivery. Raising the price without changing the
delivery does not reposition an institution; it simply makes it worse
value, and the first applicant who compares carefully will say so in
public.

**The real error was accepting the current delivery model as fixed.** The
first submission treated "self-paced, no tutorial contact" as a constraint
to price within. It is not a constraint. It is a decision, and it is the
decision that caps the College's price.

## 4. The problem the Board must decide

**The College cannot charge a premium price for a product with no human
contact in it.**

A learner's entire human contact across a four-month qualification is ten
marked assignments and one examination. There is no tutorial, no
speaking assessment with a person, no feedback conversation, no cohort,
nothing scheduled. The record is explicit: there are no live classes, no
video and no attendance tracking, and the site says so.

Against the market that is a **directed self-study programme with
assessment**. It is a good one. It is not what $95,000, €47,500 or even
$2,500-a-year corporate provision buys, and no amount of presentation
will make it so.

The Committee therefore puts the choice to the Board in these terms:

> **The College may have a premium price or the current delivery model.
> It cannot have both.**

Section 6 recommends having the premium price — by building the delivery
that earns it, pricing it as a distinct tier, and continuing to sell the
current model honestly at the level it is worth.

## 5. Tuition follows capability

The Board directed that progression be justified by what a qualification
makes a learner able to do, not by how much marking it generates. The
Committee accepts this without reservation: marking load is the
College's cost, and no applicant has ever been persuaded by a supplier's
costs.

The capability basis, drawn from the published qualification descriptors:

| Qualification | What it makes the holder able to do | Why the market values it |
|---|---|---|
| **Foundation · ECIC** | Operate in English in the situations ordinary life produces — arrival, shops, directions, first contact at work | Real and life-changing, but the beginner market is broad and price-sensitive, and this qualification opens no doors by itself |
| **Development · HCIC** | Manage a routine day and a routine work conversation without a translator | Independence, not yet advancement |
| **Application · CAEC** | Be given a task in English and produce something usable; enter foundation and pre-degree study | **The first qualification an employer or admissions officer treats as a threshold** |
| **Professional · HCAEC** | Represent an employer — run the meeting, negotiate, present, write for an external reader; enter undergraduate study | **The career-deciding qualification. This is where English stops limiting what work a person may be given.** |
| **Advanced · ACEC** | Lead in English; control nuance, register and implication; postgraduate study | Seniority. Fewer hold it, fewer can teach it |
| **Mastery · WEPC** | Operate at any level an organisation contains; publish; teach the language itself | The rarest, and the College's own name is on the certificate |

**The curve this implies is not linear, and the first submission's was.**
Value is concentrated at the Application and Professional stages, where a
qualification changes what someone is permitted to do for a living. The
recommended prices below rise gently across the first two, step hard into
the third and fourth, and continue upward for scarcity at the top.

## 6. The recommended architecture: three tiers of delivery

One qualification, one standard, one certificate. Three ways of being
taught towards it, priced by the human contact each contains.

The qualification does not change between tiers. A WEPC is a WEPC: same
outcomes, same assessment, same pass standard, same certificate, same
entry in the register. **The College sells different amounts of teaching.
It does not sell different amounts of qualification.** That principle is
what keeps a tiered price honest, and the Board should treat any future
proposal to weaken it as a proposal to sell grades.

### 6.1 Directed — available today

Self-paced study of the full curriculum, ten rubric-graded assignments
marked by a person, the examination, two resits, and the qualification.
This is the College exactly as it stands.

| Stage | Qualification | Directed | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation | ECIC | **$840** | Above ASU's $350 because ten assignments are read and judged by a person; held under four figures because a beginner qualification priced at four figures excludes the learner it exists for. |
| Development | HCIC | **$920** | A small step. Capability gains are real but incremental, and a large step here would be arithmetic pretending to be judgement. |
| Application | CAEC | **$1,240** | The first threshold qualification — academic English begins, employers and admissions officers recognise the standard. The first genuine step in value, so the first genuine step in price. |
| Professional | HCAEC | **$1,760** | The career-deciding qualification, and the largest single step in the framework. A learner who reaches this standard is permitted different work. |
| Advanced | ACEC | **$2,140** | Seniority and scarcity. Fewer learners reach it; fewer markers can assess it. |
| Mastery | WEPC | **$2,600** | The rarest qualification, the capstone examination, and the only certificate carrying the College's own name. |
| | **Six taken singly** | **$9,500** | |
| | **Pathway, committed at entry** | **$7,400** | |

The commitment saving is **$2,100** — more than the Foundation and
Development qualifications cost together ($1,760). The figure was chosen
so that sentence is true and repeatable: *commit to the framework and the
first two qualifications are, in effect, not charged.*

### 6.2 Tutored — must be built before it is sold

Everything in Directed, plus per qualification:

- **twelve scheduled one-to-one tutorials** with an appointed tutor;
- **a live speaking assessment** with a person, at the midpoint and at
  the end — the single largest gap in the College's present assessment,
  which examines writing and comprehension but never hears the learner;
- **written feedback answered**, so marking becomes a dialogue rather
  than a verdict;
- **a named tutor** for the duration.

| Stage | Tutored | Premium over Directed |
|---|---|---|
| Foundation · ECIC | **$2,180** | $1,340 |
| Development · HCIC | **$2,290** | $1,370 |
| Application · CAEC | **$2,760** | $1,520 |
| Professional · HCAEC | **$3,480** | $1,720 |
| Advanced · ACEC | **$4,060** | $1,920 |
| Mastery · WEPC | **$4,780** | $2,180 |
| **Six taken singly** | **$19,550** | |
| **Pathway, committed at entry** | **$14,800** | |

**The premium is not a margin; it is a cost with a person in it.** Twelve
tutorials plus two live assessments is roughly fourteen contact hours per
qualification. The market rate for corporate one-to-one language work is
$50–$120 an hour, and a tutor competent to take a learner to C2 sits at
the upper end of it. At the Mastery Stage the College is buying fourteen
hours of a senior practitioner's attention and reselling it inside a
qualification — the premium of $2,180 barely covers it.

At $14,800 for the tutored pathway the College sits where the Board wants
it: above every mass-market English provider, beneath the named-school
executive credentials, and defensibly so — because what separates it from
the cheap end is ninety hours of a qualified person's time, which is
exactly what separates them everywhere else in this market.

### 6.3 Executive — must be built before it is sold

Individually scoped: one-to-one throughout, scheduling around the
learner, a project drawn from their own professional context, and
assessment against their own working material.

**By quotation, with a floor of $6,900 per qualification.** No published
price. This is the tier where EF, INSEAD and every serious provider quote
individually, because the scope genuinely differs per person, and
publishing a number would either underprice the demanding cases or
frighten off the simple ones.

### 6.4 What must exist before 6.2 and 6.3 may be sold

Neither tier may be offered until all of the following are true, and the
Committee recommends the Board make this an express condition of adoption:

1. **Tutors are appointed**, with published qualifications, on the
   faculty roster.
2. **A scheduling system exists** and a learner can book, reschedule and
   attend a tutorial.
3. **Live speaking assessment is designed** — criteria, rubric, recording,
   moderation — and added to the assessment framework of every
   qualification, with Senate ratification sought alongside.
4. **A tutor rate is set** by this Board.
5. **The tiers appear in the qualification descriptors**, so a learner can
   see precisely what each buys before paying.

Selling a tutored tier before tutors exist would be the most damaging
thing the College could do, and it is the obvious commercial temptation
of this paper. The Committee names it here so that it cannot later be
done quietly.

## 7. The commercial ecosystem

The Board asked that the ecosystem be designed as carefully as the
tuition. Every item below is either deliverable today or explicitly
marked as requiring build.

### 7.1 Examination, certification and records

| Item | Fee | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| First examination attempt | **Included** | It is part of the qualification, at every tier. |
| First and second resit | **$120** each | A second marking event by a person. Set far below Cambridge's €167–€228 because this is an internal examination; pricing near a recognised certificate would imply a standing the College does not hold. |
| Certificate on conferral | **No fee** | An institution that charges to hand over what it has already assessed is selling the certificate rather than the education. |
| Graduation | **No fee** | There is no ceremony to cost. If one is ever held it returns to this Board. |
| Digital verification | **No fee, permanently** | Charging a stranger to check a credential makes the credential less trustworthy, which is the opposite of its purpose. |
| Replacement certificate | **$45** | Reissue, re-signature, re-registration. Real administrative work. |
| Transcript, any number of copies | **No fee** | It is the learner's own record. |

### 7.2 Payment

No plan carries interest, a surcharge or a late fee. **A learner in
difficulty is a learner to keep, not a margin to harvest.**

Monthly figures are the rounded payment with the remainder carried to
the final month, rather than a total chosen because it divided neatly.
The College sets a price and then arranges payment of it; it does not
set a price that happens to be divisible by twelve.

| Plan | Terms |
|---|---|
| Single qualification, in full | As section 6 |
| Single qualification, four instalments | Equal payments across the four months, first at enrolment |
| Directed pathway, twelve months | **$620 a month**, final payment $580 |
| Directed pathway, twenty-four months | **$310 a month**, final payment $270 — matching the pathway to its own duration |
| Tutored pathway, twenty-four months | **$620 a month**, final payment $540 |
| Deferred start | Pay at enrolment, begin within twelve months, price held |
| Employer or sponsor invoiced | Thirty days, single invoice, on a purchase order |

**Deferred payment for the Application Stage and above.** A learner may
begin CAEC, HCAEC, ACEC or WEPC on a deferred plan: nothing until the
qualification is completed, then twelve monthly payments at the Directed
rate plus $180. Offered only from the Application Stage, because those
are the qualifications that change earning capacity, and only to learners
who have already completed one qualification with the College — the
College carries the risk on people it has taught, not on strangers.

### 7.3 Mentoring, coaching and continuing membership

| Offer | Price | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Academic mentoring**, six sessions across a qualification | **$540** | Requires build |
| **Executive communication coaching**, per hour | **$180** | Requires build |
| **Speaking clinic**, small group, per term | **$260** | Requires build |
| **Alumni membership**, annual | **$95** | Requires build |
| **Institutional membership**, annual, per organisation | **$2,400** | Requires build |

**Alumni membership** carries continued access to the curriculum a holder
has completed, the pronunciation and listening laboratories, two speaking
clinics a year, and the annual lecture. It is deliberately cheap: its
purpose is to keep graduates attached to the College, not to be a
revenue line. **Institutional membership** gives an organisation a named
academic contact, annual cohort reporting, and priority scheduling.

### 7.4 Employers, government and partners

| Route | Terms |
|---|---|
| Employer-sponsored individual | Directed or Tutored pathway, invoiced, **less $200** for the administrative simplicity of a single payer |
| Corporate, 5–19 seats | Pathway rate **less $450 a seat** |
| Corporate, 20–99 seats | Pathway rate **less $900 a seat** |
| Corporate, 100+ | By agreement, floor **$5,200 a seat** Directed, **$10,400** Tutored |
| Government or ministry cohort | Minimum 25 seats, **$5,600 a seat** Directed, single invoice, cohort reporting, named academic contact |
| Institutional partnership | Annual licence plus per-seat, minimum three years, negotiated |
| **Employer completion rebate** | **$300 returned to the employer** per learner who completes | 

The floor exists so volume cannot argue the price below the cost of
marking: a hundred-seat contract is still a thousand assignments read by
a person. The **completion rebate** is deliberate — it aligns the College
with the employer's actual interest, which is completion rather than
enrolment, and it is the only structure in this paper that pays out when
a learner succeeds.

### 7.5 Family and regional provision

**Family enrolment.** A second member of the same household enrols at
**less $400**; a third and beyond at **less $600**. Households, not
referrals — verified by address at enrolment, capped at four.

**Regional pricing.** The Committee recommends the Board adopt the
principle now and the schedule later: **a single published price in USD
is a barrier disguised as simplicity.** $7,400 is a different proposition
in Cairo than in Kuwait City. Regional schedules should be set against
national income data once the currency architecture supports a second
settled currency — which it does not today, only USD being live. Until
then the Committee recommends **regional bursaries** (below) as the
honest interim, because a discount a learner must ask for is worse than a
price that was fair to begin with, and the College should not pretend
otherwise.

### 7.6 Scholarships and bursaries

Funded by construction, so the scheme cannot overcommit the College:

- **One full Tutored pathway scholarship for every forty paid pathway
  enrolments.** It cannot exist before the revenue that pays for it.
- **Regional access bursary: one third of tuition**, for applicants
  resident in countries where the full price exceeds one month of median
  national income. Awarded on residence, not on application quality —
  a hardship award that also requires a competitive essay is a
  scholarship wearing a bursary's coat.
- **Bursary fund: one tenth of resit income.** Built from learners who
  struggled, spent on learners who cannot pay.
- **Hardship deferral, free, on request, without documentation.** Any
  learner may pause a payment plan once per qualification. Requiring
  proof of poverty costs more in dignity than it saves in revenue.

No scholarship may be awarded before the Academic Senate has members:
an award of merit decided by nobody is a marketing device.

### 7.7 Referral and commission — RECOMMENDED AGAINST

International education runs on agent commission, typically 10–20% of
first-year tuition. **The Committee recommends the College pay none, and
that the Board record why.**

An agent paid by commission is paid to enrol, not to advise. This
qualification is not accredited, is not recognised, and cannot yet be
conferred. An intermediary explaining that honestly earns nothing; one
explaining it dishonestly earns four figures. The structure selects for
the second, and the College would be the last to find out.

Instead, **an alumni introduction credit of $200** against a returning
holder's next qualification, when someone they introduced enrols and
completes their first month. Paid to a learner rather than a
salesperson, payable only after the newcomer has stayed, capped at two
per person per year.

Revisit after accreditation. The objection is to commission before
standing, not to commission for ever.

## 8. Financial sustainability

**The one cost that scales is human attention**, and the two tiers differ
entirely in how much of it they contain.

**Directed pathway at $7,400.** Sixty marked assignments and six
examinations across the framework — sixty-six marking events, about $112
retained per event before platform, support and overhead. Whether that
is sufficient depends on the marker rate, which this Board has not set.

**Tutored pathway at $14,800.** Adds roughly ninety contact hours. At a
tutor rate of $60 an hour the direct cost is about $5,400, leaving
approximately $9,400 against the same sixty-six marking events and
overhead. At $90 an hour it is $8,100 and the tier is marginal.

**The Board cannot adopt this paper without setting two rates.** The
marker rate and the tutor rate are not administrative details; they
determine whether section 6.2 is a business or a loss. They are the same
decision as the prices.

**No revenue forecast appears.** The College has no applicants, no
conversion history and no marketing spend. A projection would be a
fabrication with a chart on it, and the Committee will not put one in
front of this Board.

**What can be said about capacity.** A tutor working thirty contact hours
a week can carry roughly ten Tutored learners per qualification cycle.
Growth in the tutored tier is therefore bounded by hiring, not by demand,
and the Board should expect that constraint to bind long before any
marketing one does.

## 5A. What was built while this paper was written

The Board asked for a robust speaking assessment framework operating at
every qualification stage, and supported it strongly. It exists.
`sql/migrations/022-speaking-assessment.sql` is in the record, mirrored
into the schema, and held by twenty-five assertions.

**Why this, first, and not a pricing revision.** Every qualification
descriptor in the Worldwide English Qualifications framework claims
spoken capability. None of them evidenced it. A candidate could hold the
WEPC — a qualification in English *communication* — without any person
having heard them say a sentence. That is not a commercial gap; it is an
academic one, and it happens to be the same gap that made a premium
price indefensible.

**What the framework contains.** Two assessments per qualification: a
midpoint carrying no weight, so a learner can use it rather than fear it,
and a summative final carrying twenty per cent. Five criteria constant
across all six qualifications — intelligibility, fluency, range,
interaction, appropriacy — so a Foundation candidate and a Mastery
candidate are judged on the same dimensions. Thirty descriptors, one per
criterion per stage, rising from *"understood by a sympathetic listener
used to non-native speech"* to *"judges what a situation requires before
speaking, including when not to."* Speaking time rises from four minutes
to fifteen; preparation from none at Foundation, where the assessment is
of speech rather than of memory, to twenty at Mastery.

**One standard, two methods — which is the Board's own principle.**
Asynchronous capture, where the candidate records against a released
prompt and a marker assesses the recording, is buildable today:
`learner_recordings` already carries media, duration, attempt, retention
and a content hash, and the upload path is tested end to end. Live
capture is richer.

**And the framework records where recording cannot honestly reach.** A
candidate speaking to a prompt is not taking a turn, cannot be
interrupted, and has nothing to respond to that they did not already
read. The College had three options — pretend otherwise, drop the
criterion, or say so. The `async_ceiling` column says so: interaction
assessed by recording reaches *Proficient* and no further, the candidate
is told before they begin, and the other four criteria are uncapped.

**This is the sharpest illustration of the paper's argument.** Option A
can assess speaking honestly, and four of five criteria fully. Only
Option B can assess a candidate's interaction at the highest band,
because only Option B has a person in the room. The difference between
the two options is not marketing. It is what the College is able to
know about its own graduates.

## 5B. Option B — the institution to be built

Option A is described by sections 6.1 and 7. This is what Option B adds,
and what it requires.

### The learner's experience, changed

A learner on Option B is not a user of a platform. At enrolment they are
assigned a **named academic adviser** who stays with them across every
qualification they take, and a **tutor** for each. They have twelve
scheduled one-to-one tutorials per qualification, a live speaking
assessment at the midpoint and the end, and written feedback they can
answer rather than only receive. They join a **cohort** that begins
together and meets fortnightly. On completion they enter an **alumni
body** with continued access, two speaking clinics a year, and an annual
lecture. If their employer sponsors them, that employer receives cohort
reporting and a named academic contact.

The difference a learner would describe is not "more content". It is that
somebody at the College knows who they are.

### What must be built

| Capability | Why the price depends on it | Scale |
|---|---|---|
| **Tutors appointed** | The premium is contact hours; without tutors there are none | One tutor carries ~10 learners per cycle |
| **Scheduling** | A tutorial that cannot be booked does not exist | Booking, rescheduling, attendance, no-show policy |
| **Live speaking assessment** | Designed *(5A)*; needs delivery, moderation and a second marker | Two per learner per qualification |
| **Academic advisers** | The relationship that makes it a college rather than a course | One adviser to ~60 learners |
| **Cohorts** | Fortnightly meeting, shared start dates, a peer group | Requires an academic calendar with real intakes |
| **Employer engagement** | Cohort reporting, completion rebates, named contact | Account management, not marketing |
| **Alumni services** | Membership, clinics, the annual lecture | Continuous, small, and never allowed to lapse |

### Operational implications the Board should weigh

**Growth becomes bounded by hiring, not by demand.** A tutor carries
about ten learners per qualification cycle. Every hundred Option B
learners is ten tutors and roughly two advisers. The College cannot
scale Option B faster than it can appoint and moderate teachers, and
appointing teachers faster than it can moderate them is how the
qualification quietly stops meaning the same thing.

**Moderation becomes the central academic task.** Six markers judging
speaking will not agree unless someone makes them agree. Option B
requires standardisation meetings, double-marking of a sample, and a
moderation record — none of which exists today.

**The College acquires a payroll.** Option A's costs are marking and
platform. Option B's are salaries, and salaries do not fall when
enrolment does. This is the single largest financial difference between
the options, and it is a risk the Board takes on deliberately or not at
all.

**Two academic appointments become urgent rather than important.** The
External Examiner, still unappointed, and the Academic Senate, which has
no members. Option B is an institution making academic judgements about
individuals at scale, and neither option should confer anything until the
Examiner exists — but Option B makes the absence conspicuous.

### Why applicants would willingly pay more

Not because the certificate is worth more. It is the same certificate,
and the Board has been clear that it must remain so.

They would pay because **the thing they are buying is different**: ninety
hours of a qualified person's attention across the framework, a named
adviser who knows their history, a cohort that expects them on Tuesday,
and an assessment that hears them speak and answers back. That is what
$50–$120 an hour buys everywhere else in this market, and it is the only
honest reason to charge more.


---

## 9. Positioning, and what the learner feels

**Where the College sits after adoption.** Above every mass-market
English provider, at or above the top of the outcome-priced corporate
band, and beneath the named-school executive credentials — with the
distance from the cheap end measured in a qualified person's hours rather
than in adjectives.

**Investment, not expense.** Both tiers are quoted per qualification and
per pathway, never per hour and never per month as a headline. A monthly
figure appears only inside a payment plan, where it belongs, because a
learner deciding whether to be qualified is not making the same decision
as a learner deciding whether to keep a subscription.

**Progression, not upsell.** Every qualification is complete. The
pathway saving is presented as a reward for commitment, never as a
penalty for caution, and the single-qualification price is a fair price
rather than a rate designed to force the pathway.

**Confidence, not persuasion.** No price ends in 99. Nothing is "from"
a figure. There are no countdowns, no expiring offers and no list price
carrying a permanent strikethrough. The College publishes what a
qualification costs and what it does not yet have, in the same typeface.

**The number that decides who can enrol** is $310 a month — the Directed
pathway across its own twenty-four months. That figure, not the headline,
determines whether a salaried adult in the College's target market can
begin at all, and the Committee regards protecting it as more important
than protecting the headline.

## 10. Risks

**The tutored tier is sold before it exists.** The gravest risk in this
paper, and entirely internal. Mitigation: section 6.4 makes five
conditions express, and the Board is asked to adopt them as conditions
rather than intentions.

**Two tiers are read as two standards.** A learner may assume the tutored
qualification is worth more. Mitigation: the qualification, assessment,
pass standard, certificate and register entry are identical, this is
stated on every qualification page, and no tier appears on the
certificate or in verification.

**The Directed tier becomes the poor relation.** If the College's
attention follows the revenue, the tier most learners actually buy
decays. Mitigation: the Board should require the tutored tier's
speaking-assessment design to be added to the Directed tier's assessment
framework as well — the assessment improves for everyone, the tutorials
do not.

**Prices are high for an unaccredited institution.** True, and the honest
answer is that the Tutored price is not paying for accreditation; it is
paying for ninety hours of teaching, which has value whatever the
certificate is worth. The Directed price stays deliberately closer to the
market for exactly this reason.

**Accreditation arrives and everything looks cheap.** A good problem, and
the architecture is built to be repriced on a governance event.
Accreditation or the appointment of an External Examiner returns this
paper to the Board.

**A learner defaults on a long plan.** Access pauses; the record persists;
nothing already earned is withdrawn for non-payment. Turning an academic
record into a debt-collection instrument is not available to this
College.

**Currency.** Only USD is settled. Every figure here is USD, and a
learner in Cairo meets a dollar price. Section 7.5 is the interim answer
and it is an imperfect one.

## 11. Alternatives considered and rejected

**Keep $19,000 and discount heavily.** Rejected. A list price nobody pays
is a lie with a strikethrough on it, and it teaches applicants to wait
for a sale.

**Raise the single-tier price to premium without changing delivery.**
Rejected, and this is the alternative the Board's return letter most
invited. It would make the College worse value at a higher price, and the
first careful applicant would say so publicly. A price is a claim about
delivery; changing only the claim is the definition of overcharging.

**Keep the first submission's $4,980.** Rejected. Defensible for the
current product, but it accepts the current delivery model as permanent
and therefore caps the institution.

**Flat price per qualification.** Rejected. It charges identically for a
beginner's paragraph and a C2 capstone, which is not simplicity but
inaccuracy.

**Subscription, cancel any time.** Rejected on academic grounds. A
subscription prices access to content; the College sells an assessed
qualification with a conferral event. A learner cancelling in month three
of four has bought nothing, and a structure making that common is badly
designed.

**Free tuition, paid assessment.** Attractive, rejected for now: it puts
the entire price on a credential with no external standing. Reconsider
after accreditation.

**Hourly pricing, like the tutored comparators.** Rejected. The College
sells a qualification of fixed designed extent, and hourly pricing
invites a comparison with live tuition it would lose.

## 12. The two options, and what the Committee advises

The Board asked for two coherent options rather than one recommendation.
Here they are, and then — because the Board is entitled to the
Committee's judgement as well as its analysis — what the Committee would
do.

### Option A — Premium Digital College

Publish section 6.1 and section 7. Pathway **$7,400**; twenty-four
monthly payments of **$310**, final $270.

**Adopt if** the Board wants the College earning and teaching this term,
with no payroll risk, growth bounded only by demand, and an honest offer
that is genuinely excellent at what it does.

**Accept, if adopting it,** that the College will be a very good
directed-study institution, that its interaction assessment will reach
*Proficient* and not beyond, and that the premium end of this market
will remain occupied by others.

### Option B — Premium Global English College

Publish section 6.1 now and section 6.2 when section 5B is built.
Pathway **$14,800**; twenty-four monthly payments of **$620**, final
$540.

**Adopt if** the Board intends the College to be an institution with
people in it, and is prepared to carry a payroll, appoint and moderate
teachers, and grow at the speed of hiring rather than of marketing.

**Accept, if adopting it,** that nothing in 6.2 may be sold until it
exists, and that the interval between resolving and delivering is the
most dangerous period in the College's history — because the commercial
temptation to sell it early will be strongest exactly when the capability
is weakest.

### What the Committee advises

**Adopt Option A now and Option B as the trajectory** — one resolution,
two horizons.

Publish the Directed tier this term at $7,400. Build section 5B against
a stated timetable. Open the Tutored tier only when the five conditions
at 6.4 are met, and price it at $14,800 when it is real.

The reasoning is that the two options are not alternatives in time. A
College cannot become Option B without first being Option A — it needs
learners before it needs cohorts, and revenue before it needs payroll.
Choosing A *instead of* B caps the institution. Choosing B *instead of*
A means selling something that does not exist. Choosing A *towards* B is
the only sequence in which every price is true on the day it is charged.

### Conditions, under either option

1. **Set the marker rate.** Under both. The prices are not safe without it.
2. **Set the tutor rate** before any Option B commitment is made.
3. **Deliver the speaking assessment under Option A too**, asynchronously
   — it is designed, the infrastructure exists, and a learner should not
   have to buy a tier to be heard.
4. **Keep the independent learner route closed** until an External
   Examiner is appointed.
5. **Appoint the External Examiner.** Under both options this is now the
   binding constraint on the College's entire purpose: it can teach,
   assess, and hear its learners speak, and it still cannot confer.
