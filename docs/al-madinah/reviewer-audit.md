# "What would they question first?"

The Founder's standing instruction, from this point to launch:

> *If a professor, an accreditation reviewer, or a respected Islamic scholar visited this
> page today, what would they question first? Resolve those questions before they become
> public criticism.*

First pass, run across all sixteen published pages. Findings are ordered by what a
reviewer reaches first, not by how hard they are to fix.

---

## What already survives the question, and should not be disturbed

Two things on this site are stronger than anything the competitor set does, and both work
by the same mechanism — **declaring a vacancy rather than concealing it**:

- **The Register of Asānīd names the Founder and states exactly what he holds** — the ten
  canonical readings with *ijāzah*, and *ijāzah* in the six principal collections of
  ḥadīth — and then enters a **vacancy**: *"Second holder of the ten readings —
  appointment precedes the opening of the Higher Diploma in the Ten Readings."* A
  programme is gated on a post being filled, in public. A reviewer who finds that stops
  worrying about the rest of the register.
- **The Standing page declares that moderation is internal until an External Examiner is
  appointed**, and every surface describing examination repeats it.

That is the correct instinct, and the recommendation below is simply to **apply it
everywhere it is not yet applied.**

---

## 1. The Academic Council appears on all sixteen pages and has no members

It seals instruments. It moderates examination. It keeps the Register of Asānīd. It opens
and closes programmes. It is the body a candidate appeals to. It is named on **every page
of the site** — and nowhere is a single member of it identified.

This is the largest unbacked claim the College makes, and it is larger than any question
of design. The first question a professor asks of any institution is *who decides?*, and
the honest answer available on this site today is: an unnamed body.

**Same for:** the **Rector**, whose hand is said to be on every engrossed certificate; the
**Trustees**, to whom safeguarding disclosures are directed; the **Registrar**; and the
**Bursar**. Five offices, invoked across the site, no person behind any of them.

**Recommended:** one rule, applied without exception — *every office the site invokes is
either filled by a named person or entered as vacant, with the consequence of the vacancy
stated.* The College already does this for the second *muqriʾ* and the External Examiner.
Doing it for five more offices costs nothing but the decision, and it converts the site's
largest weakness into a continuation of its greatest strength.

Naming one person as Rector, Registrar and Bursar is not a weakness in a young
institution. Concealing that it is one person is.

## 2. Safeguarding is an office without a person, and minors are admitted

The Safeguarding page names an office, an address and a reporting duty. No officer is
appointed. Meanwhile Ibtidā'iyyah admits from **age ten**.

This is not only a credibility question. It is the one finding on this page that carries
live risk: a disclosure made to `safeguarding@al-madinahcollege.com` today reaches nobody
— and, because the domain is unregistered, it does not even bounce to a College inbox.

**Recommended:** appoint the officer before the first minor is admitted, as the transfer
register already required, or state on the page that admission of minors is suspended
until the appointment is made. Either is defensible. The present position is not.

## 3. The Registry addresses do not exist

`al-madinahcollege.com` is unregistered. Every page invites the reader to write to
`registry@`, `bursary@` or `safeguarding@`. The Form of Application composes an
application and hands it to a mailbox that cannot receive it.

A reviewer who writes and gets a bounce has already formed a view, and no page recovers
it. **This is the single cheapest item on this list and the most damaging left undone.**

## 4. "International College" is asserted, never evidenced

The name claims an international institution. The site states Lagos, Nigeria, and the
Standing page is candid about recognition — but nothing states the **legal form**: what
entity is the College, incorporated where, under what registration number, governed by
what instrument.

A reviewer from a ministry or an accrediting body asks this in the first minute.

**Recommended:** a short constitutional statement on the Standing page — the legal entity,
its jurisdiction, its registration, and where the governing instrument may be read. If the
entity is not yet incorporated, say so plainly; the College already says harder things
than that.

## 5. Faculty are described but not enumerated

Programmes are said to be taught by "members of faculty". The Register of Asānīd names one
person. A prospective student cannot learn who would teach them, and a reviewer cannot
assess whether four faculties are staffed.

**Recommended:** a faculty register on the same pattern as the Register of Asānīd — name,
qualification, awarding institution, what they teach — including the vacancies. Four named
academics with three declared vacancies reads far better than seventeen programmes and no
names at all.

## 6. Two enrolment counts carry no denominator

The predecessor programme's 2020–2022 figures are correctly labelled as counts from that
period rather than present claims — which is right. But a reviewer will ask what happened
to those students: how many completed, how many lapsed, how many were examined. A count of
admissions without a count of outcomes is the statistic every accreditor is trained to
probe.

**Recommended:** either publish the outcomes alongside, or withdraw the figures until they
can be published together. The College has just done exactly this with the memorisation
schedules and the reasoning transfers.

---

## The order I would fix them in

1. **Register the domain.** Cheapest, most damaging, purely mechanical.
2. **Safeguarding Officer**, or suspend the admission of minors. Live risk.
3. **Name the offices** — Rector, Registrar, Bursar, Academic Council, Trustees — or enter
   them as vacant.
4. **The constitutional statement** on Standing.
5. **The faculty register.**
6. **The enrolment outcomes**, or withdraw the counts.

Items 3 to 6 are all the same act: *say who, or say that no one yet.* The College already
knows how to do it — it has done it twice, and those two entries are the most convincing
things on the site.
