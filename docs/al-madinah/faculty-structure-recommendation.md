# Faculty structure — a recommendation

Commissioned by the Founder: *"Do not remove the Faculty of Da'wah & Applied Studies
without careful academic review. Instead, present a recommendation showing the current
structure, the proposed structure, the advantages and disadvantages of each, and your
final recommendation. Base it on academic coherence, future scalability, and clarity for
students — not simply on organisational neatness."*

---

## A. The structure as it stands

| Faculty | Programmes | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| The Qur'ān and its Sciences | 6 | recitation, tajwīd, memorisation, the ten readings, *ijāzah* |
| Arabic Language | 4 | Arabic for speakers of other languages; grammar and rhetoric |
| Sharī'ah Sciences | 4 | creed, jurisprudence and its principles, ḥadīth, exegesis, sīrah |
| Da'wah & Applied Studies | 3 | Islamic communication in English, translation, teacher formation, professional studies |

Seventeen programmes across four faculties. Every one is examined and laddered.

## B. The structure proposed

| Faculty | Change |
|---|---|
| Arabic Language | unchanged |
| Qur'anic Studies | unchanged in substance; a rename |
| Islamic Studies | a widening of *Sharī'ah Sciences* |
| Professional Arabic & Special Purposes | new — and it occupies the seat *Da'wah & Applied Studies* holds now |

## C. The advantages of the proposal

1. **It puts Arabic at the centre, which is where the demand is.** Two of four faculties
   would be Arabic faculties. Every pathway the Founder has named is an Arabic pathway.
   The structure would then describe what the College is actually asked for.
2. **"Islamic Studies" travels.** A registrar in Kuala Lumpur or Amman reads it without
   translation; *Sharī'ah Sciences* is narrower than what the faculty already teaches
   (it holds ḥadīth, exegesis and sīrah, none of which is fiqh).
3. **A named home for Languages for Specific Purposes.** Arabic for medicine, for
   diplomacy, for the newsroom is a recognised academic field with its own literature.
   Giving it a faculty makes the College legible to people who already work in it.
4. **It scales.** Fifteen professional pathways can live in one faculty without
   distorting the others.

## D. The disadvantages, and they are not small

1. **Da'wah is not a "special purpose". It is a discipline.** *Da'wah* has its own
   classical literature, its own methodology, and — for a college of Islamic sciences in
   Nigeria — a particular institutional weight. Filing it under *Professional Arabic*
   describes it as a career application of a language, which is not what it is.
2. **The College would be publishing a demotion.** Da'wah is currently a faculty. After
   the change it is content inside two other faculties. Anyone comparing the prospectus
   to the present site sees a subject that was a faculty and is now not one, and no
   explanation is available that does not sound like marketing.
3. **Three live programmes need re-homing by name.** Islamic communication in English,
   translation of Islamic texts, teacher formation. The first two are Da'wah proper; the
   third is professional formation. They do not travel together.
4. **The proposed fourth faculty is the only one defined by an audience rather than a
   discipline.** *Qur'ān*, *Arabic*, *Islamic Studies* name bodies of knowledge.
   *Professional & Special Purposes* names a set of buyers. Faculties defined by audience
   have to be renamed every time the market moves; faculties defined by discipline do not.
   This is the objection that would be raised by an external reviewer, and it is the one
   the College would find hardest to answer.
5. **It is a change to the academic record.** Every certificate already issued names a
   faculty. Renaming faculties creates a cohort whose instruments name a body that no
   longer exists, which the Register must then reconcile for ever.

## E. A third option, which is what I actually recommend

**Keep five faculties, not four. Da'wah stays. Professional Arabic is added rather than
substituted.**

| Faculty | Status | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| The Qur'ān and its Sciences | unchanged | 6 programmes |
| Arabic Language | unchanged | 4 programmes |
| **Islamic Studies** | renamed from *Sharī'ah Sciences* | 4 programmes |
| **Da'wah & Applied Studies** | **retained**, narrowed to Da'wah proper | Islamic communication, translation |
| **Professional Arabic & Special Purposes** | **new** | teacher formation, and the professional pathways |

### Why this is the stronger structure

- **It is honest about what changed.** One faculty is renamed for legibility. One is
  added because the College is entering a field it was not in. Nothing is demoted, so
  nothing needs explaining away.
- **Every faculty is defined by a discipline, except one — and that one is honestly
  labelled.** *Special Purposes* is a recognised term of art in language teaching (LSP).
  It does not pretend to be a body of knowledge; it says plainly that it is a set of
  applications. A reviewer will accept a faculty that knows what it is.
- **Only one live programme moves.** Teacher formation goes from Da'wah to Professional
  Arabic, where it belongs. The other two stay. The academic record needs one line of
  reconciliation instead of three.
- **It scales in the right direction.** The fifteen professional pathways land in the new
  faculty, which is what it exists for. Da'wah keeps its own ladder and can grow its own
  programmes without competing for space with Arabic for Healthcare Professionals.
- **Five faculties is not untidy.** Al-Azhar has more. The College's own rule is that a
  place in the curriculum is earned by being *necessary, teachable at a distance,
  examinable and staffed* — not by fitting a symmetrical diagram.

### What it costs

- **Five faculties will not fit the present home-page grid**, which is built as a 2×2 of
  four cards with roman numerals I–IV. That grid needs rebuilding — which is a day's
  work, and is the correct order of events: the academic structure decides the layout,
  never the reverse.
- **The fifth faculty needs a Dean, or it should not be announced.** *Professional Arabic
  & Special Purposes* with no named academic responsible would be the largest unstaffed
  claim on the site, and the pathways beneath it multiply the exposure.

## F. The recommendation, in one line

**Rename *Sharī'ah Sciences* to *Islamic Studies*; retain *Da'wah & Applied Studies* and
narrow it to Da'wah proper; add *Professional Arabic & Special Purposes* as a fifth
faculty and move teacher formation into it — and announce the fifth faculty only when it
has a Dean.**

## G. What is still needed before any of it is published

1. **A Dean for the fifth faculty.** Without one it is *in preparation*, not *open*.
2. **A ruling on the three Da'wah programmes** — two stay, one moves. Confirm by name.
3. **A reconciliation line for the Register**, so that instruments naming *Sharī'ah
   Sciences* remain verifiable after the rename. One sentence on the Standing page.
4. **Nothing here should reach a page until 1–3 are settled.** The College publishes
   *necessary, teachable, examinable, staffed*. This proposal must pass its own test.
