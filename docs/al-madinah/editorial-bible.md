# Part 0 — The inheritance, and why it is being ended

## §0.1 There were two bibles, and neither was written for this College

The site was built on top of two editorial documents, both inherited, both written for an institution that is not this one.

**The first** was written for *WorldWide English College, London Campus* — a premium international English-language school, positioned at "learners from the GCC, Europe, Africa and Asia", charging **$19,000** for a 24-month programme measured in **720 units** at **$26.39 a unit**, with a values list, a target-audience segmentation and a CEFR ladder. It is a serious and in places excellent document: the Didone masthead with its optical-size axis, reverse type set lighter, the radius-as-a-proportion rule, the two-shadow relief system, the `.edge-lit` / `.aurum` distinction, the module markers, the ledger tables, the ration on Cinzel — all of that is real craft and all of it is still in this build, credited at `EB §9.2`, `§8.1` and `§8.5`.

**The second** was written for *Al-Madeenah International Studies* — a college with no appointed *muqriʾ*, no verified *asānīd*, and, at the time it was written, no faculty. Its academic discipline is the best of its kind I have read: notional hours instead of credit units, the equal-standard rule, the Retention Rule, the Five Returns, integrity by design instead of surveillance, assessor independence, free re-examination, bands on the instrument. Sixteen of its rules are in our Academic Regulations and are staying.

Both were consulted in good faith. Both produced good parts. And both produced one bad thing between them, which is what this Part is for.

## §0.2 The dominant thing they gave us: a voice of provisionality

Here is the finding, in one sentence.

> **Both inherited bibles were written to answer the question *how do we write honestly about a college that does not exist yet* — and this College does exist, so that question, answered on every page, made it sound like it doesn't.**

That is the dominant thing. Not a palette, not a typeface, not a rule about hours. A **register**. The site had learned to speak provisionally, and provisionality is the one register a five-hundred-year-old institution never uses.

Follow how it happened, because the mechanism matters more than the diagnosis.

The WEC bible's governing problem was that its institution was a concept. It had no address, no named faculty, no accreditation, no calendar, no students. So its central editorial invention was **an apparatus for publishing absence with dignity**: a dashed-border *Institutional Status* callout, a standing section headed *"What doesn't exist yet (do not fabricate)"*, and the principle *"silence over invention"*. For a fiction, that was not merely correct, it was honourable — it is the reason that build never lied.

Then the Al-Madeenah bible arrived and it *reinforced the same instinct with academic authority*: every claim classified before publication; a page named for what the College will not claim; **"an unstated limitation is a misrepresentation."** Which is true. And which, taken as a rule of composition rather than a rule of disclosure, licenses the writer to state a limitation *anywhere* — because how could stating one ever be wrong?

So the two combined into a habit. The prose learned that the safest sentence is a negative one. And by the time it was noticed, the College's own pages were saying, in their own voice, at the top of their own hero sections: *does not confer degrees · holds no accreditation · does not claim equivalence · moderation is internal until · not the Board's certificate · in preparation · forthcoming · not yet obtained · makes no claim of.* Five of the ten public pages carried disclaimer language, and one of them opened on it.

The failure is not honesty. **The failure is diffusion.** An institution that mentions its limitations once, in a page named for them, in a regulation, and on the instrument itself, reads as candid. An institution that mentions them in the third paragraph of every page reads as *not sure it is one*. Same facts, same integrity, opposite standing. The reader does not audit a site; the reader takes its temperature. And the temperature of a page that keeps clearing its throat is: **provisional.**

That is why the founder's instruction — *it did not give us good results* — is correct, and it is correct about something more specific than tone. It is correct about **authority**. Two documents written for institutions in preparation taught this one to write as though it were also in preparation. It is not. It has a founder with *ijāzah* in the ten readings, four faculties, an Academic Council, a published scheme of awards, a Register of Asānīd, ten numbered Regulations, and 1,550 students taught before any of this was written down.

## §0.4 What survives the supersession, and on whose authority

Nothing survives *because it was inherited*. The following survive because they were re-examined and are right, and they are now this document's own rules:

- The Didone masthead and the optical-size axis (`EB §9.2`); reverse type set lighter (`§9.3`); gold→bronze on paper (`§9.4`).
- Radius as a proportion of the shortest edge, and concentricity (`§8.1`).
- Two-part shadows: contact plus ambient (`§8.3`).
- Material light applied broadly, effect light rationed hard (`§8.5`).
- Module markers, ledger tables, engraved plates, authored art, no stock photography (`§7`, `§8.9`).
- Reduced motion resolving to the **finished** state, never a hidden one (`§12.2`).

## §0.5 What was studied in drafting this

Stated so a reader can weigh the document. These were studied from knowledge rather than fetched, and no rule below is asserted on their authority — each is asserted on ours, with the reason given.

**Institutional register.** *Statutes, Decrees and Regulations* of the University of Oxford and the *Statutes and Ordinances* of the University of Cambridge — for the plain present-indicative third person, and for the discovery that the most authoritative institutional prose in English contains almost no adjectives. The *Programme Regulations* of the University of London's external system — for how an institution that teaches nobody on site still writes as a place. Al-Azhar's prospectus register for the Arabic side. The UK QAA *Quality Code* — read chiefly as a warning: a register that describes teaching entirely in the passive and the abstract, which is how quality assurance prose becomes unreadable by the people it protects.

**Style and copy-editing.** *New Hart's Rules* and the *New Oxford Style Manual*; *The Chicago Manual of Style*, 18th edition; *The Economist Style Guide* (for the rule that a figure beats an adjective, and for its hostility to the abstract noun); Butcher's *Copy-editing*; Gowers, *The Complete Plain Words*.

**Arabic and transliteration.** The *IJMES* transliteration and word-list convention; the ALA–LC romanization tables for Arabic; the conventions of Dār al-Kutub al-ʿIlmiyyah and of the Muṣḥaf al-Madīnah for Qur'ānic citation.

**Our own record.** The audit of the predecessor site in `docs/abisulaimiy-college/`, whose failure is the most instructive document in this repository, and the two superseded bibles read end to end.

# Part I — First principles

## §1.1 Who is actually reading

Every rule in this document is answerable to four readers. Not personas — four people whose reactions are the only test that matters.

1. **A father in Ilorin,** deciding whether to enrol his eleven-year-old daughter, on a phone, on data he pays for by the megabyte. He will read the safeguarding statement before he reads the prospectus, and he will not read either if the page takes nine seconds.
2. **A thirty-four-year-old accountant in Doha,** who memorised eleven *juzʾ* as a boy and has been ashamed of it for twenty years. The one question that governs whether he ever applies is *where would I even begin, and will I be embarrassed?* Every sentence about placement is written to him.
3. **A registrar at another institution,** holding one of our certificates, deciding in ninety seconds whether it means anything. She needs the grade scale on the document, the verification route, and no adjectives at all.
4. **A scholar the founder studied under,** reading the site once. He will not object to a claim being large. He will object to one being loose.

`EB §1.1` is the tie-breaker for every disagreement below. If a rule cannot be justified to one of the four, it is not a rule, it is a preference.

## §1.2 The governing sentence

> **The College writes down what it does, in the present tense, and can do every word of it on the day the page is published.**

Present tense because it is operating. What it does, not what it aspires to. And publishable only if operable — the rule the predecessor site broke on every page, and the reason it failed.

## §1.3 The three tests

Every paragraph passes all three or it is rewritten.

1. **The operability test.** Could a member of the Registry act on this sentence tomorrow morning? A sentence nobody can act on is decoration, however true.
2. **The signature test.** Would a scholar put his name under it? This kills the loose superlative and the flattering vagueness — not the large claim.
3. **The free-student test.** Would we tell this to a student who has paid us nothing and never will? If not, the sentence is sales, and it goes.

## §1.4 The prohibition of the brochure

The College does not have a marketing voice, a value proposition, a target segment, a funnel or a tone-of-voice matrix. It has a **prospectus**, a **manual**, a **schedule of charges** and a **disclosure**. Anything that reads as though written to persuade a stranger to buy is out of register even when every word in it is true — because the reader who most needs to believe us is the one who has been sold to before by exactly that voice.

The test is mechanical: **delete the sentence and see whether any fact is lost.** If nothing is lost, it was atmosphere.

# Part II — The voice

## §2.1 Register

Third person. Present indicative. Active where there is an actor.

> The Registry issues an offer naming the programme, the level of entry, the class, the timetable, the supervising member of faculty and the mode of instruction.

Not *offers are issued*, which hides who is responsible — the QAA disease (`EB §0.5`). Not *we'll send you your offer*, which is a shop. The passive is permitted in exactly two places: where the actor is genuinely immaterial (*classes are recorded*), and where naming the actor would identify a student.

## §2.2 Person

- **The institution is "the College".** Never *we*, *us*, *our team*, *the AMC family*. The single permitted exception is direct speech in a founder's statement, signed.
- **The reader is "the student", "the applicant", "the candidate", "the guardian"** — whichever is precise at that point (`EB §6.4`). Never *you*, except in an instrument, a form label, a button, or a question the page is answering in the reader's own words.
- **A member of faculty is "a member of faculty"** — not *a tutor*, *an instructor*, *a coach*, *your teacher*.

The reason is not formality. It is that a page written in the second person cannot state an obligation without sounding like an instruction to the reader, and half of what this site has to state is an obligation on *the College*.

## §2.3 Mood

Declarative. There is no exhortation on this site: no *discover*, *unlock*, *begin your journey*, *take the next step*. A button says what happens when it is pressed — *Apply to the Registry*, *Charges and funding*, *The arrangements in full* — and never how the reader should feel about pressing it.

A rhetorical question is permitted **only** when the page then answers it and the question is one a real reader actually asks. There is exactly one on the site — *where would I even begin, and will I be embarrassed?* — and it earns its place because it is `EB §1.1`'s second reader speaking, verbatim, and the paragraph exists to answer him.

## §2.4 Sentence law

- **One clause, one obligation.** If a sentence carries two duties, a lawyer reading it in a dispute will find the ambiguity between them. Split it.
- **The load-bearing clause.** In every rule, one clause does the work. It goes last, and it is the shortest thing in the sentence: *…to the published criterion, on demand, without notice, after an interval.*
- **The em-dash is rationed to one per paragraph.** Two dashes in a paragraph turn a statement into a monologue. Where a second aside is wanted, it is a second sentence or it is not wanted.
- **No sentence over about forty words.** Not a style preference: reader two is on a phone.
- **Semicolons are for lists of parallel duties** — *sessions are recorded and open to the guardian; anyone teaching a minor is vetted before the first session; and concerns go to a Safeguarding Officer who stands outside the teaching line* — and for nothing else.

## §2.5 The banned lexicon

Each entry is banned for a stated reason, and each has a replacement. This is Appendix A in short form; the full list is there.

| Banned | Why | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| *world-class, leading, premier, prestigious, elite, unrivalled* | An institution that says it is prestigious is telling you it isn't. Prestige is an inference the reader makes from precision. | State the fact that would cause the inference. |
| *committed to excellence, dedicated to, passionate about* | Nothing is claimed; nothing can be checked. Fails `§1.4`. | Name the standard and the consequence of missing it. |
| *journey, unlock, empower, transform, elevate, discover* | The lexicon of a product launch. | *study*, *learn*, *memorise*, *be examined*, *be admitted*. |
| *cutting-edge, innovative, state-of-the-art, seamless* | Technology adjectives date within a year and were never facts. | The mechanism: *audio-first*, *works with the connection off*, *stated in megabytes*. |
| *degree-equivalent, equivalent to, on a par with* | A claim about another institution's award that we have no standing to make. | Say what our award is and what it certifies. |
| *credit unit* | Carries a regulatory meaning in Nigeria. Using it borrows an authority we have not earned. | **notional hours** (`Reg. 2`). |
| *accredited* (of ourselves), *approved*, *recognised* | Untrue. | *conferred on the College's own authority by the Academic Council*, and the Standing page. |
| *tuition-free!* as a boast | The absence of a fee is an ethic, not a promotion. State it once, flatly, and move on. | *The College charges no tuition.* |
| *simply, just, easily, hassle-free* | Tells a struggling reader their difficulty is their fault. | Delete. If a thing is easy, its brevity will say so. |
| *we're excited to, we're pleased to announce* | The College has no feelings. | The announcement. |
| *content* (for lessons), *users* (for students), *engagement* | Platform vocabulary. It turns a *madrasah* into a SaaS product. | *lessons*, *materials*, *students*, and — never as a metric on a record — see `§4.6`. |
| *utilise, commence, endeavour, in order to, prior to* | Officialese. Gowers. | *use*, *begin*, *try*, *to*, *before*. |
| *very, extremely, incredibly, truly, highly* | An intensifier is the writer's confidence leaking into the sentence. | Delete. |

## §2.6 The permitted figures

Three rhetorical moves are house style, because each of them *adds* information:

1. **The plain reversal** — state the rule, then the cost of the opposite. *A College that charges for a retake has given itself a reason to prefer failure.* This is the site's characteristic sentence and it is permitted because the second half is an argument, not an emphasis.
2. **The named cost.** Where a policy costs the College something, say what. *Individual instruction and the ijāzah route are closed to minors except where a guardian attends or the College appoints a second member of faculty, which it does without charge.* The reader's trust is bought by the clause that costs money.
3. **The short rule.** A principle compressed to something a member of faculty can remember in a corridor: *where a capable adult could acquire it alone with good materials, it is not given live time.* Bolded, once per section at most.

## §2.7 Cadence

Vary sentence length deliberately: two long, one short. **A paragraph closes on the shortest sentence in it** — *It is not.* / *They are not sold.* / *and it has the advantage of being true.* This is the only ornament the prose is allowed, and it is what makes a page of regulations readable.

## §2.8 Headings

- `h1` — a noun phrase, no full stop. *Admission to the College.*
- `h2` — **a sentence, with a full stop.** *The five stages of admission.* *What an applicant is told before paying, not after.* This is deliberate and it is unusual: a full stop on a heading tells the reader the heading is a statement the College stands behind, not a label on a box.
- `h3` — a noun phrase or a short clause, no full stop, and it must be intelligible with the body text hidden.
- **Module markers** (`.module-marker`) are the section's index entry: two or three words, no verb.
- **Eyebrows** name the document class: *Admission*, *Disclosure*, *The College*.
- No heading contains a colon. No heading asks a question.

# Part III — The Disclosure Doctrine

This Part replaces the disclaimer register described in `§0.2`. It is the most important Part in this document, and the rest of the site's authority depends on it being obeyed exactly.

## §3.1 Four classes of claim

Every factual statement published by the College belongs to exactly one class, and the writer must know which before writing it.

| Class | Test | How it is written |
|---|---|---|
| **A recorded fact** | There is a record. A named person could produce it. | Stated flatly, with the record's period where the period matters: *admitted more than 1,550 students from 22 countries*, dated to 2020–2022. |
| **A published rule** | It is in the Regulations, or it is being published here as one. | Present indicative, with the citation: *(Reg. 9.2)*. |
| **A stated estimate** | A derivation exists and can be shown. | The figure **with its derivation on the page**: 16 weeks × 3 classes × 90 minutes ⇒ 200 notional hours. Never the figure alone. |
| **An aspiration** | None of the above. | **It is not published.** Not as a fact, not as an estimate, not as a status callout, not as a *forthcoming*. It goes in the founding plan and waits. |

The fourth line is the whole discipline. Everything the predecessor site got wrong was an aspiration published as one of the first three.

## §3.2 Concentration, not diffusion

A limitation of the College is published in **at most three places**, and they are fixed:

1. **The Standing, Recognition and Disclosure page** — the full statement, in the plainest available words. This page exists so that no other page has to carry the burden.
2. **One Regulation**, where the limitation has an operative consequence.
3. **The instrument itself**, where a holder of the certificate must not be able to mistake what it certifies.

**There is no fourth place.** A prospectus page, a faculty page, a charges page or a home page mentioning a limitation a fourth time is in breach of `EB §3.2`, and the fix is deletion plus a link. The link is not a weaker disclosure; a reader who wants the limitations is *already going to the page named for them*, and a reader who does not is being told something the College has already told them.

## §3.3 One negation to a page

Outside the Standing page and the Regulations, **a page carries at most one negative statement about the College's own standing.** Count them before publishing. Five of ten pages failed this on 17 August 2026; the correction is recorded in `§3.8`.

## §3.4 A page never opens on a negation

No hero, no lede, and no first paragraph of a section states what the College is not, lacks, has not obtained, or does not claim. The opening of a page is the only paragraph most readers finish, and it is spent on what the College *is*.

This rule was written because `awards.html` closed its awards table with *"The College does not confer degrees"* as the first prose a reader met after the scheme of awards — a page about four earned awards and an *ijāzah*, leading on an absence.

## §3.5 When a negation is not a disclaimer

Three negations are not disclaimers at all, and they stay wherever they are needed:

- **A definition.** *A Certificate of Attendance attests attendance and submission and makes no claim of attainment.* This is what the instrument *is*. Deleting it would make the document dishonest.
- **A boundary.** *Whose certificate this is* — the NBAIS distinction on Awards. A candidate must never be able to mistake the issuer.
- **A promise.** *The camera is never required, in any class or any examination.* / *No member of faculty is ever alone with a student under eighteen in a private session.* A negation that binds the College is a guarantee, and guarantees are the most valuable sentences on the site. Write more of them.

The test that separates a promise from a disclaimer: **who is constrained by it?** If the sentence constrains the College, it is a promise and it stays. If it constrains what the reader may believe, it is a disclosure and it goes to `§3.2`'s three places.

## §3.6 Nothing is softened

A limitation is stated once and not cushioned. No *unfortunately*, no *at this stage*, no *we hope to*, no reassuring clause after the fact. *The College is not accredited by the National Universities Commission, by any Nigerian state ministry of education, or by any foreign accrediting body, and does not represent otherwise.* Then the operative sentence — what the Registry does about it, before admission rather than after graduation. Candour and apology are different registers and the second one destroys the first.

## §3.7 *Forthcoming* is a status, not an apology

The word is permitted, and it is load-bearing: it is how the College refuses to sell what it cannot yet teach. But it is only permitted **with the semester in which the thing opens.** *Forthcoming* alone is an aspiration published as a fact (`§3.1`). *Forthcoming, Semester II 1448* is an operational commitment a student can hold the College to.

## §3.8 Register of corrections made under this Part

| Where | Was | Now |
|---|---|---|
| `awards.html` §The awards | *The College does not confer degrees.* — opening the page's first prose paragraph | Opens on the award and its authority. The degrees point stands in full on the Standing page, which is cited. `§3.4`. |
| `regulations.html` §Reg. 1 | A plate headed *What the College does not claim*, duplicating the Standing page's *A distance is a distance* card | Recast as the positive rule it always was — what the College *does* claim — with the disclosure cited rather than repeated. `§3.2`, `§3.3`. |
| `standing.html` | Six cards of limitations | **Unchanged and correct.** This is the page the doctrine exists to protect. |

# Part IV — Numbers

## §4.1 Every figure carries a class

No number is published without the writer knowing whether it is a recorded fact, a published rule or a stated estimate (`§3.1`). A figure whose class cannot be named is not published.

## §4.2 Derivations are shown where the figure would otherwise look invented

200 notional hours is not asserted; it is derived on the page. This is not academic modesty — it is the single cheapest way to be believed. A reader who can reconstruct one of your figures stops auditing the rest.

## §4.3 No rounded marketing figure

- No *over 1,500*, no *nearly 2,000*, no *thousands of*. The record says **more than 1,550**, so that is what is published, with its period.
- **No percentage without a denominator.** A pass rate is a fraction of a number of candidates, and both are published or neither is.
- No figure is repeated in a different rounding anywhere on the site. One number, one form, everywhere.

## §4.4 Form

- **Numerals** for quantities, ages, prices, hours, pages, semesters, and anything a reader may compare. **Words** for numbers opening a sentence and for small quantities inside prose where the number is not the point (*four faculties*, *ten regulations*).
- **Dates are Hijrī and Gregorian**, in that order, joined by a solidus: *1448 / 2026*. A single-calendar date is permitted only where the source is single-calendar (*22 February 2020*).
- **Ranges take an en dash and repeat the unit where ambiguity is possible**: *4–6 semesters*, *09:00–17:00*.
- **Money**: `EB §11.5`.
- **Time is West Africa Time with the UTC offset stated** — *West Africa Time (UTC+1)* — because reader two is in Doha and reader one is not.
- **Arabic numerals in the Arabic tree stay Western digits** where the figure is compared against the English tree (prices, hours), and take Arabic-Indic only in running prose. Digit direction is a rendering matter, not an editorial one: see `§5.6`.

## §4.5 An animated figure is decoration; the value is authored

Where a figure counts up (`data-count`), the **final value is authored in the DOM** and carried in `aria-label`. Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, with no IntersectionObserver, or where the string will not parse, the figure resolves immediately to its authored value. A number that exists only as the endpoint of an animation is a number that does not exist for a screen reader, a crawler, or a reader who scrolled fast.

## §4.6 A proxy is never printed as a fact

Minutes in a video player are not attendance. Clicks are not study. Logins are not engagement with anything. **A proxy printed on an academic record is an untruth with a number attached** (`Reg. 8`). Where the College wants to show effort, it shows what was demonstrated: pages recited, returns kept, papers submitted.

# Part V — Arabic

## §5.1 The Arabic tree is authored, never translated

Every page under `/madinah/ar/` is written in Arabic as an Arabic document. It is not a rendering of the English. Where an English paragraph's rhetoric does not exist in Arabic institutional prose, the Arabic says the same thing the way an Arabic prospectus says it — and where the Arabic has a better formulation, the English is not held back to match it.

Consequence, and it is deliberate: **the two trees are the same in fact and in obligation, and need not be the same sentence for sentence.** A translation memory would be the wrong tool here.

## §5.2 Parity is absolute

A route exists in both trees or in neither. The build enforces this: a manifest entry without an `ar` block, or with an `ar` block and no `*.ar.html`, warns by name at assembly. A page that ships English-only is a page that told reader one, in Nigeria, that Arabic is the object of study and then failed to write in it.

## §5.3 Register in Arabic

Fuṣḥā, institutional, unvowelled except where a vowel disambiguates or the text is Qur'ānic. No dialect. No calques from the English chrome — a *button* is not a *زر* in the sense the interface means it; the Arabic names the action. Terms of art take their Arabic originals, not transliterated English: *كلية*, *سجل*, *مجلس علمي*, *إجازة بسند متصل*, *الرواق*.

## §5.4 Transliteration into English

Our table is IJMES-derived, with three house departures stated in Appendix B. The governing rules:

- **Full transliteration with macrons and dots** for technical terms, titles of works and names of texts, italicised on first use: *ijāzah*, *sanad muttaṣil*, *murājaʿah*, *itqān*, *ṣaḥīḥ*, *taḥfīẓ*.
- **No transliteration for words naturalised in English**: Qur'an — but the College writes **Qur'ān**, because a College of the Qur'ānic sciences carries the macron; Islam, Muslim, hadith → **ḥadīth** in academic contexts, imam, madrasah, zakāh, Ramaḍān.
- **Names of people and places take their established English form** where one exists: Madinah (not al-Madīnah in running prose, though the College's own etymology paragraph uses *al-Madīnah* deliberately), Cairo, Taif.
- ʿayn is `ʿ` (U+02BF) and hamzah is `ʾ` (U+02BE). An apostrophe is not either of them, and the two are not interchangeable — *murājaʿah* and *Qurʾān* use different characters, and getting them the wrong way round is visible to reader four.
- Never both forms in one sentence. *ijāzah* or *إجازة*, chosen for the audience of the page, with the Arabic in `<span class="r-ar" lang="ar" dir="rtl">` where both are genuinely needed.

## §5.5 The Prophet ﷺ, and diacritics

- ﷺ (U+FDFA) follows the Prophet's mention, once per passage, without a preceding comma.
- Qur'ānic text is fully vowelled and is always inside `lang="ar" dir="rtl"`.
- English-tree Arabic phrases carry `lang="ar" dir="rtl"` without exception, including inside table cells and headings. This is what makes the site legible to a screen reader in either tree, and it is checked before publication (`§13.2`).

## §5.6 RTL typographic law

- **`css/arabic.css` loads last** in the Arabic tree. It must win over the Latin tracking, and cascade order is the whole mechanism.
- **A split figure is isolated.** Any element whose digits are split into inline spans for animation (`data-assemble`) takes `display:inline-block; direction:ltr; unicode-bidi:isolate` under `[dir="rtl"]`. Without it, 17 renders as ٧١ — which it did, on every Arabic page, until it was found by rendering rather than by reading.
- **Structure mirrors; content does not.** Layout, rails and progress fill mirror. A chain of transmission, a Qur'ānic line, a musical or numerical sequence and a code sample never mirror.
- Arabic sets larger than Latin at the same nominal size; the Arabic layer carries its own scale rather than inheriting the Latin one.

## §5.7 Citation form

- **Qur'ān**: sūrah name in transliteration, then chapter:verse — *Āl ʿImrān 3:7*. Not *Q3:7*, not *Surah 3, Ayah 7*.
- **Ḥadīth**: collection, then the collection's own numbering — *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 5027*. Where a chain is published, it is published in the Register of Asānīd and cited to it, never paraphrased on a prospectus page.

# Part VI — Terminology

## §6.1 The words this College uses, and why they are earned

The inherited ban (`§0.3`) forbade the vocabulary of a college to a college. It is refused. But a word is not free either — each of these is used because a specific fact makes it true, and the fact is what licenses the word.

| Word | What licenses it |
|---|---|
| **Faculty of —** | Four constituted faculties, each with a published syllabus and appointed members. Not a marketing grouping of courses. |
| **Certificate · Diploma · Higher Diploma** | Awards conferred by the Academic Council against published criteria, each with a published entry condition, a body of work in notional hours, and an examination. The word describes *our* award and asserts nothing about anybody else's. |
| ***Ijāzah bi-sanad muttaṣil*** | A founder holding *ijāzah* in the ten readings, a Register of Asānīd kept by the Academic Council, and five conditions precedent published on the Awards page. This is the College's central proposition and it is stated without hedging. |
| **Registry · Bursary · Academic Council · Board of Trustees · Rector** | Constituted offices with published responsibilities. Each name is used only for the body that actually holds the function — the Bursary is never said to decide an academic matter, and the Academic Council is never said to handle a charge. |
| **Examination · examiner · viva · moderation** | Real instruments, described in `Reg. 5`–`Reg. 6`. |
| **The Riwāq** | The College's ledger of memorisation. Named for the *riwāq* of a mosque-college, not for a software feature. |

## §6.2 The words it never uses

*University* (of itself) · *degree* (of its own awards) · *accredited* · *credit unit* · *degree-equivalent* · *campus* (of anything but the absence of one) · *course* where *programme* is meant, and never *course* for a book · *tutor* · *user* · *content* · *enrolment funnel* · any word from the Appendix A list.

**A book is never a course.** A programme is named, and prescribed texts are listed *inside its syllabus*. This is the correction that shaped the whole Faculties page and it is not reopened.

## §6.3 Names and styling

- **Al-Madinah International College** in full on first mention in any document; **the College** thereafter; **AMC** in instruments, seals and file names.
- Arabic: **كلية المدينة العالمية**.
- The name is written *Al-Madinah* — capital A, hyphen, no macron — in the institution's own name, because that is the registered style. The city, when discussed as a city, is *al-Madīnah*. Both appear in the etymology paragraph on the About page and the difference is intentional.
- Domain **al-madinahcollege.com**; the canonical host carries `www`.
- *International* is a description of whom the College serves and never a claim of standing anywhere (`standing.html`).
- The motto **وَالرَّاسِخُونَ فِي الْعِلْمِ** — *and those firmly rooted in knowledge*, Āl ʿImrān 3:7 — is retained from the founding. The name says where the tradition comes from; the motto says what the College is for.

## §6.4 Terms of art for people

Precise, and not interchangeable: an **applicant** has written to the Registry; a **candidate** is sitting an examination; a **student** is on the register; a **guardian** stands for a student under eighteen; a **member of faculty** teaches and is appointed; an **examiner** is appointed to a paper and may not be the student's own teacher; a **holder** possesses an instrument. Using the wrong one in a rule creates a real ambiguity about who the rule binds.

## §6.5 Capitalisation

Capitalise constituted bodies and named awards (*the Academic Council*, *the Higher Diploma*, *the Register of Asānīd*, *the Riwāq*). Lower-case generic uses (*a diploma from another institution*, *a faculty meeting*). Regulations are cited capitalised and numbered — *Reg. 3.2* — and this document as *EB §3.2*.

# Part VII — The page

## §7.1 Architecture

A page is: `eyebrow` → `h1` → `lede`, then a sequence of sections each opening on a `module-marker` and an `h2`. Sections alternate ground (`section--dark` / `section--light`) and never repeat a ground twice consecutively. Every section that a reader might want to reach directly carries `data-contents`.

## §7.2 One lede to a section, three sentences at most

The lede states what the section establishes. It does not preview the section's structure (*below you will find…*) and it does not repeat the heading in longer words.

## §7.3 The measure

Body prose is capped at `--measure: 68ch` via `.r-measure`. A 1,200px container with unmeasured prose is unreadable at exactly the line lengths a founder never notices on a desktop and reader one never sees at all.

## §7.4 A table where a card grid would flatter

Structured, comparable information — awards, entry conditions, charges, governance, the ladder of certificates — goes in a **ledger table**, not a grid of cards. A card grid makes six unequal things look equal, which is flattery. Cards are for parallel *arguments*, not for parallel *data*.

## §7.5 The plate

`.r-plate` is for a rule, a limit, an address or a set of hours — a thing a reader may need to copy or cite. It is not an emphasis device, and a page with four plates has none.

## §7.6 Contents

Sections declare `data-contents`. The sticky contents rail is **withdrawn** and stays withdrawn: it rode down the screen on every long page, which is the "panel floating in the middle" the founder was right to reject. `withContentsRail()` remains in the build as a documented no-op so that nobody re-derives it from scratch.

## §7.7 Links and citations

- A link is a **noun phrase naming its destination** — *Safeguarding and the conduct of teaching*, *Charges and funding*. Never *click here*, *read more*, *learn more*, or a bare URL in prose.
- Internal citations are `Reg. n.n` with a link to the anchor, or `EB §n` in repository documents.
- A cross-reference replaces a repetition (`§3.2`). Where two pages would say the same thing, one says it and the other cites it.
- An email address is written out and is `mailto:`-linked, because reader one is copying it into a phone.

## §7.8 The close

Every page ends on a `btn-row` offering the two things a reader who finished it actually wants next — one primary in gold, one outline. Not three. Not a newsletter.

# Part VIII — Material and light

Codifies `material-note.md`, which remains the working note; this Part is the law.

## §8.1 Radius is form, not ornament

Radius is a proportion of the shortest edge (~6–10%), so it climbs with the component. It survives `[data-ornament="none"]`, because it is the *shape of the object*, not a decoration on it. **Square, by decision:** table cells and their rules, hairline dividers, chapter rules, the ledger, column keylines, grain and guilloché grounds — the page's architecture. Radius belongs to the objects placed on it. Where a curved object holds a square one, the mount curves and the table does not. **Concentricity:** inner radius = outer radius − gap.

## §8.2 Two materials, and only two

**Sapphire glass on navy** and **polished cream on paper.** Everything on the site is one or the other. A third material — a grey card, a flat white panel, a borrowed UI surface — is what makes a site look assembled from parts.

## §8.3 Rim, bevel and halo are one inset stack

A card's edge light is a single `box-shadow` inset stack, not a border plus an outline plus a filter. One stack follows `border-radius` for free, composites once, and cannot fall out of alignment with the corner. Every shadow tier is **two** shadows: a tight contact shadow that says the object touches the surface, and a wide ambient one that gives it mass. A single blurred offset always reads as a sticker.

## §8.4 Damping is asymmetric

Gold builds fast and decays slow: `--pol-in: .34s` declared on `:hover`, `--pol-out: .9s` on the resting rule. This is the whole difference between a card that *warms* and a card that *blinks*. Symmetric transitions are why hover states feel cheap.

## §8.5 The travelling light

`.aurum` — a point of light travelling the perimeter, masked to a 1px ring by `mask-composite: exclude`, rotated through a registered `@property` — is **effect**, and effect is rationed. It is allowed on the cards the founder asked for it on, on the primary call to action, on seals and awards, and on a card under the pointer. It is authored **paused**, and `js/rusukh-atelier.js` lights only what an IntersectionObserver says is on screen (`rootMargin: 140px`), pausing rather than unclassing so phase resumes. A perimeter that is always moving everywhere is a casino; one that lights where the reader is, is a threshold.

Material light (`.edge-lit`) is the opposite: applied broadly, because a one-colour 1px border asserts that every edge of an object receives identical light, which is true of nothing.

**A conic gradient without the ring mask fills its box from the centre and paints a hard pie-slice across the object's face.** This shipped once. Check the mask.

## §8.6 The pseudo-element budget

Per card: bar or rule on `::before`, orbit on `::after`, sheen in a **background layer** (`--pol-sheen-*`, animating `background-position`). Plinths are the exception — their drawn rule holds `::after`, so the orbit rides `::before`. Exceeding the budget is how the next effect silently deletes the last one.

## §8.7 Gold-struck figures

`background-clip: text` with `-webkit-text-fill-color: transparent`. The glow **must** be `filter: drop-shadow()` — `text-shadow` paints *through* a transparent fill and produces a grey ghost. Child spans created by `data-assemble` inherit the fill explicitly or the figure loses its gold one digit at a time.

## §8.8 Ornament is a level, and the lowest level still works

`[data-ornament]` steps the effect layer down for a reader who wants the document rather than the atelier. At `none`, every animation stops and every ornamental pseudo-element goes — and the page must remain a complete, legible, prestigious document. If removing ornament breaks a layout, the ornament was structural and the structure was ornamental.

## §8.9 Diagrams

Infographics are authored SVG that draws itself (`data-diagram` / `data-draw` / `data-pop`, `stroke-dashoffset` from `getTotalLength()`). Two rules: **a diagram must be fully readable in its undrawn state**, and **a diagram must not restate the paragraph beside it** — it exists to show a relationship prose is bad at (a ring of semesters, a ladder of returns), or it does not exist. No stock illustration, no icon set from a library, no raster.

# Part IX — Type

## §9.1 Three faces, three roles

**Bodoni Moda** (display: mastheads, headings, published figures) · **Cinzel** (ceremonial capitals only) · **Inter** (body, chrome, tables). Arabic: **Amiri / Cairo**, appended for `[lang="ar"]`. No fourth face. No rounded friendly sans anywhere.

**The ration on Cinzel**, kept from the inheritance because it is right: numerals, level marks and lockups; no body copy, no sub-headings, no button labels, no navigation, no run longer than four words. Perhaps forty characters to a page. Used that sparingly it says the place is old; used for a paragraph it says the place is a theme.

## §9.2 The optical-size axis is not optional

Bodoni Moda is requested as a **variable** font on `opsz` with `font-optical-sizing: auto` on `body`. A Didone's hairlines are what make it magnificent at 64px and illegible at 15px; the axis interpolates a sturdier cut as type gets smaller, so one family covers a 5rem masthead and a 1.05rem sub-heading with neither compromised. **Requesting a static instance silently switches this off** — the page still renders in the right family and is wrong everywhere.

## §9.3 Reverse type is set lighter

Light glyphs on a dark ground irradiate: the light spreads into the dark, eats the thin strokes and leaves the stems, so an identical weight looks under-inked on navy and correct on cream. Every reversed display size takes about half a weight step back. This is the correction letterpress printers made by hand for reverse panels, and it is why the light and dark sections read as one publication.

## §9.4 Gold is never small text on paper

Gold on cream computes to roughly 2.3:1, well under AA. On warm-white, cream, pearl and ivory, small caps and icon strokes take `--bronze` and display figures take the section's own accent. `--gold-soft` and `--gold-champagne` are for dark grounds only.

## §9.5 No inline styles

Body copy carries **no** `style` attributes. The type system in `css/rusukh.css` sets the measure, the heading scale, the section rhythm and the meta line; a page that needs a one-off size needs a class. The site went from 299 inline styles to 15, and the fifteen are documented exceptions in art and diagram code.

## §9.6 Emphasis

`<strong>` for the load-bearing clause of a rule; `<em>` for transliterated terms and titles. Neither for volume. No underline except on links, no small caps in running prose, and no bold heading — a heading is already a heading.

# Part X — The Riwāq

## §10.1 The record belongs to the student

The ledger of memorisation is the student's document, kept in the student's browser, exportable, and never presented as the College's assessment of them. The College examines; the Riwāq records.

## §10.2 The Five Returns

1, 3, 7, 21, 60 days — `Reg. 3.3`, implemented in `js/riwaq-store.js`, and the return index is carried on every page of the ledger so the student can see *why* today's list is today's list. A schedule the student cannot reconstruct is a schedule the student will not trust.

## §10.3 Persistence is disclosed, not implied

The Riwāq persists to `localStorage` on one device. That is stated in the interface in plain words, and the export exists because of it. A portal that quietly loses six months of a student's *ḥifẓ* record has done a specific and serious harm, and the honest sentence is the mitigation until there is a server.

## §10.4 No streak, no league table, no shame

No consecutive-day counter, no ranking against other students, no red state for a missed day. A person returning to the Qur'ān after twenty years (`§1.1`, reader two) is the reader this rule is for. The ledger shows what is due and what was demonstrated. It never shows what was missed as a judgement.

## §10.5 Legible with the connection off

The ledger renders, computes its schedule and accepts a day's work with no network. This is `§12.1` applied to the one screen a student opens daily.

# Part XI — Money

## §11.1 The ethic

> **The record is free. The artefact is bought.**

The College charges nothing to teach, to examine, to re-examine, to hear an appeal, or to issue the digital certificate and transcript that *are* the award. Its charges are for engrossed instruments, an identity card and courier — objects — and none of them is a condition of an academic outcome.

## §11.2 What may never carry a charge

Application · placement assessment · tuition · examination · re-examination · appeal · a reasonable adjustment · the digital certificate · the digital transcript · verification of an instrument by a third party · a second member of faculty appointed for a minor's session. Each of these is free because charging for it would give the College an interest in the student's failure, delay or dependence.

## §11.3 The catalogue

Charges are presented as a **catalogue of instruments**: each item priced, each item's contents listed, and **the total stated** so nobody has to add up a college's fees themselves. Naira is the primary currency and is written first; USD and other currencies follow at a stated basis. Where items are commonly taken together they are offered as a named set with its own total, and the saving against the itemised sum is shown.

## §11.4 No price is a gate

At the point of examination the examiner is not shown a candidate's mode, charges or state of payment, **because the record carries no such field** (`Reg. 9.2`). This is stated wherever money is discussed, once, as a promise (`§3.5`).

## §11.5 Form of money

`₦` with a thin space and no decimals for whole Naira (*₦45,000*); `$` for USD with decimals only where the price has them. A price is never written *from* — a *from* price is a price with a hidden one behind it. Where a price varies, the range is given (`§4.4`) or the determining factor is named.

# Part XII — Access

## §12.1 The bandwidth covenant is an editorial obligation

The students this College was built for pay for their own data, on inexpensive devices, on connections that fail. Therefore: audio is authored to stand alone rather than offered as a fallback; every lesson has a form that works with the connection off; no examination requires sustained live video; **the weight of a lesson's required materials is stated in megabytes before the student opens it**; and a dropped connection never costs submitted work.

Editorially this means the *page* obeys it too: no video background, no font that blocks first paint, no hero that needs 400KB before a sentence is readable.

## §12.2 Accessibility

- `prefers-reduced-motion` is honoured in CSS and in JS from the same query, and **every carve-out resolves to the finished state, never a hidden one.** An entrance animation merely disabled leaves the element at `opacity: 0` — for a reader with vestibular sensitivity that is not a calmer page, it is a blank one.
- Split text preserves the original string in `aria-label` and hides the spans from the accessibility tree.
- Contrast is checked against the actual computed colour, not against the token's intent. `--gold` on `--cream` fails; `.section--dark h3 { color: #fff }` at specificity (0,1,1) silently beat a card's own name colour at (0,1,0) and made every faculty name on the home page white-on-white for as long as those cards existed. **It was found by rendering the page and reading the computed value, and that is now part of `§13.2`.**
- The camera is never required — in any class, any examination, anywhere on the site.

## §12.3 Safeguarding language

Published **before** a guardian needs it, in the same register as everything else: no legalese, no reassurance, no euphemism. Each control is a sentence naming who does what — vetting before the first session, no private one-to-one with a minor, sessions recorded and open to the guardian, a Safeguarding Officer outside the teaching line. `§3.5`: these are promises, and they belong wherever a guardian is reading.

# Part XIII — Practice

## §13.1 Amendment and citation

This bible is amended by adding or replacing a numbered clause, never by silent edit. The version line at the top moves. Clauses are cited in commits, in pull requests and in code comments as `EB §n`, exactly as a decision cites `Reg. n.n` — because **a decision that cites no rule is a decision nobody can examine.**

## §13.2 The pre-publication check

No page ships until all of these have actually been done. Not reasoned about — done.

1. **Rendered at 390px and at 1280px**, in both trees, and *looked at*. Every visual fault in this project's history was found this way and none was found by reading source.
2. **Computed colour read** on any text over a coloured ground, on the rendered page.
3. **Horizontal overflow measured** — `scrollWidth` against `clientWidth` at 390 and 1280. An 82px overflow on every page once survived a rename because nobody measured.
4. **Every asset URL checked against the filesystem.** A 404 on a favicon renders nothing and logs nothing. A blanket path rename caught one, once.
5. **Negation count per page** (`§3.3`), and the opening paragraph checked against `§3.4`.
6. **Every figure's class named** (`§3.1`, `§4.1`), and any estimate's derivation present on the page.
7. **Arabic parity** — the build's warnings are empty, and every Arabic passage in the English tree carries `lang` and `dir`.
8. **Split figures isolated** under RTL (`§5.6`); check one number in the Arabic tree with your eyes.
9. **`[data-ornament="none"]`** — page still complete and still prestigious.
10. **Reduced motion** — every animated element sits at its finished state.
11. **Links and anchors resolved**, internally and to every `#anchor` cited.
12. **Banned lexicon swept** (Appendix A) and inline `style` attributes counted.

## §13.3 Where this bible is enforced in code

`scripts/build-rusukh.js` warns by name on a missing `ar` block or Arabic file (`§5.2`). `css/rusukh.css` carries the type system that makes `§9.5` possible. `js/rusukh-atelier.js` gates the orbit (`§8.5`) and resolves figures under reduced motion (`§4.5`). `js/riwaq-store.js` implements `§10.2`. The rest of this document is enforced by a human reading a rendered page, which is why `§13.2` exists.

## §13.4 Register of defects the bible found on its first pass

Recorded because a rule that finds nothing on the day it is written is a rule nobody has tested. Applying Parts III, IV, V, VII, XII and XIII to the existing site once surfaced eight defects, six of them invisible to reading and total in effect. All eight are fixed.

| Defect | Found by | Why it was invisible |
|---|---|---|
| **Every internal link in the Arabic tree pointed into the English tree** — 117 of them, the whole mega-menu and every footer register. The rewrite that prevents this was written as a literal `/rusukh/` and the rename to `/madinah/` left it matching nothing; it was also applied to page bodies only and never to the chrome. | `§13.2.11` — reading the links on a built page | The links resolved to real pages with `200`s. Nothing errored. An Arabic reader was simply moved into English on any click. |
| **Every in-prose link on a paper ground was invisible** — `brand.css` sets `a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }` globally, so a citation computed to exactly the paragraph's colour with no underline. | `§7.7`, checked as a computed style | The link worked perfectly. It just could not be seen — which mattered the moment `§3.2` made citations carry the weight the repeated disclaimers used to. |
| **The skip link was permanently visible on every Arabic page**, at every width, as a gold pill over the prayer band. Two hiding schemes crossed: `brand.css` hides it vertically and re-homes it under RTL with a physical `right`, `rusukh.css` hid it horizontally at `left: -9999px`. The `[dir="rtl"]` rule won the horizontal axis and the house layer won the vertical one. | `§13.2.1` — looking at a rendered Arabic page and asking what a gold pill was doing there | It had been in every screenshot of that tree since it was built, and read as part of the chrome. |
| **The dock seal went on pulsing under `prefers-reduced-motion`.** The carve-out was written as `.dock__seal` (0,1,0); the breath is authored as `.dock:not(.is-open) .dock__seal` (0,3,0). | `§12.2`, checked with `getAnimations()` under a reduced-motion context | A carve-out that loses the cascade looks exactly like a carve-out that works. |
| **Every date and clock in the Arabic chrome was in English** — `4 Rabiʻ I 1448 AH · 17 August 2026` in an RTL strip, `Open now`, `09:00 – 17:00` — because the formatters were pinned to `en-GB` and every time string was hand-built out of a `pad()` helper. The Arabic tree's prose carries **244 Arabic-Indic digits on the charges page and not one Western digit**, so the band was the sole exception in the whole tree. | `§5.3`, `§4.4` — reading the band's text content, not the source | The band was the last thing anyone looked at on a page and it was formatted perfectly — in the wrong language. |
| **The Hijrī date silently became the Gregorian date** in the first attempt at the fix above: `locale() + '-u-ca-islamic-umalqura'` produced `ar-u-nu-arab-u-ca-islamic-umalqura`, two `-u-` extension sequences and not a valid BCP-47 tag. `Intl` rejected it, the `catch` returned `''`, and the caller's `\|\| g` fallback printed the Gregorian date into the Hijrī slot. | Looking at the output of my own fix, which read `١٧ أغسطس ٢٠٢٦ · ١٧ أغسطس ٢٠٢٦` | A defensive `catch` and a sensible fallback turned an invalid argument into a plausible wrong answer. Both dates were beautifully formatted. |
| **Sixteen inline `style` attributes** carrying type and rhythm decisions, each made by hand and therefore made several ways — including a bronze Arabic sub-heading hard-coded onto a card that may sit on navy. | `§9.5`, `§12.2` | Individually trivial; collectively the reason two identical components looked different. |

The lesson generalises, and it is the reason `§13.2` is a list of *actions* and not of *considerations*: **six of these eight were failures of the cascade, of a build string, or of a locale tag, and not one could be found by reading the source.** Every one of them produced output that was internally consistent, well-formatted and wrong. They were found by rendering the page and interrogating the computed result.

There is a second lesson, and it is about defensive code. Three of the eight — the build's dead rewrite, the reduced-motion carve-out that lost the cascade, and the invalid locale tag caught into a Gregorian fallback — were *silent because something was there to absorb the failure*. A `try`/`catch` with a sensible fallback, a lower-specificity override, and a regex that simply matches nothing all fail in the same way: they produce a plausible result and log nothing. So `§13.2` is checked against the **rendered artefact**, never against the absence of an error.

## §13.5 What this bible does not govern

Academic policy is the Regulations' and the Academic Council's, not this document's. Where a question is academic, `EB` is silent and `Reg.` answers. Where a question is neither — a genuinely new kind of page, a new instrument, a new medium — the answer is derived from `§1.1` and `§1.2` and then **written into this document as a new clause before it is shipped**, so the next person does not have to derive it again.

# Appendix B — Transliteration

IJMES-derived. Long vowels take macrons: ā ī ū. Emphatics and the rest take dots and underdots: ḍ ḥ ṣ ṭ ẓ ḏ ṯ ġ. ʿayn is `ʿ` (U+02BF); hamzah is `ʾ` (U+02BE); tāʾ marbūṭah is `-ah` in pause and `-at` in construct.

**Three house departures, stated so they are not read as errors:**

1. **`Qurʾān` keeps the macron in all contexts,** including running prose where IJMES would naturalise to *Quran*. A College of the Qur'ānic sciences does not naturalise its own subject. (Typographically the site uses a right single quotation mark in body copy — `Qur’ān` — because a curly apostrophe is what the reading faces render well at 1rem; the hamzah proper is used in transliterated technical terms and titles.)
2. **`ijāzah`, `zakāh`, `madrasah`, `Ramaḍān` are written with final `-ah` / `-āh`** rather than IJMES's `-a`, matching Nigerian and Gulf usage — which is what readers one and two actually write.
3. **The institution's own name is `Al-Madinah`** — no macron, capital A, hyphen — because that is its registered style. The city as a city is *al-Madīnah* (`§6.3`).

**Not transliterated** (naturalised English): Islam, Muslim, imam, sheikh, sultan, Cairo, Madinah, Mecca, Taif, Ramadan *in a date*, hijra *in a date*. **Transliterated always:** every term of art, every title of a work, every technical term in the Qur'ānic sciences.

# Appendix C — The nine sentences

For anyone writing a page for this College who has two minutes. If these nine are obeyed the rest of the document is mostly commentary.

1. **Write what the College does, in the present tense, and be able to do it tomorrow.** (`§1.2`)
2. **The College, not we. The student, not you.** (`§2.2`)

# Appendix D — The site's own addresses

Added when the College moved to the root of its domain, because a URL is an
editorial decision before it is a technical one.

**The College is the root.** It was built into `/madinah/` while the domain
still served WorldWide English College at `/`. That is over: the WEC-LC build
is archived at `archive/wec-lc/`, and these pages are the site. A College whose
prospectus lives in a subfolder of somebody else's site is telling the reader
something it does not mean to.

**No URL the College has published is ever retired.** Every `/madinah/…`
address 301s to its root equivalent, permanently, and the Arabic rule is
written first so `/madinah/ar/awards/` becomes `/ar/awards/` and not
`/ar/ar/awards/`. This is `§3` applied to addresses: an instrument, a printed
letter or a review link that names a URL is a claim the College made, and it
holds.

**A redirect must land on a page that exists.** The archived redirect table
carried 74 generated routes pointing at `/academics/`, `/governance/`,
`/students/` and `/press/` — none of which this College has. None were merged.
A redirect into a 404 is worse than a 404, because it looks like the site
meant to send you there.

**A shorthand is a spoken address.** `/apply`, `/charges`, `/chains`,
`/ijazah`, `/rules`, `/disclosure` exist because the Registry will read them
down a telephone. Each resolves to a real page; a shorthand whose destination
has been retired is removed, not repointed at something adjacent.

**`/verify/` takes no wildcard, ever.** A rule pointing `/verify/*` at the
verification page would drop whatever followed the slash, so a mistyped
credential link or a re-encoded QR code would resolve to an *empty*
verification page. That is the one failure this route must never produce: it
looks like a check that ran.
