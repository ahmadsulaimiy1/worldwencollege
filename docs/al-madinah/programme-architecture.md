# Programme architecture — a study, a critique, and a proposal

Commissioned by the Founder: study the programme architecture of the Arab Community
of Lagos (arablagos.com), extract the principles behind it, critique it, and use it as
the starting point for something broader, academically stronger and institutionally
structured.

Read on 22 August 2026: the home page and four course pages —
`course-imams.html`, `course-diplomats.html`, `course-worshipper.html`,
`course-parents.html`. Quotations below are verbatim from those pages.

---

## Part I — What they do, and what is genuinely good in it

### 1. They segment by identity, not by subject

Their eight tracks are named for *who you are*, never for what is taught:

> Arabic for Fluency · Practical Arabic for Worshippers · Practical Arabic for Imams ·
> Arabic for Parents · Practical Arabic for Educators · Practical Arabic for Diplomats ·
> Conversational Arabic for Hajj Pilgrims · Arabic for Media Professionals

This is the single most transferable idea on the site, and it is worth taking seriously.
A syllabus asks a stranger to work out whether it is for them. A track tells them.
Al-Madinah currently lists four faculties and a ladder of levels — correct, and it asks
every visitor to do the translation themselves.

### 2. The headline is the outcome, not the content

> "Hold Real Conversations with Native Arabs in 12 Months"
> "Conduct formal diplomatic negotiations entirely in Arabic"
> "eliminate dependence on interpreters for formal interactions"
> "Understand the meaning of every Surah you recite regularly"
> "Command the Pulpit: Master Classical Syntax & Delivery"

Every one names a changed condition, with a period attached. None of them lists modules
first. This is right, and it costs nothing academically to do.

### 3. One verb turns a catalogue into a diagnosis

> "Find Your Track"

Not "Browse courses." The visitor is told there is a right answer for them and that the
institution will help them find it. It reframes a list as an act of guidance.

### 4. A named method, stated as a claim about the world

> "Mastery Through Living Interaction"
> "speaking-first framework"
> "Research indicates classroom Arabic in Nigeria creates a 70% gap between grammar
> knowledge and conversational fluency."

The method is given a name, a mechanism (*Daily Repetition*, *Native Exposure*) and a
problem it exists to solve. Whether or not the statistic survives scrutiny — see the
critique — the *structure* of the argument is exactly right: name the failure, name the
remedy, name the remedy's parts.

### 5. Progression is shown as time, not as a table

> "Months 1-3: Master Diplomatic Vocabulary & Protocols
> Months 4-6: Practice Formal Negotiations & Correspondence
> Months 7-8: Conduct Official Communications Confidently"

Three lines, and a reader knows what the eight months feel like.

### 6. A named scholar, with credentials attached

Dr Salahudeen M.A., President and Lead Scholar, Ph.D. (Ibadan, 2013), with a linked PDF
of credentials. Authority is attached to a person, not to an institution in the abstract.

## Part II — The critique

### The central fault: the credential is sold, not earned

This is not a matter of taste. It is visible on every course page:

| Course | Tiers | Where the certificate sits |
|---|---|---|
| Imams | ₦145,000 / ₦299,000 / ₦420,000 | "Official certification" — Scholar and Elite only |
| Diplomats | ₦159,992 / ₦279,992 / ₦399,992 | "Certificate of Completion" — Premium tier only |
| Worshippers | ₦39,999 / ₦52,999 / ₦69,999 | "Personal certificate" — Premium tier only |
| Parents | ₦27,999 / ₦49,999 / ₦87,999 | not offered |

The same teaching, at three prices, with the record of attainment attached to the price
rather than to the attainment. Two students may learn identically and only the one who
paid more leaves with evidence.

An academic reviewer needs one glance to see this, and it is not recoverable by good
design. It is also the exact inverse of what this College already publishes: *the record
is free, the artefact is bought*, and **no instrument is withheld for non-payment**. That
contrast is the strongest single thing Al-Madinah can say about itself, and it should be
said plainly rather than implied.

### No entry standard, therefore no defensible level

> "No prior Arabic knowledge required."
> "Intermediate Arabic is helpful but not required."

The eight-month diplomatic track admits a complete beginner and an intermediate speaker
to the same cohort with the same exit. Either the beginner cannot reach the outcome or
the outcome is set at the beginner's reach. Both are academic failures; the page does not
say which one it is.

### No hours, no levels, no external referent

Nothing states notional hours, a level framework, or any comparison to anything outside
the site. A registrar in another country cannot place any of it.

### Eight parallel tracks and no ladder between them

Finish *Arabic for Imams* and there is nowhere to go. There is no progression from a
worshipper's three months to a scholar's years — which means the institution can only
ever sell a first purchase.

### Assessment is a gesture

> "Private voice note submissions reviewed by senior scholars with written feedback."

No published criterion, no moderation, no second marker, no appeal, no re-examination
policy. For the Parents track, assessment exists only in the top tier — so the evidence of
whether it worked is itself an upsell.

### "Certificate of Completion" is not an academic instrument

Completion records attendance. Attainment records what was demonstrated. The College
already draws that distinction on its Awards page and should keep drawing it.

### The 81% and the 70%

> "an 81% child improvement rate"
> "Research indicates … a 70% gap"

Neither is sourced. This College's Standing page classifies every published claim as a
recorded fact, a published rule, or a stated estimate. Two unsourced percentages would
fail that test.

### Scarcity language

> "Secure your spot in the upcoming cohort" · "Secure Your Founding Spot"

Colleges publish a closing date. Training academies create urgency.

### Nothing a third party can verify

No register, no verification route, no way for an employer or a registrar to check that a
holder of the certificate holds it. Al-Madinah already has `/verify/` and a permanent
Register entry, free, for ever.

### So: why it reads as a training academy rather than an international college

Price tiers instead of academic tiers · certificates behind a paywall · no entry
requirements · no hours or levels · no progression · no moderation or appeal · no external
verification · no published regulations · scarcity marketing. Individually survivable.
Together they describe a different kind of institution.

## Part III — The proposal

### The one structural idea that changes everything

**Do not build eighteen courses. Build one ladder and eighteen doors into it.**

The Arab Community of Lagos sells eight parallel products. A college that copied that
would inherit its ceiling: no progression, no comparability, eighteen syllabi to staff, and
eighteen chances to be caught claiming something it cannot teach.

So the College separates two things that a course catalogue conflates:

- **A PROGRAMME** is examined, credentialled, laddered and belongs to a faculty. It is the
  unit of academic record. Programmes are few and they are deep.
- **A PATHWAY** is a named route *through* the programmes for a particular kind of learner
  — an itinerary, not a product. It selects modules that already exist, orders them for a
  purpose, names the exit, and states which programme it feeds into next.

A pathway therefore costs no new syllabus and no new examiner. It is a *reading* of the
curriculum, published so that a diplomat, an imam or a parent can see themselves in it.
And because every pathway lands on the same ladder, finishing one always leads somewhere.

This is broader than a course list, cheaper to staff, and academically stronger — because
the standard lives in the programme and cannot be diluted by the pathway.

### The faculties

The Founder proposes four. His structure is right; one adjustment is needed, and it is
flagged rather than made.

| Proposed | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Faculty of Arabic Language | Adopt as is. Already exists. |
| Faculty of Qur'anic Studies | Adopt, keeping the present name — **the Faculty of the Qur'ān and its Sciences** — which is the College's centre and its strongest asset. |
| Faculty of Islamic Studies | Adopt as a **rename** of the present Faculty of Sharī'ah Sciences, widened to hold the academic part of Da'wah. |
| Faculty of Professional Arabic & Special Purposes | Adopt as the fourth faculty, holding the applied part of Da'wah. |

**The flag.** The College presently has a Faculty of *Da'wah & Applied Studies* carrying
three programmes. The proposed fourth faculty replaces it. Da'wah must not simply
disappear: its academic content belongs in Islamic Studies and its applied content in
Professional Arabic, and the three existing programmes need placing by name before any of
this is published. That is a decision for the Academic Council, not for a stylesheet.

### The pathways

All eighteen the Founder listed are placed below. **Each carries a status, and the status
is published.**

The College's own rule (Faculties page) is that a place in the curriculum is earned by
four tests: *necessary, teachable at a distance, examinable, and staffed*. Publishing
eighteen pathways as though all were open would breach that rule on the day it went up —
and the first professor to ask "who teaches Arabic for Healthcare Professionals?" would
have found it. Declared status is not a hedge. It is the thing that makes the list
credible: a reviewer who sees four open, six in preparation and eight planned reads an
institution that knows what it has.

**Faculty of Arabic Language**
- Arabic for Academic Excellence
- Intensive Arabic Immersion
- Arabic Conversation Lab
- Arabic for International Students
- Arabic for Children · Arabic for Teenagers *(both gated on the Safeguarding Officer being appointed — see the transfer register)*
- Arabic for Reverts to Islam
- Arabic for Travellers

**Faculty of the Qur'ān and its Sciences**
- Qur'ān Understanding through Arabic
- Arabic for Pilgrims (Hajj & Umrah)

**Faculty of Islamic Studies**
- Arabic for Islamic Scholarship
- Arabic for Da'wah & Imams
- Arabic for Parents

**Faculty of Professional Arabic & Special Purposes**
- Arabic for Business & Professionals
- Arabic for Diplomats & International Relations
- Arabic for Healthcare Professionals
- Arabic for Media & Journalism
- Arabic for Teachers & Educators

**The status vocabulary**, to be used exactly and nowhere loosely:

- **Open** — admitting now. A named member of faculty is responsible. Syllabus, hours,
  entry requirement, assessment and exit award are all published.
- **In preparation** — the syllabus is written and the teacher is identified; a date is
  published and no application is taken.
- **Planned** — the College intends it. Nothing is published beyond the intention, and it
  is never presented as available.

No pathway may be advertised above its status. This is the Disclosure Doctrine applied to
the prospectus.

### The methodology

The Founder asks for an original methodology with memorable branding, as *Living
Interaction* is theirs. The College does not need to invent one. It already practises one,
it is already implemented in the Riwāq, and it is stronger than theirs for one reason:
**it can be tested.** "Living Interaction" is a description of a classroom. What follows is
a description of a measurement.

> ## The Itqān Method
> ### الإتقان — *not what was reached; what remains*

Four disciplines, in order:

1. **التلقّي — Talaqqī · Reception.** What is learnt is received aloud, from a teacher,
   mouth to ear, and returned aloud. This is the tradition's own answer to their
   "speaking-first framework", and it is a thousand years older and better founded.
2. **المتن — Matn · The text before the opinion.** The prescribed text is held before it
   is discussed. A student who cannot recite the matn does not debate it.
3. **المراجعة — Murājaʿah · The Five Returns.** Every portion returns at 1, 3, 7, 21 and
   60 days, whether or not the student remembers that it is due. Already built:
   `js/riwaq-store.js`, and the return index is carried on every page of the ledger.
4. **الإتقان — Itqān · Examination without notice.** Mastery is demonstrated to the
   published criterion, on demand, without warning, after an interval — Reg. 3.1. Free and
   unlimited re-examination, Reg. 3.4.

The claim that follows from it, and which no competitor here can make:

> Most institutions record what a student reached.
> This College records what a student retains.

That sentence is already on the site. The Itqān Method is its mechanism, and naming the
mechanism is what turns a good line into an academic identity.

### How the College surpasses them, stated as five differences

| | Arab Community of Lagos | Al-Madinah International College |
|---|---|---|
| **The credential** | attached to the price tier | free, for ever, to every student — the artefact alone is bought |
| **Entry** | "no prior Arabic required" on an advanced track | published entry requirement per programme; study without certification assessed on its own evidence |
| **Progression** | eight parallel tracks, no ladder | one ladder, many doors; every pathway names what it feeds |
| **Assessment** | voice notes, top tier only | published criterion, moderation, appeal at every stage, free unlimited re-examination |
| **Verification** | none | permanent Register entry and a public verification route, free, for ever |

## Part IV — What the Founder must decide before any of this is published

1. **The fourth faculty.** Does *Da'wah & Applied Studies* become *Professional Arabic &
   Special Purposes*, and where do its three existing programmes go? Da'wah cannot be
   silently dropped.
2. **Which pathways are Open.** Each requires a named member of faculty. Four honest ones
   are worth more than eighteen unstaffed ones, and the eighteen can still all be listed —
   at their true status.
3. **Children and teenagers.** Both are barred until a Safeguarding Officer is appointed,
   reporting to the Trustees and outside the teaching line. This is already the College's
   published position and it is not for a marketing decision to move.
4. **The Itqān Method.** Adopt the name, or replace it — but adopt something, and adopt it
   before the prospectus is written rather than after.
