# Appointment briefs — ready to send

Three posts stand between Worldwide English College and its first taught
cohort. Each brief below is written to be sent as it stands, to a
candidate, an agency or a colleague at another institution. Nothing in
them needs drafting first.

They are ordered by what each one unblocks, not by seniority.

---

## 1 · EXTERNAL EXAMINER

**The one that blocks conferral. Nothing the College issues is worth
issuing until this post is filled.**

### What to send a candidate

> **External Examiner — International English Fluency Certificate (IEFC)**
> Worldwide English College — London Campus
>
> Worldwide English College is seeking an External Examiner for the
> International English Fluency Certificate, a six-level CEFR-aligned
> English programme delivered online.
>
> **The College's position, stated plainly.** WEC is newly
> established. It holds no accreditation. It has taught no cohort and
> conferred no award. The full curriculum, assessment scheme, rubrics
> and marking criteria are written and can be sent to you before you
> decide. We are approaching you because the standard we have written
> cannot be confirmed by the people who wrote it.
>
> **The role.** Standard external examining practice:
> - Review a sample of marked student work at each level examined, and
>   confirm the marking matches the published criteria.
> - Confirm that each level is pitched at the CEFR band it claims.
> - Comment on the assessment instruments themselves — whether they
>   measure what they say they measure.
> - Sit at the board that approves results, and confirm or decline to
>   confirm the pass list.
> - Write an annual report. The College undertakes to publish your
>   report's findings, including where they are critical, and to
>   respond to each in writing.
>
> **What we ask of you first, before any student exists.** The first
> engagement is a review of the assessment scheme as written — the
> Assessment Handbook, the sixty rubrics, and the level examination
> specifications. That is a defined reading task with a defined output,
> and it can be done now.
>
> **What we are looking for.** MA or PhD in TESOL, Applied Linguistics
> or a cognate field, with assessment experience in English language
> teaching. Prior external examining experience is welcome and not
> required. You must have no other role at WEC and no financial
> interest in it — that independence is the entire point of the post.
>
> **Terms.** Engaged per assessment cycle, not salaried. Fee negotiable
> and paid on completion of each cycle's report. Typical commitment is
> a defined number of days per cycle.
>
> **What you would be reading.** Twelve published volumes, including a
> 1,200-hour curriculum, an assessment handbook, and a Level I
> teacher's companion in which every claim is marked with where its
> knowledge came from. All of it is available to you before you commit
> to anything.
>
> Write to info@worldwencollege.co.uk naming the post.

### Where to look for candidates

- IATEFL (International Association of Teachers of English as a Foreign
  Language) — its Testing, Evaluation and Assessment special interest
  group is the single most concentrated source of the right people.
- TESOL International Association.
- Applied Linguistics and TESOL departments at UK universities — the
  people who already examine for other providers.
- ALTE (Association of Language Testers in Europe) member organisations.
- EAQUALS, for providers who have been through inspection themselves.

### The one thing not to do

Do not appoint someone who has taught for the College, written for it,
or holds any other role in it. An examiner who is internal is not an
examiner, and the appointment would have to be undone rather than
upgraded once anyone looked.

## 2 · ACADEMIC REVIEWER

**The one that unblocks everything already written.**

### What to send a candidate

> **Academic Reviewer — WEC Press**
> Worldwide English College — London Campus
>
> Worldwide English College has produced twelve volumes covering its
> curriculum, assessment scheme, programme architecture and teaching
> support. Every one of them was reviewed, if at all, by the people who
> wrote it — which is to say not reviewed.
>
> We are seeking a reviewer to change that, and we are stating the
> problem this bluntly because it is the problem.
>
> **The role.** Read a defined list of volumes and report on them:
> whether the curriculum teaches what it claims to teach; whether the
> assessments measure what they say they measure; whether each level is
> pitched where it says it is; and what is wrong. The College undertakes
> to publish your findings against the volumes they concern, including
> where they overturn a design decision already in print.
>
> **What we are looking for.** MA TESOL, Applied Linguistics or
> equivalent, with curriculum or assessment experience. You must not be
> an author of any of the material.
>
> **Terms.** A consultancy engagement, not a salaried post — a defined
> number of days against a defined reading list, fee negotiable.
>
> Write to info@worldwencollege.co.uk naming the post.

### Why this is separate from the External Examiner

The College holds three separations as non-negotiable: an author is
never a reviewer; a reviewer is never the External Examiner; and
whoever confers an award never examined it. One person cannot fill both
posts. The Examiner's independence is what makes an award mean
something; the Reviewer's independence is what makes the published
material trustworthy. Collapsing them would save a fee and destroy both.

## 3 · PRACTISING TEACHER

**The one that produces evidence no amount of writing can produce.**

### What to send a candidate

> **Teacher — International English Fluency Certificate**
> Worldwide English College — London Campus
>
> Worldwide English College has written a complete six-level English
> programme: sixty modules, every lesson planned stage by stage with
> declared timings, every assessment written with published criteria,
> and a teaching support record of 530 entries covering the mistakes
> each point provokes, why learners make them, and what to do about it.
>
> None of it has been taught to anyone.
>
> **The role.** Teach a cohort. Any level, any hours. Then tell us what
> actually happened: where the lesson plan broke, which explanation
> reached the learner the first one did not, how long a stage really
> takes, and which of the mistakes we predicted are the mistakes people
> actually make.
>
> **Why this matters more than it sounds.** The College's teaching
> record marks every entry with where its knowledge came from — read off
> the curriculum, attested in the field, or designed by the authors. A
> fourth category exists for what was observed in a real classroom, and
> it currently contains nothing. One teacher, one cohort, one term fills
> it. That evidence cannot be reasoned out; it can only be observed.
>
> **What we are looking for.** A qualified, practising English teacher
> (CELTA/DELTA, MA TESOL or equivalent classroom experience) who is
> willing to work against a written plan and to write down where the
> plan was wrong. The second half of that sentence is the job.
>
> **Terms.** Hours and fee negotiable, including part-time and
> fractional arrangements.
>
> Write to info@worldwencollege.co.uk naming the post.

## What each appointment unblocks, in one table

| Post | Unblocks | Blocked until filled |
|---|---|---|
| External Examiner | Conferral of any award | Every certificate, every transcript's award line, the Graduate Register, the verification route |
| Academic Reviewer | Trust in twelve published volumes | Any claim that the material has been independently assessed |
| Practising teacher | The observed-in-teaching evidence state | Every claim about how the programme performs in a classroom |

None of the three is a salaried post. All three can be consultancy or
fractional engagements. The External Examiner is the only one that is
strictly sequential — it must be filled *before* the first conferral,
not after, because a credential issued without it cannot be retroactively
made independent.
