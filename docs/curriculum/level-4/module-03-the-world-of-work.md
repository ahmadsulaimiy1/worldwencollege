# WEC — Level IV, Module 3: The World of Work

*Part of `docs/curriculum-level-4-upper-intermediate.md`'s Level IV build-out.*

**Module learning objectives.** By the end of Module 3, the learner
can: form and use the third conditional to reflect on hypothetical
past outcomes; write a professional email and a short workplace
proposal in appropriate formal register; participate in a job
interview, answering behavioural questions with genuine reflection;
use professional workplace phrasal verbs and formulaic correspondence
language accurately.

**Content map (5 learning items):** module overview & key phrases;
Lesson 3.1 ("If I Had Known... — Third Conditional"); Lesson 3.2
("Dear Hiring Manager... — Professional Correspondence & the Job
Interview"); Module 3 Quiz (10 questions); Module 3 Assignment
("A Professional Email & Mock Interview").

## Reference reading: Module overview & key phrases

**Key phrases introduced this module:** If I had known..., I would
have... — With regard to.../Further to your email,... — Please find
attached... — I look forward to hearing from you. — Could you tell me
about a time when...? — Looking back, I would have approached that
differently.

**Discourse markers this module (functional set — formal
correspondence framing):** *with regard to*, *further to your
email/our conversation*, *given that*, *provided that* — formulaic
formal-register openers and conditional-adjacent connectors specific
to professional written communication, distinct from the essay-focused
cohesion devices of Module 2.

**Phrasal verbs & collocations this module:** *follow up (on
something)* (check on progress or send a reminder), *touch base
(with someone)* (make brief contact to check in), *circle back (to
something)* (return to a topic later), *reach out (to someone)*
(make initial contact, often professionally), *get back to (someone)*
(respond to them after some time).

**BrE / AmE note:** email sign-off conventions differ subtly: British
professional emails often close with **Kind regards** (or the more
formal **Yours sincerely** when the recipient is named), while American
professional emails more commonly close with **Best regards** or
simply **Best,** — none is "more correct," but using a sign-off that
matches the expected register and, where relevant, the reader's
variety, is itself a genuine professional-communication skill.

**Key vocabulary previewed:** professional-email vocabulary
(attachment, correspondence, recipient, subject line, cc/bcc),
interview vocabulary (candidate, qualification, strength, area for
development, behavioural question). Intercultural note: interview
norms — how directly to discuss achievements, how much small talk is
expected before business, how weaknesses should be framed — vary by
country and workplace culture; this module teaches one widely used
international-professional-English convention, not a universal
standard.

## Lesson 3.1 — "If I Had Known... — Third Conditional"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) form
the third conditional correctly (`If + past perfect, would have + past
participle`); (2) use it to reflect on a hypothetical different
outcome to a real past situation; (3) distinguish the third
conditional (an unreal past) from the second conditional (an unreal
present/future, Level III Module 8); (4) use the third conditional to
reflect honestly on a workplace or career decision.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Level III, Module 8 (second conditional);
Level IV, Module 1 (past perfect — the third conditional's
grammatical foundation).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor states one real (generic, non-
identifying) past regret using natural language ("I didn't apply for
that job — I wish I had") and asks learners what tense they'd need to
express "what would have happened if I had," before formal
presentation.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model: "If I had known about the deadline
earlier, I would have submitted my application on time. If she hadn't
taken that internship, she wouldn't have discovered her interest in
data analysis." Highlight explicitly: third conditional describes a
hypothetical different outcome to something that already, definitely
happened (or didn't) — genuinely impossible to change now, distinct
from the second conditional's present/future hypothetical. Contrast
directly: "If I were braver, I would apply" (second conditional, still
possible now) vs. "If I had been braver, I would have applied" (third
conditional, that specific past chance is gone).

**Guided practice (10 min).** Learners convert 8 real-sounding
workplace/career scenario pairs ("I didn't ask for feedback. I didn't
improve as quickly.") into third-conditional sentences.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners write 4-5 third-
conditional sentences reflecting on a real or invented career/work
decision, then share one with a partner, who asks a genuine follow-up
question about what actually happened instead.

**Speaking activity.** The partner reflection exchange above, followed
by a brief whole-class discussion of one especially interesting
"what if" shared per pair.

**Critical thinking / discussion prompt.** "Do you think reflecting on
'what would have happened if...' is generally useful, or can it become
unhelpful if someone does it too much? Where's the line?" — a
genuinely mature B2 question, connecting the grammar's function to a
real psychological/professional judgement about productive versus
unproductive reflection.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to someone reflecting on a
career decision using third conditional (6-7 sentences) and complete a
simple "what happened / what might have happened instead" grid.

**Reading activity — extended reading & analysis (8 min).** Read a
short professional reflection excerpt (150-180 words) using third
conditional to discuss a past career turning point. Answer 2 literal
questions and 2 analytical questions ("What does the writer's choice
to use third conditional here suggest about how they feel about that
decision now — regret, acceptance, or something else? What evidence
supports your reading?").

**Writing task (5 min).** Write 4-5 third-conditional sentences
reflecting on a real or invented workplace decision, being careful to
distinguish clearly from second-conditional forms.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill the heavily contracted,
connected pronunciation of third-conditional forms in natural speech
("If I'd known, I would've..." — often /wʊdəv/) versus their fuller
form in careful or written register.

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** A conditional-type sorting game: sort 9
example sentences into first/second/third conditional, reviewing the
full conditional system built across Levels III-IV.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks correct third-conditional
formation and correct distinction from second conditional during
independent practice — the lesson's core target error.

**Homework.** Learners choose one real or invented job they'd like to
apply for and jot down 3-4 notes about their relevant experience,
ready for Lesson 3.2's interview practice.

**Revision.** Lesson 3.2 opens with learners briefly naming their
homework job choice in one sentence.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners add one mixed-conditional
sentence as a recognition-level preview ("If I had taken that job, I
would be in a completely different city now" — a past condition with a
present result), formally taught at a later level.

## Lesson 3.2 — "Dear Hiring Manager... — Professional Correspondence & the Job Interview"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) write
a professional email in correct formal structure and register
(subject line, greeting, purpose, body, closing); (2) draft a short
workplace proposal with a clear recommendation and reasoning; (3)
answer a behavioural interview question using the STAR-style structure
(Situation, Task, Action, Result) with genuine reflection; (4) use
professional workplace phrasal verbs naturally in both writing and
speech.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Lesson 3.1 (third conditional, for
reflective interview answers), Level III Module 3 (workplace
vocabulary, a business pitch — this lesson extends professional
communication into written correspondence and interview format).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor shows one poorly-structured
professional email (no clear subject line, unclear purpose, abrupt
closing) and one well-structured one on the same topic, asking
learners to identify the differences, before formal presentation.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model the professional email structure
explicitly: **subject line** (clear and specific), **greeting**
(`Dear [Name]` or `Dear Hiring Manager` if unknown), **purpose stated
early** (`I am writing to...`), **body** (clear, organised, using
`with regard to/further to` where relevant), **closing** (`I look
forward to hearing from you`), **sign-off** (`Kind regards,`). Then
model a short workplace proposal structure: a brief context, a clear
recommendation, 1-2 supporting reasons, and a next-step suggestion.
Then introduce the STAR interview-answer structure with a model
answer to "Tell me about a time you solved a difficult problem,"
including one third-conditional reflective sentence ("If I hadn't
asked for help early on, the project would have taken much longer").

**Guided practice (10 min).** Learners are given a jumbled
professional email (parts shuffled: subject, greeting, purpose, body,
closing, sign-off) and reorder it correctly, then practise answering
2 behavioural interview questions aloud in pairs using the STAR
structure.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners draft a short professional
email (requesting information, following up on an application, or
proposing an idea) using the full structure and at least one
professional phrasal verb, then prepare a STAR-structured answer to
one interview question using their Lesson 3.1 homework notes.

**Speaking activity — interview task.** In pairs, Learner A conducts a
short mock job interview (2-3 questions, including at least one
behavioural question), Learner B answers using the STAR structure with
a genuine third-conditional reflection, then swap roles — this
lesson's core speaking task and the direct basis for Module 3's
assignment.

**Critical thinking / discussion prompt.** "Why do you think
interviewers often ask about a past difficulty rather than just asking
'what are your strengths?' What does a well-structured answer to a
difficulty question reveal that a simple strengths list doesn't?" — a
genuinely reflective question about the actual communicative purpose
behind common interview formats.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to a short mock interview
answer using the STAR structure and identify each of the four parts
(Situation, Task, Action, Result).

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read a well-structured professional
email and a short workplace proposal, labelling their structural
parts.

**Writing task (5 min).** Finalise your professional email draft,
checking register consistency (no casual language slipping into a
formal email) and correct structure.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill calm, confident interview-
answer pacing and intonation — clear stress on key result words
("...and as a RESULT, we delivered the project on time") and composed
delivery when answering an unscripted follow-up question.

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** A professional-phrasal-verb matching
game (follow up on, touch base, circle back, reach out, get back to)
matched to workplace scenarios.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks correct email structure
and register, and a genuine STAR-structured interview answer (not just
a vague description), during the speaking activity.

**Homework.** Learners refine their professional email and interview
answer based on any partner feedback, ready for Module 3's assignment.

**Revision.** This lesson opens with the Lesson 3.1 job-choice recap.
Module 3's Quiz and Assignment draw on both lessons.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners draft a brief written
workplace proposal (3-4 sentences: context, recommendation, reasoning,
next step) in addition to their email.

## Module 3 Quiz (auto-graded, 10 questions)

*Seeded verbatim into `quiz_questions` — see
`sql/seed-curriculum-level-4.sql`. Answer key marked with ✓.*

1. "If I ___ known about the deadline earlier, I would have
   submitted on time." (third conditional) — (a) have (b) had ✓ (c)
   has (d) was
2. "If she hadn't taken that internship, she ___ discovered her
   interest in data analysis." — (a) wouldn't have ✓ (b) wouldn't (c)
   didn't (d) hadn't
3. Which sentence is second conditional (still possible now), not
   third (an unchangeable past)? — (a) If I had been braver, I would
   have applied. (b) If I were braver, I would apply. ✓ (c) If I had
   known, I would have said something. (d) If she had asked, I would
   have helped.
4. "___ your email, please find the attached proposal." — (a) With
   regard to (b) Further to ✓ (c) Given that (d) Provided that
5. Which is an appropriate professional email closing before a formal
   named sign-off in British English? — (a) Kind regards ✓ (b) See ya
   (c) Talk soon (d) Bye for now
6. In American English, a common professional email closing is: — (a)
   Yours faithfully (b) Best regards ✓ (c) Ta (d) Cheers
7. What does the "R" in the STAR interview-answer structure stand
   for? — (a) Reason (b) Result ✓ (c) Response (d) Review
8. Which phrase means "make brief contact to check in"? — (a) reach
   out (b) touch base ✓ (c) circle back (d) follow up
9. "As a result, we delivered the project on time. ___, we hadn't
   asked for help early on, the project would have taken much
   longer." (completing a reflective third-conditional statement) —
   (a) If (b) Unless (c) Because (d) So
10. Which phrase means "return to a topic later"? — (a) reach out (b)
    touch base (c) circle back ✓ (d) get back to

## Module 3 Assignment — "A Professional Email & Mock Interview"

**Instructions given to the learner:** Complete two parts. **Part A
(formal correspondence, this level's third writing genre):** Write a
professional email, 150-200 words, either applying for a role,
following up after an interview, or proposing a workplace idea. Use
correct formal structure (subject line, greeting, clear purpose, body,
closing, sign-off) and include at least one phrasal verb from this
module. **Part B (interview speaking task):** Record yourself (or
perform live) answering one behavioural interview question ("Tell me
about a time when...") using the STAR structure, including at least
one third-conditional reflective sentence about what might have
happened differently.

**Grading rubric (for the instructor):**
- **Grammatical accuracy** — correct third-conditional formation,
  correct formal email structures.
- **Vocabulary range** — at least 3 distinct professional-
  correspondence or interview words used correctly, plus one phrasal
  verb/collocation from this module.
- **Task completion** — full email structure present in Part A; a
  complete STAR structure with a genuine third-conditional reflection
  in Part B.
- **Communicative quality** — does the email sound genuinely
  professional and purposeful, and does the interview answer reveal
  real reflection, not a rehearsed, generic response?
- **Discourse coherence & register** — is the email's register
  consistently formal throughout (no casual language), and does the
  interview answer sound composed and appropriately professional in
  delivery?

A grade at or above the platform's pass threshold marks Module 3
completed for the learner.
