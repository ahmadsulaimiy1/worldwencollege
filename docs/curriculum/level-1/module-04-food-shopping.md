# WEC-LC — Level I, Module 4: Food & Shopping

*Part of `docs/curriculum-level-1-foundation.md`'s Level I build-out.*

**Module learning objectives.** By the end of Module 4, the learner
can: name common foods and shops; distinguish countable and
uncountable nouns; use `some/any` correctly in statements and
questions; ask for and state prices; make a simple purchase request
(`Can I have...?`/`How much is...?`).

**Content map (5 learning items):** module overview & key phrases;
Lesson 4.1 ("Countable & Uncountable — What's in the Kitchen?");
Lesson 4.2 ("At the Shop — Prices & Requests"); Module 4 Quiz (8
questions); Module 4 Assignment ("At the Market Roleplay").

---

## Reference reading: Module overview & key phrases

**Key phrases introduced this module:** Is there any...?/Are there
any...? — I'd like some... — How much is/are...? — Can I have...,
please? — That's £.../$... — Here you are. / Thank you.

**Key vocabulary previewed:** foods (bread, rice, milk, eggs, apples,
bananas, chicken, water, coffee, tea), shops (bakery, greengrocer,
supermarket, market), numbers 20-100, money/price vocabulary.

## Lesson 4.1 — "Countable & Uncountable — What's in the Kitchen?"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1)
distinguish countable nouns (an egg, three apples) from uncountable
nouns (rice, milk, water); (2) use `some` in positive statements and
`any` in questions/negatives for both noun types; (3) name 10+ common
foods; (4) use `a/an` correctly with countable food nouns.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Module 2 (`there is/are`, `a/an`).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor shows real or pictured food items,
sorting them live into two labelled columns on the board ("things you
can count" / "things you can't count") based on learner guesses.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model: "There is some rice in the cupboard.
There are some eggs in the fridge. Is there any milk? Yes, there is
some." Highlight: countable nouns take `a/an` (singular) or a number/
`some` (plural); uncountable nouns never take `a/an` or a number,
only `some/any`.

**Guided practice (10 min).** Pair work sorting a set of 16 food-word
cards into "countable" and "uncountable" columns, then building 4
`there is/are some...` sentences each from the sorted cards.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners look at a picture of a
full fridge/cupboard and write 5 sentences about what's inside using
`some`, then ask a partner 3 `Is there any...?/Are there any...?`
questions about the same picture.

**Speaking activity.** The partner Q&A above, plus a class survey:
"Is there any coffee in your kitchen at home?"

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to a short shopping-list
dictation (6 items) and write down what's mentioned, noting countable
vs. uncountable correctly (a/three vs. some).

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read a simple recipe ingredient list and
sort the ingredients into countable/uncountable.

**Writing task (5 min).** Write a short shopping list of 6 items you
need this week, using correct countable/uncountable forms.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill the reduced, unstressed
pronunciation of `some` /səm/ in fluent speech versus its full form
when used to mean "a certain amount of, not all" (contrastive
listening + repetition).

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** Food flashcard/realia naming race,
sorted live into countable/uncountable.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks correct `some/any` choice
and countable/uncountable agreement during independent practice.

**Homework.** Learners list everything in their real fridge/cupboard
at home (6+ items) in English, ready for Lesson 4.2's shopping
roleplay.

**Revision.** Lesson 4.2 opens with a rapid countable/uncountable
sorting recap using new food words.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners write 2 sentences using `a
lot of` and `not much/not many` with food nouns (light preview).

## Lesson 4.2 — "At the Shop — Prices & Requests"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) ask
`How much is/are...?` correctly for singular/plural items; (2) state
and understand prices in English; (3) make a polite purchase request
(`Can I have..., please?`); (4) respond appropriately when paying and
receiving change.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Lesson 4.1 (food vocabulary,
countable/uncountable).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor holds up priced items (real or
pictured) and models "How much is this? It's $2." for 3-4 items before
formal presentation.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model shop dialogue:
> Shopkeeper: Can I help you?
> Customer: Yes, please. Can I have some apples?
> Shopkeeper: Sure. That's $3.
> Customer: Here you are. Thank you!
Highlight: `How much is` (singular/uncountable) vs. `How much are`
(plural). Drill numbers 20-100 and simple prices ($3.50, £12).

**Guided practice (10 min).** Pair work with priced picture cards:
Learner A asks "How much is/are the...?", Learner B answers with the
price shown, then swap.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners perform the full shop
dialogue in pairs using their Lesson 4.1 homework shopping list,
taking turns as shopkeeper and customer, substituting real items and
invented prices.

**Speaking activity.** The shop-dialogue roleplay above — this
lesson's core speaking task.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to 3 short shop exchanges and
write down the item and price mentioned in each.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read a simple price list/menu and answer
3 questions ("How much is the coffee?").

**Writing task (5 min).** Write a short 4-line shop dialogue (buyer and
seller) using at least 2 items and prices from this module.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill number pronunciation
(commonly confused pairs: thirteen/thirty, fourteen/forty) and rising
intonation on "How much is/are...?"

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** Price-matching game (item cards to price
cards) in pairs.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor observes the shop-dialogue
roleplay for correct `is/are` choice and appropriate politeness
phrases (`please`, `thank you`).

**Homework.** Learners practise the shop dialogue once more at home
(with a family member or alone) before Module 4's assignment roleplay.

**Revision.** This lesson opens with the countable/uncountable recap
noted above. Module 4's Quiz and Assignment draw on both lessons.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners extend the dialogue with a
complaint/request for a different item ("Do you have any bigger
ones?").

## Module 4 Quiz (auto-graded, 8 questions)

*Seeded verbatim into `quiz_questions` — see
`sql/seed-curriculum-level-1.sql`. Answer key marked with ✓.*

1. "There is ___ milk in the fridge." — (a) a (b) some ✓ (c) many (d)
   an
2. "Is there ___ bread?" — (a) some (b) any ✓ (c) a (d) much
3. Which is uncountable? — (a) apple (b) egg (c) rice ✓ (d) banana
4. "How much ___ the apples?" — (a) is (b) are ✓ (c) am (d) be
5. "Can I ___ some coffee, please?" — (a) have ✓ (b) has (c) having
   (d) had
6. "That's $5. Here you ___." — (a) am (b) is (c) are ✓ (d) be
7. Where do you buy bread? — (a) greengrocer (b) bakery ✓ (c) bank (d)
   station
8. "There are ___ eggs in the box." — (a) some ✓ (b) any (c) a (d) an

## Module 4 Assignment — "At the Market Roleplay"

**Instructions given to the learner:** Record yourself (or perform
live) a short shop dialogue, 45-60 seconds, playing both the customer
and the shopkeeper (or with a partner if available). Include: a
greeting, a purchase request using `Can I have...?`, at least 2 food
items (at least one countable, one uncountable), a `How much is/are...?`
question with a stated price, and a polite closing exchange.

**Grading rubric (for the instructor):**
- **Grammatical accuracy** — correct `some/any`, correct `is/are` with
  prices.
- **Vocabulary range** — at least 2 distinct food items, at least one
  price stated correctly.
- **Task completion** — all required dialogue elements present.
- **Delivery & politeness** — appropriate `please`/`thank you` usage,
  audible and reasonably fluent for A1.

A grade at or above the platform's pass threshold marks Module 4
completed for the learner.
