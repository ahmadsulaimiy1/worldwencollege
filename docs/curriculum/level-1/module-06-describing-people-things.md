# WEC-LC — Level I, Module 6: Describing People & Things

*Part of `docs/curriculum-level-1-foundation.md`'s Level I build-out.*

**Module learning objectives.** By the end of Module 6, the learner
can: describe people's appearance and things using adjectives; use
possessive adjectives (my/your/his/her/our/their) and `'s` correctly;
put adjectives in the correct order before a noun (basic: size + colour);
describe and compare two people or objects simply.

**Content map (5 learning items):** module overview & key phrases;
Lesson 6.1 ("What Does She Look Like? — Describing People"); Lesson
6.2 ("Whose Is It? — Possessives & Describing Things"); Module 6 Quiz
(8 questions); Module 6 Assignment ("Describe a Family Member").

---

## Reference reading: Module overview & key phrases

**Key phrases introduced this module:** What does he/she look like? —
He/She has.../He/She is... — It's mine/yours/his/hers/ours/theirs. —
Whose is this? — It's a big red bag.

**Key vocabulary previewed:** appearance adjectives (tall, short,
young, old, thin, curly/straight hair, brown/blue eyes), general
adjectives (big, small, new, old, expensive, cheap), colours.

## Lesson 6.1 — "What Does She Look Like? — Describing People"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1)
describe a person's appearance using `He/She is.../He/She has...`; (2)
use 8+ appearance adjectives correctly; (3) ask `What does he/she look
like?`; (4) use `has/have got` (light preview) for physical features.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Module 3 (family vocabulary, `have/has`
preview), Module 1 (`to be`).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor describes their own appearance aloud
("I have short hair. I am tall.") while pointing to themselves,
modelling before formal presentation.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model: "What does your brother look like?
He's tall. He has short black hair." Highlight: `is/are` + adjective
for general qualities (tall, young) vs. `has/have` + noun for specific
features (hair, eyes). Introduce 8-10 appearance adjectives with
picture support.

**Guided practice (10 min).** Pair work: Learner A describes a
mystery picture person using 3 sentences, Learner B guesses which of 4
pictured people is being described.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners describe a real classmate
(with permission, kept respectful and positive) using at least 3
target sentences, for a partner to guess who it is.

**Speaking activity.** The "guess who" describing activity above.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to 3 short person descriptions
and match each to the correct picture from a set of 5.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read 3 short "missing person"-style
description cards (playful, not distressing framing — e.g. "lost
teddy bear" descriptions) and match to pictures.

**Writing task (5 min).** Write 4 sentences describing a real or
famous person's appearance.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill weak-form `has` /həz/ in
connected speech ("She has short hair") versus its full form in short
answers ("Yes, she has").

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** Appearance-adjective picture-matching
game.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks correct `is/has` choice
during the guess-who activity.

**Homework.** Learners write a description of one family member's
appearance (3-4 sentences), ready for Module 6's assignment.

**Revision.** Lesson 6.2 opens with 2-3 learners reading their
homework description aloud for the class to guess the relationship.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners add a comparison sentence
("My brother is taller than me") as a light preview of Level II's
comparatives.

## Lesson 6.2 — "Whose Is It? — Possessives & Describing Things"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) use
possessive adjectives (my/your/his/her/our/their) correctly; (2) use
possessive pronouns (mine/yours/his/hers/ours/theirs) in short
answers; (3) ask `Whose is this/are these?`; (4) order two adjectives
correctly before a noun (size + colour: "a big red bag").

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Lesson 6.1 (adjectives), Module 3
(possessive `'s`).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor picks up a few learners' real
belongings (with permission) and asks "Whose is this?", eliciting
"It's mine!"/"It's his."

**Presentation (10 min).** Model: "This is my bag. Is this your pen?
No, it's not mine, it's his." Contrast possessive adjective + noun
(my bag) with possessive pronoun alone (it's mine). Introduce basic
two-adjective ordering with examples ("a big red bag", "a small blue
car").

**Guided practice (10 min).** Pair work: mix a small pile of real
classroom belongings, take turns asking "Whose is this?" and
answering with the correct possessive pronoun.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners describe 4 of their own
real belongings using size + colour + noun ("my small black bag"),
then swap descriptions with a partner who tries to identify which
item is being described from a set on the table.

**Speaking activity.** The belongings-identification exchange above.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to 4 short "whose is this"
exchanges and note who each item belongs to.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read 4 short "lost and found" notices
and match each description to the correct pictured item.

**Writing task (5 min).** Write a short "lost and found" notice
describing one of your own real or imagined lost items using 2
adjectives in correct order.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill the pronunciation
distinction between `whose` /huːz/ and `who's` /huːz/ (same sound,
different spelling/meaning) — a genuine, useful A1 noticing point, not
formally tested at this level, only raised for awareness.

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** "Whose is it?" belongings game in small
groups.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks correct possessive
adjective/pronoun choice and adjective order during independent
practice.

**Homework.** Learners write 3 sentences describing 3 different
belongings (their own or family members') using possessives and
two-adjective ordering.

**Revision.** This lesson opens with the Lesson 6.1 family-description
sharing recap. Module 6's Quiz and Assignment draw on both lessons.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners write a short "lost and
found" notice combining a person description (Lesson 6.1) with an
object description (this lesson) — e.g. describing a lost child's
teddy bear and the child.

## Module 6 Quiz (auto-graded, 8 questions)

*Seeded verbatim into `quiz_questions` — see
`sql/seed-curriculum-level-1.sql`. Answer key marked with ✓.*

1. "She ___ long brown hair." — (a) is (b) has ✓ (c) have (d) are
2. "He ___ tall and young." — (a) has (b) is ✓ (c) have (d) are
3. "___ is this bag? — It's mine." — (a) Who (b) Whose ✓ (c) Which
   (d) What
4. "This isn't my pen. It's ___." — (a) her (b) hers ✓ (c) she (d)
   she's
5. Which sentence has the correct adjective order? — (a) a red big
   bag (b) a big red bag ✓ (c) a bag big red (d) a big bag red
6. "That's not our car. It's ___." — (a) their (b) theirs ✓ (c) they
   (d) them
7. "Is this ___ book?" (asking someone directly) — (a) you (b) your
   ✓ (c) yours (d) you're
8. "My sister has short ___ hair." — (a) curly ✓ (b) tall (c)
   expensive (d) cheap

## Module 6 Assignment — "Describe a Family Member"

**Instructions given to the learner:** Write (or record) 5-6 sentences
describing one family member's appearance and one of their belongings.
Use at least 2 `has/is` appearance sentences, correct possessive
adjectives, and one two-adjective description (size + colour) of an
object belonging to them.

**Grading rubric (for the instructor):**
- **Grammatical accuracy** — correct `is/has` choice, correct
  possessives, correct adjective order.
- **Vocabulary range** — at least 3 distinct adjectives used
  correctly.
- **Task completion** — describes both a person and an object as
  instructed.
- **Clarity** — description is specific and coherent, not a list of
  disconnected words.

A grade at or above the platform's pass threshold marks Module 6
completed for the learner.
