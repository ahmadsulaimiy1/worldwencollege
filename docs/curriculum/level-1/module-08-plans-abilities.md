# WEC — Level I, Module 8: Plans & Abilities

*Part of `docs/curriculum-level-1-foundation.md`'s Level I build-out.*

**Module learning objectives.** By the end of Module 8, the learner
can: talk about ability using `can/can't`; talk about near-future
plans using `going to`; ask and answer `Can you...?` and `What are
you going to do...?`; distinguish ability from future intention.

**Content map (5 learning items):** module overview & key phrases;
Lesson 8.1 ("Can You...? — Talking About Ability"); Lesson 8.2 ("I'm
Going To... — Talking About Plans"); Module 8 Quiz (8 questions);
Module 8 Assignment ("My Weekend Plans").

---

## Reference reading: Module overview & key phrases

**Key phrases introduced this module:** I can/can't... — Can you...?
Yes, I can. / No, I can't. — I'm going to... — What are you going to
do this weekend? — He's/She's going to...

**Key vocabulary previewed:** ability verbs (swim, cook, drive, play
an instrument, speak a language, ride a bike), weekend/holiday-plan
vocabulary (visit, travel, meet, relax, go shopping).

## Lesson 8.1 — "Can You...? — Talking About Ability"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) use
`can/can't` correctly to talk about ability; (2) ask `Can you...?` and
give short answers; (3) use 8+ ability-related verbs; (4) form the
negative `can't` correctly (not `can not` in speech).

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Module 1 (`to be`, question formation).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor asks the class a show-of-hands
question ("Who can swim?") before formal presentation, modelling the
question naturally.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model: "I can cook. I can't drive. Can you
swim? Yes, I can. / No, I can't." Highlight: `can/can't` + base verb,
same form for all subjects (no `-s` — a genuinely useful A1 contrast
with the present-simple `-s` rule from Module 3).

**Guided practice (10 min).** Pair work: Learner A asks "Can you...?"
using 5 verbs from a prompt list, Learner B answers truthfully with
short answers, then swap.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Class mingling survey: learners ask
4 different classmates "Can you...?" questions and record who can/
can't do each of 4 abilities on a simple grid.

**Speaking activity.** The mingling survey above, followed by 2-3
learners reporting one surprising finding to the class ("Maria can
play the guitar!").

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to 4 short self-descriptions of
ability and match each speaker to their abilities on a worksheet.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read 3 short "About Me" profile texts
and answer 3 comprehension questions about what each person can/can't
do.

**Writing task (5 min).** Write 4 sentences about your own real
abilities, at least one negative (`can't`).

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill the reduced, unstressed
`can` /kən/ in positive statements versus the fully stressed, clearly
different `can't` /kɑːnt/ — the single most important A1 listening
discrimination point in this lesson, since the two are easily confused
at natural speed.

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** Ability-verb charades (act out an
ability for classmates to name).

**Formative assessment.** Instructor listens during the mingling
survey for clear `can`/`can't` pronunciation distinction and correct
base-verb form.

**Homework.** Learners ask one family member 3 `Can you...?` questions
(in English if possible) and note the answers for next lesson.

**Revision.** Lesson 8.2 opens with 2-3 learners sharing their
homework family-ability findings.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners add `well`/`a little` to
qualify an ability ("I can cook well" / "I can swim a little").

## Lesson 8.2 — "I'm Going To... — Talking About Plans"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) use
`going to` correctly to talk about near-future plans and intentions;
(2) ask `What are you going to do...?` and answer appropriately; (3)
distinguish `going to` (plan/intention) from `can` (ability); (4) talk
about weekend/holiday plans using target vocabulary.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Lesson 8.1 (`can/can't`), Module 3 (time
expressions).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor states one real plan for after class
("I'm going to have lunch") and one real ability ("I can speak two
languages"), asking learners to identify which sentence is about the
future and which is about ability — direct contrastive noticing before
formal presentation.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model: "What are you going to do this
weekend? I'm going to visit my parents. She's going to go shopping."
Highlight the form: `am/is/are + going to + base verb`. Explicitly
contrast with Lesson 8.1: `can` = ability (a skill you have); `going
to` = a plan (something you intend to do).

**Guided practice (10 min).** Pair work: Learner A asks "What are you
going to do this weekend?", Learner B answers using at least 2 plans
from the target vocabulary list, then swap.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners write their real plans for
the coming weekend (4-5 sentences using `going to`), then interview 2
classmates about their plans and note one plan each classmate shares.

**Speaking activity.** The plans interview above — this lesson's core
speaking task, and Module 8's assignment topic.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to 3 people describing their
weekend plans and match each speaker to their correct plan on a
worksheet.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read a short "This Weekend" announcement
text (e.g. a simple community noticeboard style text) and answer 3
comprehension questions about planned activities.

**Writing task (5 min).** Write a short message to a friend (4-5
sentences) telling them your weekend plans, using `going to`.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill the natural connected-speech
contraction `gonna` for recognition (listening only — not required for
production at this level, but important so learners aren't confused
hearing it in natural speech) versus the full, correct written/spoken
`going to` used in this lesson's target production.

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** Weekend-plan picture cards matched to
`going to` sentence starters.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks correct `going to` form
and correct distinction from `can` during independent practice.

**Homework.** Learners finalise and rehearse their weekend-plans
description for Module 8's assignment.

**Revision.** This lesson opens with the Lesson 8.1 family-ability
sharing recap. Module 8's Quiz and Assignment draw on both lessons.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners add one ability-related
justification to a plan ("I'm going to go swimming because I can
swim well").

## Module 8 Quiz (auto-graded, 8 questions)

*Seeded verbatim into `quiz_questions` — see
`sql/seed-curriculum-level-1.sql`. Answer key marked with ✓.*

1. "I ___ speak French, but I can't speak German." — (a) can ✓ (b)
   cans (c) canning (d) could
2. "___ you swim?" — (a) Do (b) Can ✓ (c) Are (d) Does
3. "No, I ___." — (a) don't (b) not (c) can't ✓ (d) isn't
4. "She ___ going to visit her grandmother tomorrow." — (a) am (b) is
   ✓ (c) are (d) do
5. "What are you ___ to do this weekend?" — (a) go (b) going ✓ (c)
   goes (d) went
6. Which sentence is about ability, not a plan? — (a) I'm going to
   cook dinner. (b) I can cook well. ✓ (c) She's going to visit Rome.
   (d) They're going to play football.
7. "We ___ going to travel next month." — (a) am (b) is (c) are ✓ (d)
   be
8. "He can play the guitar ___." — (a) good (b) well ✓ (c) fine (d)
   nice

## Module 8 Assignment — "My Weekend Plans"

**Instructions given to the learner:** Record yourself (or write) 5-6
sentences about your real plans for the coming weekend, using `going
to`. Include at least one ability-related sentence using `can/can't`
that connects logically to one of your plans (e.g. "I'm going to go
swimming. I can swim well.").

**Grading rubric (for the instructor):**
- **Grammatical accuracy** — correct `going to` form (am/is/are +
  going to + base verb), correct `can/can't` form.
- **Vocabulary range** — at least 3 distinct planned activities named.
- **Task completion** — at least one `can/can't` sentence logically
  connected to a plan, as instructed.
- **Coherence** — plans are specific and clearly future-oriented, not
  confused with present or past tense.

A grade at or above the platform's pass threshold marks Module 8
completed for the learner.
