# AIPC — Level I, Module 9: Health & Feelings

*Part of `docs/curriculum-level-1-foundation.md`'s Level I build-out.*

**Module learning objectives.** By the end of Module 9, the learner
can: name common body parts and health problems; describe how they
feel; use `have/has` fully (this module completes what Modules 3 and
6 previewed); give and receive simple health advice using `should`.

**Content map (5 learning items):** module overview & key phrases;
Lesson 9.1 ("How Are You Feeling? — Body & Health"); Lesson 9.2 ("You
Should... — Giving Simple Advice"); Module 9 Quiz (8 questions);
Module 9 Assignment ("At the Doctor's Roleplay").

---

## Reference reading: Module overview & key phrases

**Key phrases introduced this module:** How are you feeling? — I have
a headache/a cold/a stomachache. — I feel sick/tired/better. — You
should.../You shouldn't... — Get well soon!

**Key vocabulary previewed:** body parts (head, stomach, back, throat,
arm, leg, ear, tooth), health problems (headache, cold, fever, cough,
stomachache), feelings (happy, sad, tired, sick, better, worried).

## Lesson 9.1 — "How Are You Feeling? — Body & Health"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) name
8+ body parts; (2) describe common health problems using `have/has` (a
health-problem noun); (3) describe feelings using `feel + adjective`;
(4) ask `What's the matter?/How are you feeling?` appropriately.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Module 3 and Module 6 (`have/has`
previewed for family and appearance — this lesson is where it's used
fully and explicitly for the first time).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor mimes 3-4 common ailments (holding
head, coughing) for learners to guess before formal presentation.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model: "What's the matter? I have a
headache. I feel terrible." Consolidate `have/has` fully: `I have a
headache. She has a cold.` Introduce `feel + adjective` as a separate,
equally important pattern (`I feel sick` — no `have` here). Present
8-10 body-part and health-problem words with picture support.

**Guided practice (10 min).** Pair work: Learner A mimes an ailment,
Learner B guesses and states it using `have/has`, then swap, 5 rounds.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners are given 4 short "sick
day" scenario cards and role-play a short phone call explaining why
they can't come to school/work today, using `have/has` and `feel`.

**Speaking activity.** The sick-day phone-call roleplay above.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to 4 short exchanges about how
someone is feeling and match each speaker to their correct ailment.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read 3 short "Dear Doctor" letters (very
simple, A1-level) describing symptoms and match each to the correct
advice card from Lesson 9.2's target language (previewed here,
formally practised next lesson).

**Writing task (5 min).** Write 3-4 sentences describing how you feel
today (real or invented) using `have/has` and `feel`.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill body-part word stress
(STOMach, HEADache) and the `/eɪ/` vs `/e/` vowel contrast in commonly
confused pairs relevant to this vocabulary set.

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** Body-part labelling diagram race in
pairs.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks correct `have/has` vs.
`feel + adjective` choice during the roleplay.

**Homework.** Learners write 2 sentences about a time they felt sick
(using simple past `had` as a light preview, connecting back to
Module 7) and 2 sentences about how they feel today.

**Revision.** Lesson 9.2 opens with 2-3 learners sharing their
homework sentences as a warm-up.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners add a cause to their
health-problem sentence ("I have a headache because I didn't sleep
well" — a light preview of `because`, formally taught in Level III).

## Lesson 9.2 — "You Should... — Giving Simple Advice"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) give
simple advice using `should/shouldn't + base verb`; (2) respond
appropriately to advice; (3) connect a health problem to appropriate
advice logically; (4) use polite well-wishing expressions
(`Get well soon!`).

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Lesson 9.1 (health vocabulary, `have/
has`/`feel`).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor states a problem ("I'm very tired
today") and elicits informal advice from learners in their own words
before formal presentation, to surface what they already intuitively
know.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model: "I have a headache. — You should
rest. You shouldn't look at your phone." Highlight: `should/shouldn't
+ base verb`, same form for all subjects (another useful A1 contrast
with present-simple `-s`, echoing Module 8's `can/can't` pattern).

**Guided practice (10 min).** Pair work: Learner A states a health
problem from a prompt card, Learner B gives one piece of matching
advice using `should`, then swap, 5 rounds.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners work in small groups
matching 6 health-problem cards to 6 advice cards (more than one
logical match is acceptable), then justify one match aloud to the
class.

**Speaking activity.** The problem-and-advice exchange, plus the
group justification above.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to 3 short problem-and-advice
exchanges and note whether the advice given matches the problem
logically (a simple yes/no judgement task).

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read the 3 "Dear Doctor" letters from
Lesson 9.1 again, now matching each to a written advice response using
`should/shouldn't`.

**Writing task (5 min).** Write a short advice reply (3-4 sentences)
to one "Dear Doctor" letter, using at least 2 `should/shouldn't`
sentences.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill the correct pronunciation of
`should` /ʃʊd/ (commonly mispronounced by A1 learners) and the silent
`l` awareness point (not formally tested, noted for recognition).

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** Problem-advice matching game, timed, in
small groups.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks that advice given
logically matches the stated problem and uses correct `should/
shouldn't` form.

**Homework.** Learners prepare a short "sick day" roleplay script
(problem + advice + well-wishing) for Module 9's assignment.

**Revision.** This lesson opens with the Lesson 9.1 homework-sentence
recap. Module 9's Quiz and Assignment draw on both lessons.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners add a second piece of advice
using `shouldn't` to their roleplay script.

## Module 9 Quiz (auto-graded, 8 questions)

*Seeded verbatim into `quiz_questions` — see
`sql/seed-curriculum-level-1.sql`. Answer key marked with ✓.*

1. "I ___ a terrible headache." — (a) am (b) have ✓ (c) has (d) feel
2. "I ___ very tired today." — (a) have (b) has (c) feel ✓ (d) feels
3. "She ___ a cold." — (a) have (b) has ✓ (c) is (d) feel
4. "You ___ rest if you feel sick." — (a) should ✓ (b) shoulds (c)
   shouldn't (d) must not
5. "You ___ stay up too late — you need sleep." — (a) should (b)
   shouldn't ✓ (c) can (d) have
6. Which body part do you use to listen? — (a) throat (b) arm (c) ear
   ✓ (d) tooth
7. "What's the ___?" (asking what's wrong) — (a) matter ✓ (b) feeling
   (c) health (d) problem
8. A polite thing to say to someone who is sick: — (a) Get well soon!
   ✓ (b) Well done! (c) Good luck! (d) See you later!

## Module 9 Assignment — "At the Doctor's Roleplay"

**Instructions given to the learner:** Record yourself (or perform
with a partner) a short roleplay, 45-60 seconds, as a patient
describing a health problem to a doctor (or friend) and receiving
advice. Include: a greeting, at least one `have/has` or `feel`
sentence describing the problem, at least two pieces of advice using
`should/shouldn't`, and a polite closing.

**Grading rubric (for the instructor):**
- **Grammatical accuracy** — correct `have/has`/`feel` choice, correct
  `should/shouldn't` form.
- **Vocabulary range** — at least 2 health/body words and 2 advice
  verbs used correctly.
- **Task completion** — all required elements present.
- **Logical coherence** — the advice given genuinely matches the
  stated problem.

A grade at or above the platform's pass threshold marks Module 9
completed for the learner.
