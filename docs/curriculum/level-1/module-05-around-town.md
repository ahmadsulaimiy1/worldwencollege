# WEC-LC — Level I, Module 5: Around Town

*Part of `docs/curriculum-level-1-foundation.md`'s Level I build-out.*

**Module learning objectives.** By the end of Module 5, the learner
can: ask for and give simple directions; use prepositions of place
correctly (in, on, at, next to, opposite, between, near); use
imperatives to give instructions; understand and follow a short set of
spoken directions to a nearby place.

**Content map (5 learning items):** module overview & key phrases;
Lesson 5.1 ("Where Is It? — Prepositions of Place"); Lesson 5.2
("Excuse Me, How Do I Get To...? — Giving Directions"); Module 5 Quiz
(8 questions); Module 5 Assignment ("Direct a Visitor").

## Reference reading: Module overview & key phrases

**Key phrases introduced this module:** Excuse me, where is the...? —
Go straight on. / Turn left/right. — It's next to.../opposite.../
between... — It's on the corner of... — Is it far from here? — It's
five minutes' walk.

**Key vocabulary previewed:** prepositions of place (in, on, at, next

straight, turn left/right, cross), places (from Module 2, extended

## Lesson 5.1 — "Where Is It? — Prepositions of Place"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) use 6+
prepositions of place correctly to locate objects and places; (2) ask
`Where is the...?` and answer using a preposition of place; (3)
distinguish `next to`/`opposite`/`between` on a simple map.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Module 2 (city-place vocabulary, `this/
that`).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor places a real object in different
positions relative to another object (a pen on/in/next to a book),
narrating each placement once before formal presentation.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model using a simple street-map diagram:
"The bank is next to the supermarket. The park is opposite the
school. The hotel is between the bank and the station." Highlight each
preposition with a simple diagram icon on the board.

**Guided practice (10 min).** Pair work: Learner A names a place on
the map, Learner B says its location using a preposition, then swap;
repeat for 6 places.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners are given a map with 3
places missing and a list of prepositional clues; they place the
missing buildings correctly based on the clues, then check with a
partner.

**Speaking activity.** Learners describe their real route from home to
school/work using at least 3 prepositions of place.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to 4 short location
descriptions and identify which building on a map is being described.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read a short paragraph describing a
street and draw the buildings in the correct positions on a blank map.

**Writing task (5 min).** Write 4 sentences locating real places in
your own town using prepositions from this lesson.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill word stress on two-word
prepositional phrases (next TO, opPOsite, beTWEEN) and linking between
preposition and following noun.

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** Preposition mime/action game (place an
object correctly on instruction) plus a map-labelling race.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks correct preposition choice
during the independent map-completion task.

**Homework.** Learners draw a simple map of 4 real places near their
home with correct prepositional relationships noted.

**Revision.** Lesson 5.2 opens with learners sharing their homework
map in pairs as a warm-up.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners add `in front of` and
`behind` to their homework map description.

## Lesson 5.2 — "Excuse Me, How Do I Get To...? — Giving Directions"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) ask
for directions politely (`Excuse me, how do I get to...?`); (2) give
simple directions using imperatives (`Go straight on. Turn left/
right. Cross the road.`); (3) understand and follow a short sequence
of spoken directions; (4) estimate/state simple walking distance
(`It's five minutes' walk`).

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Lesson 5.1 (prepositions of place, town
vocabulary).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor gives 3 quick physical imperatives
("Stand up. Turn left. Sit down.") as a total-physical-response
warm-up before introducing direction-specific imperatives.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model dialogue with a street map:
> A: Excuse me, how do I get to the library?
> B: Go straight on, then turn left. It's on the corner, next to the
> pharmacy. It's about five minutes' walk.
> A: Thank you very much!
Highlight the imperative form (no subject pronoun) and the politeness
frame around it.

**Guided practice (10 min).** Pair work with a street map: Learner A
asks for directions to a place, Learner B gives a 2-3 step direction
using imperatives; swap after each round, 4 rounds total.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners follow a partner's spoken
directions (not written) by tracing a route with their finger on a
blank map, then check if they arrived at the correct destination.

**Speaking activity.** The direction-giving/following pair exchange
above — the lesson's core task.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to a short set of directions (4
steps) and draw the route on a map, marking the final destination.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read written directions to a place and
identify the destination on a map from 4 options.

**Writing task (5 min).** Write directions from the school/class
location to one real nearby place, using at least 3 imperatives.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill imperative sentence stress
(the main verb carries the stress: GO straight, TURN left) and
politeness-softening intonation on "Excuse me."

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** Direction-verb charades (act out go
straight/turn left/turn right/cross) in small groups.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks whether learners can both
give and correctly follow a 2-3 step direction during guided/
independent practice.

**Homework.** Learners write directions from their home to one place
they visit often, ready to read aloud next lesson.

**Revision.** This lesson opens with the Lesson 5.1 homework-map
sharing recap. Module 5's Quiz and Assignment draw on both lessons.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners add a second destination to
their direction-giving dialogue, requiring a longer, multi-step
direction.

   (d) minuting
8. "The pharmacy is ___ the corner." — (a) on ✓ (b) at (c) in (d)
   next

## Module 5 Assignment — "Direct a Visitor"

**Instructions given to the learner:** Imagine a visitor to your town
asks you for directions to a real place near where you live. Record
yourself (or write) giving clear directions, 45-60 seconds or 5-6
sentences. Include: a polite opening, at least 2 imperative direction
instructions, at least 1 preposition of place describing the
destination's location, and an estimated walking time.

**Grading rubric (for the instructor):**
- **Grammatical accuracy** — correct imperative forms, correct
  preposition of place.
- **Vocabulary range** — at least 2 distinct direction verbs used.
- **Task completion** — all required elements present (opening,
  directions, location description, time estimate).
- **Clarity** — directions are logical and could realistically be
  followed by a visitor.

A grade at or above the platform's pass threshold marks Module 5
completed for the learner.
