# WEC-LC — Level I, Module 10: Review & Consolidation

*Part of `docs/curriculum-level-1-foundation.md`'s Level I build-out.
Per `docs/curriculum-framework.md`'s assessment strategy, this module
is wholly revision and the level's end-of-level examination — it does
not introduce new grammar or vocabulary.*

**Module learning objectives.** By the end of Module 10, the learner
can: demonstrate cumulative command of every grammar point and
vocabulary set from Modules 1-9; complete WEC-LC's Foundation-level
(A1) end-of-level examination across grammar/vocabulary, listening,
reading, writing, and speaking; identify their own remaining weak
areas ahead of Level II.

**Content map (4 learning items):** Level I revision guide (cumulative
summary); Revision Lesson (structured cross-module consolidation
activities); Foundation-Level Mock Exam — Grammar & Vocabulary (20
auto-graded questions, one drawn from each module's core target
language); Foundation-Level Mock Exam — Speaking & Writing
(comprehensive, rubric-graded).

---

## Level I revision guide (cumulative summary)

**Module 1 — Meeting People:** `to be` (am/is/are), subject pronouns,
basic question formation; greetings, personal information,
nationalities.

**Module 2 — Everyday Objects & Places:** `there is/are`,
demonstratives (this/that/these/those); classroom, home, and city
vocabulary.

**Module 3 — Family & Routines:** present simple (incl. third-person
`-s`), telling the time; family members, daily-routine verbs, time
expressions.

**Module 4 — Food & Shopping:** countable/uncountable nouns, `some/
any`; food, shops, numbers, money/prices.

**Module 5 — Around Town:** prepositions of place, imperatives; town
places, direction language.

**Module 6 — Describing People & Things:** possessive adjectives/
pronouns, `'s`, adjective order; appearance, colours, general
adjectives.

**Module 7 — Past Experiences I:** simple past (regular `-ed` and
common irregular forms), past questions/negatives; past-time
expressions.

**Module 8 — Plans & Abilities:** `can/can't` (ability), `going to`
(near-future plans); ability verbs, weekend/holiday-plan vocabulary.

**Module 9 — Health & Feelings:** `have/has` and `feel + adjective`
(fully consolidated), `should/shouldn't`; body parts, health problems,
feelings.

**Structural thread across the level:** every module built toward one
consistent skill — introducing yourself and your world (Modules 1-4),
navigating and describing it (Modules 5-6), then talking about time
beyond the present moment (Modules 7-9: past, future, and advice).
Module 10 tests all of it together, not in isolation.

## Revision Lesson — Structured Consolidation Activities

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1)
correctly select the right grammar structure from Modules 1-9 given a
real-use context (not just when a single structure is drilled in
isolation); (2) recall and use at least 60 headwords from across the
level's vocabulary sets; (3) self-identify at least one personal area
needing further revision before the mock exam.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** All of Modules 1-9.

**Warm-up (5 min).** "Grammar auction" — learners bid points on
whether 6 example sentences (each drawn from a different module) are
grammatically correct or incorrect, surfacing residual errors playfully
before the graded exam.

**Presentation/consolidation (15 min).** Instructor leads a
structure-selection drill: a series of real-life prompts, each
requiring a different module's grammar to answer correctly ("Describe
your best friend" → Module 6; "What did you do last weekend?" →
Module 7; "What are your plans for tonight?" → Module 8) — the point
is choosing the right tool for the task, the core skill Module 10
assesses.

**Guided practice (15 min).** Learners rotate through 4 stations (in
pairs or small groups), each station reviewing 2-3 modules' target
language through a quick game (matching, sorting, or a short
speaking prompt) — Family/Routines + Food/Shopping; Around Town +
Describing People/Things; Past Experiences + Plans/Abilities; Health/
Feelings + a cumulative review.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners complete a self-assessment
checklist (one line per module: "I can... ✓/needs practice") and
circle their two weakest areas.

**Speaking activity.** The structure-selection drill and station
rotation above are both fundamentally speaking-driven.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to a single extended monologue
(someone introducing themselves, describing their family, routine,
and weekend plans, referencing a past holiday) — a genuinely
cumulative listening task mirroring the mock exam's listening
component.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read a similarly cumulative short text
and answer mixed comprehension questions spanning several grammar
points.

**Writing task (5 min).** Write one paragraph (5+ sentences) that uses
at least 4 different grammar points from across the level.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Rapid-fire review drill of the
level's key pronunciation points: `-s`/`-ed` endings, `can/can't`,
`this/these`, weak `there`.

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** A cumulative vocabulary relay game
covering all 9 modules' word sets.

**Formative assessment.** The self-assessment checklist above,
reviewed individually with the instructor if time allows, directly
informing what each learner should revise before the mock exam.

**Homework.** Learners revise their self-identified weak areas using
the module they struggled with most.

**Revision.** This entire lesson is revision by design.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners help peers at weaker
stations during the rotation activity — informal peer teaching.

## Foundation-Level Mock Exam — Speaking & Writing (comprehensive, rubric-graded)

**Instructions given to the learner:** This is your Level I final
assessment. Complete both parts.

**Part A — Speaking (2-3 minutes, recorded or live with your
instructor).** Give an extended self-introduction covering: your name,
where you're from, and where you live (Module 1); your family (Module
3); your daily routine with at least one time expression (Module 3);
one thing you did last weekend, using at least one regular and one
irregular past verb (Module 7); one plan for the near future using
`going to` (Module 8); and one thing you can/can't do (Module 8).
Respond to at least one follow-up question from your instructor.

**Part B — Writing (10-12 sentences).** Write a personal letter to a
new pen pal covering: an introduction (Module 1); a description of
your home or room using `there is/are` and `this/that` (Module 2); a
description of one family member's appearance (Module 6); how you're
feeling today and one piece of health advice you'd give a friend
(Module 9); and directions from your home to one place you often
visit (Module 5).

**Grading rubric (for the instructor), weighted toward listening and
speaking per the Foundation-level assessment strategy:**
- **Grammatical range and accuracy** — correct, varied use of the
  level's grammar points across both parts, not just one or two
  structures repeated.
- **Vocabulary range** — words drawn from at least 6 of the 9 modules
  across both parts combined.
- **Task completion** — every required element present in both Part A
  and Part B.
- **Fluency and delivery (Part A)** — reasonably fluent for A1,
  audible, able to respond to an unscripted follow-up question.
- **Coherence (Part B)** — the letter reads as one connected personal
  message, not disconnected sentences.

**Progression requirement.** A grade at or above the platform's pass
threshold on this comprehensive assessment marks Level I as complete
for the learner and — per Executive Decision #1's progressive
full-programme unlock — is the human (staff) judgement that, for a
full-programme student, triggers Level II's enrolment to unlock
automatically via `functions/_lib/student/progression.js`'s
`completeLevel()`.
