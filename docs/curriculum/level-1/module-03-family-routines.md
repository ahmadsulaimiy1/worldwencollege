# WEC — Level I, Module 3: Family & Routines

*Part of `docs/curriculum-level-1-foundation.md`'s Level I build-out.*

**Module learning objectives.** By the end of Module 3, the learner
can: name immediate and extended family members; describe their daily
routine using present simple; tell the time and state when daily
activities happen; ask and answer `What time do you...?`/`What do you
do every day?`; use present-simple third-person `-s` correctly.

**Content map (5 learning items):** module overview & key phrases;
Lesson 3.1 ("My Family"); Lesson 3.2 ("My Day — Present Simple &
Time"); Module 3 Quiz (8 questions); Module 3 Assignment ("A Typical
Day in My Life").

---

## Reference reading: Module overview & key phrases

**Key phrases introduced this module:** This is my.../I have a... —
What time is it? / It's ... o'clock — What time do you...? / I ... at
... — Every day I... — On weekdays/weekends I...

**Key vocabulary previewed:** family members (mother, father, sister,
brother, grandmother, grandfather, wife, husband, son, daughter),
daily-routine verbs (wake up, get up, have breakfast, go to work/
school, come home, have dinner, go to bed), time expressions (o'clock,
half past, quarter past/to, in the morning/afternoon/evening/at
night).

## Lesson 3.1 — "My Family"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) name
8+ family members; (2) describe your family using `I have.../This is
my...`; (3) ask `Do you have any brothers/sisters?` and answer with
short forms; (4) use possessive `'s` correctly for family
relationships (e.g. "my father's sister").

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Module 2 (`there is/are`, possessive
groundwork from `this/that`).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor shows a simple family-tree picture of
their own (or a model) family, pointing and naming each person once.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model: "This is my mother. Her name is
Anna. I have two brothers and one sister." Introduce the family-tree
vocabulary set (8-10 words) with a labelled diagram. Highlight `have/
has`: "I have a sister" vs. "She has two children" (light preview —
full `have/has` practice comes in Module 9; here it's used only in
first person and taught as a fixed useful chunk).

**Guided practice (10 min).** Learners label a blank family-tree
worksheet with the vocabulary just presented, then in pairs quiz each
other pointing at the diagram: "Who is this?"

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners draw and label their own
real family tree (or an invented one, for privacy, explicitly offered
as an option), then describe it to a partner using `This is my.../I
have...`.

**Speaking activity.** The family-tree description exchange above,
plus a short "Do you have any brothers or sisters?" mingling round
with 4 classmates.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to someone describing their
family (4-5 sentences, read twice); complete a simple family-tree
worksheet by writing names in the correct boxes.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read a short family description
paragraph and answer 3 simple comprehension questions ("How many
sisters does she have?").

**Writing task (5 min).** Write 3-4 sentences describing your own (or
an invented) family using target vocabulary and `have/has`.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill the `/f/` vs. `/v/` sound
contrast (father/brother; wife/husband) and correct stress on
two-syllable family words (grandmother, daughter).

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** Family-tree labelling race in pairs or
small groups.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks each learner can correctly
name and describe at least 4 family relationships during
independent practice.

**Homework.** Learners bring one real (or drawn) family photo/sketch
and 3 written sentences about it for next lesson.

**Revision.** Lesson 3.2 opens with 2-3 learners briefly sharing their
homework family description as a warm-up.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners add extended family
(cousin, aunt, uncle, niece, nephew) and describe one extended-family
relationship.

## Lesson 3.2 — "My Day — Present Simple & Time"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) tell
the time in English (o'clock, half past, quarter past/to); (2)
describe a daily routine using present simple with correct
third-person `-s`; (3) ask and answer `What time do you...?`; (4)
sequence daily activities using `first, then, after that, finally`
(light preview of Module 9's fuller sequencing work).

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Lesson 3.1 (family vocabulary), Module 1
(`to be`, question formation).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor draws a large clock face, calling out
times and having learners point to where the hands should be.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model: "I wake up at 7 o'clock. I have
breakfast at half past seven. I go to work at eight o'clock." Drill
telling the time (o'clock, half past, quarter past/to) with a
practice clock. Highlight present-simple third-person `-s`: "I wake
up" vs. "She wakes up" — explicit contrast on the board.

**Guided practice (10 min).** Pair work: Learner A says a time on a
practice clock, Learner B says what they typically do at that time,
using the routine-verb list; swap roles after 5 rounds.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners sequence 6 jumbled routine
pictures into a logical daily order and narrate their own version
aloud to a partner using time expressions and `first/then/after
that`.

**Speaking activity.** The narrated daily-routine sequencing above.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to someone narrating their
daily routine (6 sentences, times included); complete a simple
timeline worksheet.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read a short "A Day in the Life of..."
paragraph and answer 3 comprehension questions about times and
activities.

**Writing task (5 min).** Write 5-6 sentences describing your own
typical day with times, using present simple correctly.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill the three pronunciations of
present-simple `-s`/`-es` endings (/s/, /z/, /ɪz/) using the routine
verbs from this lesson (wakes, goes, watches).

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** Time-telling clock-matching game plus a
routine-verb sequencing card sort.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks third-person `-s` accuracy
during independent practice — the lesson's core target error.

**Homework.** Learners write out their real daily schedule for
tomorrow with times, ready to compare with a partner next lesson.

**Revision.** This lesson opens with the Lesson 3.1 family-sharing
recap (see above). Module 3's Quiz and Assignment draw on both
lessons.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners write 2 additional sentences
contrasting weekday and weekend routines ("On weekdays I... but on
weekends I...").

## Module 3 Quiz (auto-graded, 8 questions)

*Seeded verbatim into `quiz_questions` — see
`sql/seed-curriculum-level-1.sql`. Answer key marked with ✓.*

1. "She ___ two brothers." — (a) have (b) has ✓ (c) is (d) are
2. "I ___ up at 7 o'clock every day." — (a) wake ✓ (b) wakes (c)
   waking (d) woke
3. "He ___ to work at 8 o'clock." — (a) go (b) goes ✓ (c) going (d)
   gone
4. It is 7:30. How do you say this time? — (a) Half past seven ✓ (b)
   Quarter past seven (c) Seven o'clock (d) Quarter to seven
5. "This is my father's sister. She is my ___." — (a) grandmother (b)
   aunt ✓ (c) cousin (d) niece
6. "What ___ do you have breakfast?" — (a) time ✓ (b) when (c) who (d)
   which
7. "I have dinner ___ the evening." — (a) at (b) on (c) in ✓ (d) to
8. Which word means "comes before all the others"? — (a) then (b)
   first ✓ (c) finally (d) after

## Module 3 Assignment — "A Typical Day in My Life"

**Instructions given to the learner:** Write (or record) 6-8 sentences
describing your typical weekday. Include at least 4 different times
(using o'clock/half past/quarter past/to), at least 4 routine verbs
from this module, and correct present-simple third-person `-s` if you
describe someone else's day (e.g. a family member's routine) as well
as your own.

**Grading rubric (for the instructor):**
- **Grammatical accuracy** — present-simple `-s` agreement, correct
  time expressions.
- **Vocabulary range** — at least 4 distinct routine verbs and 2
  family members if describing someone else.
- **Task completion** — at least 6 sentences, at least 4 times stated.
- **Coherence** — activities presented in a logical time sequence.

A grade at or above the platform's pass threshold marks Module 3
completed for the learner.
