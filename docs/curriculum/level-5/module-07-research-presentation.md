# AIPC — Level V, Module 7: Research & Presentation

*Part of `docs/curriculum-level-5-advanced.md`'s Level V build-out.*

**Module learning objectives.** By the end of Module 7, the learner
can: structure a complex, multi-layered argument using advanced
discourse markers; write a concise conference abstract and a fuller
research essay; structure and deliver a 6-8 minute research-informed
presentation; facilitate audience Q&A actively and skilfully, not just
respond to it.

**Content map (5 learning items):** module overview & key phrases;
Lesson 7.1 ("Structuring the Argument — Discourse Markers for Complex
Argumentation"); Lesson 7.2 ("The Floor Is Yours — Structuring &
Delivering a Research-Informed Presentation & Facilitating Q&A");
Module 7 Quiz (10 questions); Module 7 Assignment ("A Research Essay,
Conference Abstract & Presentation").

## Reference reading: Module overview & key phrases

**Key phrases introduced this module:** Insofar as... — Notwithstanding
[X],... — By the same token,... — Conversely,... — That said,... —
With this in mind,...

**Discourse markers this module (functional set — complex argument
structuring):** *insofar as* (to the extent that — a precise
qualifier), *notwithstanding* (despite — a more formal alternative to
`despite/although`), *by the same token* (applying the same reasoning
to a related point), *conversely* (introducing a contrasting parallel
point), *that said* (a pivot acknowledging a prior point before
adding a qualification) — the level's most structurally sophisticated
connector set, capable of holding a genuinely multi-layered argument
together across many moves, not just a simple two-part contrast.

**Phrasal verbs & collocations this module:** *narrow down [a topic]*
(reduce a broad topic to a specific, manageable focus), *home in on
[a finding]* (focus precisely on a specific, significant result),
*tease out [a nuance]* (carefully extract a subtle distinction through
analysis), *open up [a discussion]* (invite broader participation),
*bring [something] to a close* (end it in a considered, deliberate
way, distinct from simply stopping).

**BrE / AmE note:** British English uses **postgraduate** (or
"postgrad") for study beyond a first (bachelor's) degree — a master's
or doctoral programme — while American English uses **graduate**
(as in "graduate school," "graduate student") for the identical level
of study; a British "graduate" simply means someone who has completed
any degree, so "graduate student" in the British sense would be
ambiguous in a way it isn't in American English — worth knowing
precisely in an academic/research context.

**Key vocabulary previewed:** research vocabulary (research question,
scope, methodology, findings, implications, limitations), presentation
vocabulary (abstract, keynote, discussant, plenary, breakout session).
Intercultural note: conference and academic presentation norms
(how much audience interruption is expected during a talk, how
Q&A is typically structured, how directly a presenter's claims may be
challenged) vary by academic discipline and country; this module
teaches one widely used international-academic convention.

## Lesson 7.1 — "Structuring the Argument — Discourse Markers for Complex Argumentation"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) use
`insofar as, notwithstanding, by the same token, conversely, that
said` correctly to structure a multi-layered argument; (2) build an
argument with more than a simple two-part contrast, using several
connected moves; (3) narrow a broad research topic down to a specific,
answerable focus; (4) write a concise conference abstract.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Level V, Module 2 (essay-level cohesion —
this lesson adds a further, more structurally sophisticated connector
set for genuinely complex, multi-move argumentation).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor presents a short argument using only
`however` and `because` repeatedly, then a revised version using this
module's richer connector set, asking learners which sounds more
sophisticated and precisely structured, before formal presentation.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model a multi-move argument: "Insofar as
remote work increases flexibility, it benefits most employees.
Notwithstanding this, certain roles genuinely require in-person
collaboration. By the same token, some industries face constraints
that others don't. Conversely, fully remote industries show few such
limitations. That said, the overall trend favours flexible
arrangements where feasible." Highlight explicitly: each connector
performs a distinct structural job — qualifying scope, conceding
despite a point, extending reasoning by analogy, introducing a
parallel contrast, pivoting to a considered final position — allowing
a writer to hold multiple related moves together in one coherent
argument, not just a single contrast.

**Guided practice (10 min).** Learners complete 8 sentence pairs using
the correct connector from this module's set for the structural
relationship described (qualification, concession, analogy, contrast,
pivot).

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners write a short multi-move
argument (5-6 sentences) on a topic of their choice, using at least 4
different connectors from this module's set, then read it to a
partner, who identifies each connector's structural function.

**Speaking activity.** The partner identification exchange above,
followed by a brief whole-class share of one particularly well-
structured multi-move argument per pair.

**Critical thinking / discussion prompt.** "Why do you think a
sophisticated argument often needs more than just 'X, but Y' —
what does a multi-move structure (qualification, concession, analogy,
contrast, pivot) let a writer do that a simple two-part contrast
can't?" — a genuinely sophisticated C1 question about argumentative
structure itself.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to a short complex argument (7-8
sentences, using several connectors from this module) and identify
each connector and its structural function.

**Reading activity — extended reading & critical evaluation (8 min).**
Read a short academic-style argument excerpt (180-200 words) using
this module's connector set. Answer 2 literal questions and 2
evaluative questions ("Does the argument's structure genuinely add up
to a coherent position, or does it feel like a list of disconnected
qualifications? What would you change?").

**Writing task (5 min).** Narrow a broad topic of your choice down to
a specific, answerable research focus (one sentence), then write a
150-200 word conference abstract: background, focus/question, an
anticipated or actual key finding, and its implication.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill measured, deliberate pacing
and clear stress on each connector when reading a complex, multi-move
argument aloud — each connector marking a genuine structural pivot the
listener needs to track.

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** A connector-function matching game:
match 10 example sentences to the correct connector from this module's
set based on the structural relationship needed.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks correct, purposeful
(not random) use of the connector set and a genuinely narrowed,
specific research focus, during independent practice.

**Homework.** Learners finalise their conference abstract and research
focus, ready for Lesson 7.2's presentation work.

**Revision.** Lesson 7.2 opens with learners briefly sharing their
research focus in one sentence.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners add one additional connector
from the set to extend their multi-move argument with a further
qualification or parallel point.

## Lesson 7.2 — "The Floor Is Yours — Structuring & Delivering a Research-Informed Presentation & Facilitating Q&A"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1)
structure a 6-8 minute research-informed presentation (context,
research question, findings/argument, implications, conclusion); (2)
deliver it with sustained, well-paced formal register; (3) actively
facilitate Q&A — inviting questions, managing time, and synthesising
across multiple questions, not just answering one at a time; (4) write
a fuller research essay expanding on the conference abstract.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Lesson 7.1 (complex argumentation,
conference abstract), Level IV Module 6 (the 4-5 minute presentation —
this lesson extends duration, structural complexity, and Q&A
facilitation).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor contrasts a presenter who simply
answers questions one at a time with one who actively facilitates
("That's a great question — it actually connects to what [another
audience member] asked a moment ago...") and asks learners which
creates a more genuinely engaged discussion, before formal
presentation.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model the extended presentation structure
explicitly: **context** (why this matters, briefly); **research
question/focus** (precisely stated, recycling Lesson 7.1's narrowed
focus); **findings/argument** (the substantive core, using this
module's connector set to structure multiple points); **implications**
(why the findings matter beyond the immediate topic); **conclusion**
(a clear, memorable closing statement). Then model active Q&A
facilitation: explicitly inviting questions (`I'd welcome your
questions — what's on your mind?`), synthesising across questions
(`That connects to an earlier question about...`), and gracefully
managing time or redirecting an off-topic question (`That's a rich
question — in the interest of time, let's connect after for that one,
but briefly...`).

**Guided practice (10 min).** In pairs, learners take turns delivering
a 2-minute mini-version of their presentation, while the partner asks
2 questions and the presenter practises facilitating (inviting further
questions, connecting to earlier points) rather than just answering.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners develop their full
presentation outline (context, research question, findings,
implications, conclusion) from their Lesson 7.1 conference abstract,
and rehearse it once.

**Speaking activity — flagship research-informed presentation.**
Learners deliver their full 6-8 minute presentation to a small group
(or the class), facilitating a genuine Q&A round afterward (inviting,
synthesising, and gracefully managing at least 3 questions) — this
level's flagship, most sustained speaking task, and the direct basis
for Module 7's assignment.

**Critical thinking / discussion prompt.** "What's the difference
between a presenter who merely tolerates questions and one who
genuinely uses Q&A to strengthen their argument? What does the second
kind of presenter actually do differently?" — directly reinforces the
lesson's active-facilitation skill as a genuine intellectual practice,
not just a social nicety.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to a presenter facilitating
Q&A (3-4 exchanges) and identify moments of active facilitation
(inviting, synthesising, time-managing) versus simple question-
answering.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read a short written presentation
outline/script excerpt and label its five structural parts (context,
question, findings, implications, conclusion).

**Writing task (5 min).** Expand your conference abstract into the
opening two paragraphs (context and research question) of your full
research essay.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill sustained vocal energy and
pacing across a longer, 6-8 minute talk — varied pitch to prevent
monotony over an extended duration, and warm, inviting intonation
specifically for the Q&A-invitation phrases.

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** A presentation/research vocabulary
matching game (research question, scope, methodology, findings,
implications, limitations, abstract, plenary) with simple definitions.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks for a genuine five-part
presentation structure and active (not passive) Q&A facilitation
during the presentation task.

**Homework.** Learners complete their full research essay draft,
ready for Module 7's assignment.

**Revision.** This lesson opens with the Lesson 7.1 research-focus
recap. Module 7's Quiz and Assignment draw on both lessons.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners prepare one anticipated
difficult question in advance and rehearse a response that uses this
module's connector set to structure a nuanced answer.

   Notwithstanding ✓ (c) By the same token (d) Conversely

   Conversely (c) Insofar as (d) Notwithstanding

## Module 7 Assignment — "A Research Essay, Conference Abstract & Presentation"

**Instructions given to the learner:** Complete three connected parts
on one narrowed research focus of your choice. **Part A: a conference
abstract** (150-200 words) — this level's seventh writing genre.
**Part B: a research essay** (450-550 words) expanding on the
abstract, using at least 3 connectors from this module's set to
structure a multi-move argument — this level's eighth writing genre.
**Part C (speaking, the flagship task):** Record yourself (or perform
live) a 6-8 minute research-informed presentation based on your essay,
structured in five clear parts (context, research question, findings,
implications, conclusion), and facilitate a Q&A round (real or
simulated) with at least 3 questions, actively inviting and
synthesising rather than just answering.

**Grading rubric (for the instructor):**
- **Grammatical accuracy** — correct, purposeful use of this module's
  advanced connector set.
- **Vocabulary range** — at least 4 distinct research/presentation
  words used correctly, plus one phrasal verb/collocation from this
  module.
- **Task completion** — abstract, essay, and a fully structured
  presentation with active Q&A facilitation all present.
- **Evidence & argument quality** — is the research focus genuinely
  narrowed and specific, and does the multi-move argument in the
  essay hold together as one coherent position?
- **Discourse coherence & register** — is the register consistently
  formal and academic across all three parts, and does the
  presentation's delivery reinforce (not undercut) the written
  argument's sophistication?

A grade at or above the platform's pass threshold marks Module 7
completed for the learner.
