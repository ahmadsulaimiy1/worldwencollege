# WEC-LC — Level II, Module 7: Food, Health & Habits

*Part of `docs/curriculum-level-2-elementary.md`'s Level II build-out.*

**Module learning objectives.** By the end of Module 7, the learner
can: describe habitual frequency precisely using adverbs of frequency;
give and justify lifestyle advice using an extended range of
`should/shouldn't`; discuss diet and exercise habits; recognise
BrE/AmE food-vocabulary differences.

**Content map (5 learning items):** module overview & key phrases;
Lesson 7.1 ("How Often? — Adverbs of Frequency"); Lesson 7.2 ("You
Really Should... — Lifestyle Advice"); Module 7 Quiz (10 questions);
Module 7 Assignment ("A Healthier Me — A Habit Plan").

---

## Reference reading: Module overview & key phrases

**Key phrases introduced this module:** always/usually/often/
sometimes/rarely/never — How often do you...? — You should really.../
You'd better... — If I were you, I'd... — It's important to.../It's
bad for you to...

**BrE / AmE note:** food vocabulary carries some of the most common
BrE/AmE differences learners encounter in real life: **chips** (BrE,
what Americans call **fries**) vs. **crisps** (BrE) / **chips** (AmE,
the snack in a bag); **biscuit** (BrE) / **cookie** (AmE); **sweets**
(BrE) / **candy** (AmE); **courgette** (BrE) / **zucchini** (AmE);
**aubergine** (BrE) / **eggplant** (AmE). This is genuinely one of the
highest-practical-value BrE/AmE vocabulary sets in the whole
programme, since it affects reading a menu or a recipe immediately.

**Key vocabulary previewed:** frequency adverbs (always, usually,
often, sometimes, rarely, never), health/lifestyle vocabulary
(balanced diet, regular exercise, get enough sleep, stay hydrated,
cut down on, give up).

## Lesson 7.1 — "How Often? — Adverbs of Frequency"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) use 6+
adverbs of frequency correctly and in the correct position in a
sentence; (2) ask and answer `How often do you...?`; (3) rank
frequency adverbs from "always" (100%) to "never" (0%); (4) use
frequency expressions with a specific time period (`twice a week`,
`three times a month`).

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Level I, Module 3 (present simple for
routine — frequency adverbs modify this same tense).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor makes 3 true statements about their
own routine using different frequency adverbs ("I always drink coffee
in the morning. I rarely eat breakfast.") before formal presentation.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model: "I usually go to bed at 11pm. I
sometimes skip breakfast. I never eat fast food." Highlight word
order: frequency adverbs go *before* the main verb but *after* `be`
("I am always tired" vs. "I always go"). Introduce the "twice a
week"/"three times a month" alternative pattern for more specific
frequency.

**Guided practice (10 min).** Pair work: learners complete 8 sentences
with the correct frequency adverb based on a percentage/frequency
prompt (e.g. "100% = ___", "0% = ___"), then check correct word order
placement.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners interview a partner using
5 `How often do you...?` questions about health/lifestyle habits
(exercise, eat vegetables, sleep 8 hours, drink water, eat fast food),
recording the answers.

**Speaking activity.** The frequency-interview exchange above, plus a
brief class summary: "Most people in our class usually/rarely..."

**Critical thinking / discussion prompt.** "Do you think people are
usually honest with themselves about their own habits? Why might
someone say they 'sometimes' do something when it's really 'often'?"
— a genuinely reflective, slightly playful question about self-
awareness that also reinforces the vocabulary's precise gradations.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to someone describing their
weekly habits (6 sentences, varied frequency adverbs) and complete a
frequency-grid worksheet.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read a short lifestyle survey result
text ("80% of people usually...") and answer 3 comprehension
questions.

**Writing task (5 min).** Write 5-6 sentences about your own real
habits using at least 4 different frequency adverbs, correctly
positioned.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill word stress on multi-
syllable frequency adverbs (USually, SOMEtimes, occaSIONally) and
correct sentence rhythm with the adverb inserted mid-sentence.

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** A frequency-adverb scale-ordering
activity (physically or on paper, ordering 6 adverbs from 0% to 100%).

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks correct adverb position
(before main verb, after `be`) during independent practice — the
lesson's core target error.

**Homework.** Learners note their real answers to 3 of the interview
questions from class, ready for Lesson 7.2's advice-giving practice.

**Revision.** Lesson 7.2 opens with a quick recap using the homework
frequency data as advice-giving material.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners add `hardly ever` and
`occasionally` to their frequency vocabulary, correctly placed on the
0%-100% scale.

## Lesson 7.2 — "You Really Should... — Lifestyle Advice"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) give
lifestyle advice using an extended range of `should/shouldn't`
(`really should`, `'d better`, `if I were you, I'd`); (2) justify
advice with a health-related reason; (3) respond to advice
appropriately (accepting or politely pushing back); (4) distinguish
strength of advice (`should` vs. the stronger `'d better`).

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Lesson 7.1 (frequency adverbs), Level I,
Module 9 (`should/shouldn't`, light introduction — this lesson extends
it substantially).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor states a habit ("I never exercise")
and elicits informal advice from learners in their own words before
formal presentation, to surface what they already intuitively know.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model: "You should really get more sleep.
If I were you, I'd cut down on sugar. You'd better see a doctor if
that continues." Highlight the strength scale: `should` (general
advice) < `should really`/`if I were you, I'd` (stronger,
more personal) < `'d better` (strongest, implies a negative
consequence if ignored). This nuance — several ways to give advice at
different strengths — is a genuine step up in sophistication from
Level I's single flat `should/shouldn't`.

**Guided practice (10 min).** Pair work: Learner A states a real or
invented lifestyle habit (using frequency adverbs from Lesson 7.1),
Learner B gives advice at an appropriate strength, then swap.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners choose one real habit they
personally want to change, describe it (frequency + honest self-
assessment), then get advice from 2 different classmates in a small
group, noting which advice they found most helpful and why.

**Speaking activity.** The small-group advice exchange above — this
lesson's core task, and the direct basis for Module 7's assignment.

**Critical thinking / discussion prompt.** "Is it always helpful when
someone gives you advice you didn't ask for? When is unsolicited
advice welcome, and when isn't it?" — a genuinely mature discussion
question, appropriate to this level's growing communicative and
intercultural awareness (directness/advice-giving norms vary
significantly across cultures, relevant to WEC-LC's international
learner base).

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to 3 short advice exchanges and
rank the strength of advice given in each (mild/moderate/strong).

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read a short "Dear Coach" advice-column
letter and response, identifying the advice given and its strength.

**Writing task (5 min).** Write a short advice reply (4-5 sentences)
to a friend who has described one unhealthy habit, using at least 2
different advice structures at different strengths.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill the natural contraction
`'d better` /əd ˈbetə/ in connected speech, and the stress pattern in
"If I WERE you" (a subjunctive form flagged for recognition, not
formally taught as grammar until Level III/IV's conditional work).

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** An advice-strength card-sorting game
(sorting 9 advice phrases into mild/moderate/strong columns).

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks that advice strength
matches the seriousness of the described habit (not using `'d better`
for a trivial issue) during group discussion.

**Homework.** Learners choose the single piece of advice they found
most useful and write 2-3 sentences on how they'd actually try to
follow it, ready for Module 7's assignment.

**Revision.** This lesson opens with the Lesson 7.1 frequency-data
recap used as advice-giving material. Module 7's Quiz and Assignment
draw on both lessons.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners write one piece of advice
using each of the three strength levels for the same habit, explaining
how the meaning shifts.

## Module 7 Quiz (auto-graded, 10 questions)

*Seeded verbatim into `quiz_questions` — see
`sql/seed-curriculum-level-2.sql`. Answer key marked with ✓.*

1. "I ___ go to bed at 11pm." (this is my normal routine) — (a)
   usually ✓ (b) am usually (c) usual (d) usually am
2. "I am ___ tired in the morning." (frequency + "to be") — (a)
   always ✓ (b) am always (c) always am (d) usual
3. "___ do you exercise?" — (a) How much (b) How often ✓ (c) How
   many (d) How long
4. "I go to the gym ___ a week." — (a) two time (b) twice ✓ (c)
   two times a (d) second time
5. In British English, "chips" usually means the same as the American
   word: — (a) crisps (b) fries ✓ (c) cookies (d) candy
6. "You ___ better see a doctor." — (a) had ✓ (b) have (c) should (d)
   would
7. "If I ___ you, I'd cut down on sugar." — (a) am (b) was (c) were
   ✓ (d) will be
8. Which is the strongest piece of advice? — (a) You could try... (b)
   You should... (c) You'd better... ✓ (d) Maybe you should...
9. "She ___ eats vegetables — almost every meal." — (a) never (b)
   rarely (c) usually ✓ (d) hardly ever
10. "It's important ___ stay hydrated." — (a) to ✓ (b) for (c) that
    (d) of

## Module 7 Assignment — "A Healthier Me — A Habit Plan"

**Instructions given to the learner:** Write (or record) 8-10
sentences. First, describe 3 of your real current habits using
frequency adverbs (at least one healthy, at least one you'd like to
change). Then, give yourself advice for the habit you'd like to
change, using at least two different advice structures at different
strengths (e.g. `should` and `'d better`, or `if I were you, I'd`).
Explain briefly why the change matters to you.

**Grading rubric (for the instructor):**
- **Grammatical accuracy** — correct frequency-adverb position, correct
  advice structures.
- **Vocabulary range** — at least 4 distinct frequency/health words
  used correctly.
- **Task completion** — 3 habits described, advice given at two
  different strengths, a personal reason stated.
- **Communicative quality** — is the self-reflection genuine and
  specific (not generic "I should exercise more"), and does the advice
  strength genuinely match the seriousness the writer assigns to the
  habit?

A grade at or above the platform's pass threshold marks Module 7
completed for the learner.
