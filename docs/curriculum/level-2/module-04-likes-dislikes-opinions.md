# WEC — Level II, Module 4: Likes, Dislikes & Opinions

*Part of `docs/curriculum-level-2-elementary.md`'s Level II build-out.*

**Module learning objectives.** By the end of Module 4, the learner
can: express likes, dislikes, and preferences using gerunds
correctly; give a simple opinion and a reason for it; agree and
disagree politely; use `prefer... to...` and `would rather` to compare
preferences; recognise BrE/AmE differences in opinion-softening
phrases.

**Content map (5 learning items):** module overview & key phrases;
Lesson 4.1 ("I Love Reading — Gerunds After Verbs"); Lesson 4.2 ("I
Think... — Giving and Responding to Opinions"); Module 4 Quiz (10
questions); Module 4 Assignment ("My Opinion — A Short Debate").

---

## Reference reading: Module overview & key phrases

**Key phrases introduced this module:** I love/like/enjoy/don't
mind/dislike/hate + -ing — In my opinion,.../I think.../I don't
think... — I agree/I disagree, because... — I'd prefer... to.../I'd
rather... — That's a good point, but...

**BrE / AmE note:** softening an opinion is handled slightly
differently in register: British speakers often use "I'd say..." or
"I suppose..." before a direct opinion in informal speech; American
speakers more often use "I feel like..." (e.g. "I feel like the book
was better than the film"), which sounds notably informal/dispreferred
in written British academic register. Neither is "more correct" —
WEC teaches "In my opinion.../I think..." as the neutral,
register-safe form suitable in both varieties and in writing.

**Key vocabulary previewed:** opinion verbs (love, like, enjoy, don't
mind, dislike, hate, prefer), agreement/disagreement language (I
agree, I see your point, I'm not so sure, I disagree), comparison
connectors (whereas, on the other hand).

## Lesson 4.1 — "I Love Reading — Gerunds After Verbs"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) form
the gerund (`-ing` form as a noun) correctly after opinion verbs; (2)
rank preferences from strongest to weakest (love → like → don't mind →
dislike → hate); (3) use `prefer + gerund + to + gerund` to compare two
activities; (4) avoid the common error of using `to + base verb`
after these opinion verbs.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Level I, Module 8 (`can/can't` — a
different but structurally similar "verb + base verb" pattern this
lesson deliberately contrasts, since gerunds after opinion verbs are
often confused with infinitives).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor mimes 3 activities (reading, cooking,
swimming) with an exaggerated happy/neutral/unhappy face for each,
eliciting "You like reading!"/"You hate cooking!" from learners before
formal presentation.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model: "I love reading. I don't mind
cooking, but I hate cleaning. I prefer walking to driving." Highlight
on the board: these verbs take the `-ing` form (a gerund, functioning
as a noun), never `to + base verb` — explicitly flag "I love to read"
as also acceptable informally but "I love reading" as the pattern this
lesson focuses on and the one used in the comparison structure
(`prefer + gerund + to + gerund`) that only works with `-ing`.

**Guided practice (10 min).** Pair work: learners are given a "like
scale" (love/like/don't mind/dislike/hate) and 8 activity pictures;
they build one true sentence per activity using the correct verb and
gerund form.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners rank 6 real leisure
activities on their own like-scale, then compare with a partner using
`I prefer ___ to ___` for at least 2 pairs of activities.

**Speaking activity.** The preference-comparison exchange above, plus
a class survey: each learner shares their strongest "love" and
strongest "hate" activity.

**Critical thinking / discussion prompt.** "Do you think people's
preferences usually stay the same their whole life, or do they change?
Give an example." — small-group discussion, encouraging learners to
connect this lesson's language to Module 1's `used to` ("I used to
hate vegetables, but now I like them").

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to someone describing 5
preferences and note which activities they love/like/dislike/hate on
a worksheet.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read a short "About Me" hobbies
paragraph and answer 3 comprehension questions about the writer's
preferences.

**Writing task (5 min).** Write 4-5 sentences about your own real
leisure preferences using at least 3 different opinion verbs and one
`prefer... to...` comparison.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill sentence stress on the
opinion verb itself (I LOVE reading; I HATE cleaning) — the verb, not
the activity, usually carries the emotional stress in natural speech.

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** A "like scale" card-sorting game ranking
8 activity cards from most to least liked, compared across pairs.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks correct gerund formation
(not infinitive) after opinion verbs during independent practice — the
lesson's core target error.

**Homework.** Learners prepare 2 sentences using `would rather` (a
light preview: "I'd rather read than watch TV") ready for Lesson 4.2's
opening recap.

**Revision.** Lesson 4.2 opens with a quick share of the homework
`would rather` sentences.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners add a reason to each
preference sentence using `because` (recycling Module 2's target
connector): "I prefer walking to driving because it's healthier."

## Lesson 4.2 — "I Think... — Giving and Responding to Opinions"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) give
an opinion using `I think.../In my opinion...` and support it with a
reason; (2) agree and disagree politely, without being blunt or
dismissive; (3) respond to someone else's opinion with a follow-up
question or a counter-point; (4) recognise the difference between a
fact and an opinion.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Lesson 4.1 (preference language), Level I,
Module 5 imperatives (as a register contrast — this lesson is about
softened, polite disagreement, not direct instruction).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor states one opinion ("I think coffee is
better than tea") and one fact ("Tea contains caffeine"), asking
learners which is which, before formally distinguishing the two.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model:
> A: In my opinion, online classes are more convenient than in-person
> ones.
> B: I agree — you can study anywhere. But I think in-person classes
> are more motivating.
> A: That's a good point. I hadn't thought of that.
Highlight the polite-disagreement frame: agree with something first
("That's true, but..."/"I see what you mean, however...") before
adding a different view — softening disagreement is presented
explicitly as a skill, not just a grammar point.

**Guided practice (10 min).** Pair work: learners are given 5 mild
opinion prompts (online vs. in-person learning, city vs. countryside
living, etc.) and practise stating an opinion, then politely agreeing
or disagreeing with a partner's response.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners choose one of 4 discussion
topics (already-familiar, non-controversial: technology in daily
life, city vs. countryside, reading vs. watching films, morning vs.
evening person) and prepare a short opinion + reason, then discuss in
a group of 3-4, practising both agreement and polite disagreement.

**Speaking activity.** The small-group discussion above — this
lesson's core task, and the direct basis for Module 4's assignment.

**Critical thinking / discussion prompt.** "Is it possible for two
people to both be 'right' when they disagree about an opinion (not a
fact)? Why or why not?" — a genuinely reflective question about the
nature of opinion itself, appropriate as this level's first explicit
fact-vs-opinion distinction work.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to a short discussion (2
speakers, 6-8 exchanges) and note each speaker's opinion and one
reason they give.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read two short opinion paragraphs
arguing opposite views on the same simple topic and identify each
writer's main opinion and supporting reason.

**Writing task (5 min).** Write a short paragraph (4-5 sentences)
giving your opinion on one of the four discussion topics, with at
least one clear reason.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill the falling intonation
pattern on confident opinion statements ("I THINK online classes are
better") versus the softer, slightly rising pattern used when
politely disagreeing ("I see your point, but...").

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** Agreement/disagreement phrase matching
game (strong agreement → mild agreement → mild disagreement → strong
disagreement, sorted by politeness/strength).

**Formative assessment.** Instructor listens during group discussion
for polite disagreement framing (not blunt "No, you're wrong") — the
lesson's key communicative-competence target, not just grammatical
accuracy.

**Homework.** Learners choose their strongest opinion from the lesson
and write 2 additional supporting reasons, ready for Module 4's
assignment.

**Revision.** This lesson opens with the Lesson 4.1 `would rather`
recap. Module 4's Quiz and Assignment draw on both lessons.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners practise responding to an
opinion they personally disagree with, using at least two different
softening phrases in the same conversation.

## Module 4 Quiz (auto-graded, 10 questions)

*Seeded verbatim into `quiz_questions` — see
`sql/seed-curriculum-level-2.sql`. Answer key marked with ✓.*

1. "I love ___ books." — (a) read (b) reading ✓ (c) to reading (d)
   reads
2. "She hates ___ up early." — (a) waking ✓ (b) wake (c) to wake (d)
   wakes
3. "I prefer tea ___ coffee." — (a) than (b) to ✓ (c) that (d) from
4. "In my ___, this is the best option." — (a) idea (b) think (c)
   opinion ✓ (d) mind
5. "I ___ disagree — I think the film was better." — (a) totally
   don't (b) really (c) don't ✓ (d) not
6. "That's a good point, ___ I still think..." — (a) so (b) but ✓ (c)
   because (d) and
7. Which sentence expresses an opinion, not a fact? — (a) Water boils
   at 100°C. (b) London is the capital of the UK. (c) I think this
   book is boring. ✓ (d) The meeting is at 3pm.
8. "I'd ___ walk than take the bus." — (a) prefer (b) rather ✓ (c)
   like (d) want
9. "He doesn't ___ waiting in queues." — (a) mind ✓ (b) minds (c)
   minding (d) to mind
10. Which response politely disagrees rather than bluntly rejecting
    the idea? — (a) No, that's wrong. (b) I see your point, but I
    think... ✓ (c) That's not true. (d) I don't agree at all.

## Module 4 Assignment — "My Opinion — A Short Debate"

**Instructions given to the learner:** Record yourself (or write) a
short response, 6-8 sentences, on one of these topics: online learning
vs. in-person learning; city life vs. countryside life. State your
opinion clearly, give at least two reasons using gerunds and/or
`prefer...to...` where natural, and include one sentence that
acknowledges a different view politely before restating your own
position (e.g. "Some people think..., but I believe...").

**Grading rubric (for the instructor):**
- **Grammatical accuracy** — correct gerund formation after opinion
  verbs, correct `prefer...to.../would rather` usage.
- **Vocabulary range** — at least 3 distinct opinion/preference
  expressions used correctly.
- **Task completion** — a clear opinion, at least two reasons, one
  acknowledgement of an alternative view.
- **Communicative quality** — is the opinion genuinely justified with
  specific reasons (not just asserted), and does the alternative-view
  acknowledgement sound authentically polite rather than a formulaic
  bolt-on sentence?

A grade at or above the platform's pass threshold marks Module 4
completed for the learner.
