# WEC — Level II, Module 3: Work & Study

*Part of `docs/curriculum-level-2-elementary.md`'s Level II build-out.*

**Module learning objectives.** By the end of Module 3, the learner
can: describe what they are doing right now versus what they usually
do, choosing correctly between present continuous and present simple;
talk about their job or studies and routine responsibilities; discuss
future ambitions with a reason; use 12+ work/study vocabulary items;
recognise key BrE/AmE workplace-vocabulary differences.

**Content map (5 learning items):** module overview & key phrases;
Lesson 3.1 ("What Are You Doing? — Present Continuous vs. Present
Simple"); Lesson 3.2 ("My Job, My Studies — Work & Ambition"); Module
3 Quiz (10 questions); Module 3 Assignment ("A Day in My Working
Life").

---

## Reference reading: Module overview & key phrases

**Key phrases introduced this module:** What are you doing at the
moment? — I'm working on... — I usually.../I'm currently... — What do
you do? (job) vs. What are you doing? (right now) — I'd like to become
a... because...

**BrE / AmE note:** **CV** (BrE, curriculum vitae) / **résumé** (AmE)
— both mean the document summarising your work history, though in the
US a CV specifically refers to an academic-style long-form document,
a nuance worth flagging for learners heading into international
business contexts. Also: **flat** (BrE) / **apartment** (AmE) often
appears in work-relocation contexts; **holiday** (BrE) / **vacation**
(AmE) from Module 2 recurs here in a workplace-leave context ("annual
leave" is the neutral, formal term used in both varieties, and the
safest choice in international professional writing).

**Key vocabulary previewed:** job/workplace nouns (colleague, manager,
deadline, meeting, project, client), study nouns (assignment, lecture,
exam, degree, classmate), ambition language (I'd like to become..., my
goal is to..., I'm planning to study...).

## Lesson 3.1 — "What Are You Doing? — Present Continuous vs. Present Simple"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) use
present continuous correctly for actions happening right now or
temporarily; (2) use present simple correctly for habits, routines,
and permanent facts; (3) choose correctly between the two based on
context; (4) recognise state verbs (know, like, believe, want) that
are rarely used in continuous form, even when describing a current
state.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Level I, Module 3 (present simple); Level
II, Module 1 (past continuous, as the pattern this lesson extends into
the present).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor performs one action ("I'm writing on
the board") while stating one routine fact about themselves ("I teach
English every day") — two present-tense sentences, visibly different
in meaning, before any rule is named.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model: "I usually work in an office, but
this week I'm working from home." Highlight: present simple = routine/
habit/permanent fact (usually, every day, always); present continuous
= happening now or temporarily (at the moment, this week, currently).
Introduce state verbs explicitly: "I know the answer" (correct) vs.
~~"I'm knowing the answer"~~ (incorrect) — a genuinely important A2
accuracy point, since learners overusing continuous forms is a common
error at this stage.

**Guided practice (10 min).** Pair work: learners sort 10 sentence
prompts into "routine" (present simple needed) and "right now/
temporary" (present continuous needed), then complete each with the
correct form.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners write 3 sentences about
their real routine (present simple) and 3 sentences about something
different they're doing "this week/month" (present continuous), then
interview a partner about both.

**Speaking activity.** The partner interview above, focused on
genuinely distinguishing "always true" from "true right now."

**Critical thinking / discussion prompt.** "Do you think it's healthy
to have a fixed daily routine, or is it better to do different things
all the time? Why?" — small-group discussion, directly recycling both
target structures (routine language for the "fixed" side, continuous/
temporary language for the "different things" side) inside a genuine
opinion exchange.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to someone describing their
week (mixing routine and temporary/current activities) and sort what
they say into the two categories on a worksheet.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read a short "About Me" workplace
profile text and identify which sentences describe permanent facts
and which describe something temporary/current.

**Writing task (5 min).** Write a short paragraph (5-6 sentences)
about your real week, using both present simple (routine) and present
continuous (something different happening this week) correctly.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill the contracted `'m/'re/'s +
-ing` connected-speech forms ("I'm WORKing", "She's STUDYing") versus
the fuller, more formal pronunciation appropriate for professional
contexts — a register point relevant to this module's professional
focus.

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** A state-verb vs. action-verb sorting
game (know, want, have [possession] vs. run, write, cook) to reinforce
which verbs resist the continuous form.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks correct tense selection by
context (not just correct formation) and correct avoidance of
continuous forms with state verbs.

**Homework.** Learners note 3 things that are "usually true" about
their work/study life and 3 things that are different "this week,"
ready for Lesson 3.2.

**Revision.** Lesson 3.2 opens with 2-3 learners sharing their
homework routine/temporary contrasts.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners add one sentence using
present continuous for a fixed future arrangement ("I'm meeting my
manager tomorrow") — a light preview of a genuinely common native-
speaker usage, formally developed at Level III.

## Lesson 3.2 — "My Job, My Studies — Work & Ambition"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) use
12+ work/study vocabulary items correctly; (2) describe your job or
studies and typical responsibilities; (3) discuss a future ambition
using `I'd like to.../My goal is to...` with a reason; (4) ask
appropriate professional/academic questions about someone else's work
or studies.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Lesson 3.1 (present simple/continuous),
Level I, Module 8 (`going to` for near-future plans, as a contrast
point with this lesson's more aspirational `I'd like to`).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor asks the class "What do you want to be
when you're older?" — a familiar question reframed for adult/young-
adult learners as "What's one professional goal you have?" — eliciting
informal responses before formal presentation.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model: "I work as a teacher. My main
responsibility is planning lessons. I'd like to become a head teacher
one day because I enjoy leading a team." Introduce 10-12 work/study
nouns and the ambition-language frame (`I'd like to become.../My goal
is to.../I'm planning to study...` + `because` for justification,
recycling Module 2's `because` pattern in a new context).

**Guided practice (10 min).** Pair work: Learner A asks about
Learner B's real (or aspirational, if a student) job/studies using 4
target questions (`What do you do? What's your main responsibility?
What do you like about it? What's your goal?`), then swap.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners prepare a short "About My
Work/Studies" profile (4-5 sentences: role, responsibility, one thing
they enjoy, one future goal with a reason), then present it to a small
group, who each ask one follow-up question.

**Speaking activity.** The profile presentation and follow-up
questions above — this lesson's core speaking task, and Module 3's
assignment topic.

**Critical thinking / discussion prompt.** "Is it more important to
enjoy your job or to earn a lot of money? Can you have both?" — a
genuinely debatable question, discussed in small groups, encouraging
learners to weigh competing values rather than give a single "correct"
answer.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to 3 people briefly describing
their jobs and goals, and match each speaker to the correct summary
card.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read 2 short professional profile texts
(e.g. a simple "team member spotlight" style text) and answer
comprehension questions about role and ambition.

**Writing task (5 min).** Write a short "About Me" professional
paragraph (5-6 sentences) suitable for a simple introduction message
to a new colleague or classmate.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill word stress in multi-
syllable job/workplace nouns (colLEAGUE, deADline, MANager) and the
BrE/AmE stress difference in a small number of relevant words if the
class's real professions surface any (flagged case-by-case by the
instructor rather than a fixed drill list, since relevance depends on
the specific class).

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** A work/study vocabulary matching game
(word to definition or to a workplace scenario picture).

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks correct use of ambition
language with `because`-justification during the group presentation.

**Homework.** Learners finalise their "About My Work/Studies" profile
for Module 3's assignment, adding one more specific detail.

**Revision.** This lesson opens with the Lesson 3.1 routine/temporary
recap. Module 3's Quiz and Assignment draw on both lessons.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners add one sentence comparing
their current role to a past one using `used to` (recycling Module
1's target grammar in a professional context — "I used to work in
retail, but now I work in an office").

## Module 3 Quiz (auto-graded, 10 questions)

*Seeded verbatim into `quiz_questions` — see
`sql/seed-curriculum-level-2.sql`. Answer key marked with ✓.*

1. "I ___ in an office, but this week I ___ from home." — (a) work /
   work (b) work / am working ✓ (c) am working / work (d) am working /
   am working
2. "She ___ the answer." (a fact, not a temporary state) — (a) is
   knowing (b) knows ✓ (c) is know (d) know
3. "Look! It ___ outside." — (a) rains (b) is raining ✓ (c) rain (d)
   raining
4. "I usually ___ the bus to work." — (a) am taking (b) take ✓ (c)
   takes (d) taking
5. "My main ___ is planning the weekly schedule." — (a) responsibility
   ✓ (b) deadline (c) colleague (d) client
6. "I'd like to ___ a manager one day." — (a) become ✓ (b) becoming
   (c) becomes (d) became
7. Which word means "the document that summarises your work history"
   in British English? — (a) résumé (b) portfolio (c) CV ✓ (d)
   profile
8. "I enjoy my job ___ it's challenging and creative." — (a) so (b)
   but (c) because ✓ (d) although
9. "At the moment, I ___ on a big project." — (a) work (b) am working
   ✓ (c) works (d) worked
10. Which verb is rarely used in the continuous form? — (a) run (b)
    cook (c) know ✓ (d) write

## Module 3 Assignment — "A Day in My Working Life"

**Instructions given to the learner:** Record yourself (or write) 8-10
sentences describing your real (or realistic future) work or study
life. Include: at least 2 sentences using present simple for routine
responsibilities; at least 2 sentences using present continuous for
something you're currently working on or that's different this week;
one sentence stating a professional or academic goal using `I'd like
to.../My goal is to...` with a `because` justification; and at least 4
work/study vocabulary items from this module.

**Grading rubric (for the instructor):**
- **Grammatical accuracy** — correct present simple/continuous choice
  by context, correct avoidance of continuous with state verbs.
- **Vocabulary range** — at least 4 distinct work/study words used
  correctly.
- **Task completion** — all required elements (routine, current
  activity, goal with reason) present.
- **Communicative quality** — is the stated goal genuinely explained
  with a real, specific reason, and does the description sound like a
  real person's working life, not a list of memorised sentence
  patterns?

A grade at or above the platform's pass threshold marks Module 3
completed for the learner.
