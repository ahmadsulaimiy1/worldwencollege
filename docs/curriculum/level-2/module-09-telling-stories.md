# AIPC — Level II, Module 9: Telling Stories

*Part of `docs/curriculum-level-2-elementary.md`'s Level II build-out.*

**Module learning objectives.** By the end of Module 9, the learner
can: narrate a personal story with a clear beginning, middle, and end;
sequence events precisely using a range of time connectors; combine
past simple and past continuous fluently within a single narrative
(recycling and consolidating Module 1's grammar in extended,
connected discourse); keep a listener engaged with basic narrative
techniques (a hook, a turning point, a resolution).

**Content map (5 learning items):** module overview & key phrases;
Lesson 9.1 ("First... Then... Suddenly... — Sequencing a Story");
Lesson 9.2 ("You'll Never Believe What Happened — Telling an
Engaging Story"); Module 9 Quiz (10 questions); Module 9 Assignment
("Tell Me a Story").

---

## Reference reading: Module overview & key phrases

**Key phrases introduced this module:** First.../After that.../Then...
— Suddenly.../All of a sudden... — In the end.../Finally... — You'll
never believe what happened! — Guess what happened next...

**BrE / AmE note:** narrative connectors themselves are almost
entirely shared between British and American English, but this
module's reference reading flags one small, genuinely useful
difference: British speakers often say **"at the weekend"** when
placing a story in time ("It happened at the weekend"), while American
speakers say **"on the weekend"** — the same preposition pattern
first introduced with scheduling language in Module 5, now recycled in
a storytelling context.

**Key vocabulary previewed:** sequencing connectors (first, after
that, next, then, suddenly, all of a sudden, eventually, in the end,
finally), narrative-shape vocabulary (a hook/opening line, a turning
point, a problem, a resolution), reaction language for listeners
(No way! What happened next? That's amazing!).

## Lesson 9.1 — "First... Then... Suddenly... — Sequencing a Story"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) use a
full range of sequencing connectors to order events clearly (first,
after that, next, then, suddenly, all of a sudden, eventually, in the
end, finally); (2) combine past simple (main events) and past
continuous (background/interrupted action) correctly within a single
connected narrative; (3) use "suddenly"/"all of a sudden" specifically
to mark an unexpected interruption to the background action; (4) tell
a short story (5-6 sentences) with clear, correctly sequenced
structure.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Level II, Module 1 (past simple vs. past
continuous, taught with isolated example sentences — this lesson
extends that grammar into connected, multi-sentence narrative for the
first time).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor tells a very short story (3-4
sentences) about their own day using 3 different sequencing connectors
before formal presentation, then asks learners to identify the
connectors they heard.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model a short narrative:
> First, I left the house at 8am. After that, I walked to the station.
> I was waiting for my train when suddenly, I realised I'd forgotten
> my phone. In the end, I went back home to get it, and I was late for
> work.
Highlight the structure explicitly: sequencing connectors carry the
story's main timeline forward (First.../After that.../In the end...),
while past continuous ("I was waiting") sets up background action
that "suddenly" then interrupts — this is Module 1's grammar, now
doing real narrative work rather than appearing in single, isolated
example sentences.

**Guided practice (10 min).** Learners are given 6 jumbled story
sentences (a short narrative cut into pieces) and reorder them using
the sequencing connectors as clues, then read the story aloud.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners write a short 5-6 sentence
story about a real or invented minor mishap (missing a bus, losing
something, a surprise visitor), using at least 4 different sequencing
connectors and at least one past continuous "interrupted action"
sentence.

**Speaking activity.** Learners tell their mishap story to a partner
from memory (not reading it aloud), who listens for and names the
sequencing connectors used.

**Critical thinking / discussion prompt.** "Why do you think the word
'suddenly' makes a story more interesting to listen to? What would the
story feel like without it?" — a genuinely reflective question linking
a specific grammar choice to its effect on the listener, appropriate
groundwork for Lesson 9.2's focus on engagement.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to a short narrated story (7-8
sentences) and number a set of 6 picture panels in the correct order
based on the sequencing connectors used.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read a short written anecdote and
underline every sequencing connector used, then identify which
sentences use past continuous and why.

**Writing task (5 min).** Rewrite 4 unconnected simple sentences
("I woke up late. I missed the bus. I ran to the station. I caught the
train.") into a connected short narrative using sequencing connectors.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill sentence stress on key
sequencing connectors at the start of a sentence ("SUDDenly...",
"FINally...") and the natural pause that typically follows them in
spoken narrative.

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** A sequencing-connector ordering game:
arrange 9 connector cards (first, after that, next, then, suddenly,
all of a sudden, eventually, in the end, finally) along a rough
"early story" to "late story" timeline, discussing that some (like
"then" and "next") are flexible while others (like "finally") are
typically story-final.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks correct connector variety
(not repeatedly using only "then") and correct past continuous
"interruption" formation during independent practice.

**Homework.** Learners choose one real memorable event from their own
life (nothing too personal — a funny, surprising, or minor-mishap
story is ideal) and jot down 5-6 rough notes on what happened, in
order, ready for Lesson 9.2's engagement techniques.

**Revision.** Lesson 9.2 opens with learners briefly retelling their
homework story notes in rough form, before refining them.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners add a second past continuous
"interrupted action" sentence to their mishap story, using a different
interrupting connector ("just as," as a recognition-only preview of
more advanced connectors taught formally in later levels).

## Lesson 9.2 — "You'll Never Believe What Happened — Telling an Engaging Story"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) open a
story with a "hook" that creates listener interest; (2) identify and
narrate a clear turning point or problem within a story; (3) close a
story with a satisfying resolution; (4) use listener-reaction language
appropriately when someone else is telling a story.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Lesson 9.1 (sequencing connectors, past
simple/continuous narrative).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor tells the same short story twice: once
flatly with no hook or shape ("I went shopping. I bought a shirt. I
went home."), and once shaped with a hook and a turning point
("You'll never believe what happened at the shopping centre
yesterday..."). Learners compare which version they'd rather listen
to and why, before formal presentation.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model the three-part narrative shape
explicitly: a **hook** ("You'll never believe what happened..." / "So
this is a funny story...") to open; a **turning point or problem**
(what went wrong, what was surprising) as the middle; and a
**resolution** ("In the end..." / "Finally...") to close. Also model
listener-reaction phrases (No way! What happened next? That's
amazing! Oh no, really?) and highlight that a good storyteller pauses
to let a listener react — storytelling in conversation is
interactive, not a monologue.

**Guided practice (10 min).** Pair work: Learner A retells their
Lesson 9.1 homework story using the full hook → turning point →
resolution shape; Learner B listens actively, using at least 3
different reaction phrases at natural points, then swap.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners revise their homework
story notes into a fully shaped short story (a hook, a clear turning
point, a resolution) and rehearse telling it once more, silently or to
themselves, before the final speaking activity.

**Speaking activity.** Learners tell their fully shaped personal story
to a new partner (or small group), who reacts naturally throughout —
this lesson's core task, and the direct basis for Module 9's
assignment.

**Critical thinking / discussion prompt.** "Think of a story someone
has told you that you still remember well. What made it memorable —
the events themselves, or the way it was told?" — connects the
lesson's narrative-technique focus to learners' own real experience as
listeners, a genuinely reflective closing discussion for the module.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to a well-told short personal
anecdote and identify its hook, turning point, and resolution.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read a short written personal story
(a blog-style anecdote) and label its hook, turning point, and
resolution.

**Writing task (5 min).** Write a strong one-sentence "hook" opener
for 3 different story ideas (a travel mishap, a surprising meeting, a
lucky coincidence).

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill natural, animated intonation
for storytelling hooks and reaction phrases ("No WAY!", "What happened
NEXT?") — noticeably more expressive pitch range than neutral
statement intonation, since storytelling is a genuinely performative
speech act at this level.

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** A hook-writing challenge: learners write
and compare hooks for the same story prompt, voting on which is most
engaging and explaining why.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks for a genuine three-part
shape (not just a flat chronological list) and appropriate listener
reactions during the speaking activity.

**Homework.** Learners finalise their personal story (hook, turning
point, resolution, correct sequencing and past simple/continuous use)
for Module 9's assignment.

**Revision.** This lesson opens with the Lesson 9.1 homework-story
recap. Module 9's Quiz and Assignment draw on both lessons.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners add one moment of reported
speech to their story ("She said, 'I can't believe it!'") as a
recognition-only preview of reported speech, formally taught in a
later level.

## Module 9 Quiz (auto-graded, 10 questions)

*Seeded verbatim into `quiz_questions` — see
`sql/seed-curriculum-level-2.sql`. Answer key marked with ✓.*

1. "___, I left the house at 8am." (the very first event) — (a)
   Finally (b) First ✓ (c) Suddenly (d) Eventually
2. "I was waiting for the bus when ___ it started to rain." — (a)
   first (b) after that (c) suddenly ✓ (d) finally
3. "I ___ waiting for the train when I realised I'd forgotten my
   phone." (background action) — (a) was ✓ (b) am (c) were (d) is
4. "___, everything worked out fine." (the closing event) — (a) First
   (b) Then (c) In the end ✓ (d) Suddenly
5. Which word usually signals an unexpected interruption to a story? —
   (a) after that (b) next (c) suddenly ✓ (d) finally
6. "You'll ___ believe what happened!" — (a) never ✓ (b) not (c) no
   (d) any
7. Which is a good example of a storytelling "hook"? — (a) I went
   shopping yesterday. (b) You'll never believe what happened at the
   shopping centre! ✓ (c) I bought a shirt. (d) Then I went home.
8. Which is an appropriate listener-reaction phrase? — (a) That's
   amazing! ✓ (b) I don't care. (c) Stop talking. (d) That's not true.
9. "In British English, something might happen ___ the weekend." — (a)
   in (b) at ✓ (c) on (d) for
10. Which sentence correctly combines past continuous and past
    simple? — (a) I was walk home when it started to rain. (b) I was
    walking home when it started to rain. ✓ (c) I walk home when it
    was starting to rain. (d) I am walking home when it started to
    rain.

## Module 9 Assignment — "Tell Me a Story"

**Instructions given to the learner:** Record yourself (or perform
live) telling a real or realistic personal story, 60-90 seconds long.
Your story must include: a clear hook to open; at least 4 different
sequencing connectors; at least one past continuous sentence
interrupted by a past simple event using "suddenly" or "all of a
sudden"; a clear turning point or problem; and a resolution to close.

**Grading rubric (for the instructor):**
- **Grammatical accuracy** — correct past simple/past continuous
  combination, correct sequencing connector use.
- **Vocabulary range** — at least 4 different sequencing connectors
  used correctly and without excessive repetition of any single one.
- **Task completion** — hook, turning point, and resolution all
  clearly present, forming a genuine three-part narrative shape.
- **Communicative quality** — is the story genuinely engaging to
  listen to (not just grammatically correct but flatly delivered), and
  does it flow as connected discourse rather than a list of
  disconnected sentences?

A grade at or above the platform's pass threshold marks Module 9
completed for the learner.
