# WEC-LC — Level II, Module 5: Making Plans

*Part of `docs/curriculum-level-2-elementary.md`'s Level II build-out.*

**Module learning objectives.** By the end of Module 5, the learner
can: choose correctly between `will`, `going to`, and present
continuous for future meaning; make, accept, and decline invitations
politely; arrange a specific time/place to meet; negotiate a change of
plan.

**Content map (5 learning items):** module overview & key phrases;
Lesson 5.1 ("Will, Going To, or Present Continuous? — Future Forms
Review"); Lesson 5.2 ("Are You Free on Saturday? — Invitations &
Arrangements"); Module 5 Quiz (10 questions); Module 5 Assignment
("Arrange a Meet-Up").

---

## Reference reading: Module overview & key phrases

**Key phrases introduced this module:** Would you like to...? — I'm
free on.../I'm busy on... — Shall we...? — Let's... — I'm afraid I
can't, but how about...? — That works for me. / Sounds good.

**BrE / AmE note:** `Shall we...?` (a polite British suggestion form)
sounds distinctly formal/dated in American English, where `How about
we...?` or `Let's...` is far more natural; both are taught here, with
`Shall we` flagged as the more British-coded option. Diary/calendar
vocabulary also differs slightly: British speakers say "I'm free at
the weekend"; American speakers say "I'm free this weekend" — a small
but genuinely common difference.

**Key vocabulary previewed:** invitation/arrangement verbs (arrange,
confirm, reschedule, cancel, postpone), time-arrangement phrases (how
about..., what time suits you, I'll pencil it in), polite refusal
language (I'm afraid..., unfortunately..., I already have plans).

## Lesson 5.1 — "Will, Going To, or Present Continuous? — Future Forms Review"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1)
distinguish `will` (a spontaneous decision or prediction) from `going
to` (a prior plan or intention, reviewed from Level I); (2) use
present continuous for a fixed, arranged future event (recycling
Module 3's light preview); (3) choose the correct future form based on
context; (4) use `will` correctly for offers and promises.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Level I, Module 8 (`going to`); Level II,
Module 3 (present continuous, including its fixed-arrangement
extension previewed there).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor asks "What are you doing this
weekend?" (elicits going to/present continuous from prior knowledge),
then poses a spontaneous problem ("I've forgotten my pen!") and
models the spontaneous-decision response ("I'll lend you mine") before
formal presentation.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model three contrasting sentences: "I'm
going to visit my parents this weekend" (a plan, decided before now);
"I'm meeting my manager at 3pm on Thursday" (a fixed arrangement,
present continuous); "It's cold — I'll close the window" (a
spontaneous decision, `will`, decided at the moment of speaking).
Highlight the decision-timing test explicitly: was it decided before
now (`going to`/present continuous) or right now (`will`)?

**Guided practice (10 min).** Learners read 8 short context sentences
and choose the correct future form (will/going to/present continuous)
for each, justifying their choice using the decision-timing test.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners write 5 sentences about
their real near-future plans, deliberately using all three forms at
least once each, then explain to a partner why they chose each form.

**Speaking activity.** The partner explanation exchange above —
explicitly testing metalinguistic awareness (can the learner explain
*why*, not just produce correctly), a genuine step up from Level I's
purely productive focus.

**Critical thinking / discussion prompt.** "Do you prefer to plan
things carefully in advance, or decide things spontaneously? What are
the advantages of each?" — connects the grammar distinction
(pre-planned vs. spontaneous) directly to a real personality/lifestyle
discussion.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to 5 short exchanges and
identify whether each speaker is making a spontaneous decision,
stating a plan, or confirming an arrangement.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read a short diary/calendar text mixing
all three future forms and answer 3 comprehension questions about
what's planned versus what's still undecided.

**Writing task (5 min).** Write a short message to a friend describing
your plans for next week, using at least 2 of the 3 future forms
correctly.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill the contracted `'ll` in
natural speech ("I'll help you") versus its full form `will` used for
emphasis or in careful/written register.

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** A future-forms sorting game: 12 example
sentences sorted into "plan," "arrangement," or "spontaneous decision"
columns.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks not just correct form but
correct *justification* during the partner explanation activity — the
lesson's real target skill at this level.

**Homework.** Learners check their real weekly schedule and note 3
fixed arrangements (using present continuous) for Lesson 5.2.

**Revision.** Lesson 5.2 opens with learners sharing their homework
fixed-arrangement sentences.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners write one sentence using
`will` for a promise ("I'll call you when I arrive") — a genuinely
distinct use from prediction/spontaneous decision, previewed here.

## Lesson 5.2 — "Are You Free on Saturday? — Invitations & Arrangements"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) make
an invitation politely (`Would you like to...?`); (2) accept an
invitation enthusiastically and decline one politely with a reason;
(3) suggest an alternative time/place when declining; (4) confirm a
final arrangement clearly (who, what, when, where).

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Lesson 5.1 (future forms), Level I, Module
3 (telling the time).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor invites 2-3 learners to a (pretend)
event using `Would you like to...?`, modelling both an accept and a
polite decline response before formal presentation.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model dialogue:
> A: Would you like to come to the cinema on Saturday?
> B: I'd love to! What time?
> A: How about 7pm?
> B: Sounds good. I'll meet you there.

> C: Are you free for lunch tomorrow?
> D: I'm afraid I can't — I already have plans. How about Friday
> instead?
> C: Friday works for me.
Highlight the polite-decline structure: apologise/soften → give a
brief reason → offer an alternative — a genuine communicative skill,
not just a grammar pattern.

**Guided practice (10 min).** Pair work: Learner A invites Learner B
to 3 different events from prompt cards; Learner B accepts one,
declines one with an alternative, and negotiates the time for the
third.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners plan a real (or realistic
invented) get-together with 2 different classmates in turn, confirming
a specific day, time, and place for each — practising the full
invite → respond → confirm cycle twice.

**Speaking activity.** The two full invitation cycles above — this
lesson's core task and the direct basis for Module 5's assignment.

**Critical thinking / discussion prompt.** "Why is it important to
give a reason when you decline an invitation, rather than just saying
'no'?" — a short discussion connecting grammar-level politeness
strategies to real social/intercultural awareness, especially relevant
for WEC-LC's internationally diverse learner base.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to 3 invitation exchanges and
note whether each is accepted, declined, or rescheduled, and to when.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read a short exchange of text messages
arranging a meet-up and answer 3 comprehension questions about the
final confirmed plan.

**Writing task (5 min).** Write a short message inviting a friend to
an event, including a specific day and time.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill polite, rising intonation on
invitations (`Would you like to...?`) versus the warmer, falling
intonation of enthusiastic acceptance (`I'd LOVE to!`).

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** An invitation-response matching game
(accept/decline/reschedule phrases sorted by function).

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks that a decline is always
followed by a reason and/or alternative — never a bare "no" — during
guided/independent practice.

**Homework.** Learners write a short final confirmation message
summarising one of their independent-practice arrangements (who,
what, when, where), ready for Module 5's assignment.

**Revision.** This lesson opens with the Lesson 5.1 fixed-arrangement
sharing recap. Module 5's Quiz and Assignment draw on both lessons.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners add a rescheduling exchange
("Actually, something's come up — can we move it to Sunday instead?")
to one of their arrangement dialogues.

## Module 5 Quiz (auto-graded, 10 questions)

*Seeded verbatim into `quiz_questions` — see
`sql/seed-curriculum-level-2.sql`. Answer key marked with ✓.*

1. "It's cold in here. I ___ close the window." (decided now) — (a)
   am going to (b) will ✓ (c) am closing (d) close
2. "I ___ my parents this weekend." (already decided) — (a) am going
   to visit ✓ (b) will visit (c) visit (d) visited
3. "I ___ my manager at 3pm on Thursday." (a fixed arrangement) — (a)
   will meet (b) am meeting ✓ (c) meet (d) am going to meeting
4. "___ you like to come to the cinema?" — (a) Would ✓ (b) Will (c)
   Do (d) Are
5. "I'd ___ to! What time?" — (a) like (b) love ✓ (c) prefer (d)
   rather
6. "I'm afraid I can't -- I already ___ plans." — (a) have ✓ (b) has
   (c) having (d) had
7. "How about Friday ___?" — (a) already (b) instead ✓ (c) also (d)
   too
8. Which is a polite way to decline an invitation? — (a) No. (b) I
   can't. (c) I'm afraid I can't, but how about Friday? ✓ (d) I don't
   want to.
9. "I promise I ___ call you when I arrive." — (a) am going to (b)
   will ✓ (c) am calling (d) call
10. Which future form usually describes a spontaneous decision made
    at the moment of speaking? — (a) going to (b) present continuous
    (c) will ✓ (d) present simple

## Module 5 Assignment — "Arrange a Meet-Up"

**Instructions given to the learner:** Record yourself (or write) a
short dialogue, 8-10 exchanges, in which you invite someone to an
event, they initially can't make it and suggest an alternative, and
you agree on a final day, time, and place. Use at least one example of
`will`, one of `going to`, and one of present continuous for a fixed
arrangement.

**Grading rubric (for the instructor):**
- **Grammatical accuracy** — all three future forms used correctly and
  appropriately for their context.
- **Vocabulary range** — at least 3 distinct invitation/arrangement
  phrases used correctly.
- **Task completion** — a full invite → decline-with-alternative →
  confirm cycle present.
- **Communicative quality** — does the decline sound genuinely polite
  (reason + alternative), and does the dialogue end with a clearly
  confirmed arrangement (who, what, when, where all stated)?

A grade at or above the platform's pass threshold marks Module 5
completed for the learner.
