# WEC — Level II, Module 10: Review & Consolidation

*Part of `docs/curriculum-level-2-elementary.md`'s Level II build-out.
Per `docs/curriculum-framework.md`'s assessment strategy, this module
is wholly revision and the level's end-of-level examination — it does
not introduce new grammar or vocabulary.*

**Module learning objectives.** By the end of Module 10, the learner
can: demonstrate cumulative command of every grammar point and
vocabulary set from Modules 1-9; recognise and correctly use the
British/American English differences introduced across the level;
complete WEC's Elementary-level (A2) end-of-level examination
across grammar/vocabulary, listening, reading, writing, and speaking;
identify their own remaining weak areas ahead of Level III.

**Content map (4 learning items):** Level II revision guide (cumulative
summary); Revision Lesson (structured cross-module consolidation
activities); Elementary-Level Mock Exam — Grammar & Vocabulary (20
auto-graded questions, one drawn from each module's core target
language, plus two cumulative BrE/AmE items); Elementary-Level Mock
Exam — Speaking & Writing (comprehensive, rubric-graded).

---

## Level II revision guide (cumulative summary)

**Module 1 — Life Stories:** past simple vs. past continuous, `used
to`; biography/life-event vocabulary, time-sequencing connectors.

**Module 2 — Travel & Transport:** comparatives/superlatives; travel,
transport, booking vocabulary.

**Module 3 — Work & Study:** present continuous (current) vs. present
simple (habit); jobs, workplace, study vocabulary.

**Module 4 — Likes, Dislikes & Opinions:** gerunds after opinion
verbs, `prefer...to...`, `would rather`; opinion/preference language,
polite agreement/disagreement.

**Module 5 — Making Plans:** `will` vs. `going to` vs. present
continuous for future; invitations, arrangements, polite refusal.

**Module 6 — Homes & Neighbourhoods:** `there was/were`, `used to be`,
comparatives recycled, precise prepositions of place; housing,
neighbourhood vocabulary.

**Module 7 — Food, Health & Habits:** adverbs of frequency, extended
`should/shouldn't` (`should really`, `'d better`, `if I were you,
I'd`); lifestyle, health vocabulary.

**Module 8 — Shopping & Services:** past simple questions extended to
fact-finding sequences, polite complaint language; shopping, services,
money vocabulary.

**Module 9 — Telling Stories:** sequencing connectors, past simple/
past continuous combined in connected narrative; storytelling
vocabulary, narrative shape (hook, turning point, resolution).

**Structural thread across the level:** Level II took Level I's
isolated single-structure grammar and put it to real communicative
work — contrasting structures against each other in the same lesson
(Module 1's past forms, Module 5's three future forms), extending
short exchanges into connected sequences and full narratives (Module
8's fact-finding questions, Module 9's storytelling), and adding a
genuine critical-thinking and intercultural layer (the discussion
prompts, and the BrE/AmE notes running through every module) that
Level I's more foundational scope did not yet require. Module 10 tests
all of it together, not in isolation.

**Cumulative BrE/AmE reference (all differences introduced this
level):** flat/apartment, ground floor/first floor (Module 6); till/
register or checkout, queue/line, shop assistant/sales associate,
shopping trolley/shopping cart (Module 8); chips/fries, crisps/chips,
biscuit/cookie, sweets/candy, courgette/zucchini, aubergine/eggplant
(Module 7); "Shall we...?"/"How about we...?", free at the weekend/
free this weekend (Module 5); at the weekend/on the weekend (Module
9); opinion-softening register ("I'd say.../I suppose..." vs. "I feel
like...", Module 4).

## Revision Lesson — Structured Consolidation Activities

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1)
correctly select the right grammar structure from Modules 1-9 given a
real-use context; (2) recall and use at least 60 headwords from across
the level's vocabulary sets; (3) correctly identify at least 6
BrE/AmE vocabulary pairs from across the level; (4) self-identify at
least one personal area needing further revision before the mock exam.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** All of Modules 1-9.

**Warm-up (5 min).** "Grammar auction" — learners bid points on
whether 6 example sentences (each drawn from a different module) are
grammatically correct or incorrect, surfacing residual errors playfully
before the graded exam.

**Presentation/consolidation (15 min).** Instructor leads a
structure-selection drill: a series of real-life prompts, each
requiring a different module's grammar to answer correctly ("Tell me
about a memorable event from your past" → Module 9; "What are your
plans for next weekend?" → Module 5; "What do you think about working
from home?" → Module 4) — the point is choosing the right tool for the
task, the core skill Module 10 assesses.

**Guided practice (15 min).** Learners rotate through 4 stations (in
pairs or small groups), each station reviewing 2-3 modules' target
language through a quick game or short speaking prompt — Life
Stories + Travel/Transport; Work/Study + Opinions; Making Plans +
Homes/Neighbourhoods; Food/Health + Shopping/Services, with a final
"Tell a quick story" cumulative station drawing on Module 9.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners complete a self-assessment
checklist (one line per module: "I can... ✓/needs practice") and
circle their two weakest areas.

**Speaking activity.** The structure-selection drill and station
rotation above are both fundamentally speaking-driven.

**Critical thinking / discussion prompt.** "Across this whole level,
which topic did you find easiest to talk about, and which did you find
hardest? What made the difference — the grammar, the vocabulary, or
the topic itself?" — a genuinely reflective, level-spanning question
that helps learners diagnose their own learning process, not just
their grammar accuracy.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to a single extended monologue
(someone narrating a memorable past event, describing their job,
giving an opinion on a topic, and mentioning weekend plans) — a
genuinely cumulative listening task mirroring the mock exam's
listening component.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read a similarly cumulative short text
and answer mixed comprehension questions spanning several grammar
points and at least one BrE/AmE vocabulary item.

**Writing task (5 min).** Write one paragraph (6+ sentences) that uses
at least 4 different grammar points from across the level.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Rapid-fire review drill of the
level's key pronunciation points: weak-form `was/were`, sentence
stress on frequency adverbs, question intonation sequences, storytelling
intonation.

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** A cumulative vocabulary relay game
covering all 9 modules' word sets, including a dedicated BrE/AmE
matching round.

**Formative assessment.** The self-assessment checklist above,
reviewed individually with the instructor if time allows, directly
informing what each learner should revise before the mock exam.

**Homework.** Learners revise their self-identified weak areas using
the module they struggled with most.

**Revision.** This entire lesson is revision by design.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners help peers at weaker
stations during the rotation activity — informal peer teaching.

## Elementary-Level Mock Exam — Grammar & Vocabulary (20 questions)

*Seeded verbatim into `quiz_questions` — see
`sql/seed-curriculum-level-2.sql`. One question drawn from each
module's core target language (Modules 1-9), plus two cumulative
BrE/AmE items, for 20 total. Answer key marked with ✓.*

1. (M1) "I ___ TV when the phone rang." — (a) watch (b) watched (c)
   was watching ✓ (d) am watching
2. (M1) "I ___ play football every weekend when I was a child." — (a)
   used to ✓ (b) use to (c) am used to (d) was using to
3. (M2) "This hotel is ___ than the last one we stayed in." — (a)
   comfortable (b) more comfortable ✓ (c) most comfortable (d) comfortabler
4. (M2) "This is the ___ flight of the three." — (a) cheaper (b)
   more cheap (c) cheapest ✓ (d) most cheap
5. (M3) "She ___ for a new job at the moment." — (a) looks (b) is
   looking ✓ (c) look (d) looked
6. (M3) "He ___ to the gym every Tuesday." (a routine) — (a) is going
   (b) go (c) goes ✓ (d) going
7. (M4) "I really enjoy ___ new languages." — (a) learn (b) learning
   ✓ (c) to learning (d) learns
8. (M4) "I'd ___ stay home than go out tonight." — (a) prefer (b)
   like (c) rather ✓ (d) want
9. (M5) "I ___ visit my parents this weekend." (already decided) — (a)
   will visit (b) am going to visit ✓ (c) visit (d) am visit
10. (M5) "I'm afraid I can't -- I already ___ plans." — (a) have ✓ (b)
    has (c) having (d) had
11. (M6) "There ___ a cinema here, but it closed." — (a) is (b) are
    (c) used to be ✓ (d) uses to be
12. (M6) "The shop is ___ walking distance of my flat." — (a) at (b)
    in (c) within ✓ (d) on
13. (M7) "I am ___ tired in the morning." (frequency + "to be") — (a)
    always ✓ (b) am always (c) always am (d) usual
14. (M7) "You ___ better see a doctor." — (a) had ✓ (b) have (c)
    should (d) would
15. (M8) "___ you buy this online?" — (a) Do (b) Did ✓ (c) Were (d)
    Have
16. (M8) "I'd like to make a ___." — (a) complain (b) complaint ✓ (c)
    complaining (d) complained
17. (M9) "I ___ waiting for the train when suddenly, I realised I'd
    forgotten my phone." — (a) was ✓ (b) am (c) were (d) is
18. (M9) Which word usually signals an unexpected interruption to a
    story? — (a) after that (b) next (c) suddenly ✓ (d) finally
19. (Cumulative BrE/AmE) In British English, "chips" usually means the
    same as the American word: — (a) crisps (b) fries ✓ (c) cookies
    (d) candy
20. (Cumulative BrE/AmE) In American English, the ___ floor is at
    street level. — (a) ground (b) first ✓ (c) second (d) lobby

## Elementary-Level Mock Exam — Speaking & Writing (comprehensive, rubric-graded)

**Instructions given to the learner:** This is your Level II final
assessment. Complete both parts.

**Part A — Speaking (3-4 minutes, recorded or live with your
instructor).** Give an extended talk covering: a memorable past event,
narrated with sequencing connectors and at least one past
simple/past continuous combination (Modules 1 and 9); a comparison
between two places you know, using comparatives or superlatives
(Module 2); your current job or studies compared with your general
routine (Module 3); your opinion on one topic of your choice, with a
reason (Module 4); one plan for the near future (Module 5); and a
piece of lifestyle advice you'd give a friend (Module 7). Respond to
at least one follow-up question from your instructor, and use at
least one BrE/AmE vocabulary item correctly and appropriately for the
variety you are using.

**Part B — Writing (12-15 sentences).** Write a personal email to a
friend covering: a description of your neighbourhood, then and now,
using `there was/were` and `there is/are` (Module 6); a short
complaint-and-resolution story about a shopping or service problem,
using past simple questions and polite complaint language (Module 8);
an invitation to meet up, including a specific day, time, and place
(Module 5); and a closing paragraph reflecting on what you're most
looking forward to.

**Grading rubric (for the instructor), weighted toward listening and
speaking per the Elementary-level assessment strategy:**
- **Grammatical range and accuracy** — correct, varied use of the
  level's grammar points across both parts, not just one or two
  structures repeated.
- **Vocabulary range** — words drawn from at least 6 of the 9 modules
  across both parts combined, including at least one correctly used
  BrE/AmE item.
- **Task completion** — every required element present in both Part A
  and Part B.
- **Fluency and delivery (Part A)** — reasonably fluent for A2,
  audible, able to respond to an unscripted follow-up question, able
  to sustain a longer turn than Level I's exam required.
- **Coherence (Part B)** — the email reads as one connected personal
  message with clear paragraph-level organisation, not disconnected
  sentences.
- **Communicative quality** — is the content genuinely specific and
  personal (not generic template language), consistent with the
  "communicative quality" criterion introduced across Level II's
  module assignments.

**Progression requirement.** A grade at or above the platform's pass
threshold on this comprehensive assessment marks Level II as complete
for the learner and — per Executive Decision #1's progressive
full-programme unlock — is the human (staff) judgement that, for a
full-programme student, triggers Level III's enrolment to unlock
automatically via `functions/_lib/student/progression.js`'s
`completeLevel()`.
