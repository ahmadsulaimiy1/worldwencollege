# WEC-LC — Level II, Module 2: Travel & Transport

*Part of `docs/curriculum-level-2-elementary.md`'s Level II build-out.*

**Module learning objectives.** By the end of Module 2, the learner
can: compare two or more things using comparative and superlative
adjectives; discuss and justify a travel choice; use 12+ travel/
transport vocabulary items; understand and produce simple booking-
related language; recognise key BrE/AmE travel-vocabulary differences.

**Content map (5 learning items):** module overview & key phrases;
Lesson 2.1 ("Which Is Better? — Comparatives & Superlatives"); Lesson
2.2 ("Booking a Trip — Travel & Transport Vocabulary"); Module 2 Quiz
(10 questions); Module 2 Assignment ("Plan and Justify a Trip").

---

## Reference reading: Module overview & key phrases

**Key phrases introduced this module:** ___ is faster/cheaper/more
comfortable than ___. — Which is the best way to get to...? — I'd
prefer to... because... — How long does it take? — I'd like to book
a...

**BrE / AmE note:** several everyday travel words differ: **holiday**
(BrE) / **vacation** (AmE); **coach** (BrE, a long-distance bus) /
**motorcoach** or simply **bus** (AmE); **return ticket** (BrE) /
**round-trip ticket** (AmE); **petrol** (BrE) / **gas** (AmE, short for
gasoline). WEC-LC materials use British terms as the house style;
learners should recognise both, since authentic contemporary content
(articles, apps, films) mixes them constantly.

**Key vocabulary previewed:** transport modes (plane, train, coach/
bus, ferry, underground/subway), travel actions (book, check in,
board, depart, arrive, transfer), comparison adjectives (fast/faster/
fastest, expensive/more expensive/most expensive, good/better/best).

## Lesson 2.1 — "Which Is Better? — Comparatives & Superlatives"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) form
regular comparatives (`-er`/`more`) and superlatives (`-est`/`most`)
correctly, including common irregulars (good/better/best, bad/worse/
worst); (2) use `than` correctly in comparisons; (3) compare three or
more options and identify "the most/-est"; (4) justify a comparative
opinion with a reason (`because`).

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Level I, Module 6 (basic adjectives).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor holds up or shows two contrasting
transport-related pictures (a bicycle, a plane) and elicits informal
comparisons from learners in their own words before formal
presentation.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model: "The train is faster than the
coach. The plane is the fastest, but it's also the most expensive."
Highlight the rule split: short adjectives (1 syllable, or 2 ending in
`-y`) take `-er/-est` (fast→faster→fastest, easy→easier→easiest);
longer adjectives take `more/most` (expensive→more expensive→most
expensive); irregulars (good/bad/far) are memorised exceptions.
Introduce `because` explicitly as the tool for justifying a
comparison — a genuinely new, useful connector at this level.

**Guided practice (10 min).** Pair work: learners compare 3 transport
options on a prompt card (speed, cost, comfort) using the correct
comparative/superlative form for each, then state which is "the best"
overall and why.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners rank 4 real or realistic
travel options for a specific trip (e.g. "getting from your home to
the city centre") from best to worst, writing 3 comparative sentences
and one superlative conclusion.

**Speaking activity.** Learners present their ranking to a partner,
who must ask at least one follow-up "Why?" question — direct practice
of justifying an opinion, not just stating one.

**Critical thinking / discussion prompt.** "Is the fastest way to
travel always the best way? What else matters besides speed?" —
small-group discussion encouraging learners to weigh multiple factors
(cost, comfort, environmental impact, experience) rather than a single
dimension.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to two people comparing travel
options (5-6 exchanges) and note which option they eventually choose
and why.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read a short comparison text (e.g. "Bus
vs. Train: Which Should You Choose?" style) and answer 3 comprehension
questions.

**Writing task (5 min).** Write 4 sentences comparing two forms of
transport you know well, including at least one superlative.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill the `-er`/`-est` suffix
pronunciation and word stress shift in longer comparative phrases
("more comFORTable" — stress stays on the adjective, not "more").

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** A comparative-adjective transformation
drill (call the base form, learners produce comparative and
superlative) covering both regular and irregular forms.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks correct `-er/-est` vs.
`more/most` selection (based on syllable count) during independent
practice — the lesson's core target error.

**Homework.** Learners research (or recall) 3 real facts comparing two
cities or places they know, ready to share as comparative sentences
next lesson.

**Revision.** Lesson 2.2 opens with 2-3 learners sharing their
homework comparisons.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners add `a bit`, `much`, or `far`
to intensify a comparison ("The plane is far more expensive than the
coach").

## Lesson 2.2 — "Booking a Trip — Travel & Transport Vocabulary"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) use
12+ travel and transport vocabulary items correctly; (2) understand
and produce simple booking language (`I'd like to book...`); (3) ask
and answer `How long does it take?`/`How much is it?` in a travel
context; (4) understand a simple travel itinerary or timetable.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Lesson 2.1 (comparatives), Level I, Module
4 (prices, `how much`).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor shows a simple travel itinerary or
ticket (real or a realistic mock-up) and elicits what information it
contains before formal vocabulary presentation.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model a booking dialogue:
> A: I'd like to book a return ticket to Manchester, please.
> B: Certainly. Would you prefer the morning or afternoon train?
> A: The morning one. How long does it take?
> B: About two hours.
Introduce 10-12 travel-process verbs and nouns (book, check in, board,
depart, arrive, transfer, platform, gate, timetable, itinerary) with
picture/realia support.

**Guided practice (10 min).** Pair work: Learner A is a booking agent,
Learner B is a customer; using a simple prompt card (destination,
time preference), they perform a short booking exchange, then swap
roles.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners read a simple travel
itinerary (2-3 legs, with times and transport modes) and answer 4
comprehension questions about it, then write their own short 2-leg
itinerary for an invented trip.

**Speaking activity.** The booking-exchange roleplay above — this
lesson's core speaking task, and the foundation of Module 2's
assignment.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to a short airport/station
announcement (a realistic, generic style, not naming any real
carrier) and extract 3 key pieces of information (gate/platform,
time, destination).

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read the sample itinerary again plus one
more, comparing the two for a simple task ("Which trip has more
transfers?").

**Writing task (5 min).** Write a short message to a friend describing
your travel plans using at least 4 target vocabulary items and one
comparative from Lesson 2.1.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill the BrE/AmE pronunciation
difference in "schedule" (BrE /ˈʃedjuːl/, AmE /ˈskedʒuːl/) as a
listening-recognition point (both are correct; recognising the
difference matters more than choosing one for production at this
level).

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** A travel-vocabulary bingo or matching
game using the 10-12 words from this lesson.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor observes the booking roleplay for
correct use of booking language and appropriate politeness.

**Homework.** Learners research (or invent, realistically) a real trip
they'd like to take, noting transport options, approximate cost, and
travel time, ready for Module 2's assignment.

**Revision.** This lesson opens with the itinerary-reading vocabulary
recap. Module 2's Quiz and Assignment draw on both lessons.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners add a "what could go wrong"
sentence to their itinerary (a delay, a missed connection) using
`might` as a light preview (formally taught at Level III).

## Module 2 Quiz (auto-graded, 10 questions)

*Seeded verbatim into `quiz_questions` — see
`sql/seed-curriculum-level-2.sql`. Answer key marked with ✓.*

1. "The train is ___ than the bus." (fast) — (a) fastest (b) faster ✓
   (c) more fast (d) most fast
2. "This is ___ hotel in the city." (expensive) — (a) more expensive
   (b) expensiver (c) the most expensive ✓ (d) the expensivest
3. "Flying is ___ than taking the train, but it's much faster." (bad)
   — (a) worse ✓ (b) more bad (c) badder (d) worst
4. "This is ___ way to travel." (good) — (a) the goodest (b) the best
   ✓ (c) the better (d) more good
5. "I'd ___ to book a ticket, please." — (a) like ✓ (b) liking (c)
   likes (d) liked
6. "How ___ does the journey take?" — (a) much (b) long ✓ (c) many (d)
   far
7. Which British word means the same as the American "vacation"? —
   (a) trip (b) journey (c) holiday ✓ (d) travel
8. "The train ___ from Platform 4 at 9am." — (a) departs ✓ (b)
   arrives (c) boards (d) transfers
9. "The bus is cheaper, ___ it takes much longer." — (a) so (b) but ✓
   (c) because (d) and
10. Which is a British term for a long-distance bus? — (a) subway (b)
    coach ✓ (c) trolley (d) shuttle

## Module 2 Assignment — "Plan and Justify a Trip"

**Instructions given to the learner:** Record yourself (or write) a
description of a real or realistic trip you'd like to take. Compare
at least two transport options using comparatives (e.g. faster,
cheaper, more comfortable) and state which one you'd choose and why,
using `because`. Include at least one piece of booking language
(`I'd like to book...`) and at least 3 travel/transport vocabulary
items from this module.

**Grading rubric (for the instructor):**
- **Grammatical accuracy** — correct comparative/superlative forms,
  correct `because` usage.
- **Vocabulary range** — at least 3 distinct travel/transport words
  used correctly.
- **Task completion** — a clear comparison, a stated choice, a reason,
  and booking language all present.
- **Communicative quality** — does the learner genuinely justify their
  choice with a real reason (not just "because it's better"), showing
  actual comparative reasoning, not memorised phrases alone?

A grade at or above the platform's pass threshold marks Module 2
completed for the learner.
