# WEC — Level II, Module 6: Homes & Neighbourhoods

*Part of `docs/curriculum-level-2-elementary.md`'s Level II build-out.*

**Module learning objectives.** By the end of Module 6, the learner
can: describe a past home or neighbourhood using `there was/were`;
compare where they used to live with where they live now; use
prepositions of place with more precision than Level I's introductory
treatment; describe advantages and disadvantages of a place to live.

**Content map (5 learning items):** module overview & key phrases;
Lesson 6.1 ("There Was a Park Near My House — Past Existence &
Description"); Lesson 6.2 ("City or Countryside? — Comparing Places to
Live"); Module 6 Quiz (10 questions); Module 6 Assignment ("My
Neighbourhood, Then and Now").

---

## Reference reading: Module overview & key phrases

**Key phrases introduced this module:** There was/were... — It used
to be.../Now it's... — One advantage/disadvantage of... is... — It's
within walking distance of... — The best thing about my neighbourhood
is...

**BrE / AmE note:** housing vocabulary differs more than almost any
other everyday topic: **flat** (BrE) / **apartment** (AmE); **ground
floor** (BrE, the floor at street level) / **first floor** (AmE, the
same floor — Britain's "first floor" is one level up, America's is at
street level, a genuinely confusing false-friend pair worth explicit
attention); **garden** (BrE, any private outdoor space at a house) /
**yard** (AmE); **block of flats** (BrE) / **apartment building**
(AmE).

**Key vocabulary previewed:** housing types (flat/apartment, house,
terraced house, detached house, block of flats), neighbourhood
features (park, playground, shops, public transport links, green
space), description adjectives (quiet, lively, convenient, safe,
crowded).

## Lesson 6.1 — "There Was a Park Near My House — Past Existence & Description"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) use
`there was/there were` correctly for singular/plural past existence;
(2) describe a past home or neighbourhood in reasonable detail; (3)
contrast `there was/were` (past) with `there is/are` (present, from
Level I) explicitly; (4) use `used to be` as an alternative way to
describe a past state (recycling Module 1's `used to`).

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Level I, Module 2 (`there is/are`); Level
II, Module 1 (`used to`).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor describes their own childhood street
from memory ("There was a small shop on the corner. There were lots
of trees.") before formal presentation, modelling naturally.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model: "When I was a child, there was a
park near my house. There were always children playing there. It used
to be very quiet." Highlight the direct past-tense mapping: `there
is` → `there was`; `there are` → `there were` — the same
singular/plural agreement rule from Level I, now in the past.

**Guided practice (10 min).** Pair work: learners are given a "then"
picture of an old neighbourhood and describe it using `there was/
were`, then compare with a "now" picture of the same place using
`there is/are`.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners describe a real
neighbourhood from their past (childhood home, a previous city) using
at least 4 `there was/were` sentences, then share with a partner who
asks 2 follow-up questions.

**Speaking activity.** The partner description-and-follow-up exchange
above.

**Critical thinking / discussion prompt.** "What is one thing about
the place you grew up that you miss, and one thing you don't miss?" —
a genuinely reflective personal question that naturally elicits both
positive and negative past-tense description.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to someone describing their
childhood neighbourhood (6 sentences) and complete a simple "then"
picture-labelling worksheet based on what's mentioned.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read a short "How My Neighbourhood Has
Changed" text and answer 3 comprehension questions distinguishing past
from present.

**Writing task (5 min).** Write 5-6 sentences describing a place from
your past using `there was/were` and `used to be`.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill the weak-form pronunciation
of `was/were` in `there was/there were` (echoing Module 1's
weak-form work on `was/were`, now applied to a new structure).

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** A housing/neighbourhood-features
picture-matching game.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks correct `was/were`
singular/plural agreement — the same target error pattern as Level I's
`there is/are`, now recurring in the past tense.

**Homework.** Learners note 3 things that used to be true about their
home/neighbourhood and 3 things that are true now, ready for Lesson
6.2.

**Revision.** Lesson 6.2 opens with 2-3 learners sharing their
homework then/now contrasts.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners add a reason for one change
("There used to be a cinema, but it closed because a new shopping
centre opened").

## Lesson 6.2 — "City or Countryside? — Comparing Places to Live"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1)
describe advantages and disadvantages of a place using structured
language; (2) use precise prepositions of place to describe location
relative to amenities; (3) compare two places to live using
comparatives (recycling Module 2's target grammar); (4) give a
balanced opinion weighing more than one factor.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Lesson 6.1 (housing/neighbourhood
vocabulary), Level II Module 2 (comparatives), Level I Module 5
(prepositions of place, extended here with more precision).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor shows two contrasting pictures (a busy
city street, a quiet village) and elicits one word to describe each
before formal presentation.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model: "One advantage of living in the city
is that it's convenient — everything is within walking distance. One
disadvantage is that it can be crowded and noisy. The countryside is
quieter, but it's further from public transport." Highlight the
advantage/disadvantage frame as a structured way to give a balanced
opinion (recycling Module 4's opinion-giving skills in a new context).

**Guided practice (10 min).** Pair work: learners are given a
"city vs. countryside" chart and complete it with one advantage and
one disadvantage of each, using target vocabulary and comparatives.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners choose which they'd
personally prefer — city or countryside living — and prepare a short
justified opinion (recycling Module 4's `I think.../because`) weighing
at least two factors, then discuss in a small group.

**Speaking activity.** The small-group discussion above — this
lesson's core task, and the direct basis for Module 6's assignment.

**Critical thinking / discussion prompt.** "Is the 'best' place to
live the same for everyone, or does it depend on the person? What
factors matter most to you personally?" — explicitly building toward
genuine communicative competence: weighing multiple factors and
personalising an answer, not reciting a memorised opinion.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to two people discussing where
to live and note each person's stated advantages/disadvantages and
final preference.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read a short comparison article ("City
Living vs. Country Living") and identify the main advantage and
disadvantage given for each.

**Writing task (5 min).** Write a short paragraph (5-6 sentences)
comparing city and countryside living and stating your own preference
with a reason.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill sentence stress in
advantage/disadvantage structures ("One ADvantage is..."; "One
disADvantage is...") — correct stress placement on these specific
compound-feeling words.

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** An advantage/disadvantage card-sorting
game using 10 city/countryside feature cards.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks that opinions are
genuinely balanced (both an advantage and a disadvantage considered)
rather than one-sided, during group discussion.

**Homework.** Learners finalise their neighbourhood description
(combining Lesson 6.1's past description with this lesson's present
comparison) for Module 6's assignment.

**Revision.** This lesson opens with the Lesson 6.1 then/now recap.
Module 6's Quiz and Assignment draw on both lessons.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners add a third option to their
comparison (a suburban town) and rank all three by personal
preference.

## Module 6 Quiz (auto-graded, 10 questions)

*Seeded verbatim into `quiz_questions` — see
`sql/seed-curriculum-level-2.sql`. Answer key marked with ✓.*

1. "There ___ a park near my house when I was young." — (a) is (b)
   was ✓ (c) were (d) be
2. "There ___ lots of trees on my street." — (a) was (b) were ✓ (c)
   is (d) has
3. "It ___ be very quiet here, but now it's busy." — (a) is used to
   (b) used to ✓ (c) use to (d) using to
4. In British English, a "flat" is the same as which American word? —
   (a) house (b) apartment ✓ (c) yard (d) block
5. "One ___ of city life is convenient transport." — (a) advantage ✓
   (b) advantaged (c) advantages (d) advantageous
6. "The shop is ___ walking distance of my flat." — (a) at (b) in (c)
   within ✓ (d) on
7. "The countryside is quieter, ___ it's further from the city." —
   (a) so (b) but ✓ (c) because (d) also
8. Which British term means the same as the American "yard" (private
   outdoor space at a house)? — (a) park (b) garden ✓ (c) court (d)
   green
9. "There ___ a cinema here, but it closed." — (a) is (b) are (c)
   used to be ✓ (d) uses to be
10. "In American English, the ___ floor is at street level." — (a)
    ground (b) first ✓ (c) second (d) lobby

## Module 6 Assignment — "My Neighbourhood, Then and Now"

**Instructions given to the learner:** Write (or record) 8-10
sentences describing a neighbourhood you know well. Include: at least
2 sentences using `there was/were` or `used to be` to describe how it
was in the past; at least 2 sentences using `there is/are` to describe
it now; one advantage and one disadvantage of living there, using the
target structure; and a comparative sentence comparing it to another
place you know.

**Grading rubric (for the instructor):**
- **Grammatical accuracy** — correct past/present `there is/are/was/
  were` agreement, correct comparative form.
- **Vocabulary range** — at least 4 distinct housing/neighbourhood
  words used correctly.
- **Task completion** — past description, present description,
  advantage, disadvantage, and comparison all present.
- **Communicative quality** — does the description genuinely convey
  what changed and why it matters to the writer, and is the
  advantage/disadvantage balanced rather than one-sided?

A grade at or above the platform's pass threshold marks Module 6
completed for the learner.
