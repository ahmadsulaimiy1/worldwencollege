# WEC-LC — Level III, Module 2: Education & Learning

*Part of `docs/curriculum-level-3-intermediate.md`'s Level III build-out.*

**Module learning objectives.** By the end of Module 2, the learner
can: describe an ongoing course of study using present perfect
continuous; define or specify a course, subject, or person using a
defining relative clause; take structured notes from a short spoken
talk; summarise a short text or talk in your own words; discuss study
habits and learning preferences with reasons.

**Content map (5 learning items):** module overview & key phrases;
Lesson 2.1 ("I've Been Studying... — Present Perfect Continuous &
Defining Relative Clauses"); Lesson 2.2 ("Taking Notes — Note-Taking,
Summarising & Paraphrasing"); Module 2 Quiz (10 questions); Module 2
Assignment ("My Learning Journey — A Short Reflective Report").

## Reference reading: Module overview & key phrases

**Key phrases introduced this module:** I've been studying... for/
since... — a course that.../a subject which... — What I find most
useful is... — In other words,.../To put it another way,... — According
to the talk/text,...

**Discourse markers this module (functional set — rephrasing and
attributing):** *in other words*, *to put it another way* (signal a
paraphrase — restating an idea in your own words); *according to*
(attribute an idea to its source — the beginning of the citation
awareness fully developed in Module 9).

**Phrasal verbs & collocations this module:** *keep up with
[coursework]* (stay on schedule with ongoing work), *fall behind*
(the opposite — stop keeping pace), *take in [information]* (absorb/
understand something you read or hear), *make notes/take notes* (a
genuinely useful near-synonym pair — both correct, "take notes" the
more common collocation), *hands-on learning* (learning through direct
practice, not just theory).

**BrE / AmE note:** higher-education vocabulary differs noticeably:
British **university** and American **college** are both used for
higher education generally, but American English also uses "college"
specifically for a 4-year undergraduate institution (a British
"college" more often means a secondary or further-education
institution, or a division within a university — a genuine
false-friend worth flagging); British students **do a degree** or
**read a subject** ("she read law at university" — a distinctly
British usage), while American students **major in a subject**;
British **marks** vs. American **grades** for assessment results.

**Key vocabulary previewed:** academic vocabulary I (lecture, seminar,
assignment, deadline, coursework, tutor/professor, plagiarism,
independent study), study-habit vocabulary (procrastinate, revise,
cram, prioritise). Intercultural note: educational systems and study
expectations vary significantly by country — this module's speaking
tasks deliberately invite comparison, not a single "correct" model of
what studying should look like.

## Lesson 2.1 — "I've Been Studying... — Present Perfect Continuous & Defining Relative Clauses"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) form
present perfect continuous correctly (`have/has been + -ing`); (2) use
it to describe an activity that started in the past and continues now,
with `for`/`since`; (3) form a defining relative clause with `who/
which/that` to specify exactly which person, course, or thing you
mean; (4) combine both structures to describe your own study
situation precisely.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Level III, Module 1 (present perfect
simple); Level I, Module 3 (present simple for routine, the
"non-continuing" contrast point).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor states how long they've been teaching
("I've been teaching English for [several] years") and elicits a
guess from learners on the structure's meaning before formal
presentation.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model: "I've been studying English since
2022. I've been taking a course that focuses on business
communication." Highlight two things at once: present perfect
continuous emphasises the *ongoing, continuing* nature of an activity
(contrast explicitly with Module 1's present perfect simple, which
reports an experience without emphasising duration); and the defining
relative clause ("a course *that* focuses on...") specifies exactly
which course, with no comma — removing it would leave the sentence
incomplete or unclear, the test for "defining."

**Guided practice (10 min).** Learners combine 8 sentence pairs into
one sentence using a defining relative clause ("I'm taking a course.
The course focuses on marketing." → "I'm taking a course that focuses
on marketing."), then add a present perfect continuous sentence about
their own real study history.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners write 4-5 sentences
describing their own English-learning journey so far, using present
perfect continuous for duration and at least 2 defining relative
clauses to specify particular courses, teachers, or resources, then
compare with a partner.

**Speaking activity.** The partner comparison above, followed by a
short "who has been studying longest?" whole-class informal poll — a
light, low-pressure use of the target structure across the room.

**Critical thinking / discussion prompt.** "Do you think the amount of
time someone has spent studying something always matches how good
they are at it? Why or why not?" — a genuinely debatable B1 question
that questions the very structure just practised, a deliberately
critical framing appropriate to this level's "analyse information
critically" objective.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to a student describing their
course of study (using present perfect continuous and at least 2
defining relative clauses) and complete a simple fact-grid worksheet.

**Reading activity — extended reading & inference (8 min).** Read a
short university-life text (150 words) about a student's study
routine. Answer 2 literal questions and 2 inference questions ("The
text doesn't say the student is stressed, but what evidence suggests
they might be?").

**Writing task (5 min).** Write 4-5 sentences describing something
you've been doing for a period of time (a hobby, a habit, a course),
including at least one defining relative clause.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill the connected, weak-form
pronunciation of `been` /bɪn/ in fast speech ("I've been STUDying")
versus its stressed form in short answers ("Yes, I HAVE been"); drill
`that`/`which`/`who` as unstressed function words within a defining
relative clause, so the clause flows naturally rather than sounding
like three separate chunks.

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** An academic-vocabulary matching game
(lecture, seminar, assignment, deadline, coursework, tutor,
plagiarism, independent study) paired with simple definitions.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks correct present perfect
continuous formation with `for`/`since`, and correct (no comma,
essential) defining relative clause formation, during independent
practice.

**Homework.** Learners note 3 real facts about their own learning
history (what, how long, using which course/resource) ready for
Lesson 2.2's note-taking and summarising practice.

**Revision.** Lesson 2.2 opens with a brief recap using the homework
facts as note-taking material.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners add a non-defining relative
clause example for contrast (recognition only, formally taught at a
later level) — e.g. "My English teacher, who has taught for ten
years, ..." — noting the comma changes the clause from essential to
"extra information."

## Lesson 2.2 — "Taking Notes — Note-Taking, Summarising & Paraphrasing"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) take
brief, structured notes from a short spoken talk, capturing main
points rather than every word; (2) summarise a short text or talk in
2-3 sentences, in your own words; (3) paraphrase a specific sentence
without changing its meaning; (4) use `in other words`/`to put it
another way` to signal you are rephrasing.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Lesson 2.1 (academic vocabulary,
listening for detail).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor reads a short paragraph aloud once,
at normal speed, and asks learners to write down only the 3 most
important words or ideas they caught — surfacing, before any technique
is taught, that trying to write everything down is neither possible
nor useful.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model a simple, practical note-taking
system: headings, short phrases (not full sentences), abbreviations
and symbols (&, w/, ->, e.g.), and numbers/bullets for structure.
Then model summarising a short paragraph aloud in one sentence, and
paraphrasing a specific sentence ("Many students find it difficult to
manage their time" → "In other words, time management is a common
challenge for students") — explicitly naming this as a distinct skill
from summarising (paraphrasing keeps roughly the same length and
detail, just different words; summarising deliberately shortens).

**Guided practice (10 min).** Learners listen to a short talk (played
twice) and take notes using the modelled system, then compare notes
with a partner and discuss what each of them chose to include or
leave out, and why.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Using their notes, learners write a
2-3 sentence summary of the talk, then paraphrase one specific sentence
from a short written excerpt of the same topic, using `in other
words`/`to put it another way`.

**Speaking activity.** Learners take turns explaining their summary
to a partner from their notes alone (not reading a pre-written
summary aloud) — genuine, spontaneous summarising under light time
pressure, the real-world skill this lesson targets.

**Critical thinking / discussion prompt.** "When you take notes, how
do you decide what's important enough to write down and what isn't?
Is this a skill you think can be taught, or does it just come with
practice?" — a genuinely metacognitive B1 question about the learner's
own thinking process, appropriate to this level's growing academic
self-awareness.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to a second, different short
talk and take notes independently (no modelling this time), then
self-check against 3 key points the instructor names afterward.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read a short academic-style paragraph
and write one paraphrase sentence and one one-sentence summary of it,
comparing the two side by side to see the difference in practice.

**Writing task (5 min).** Using your Lesson 2.1 homework facts, write
a short paragraph about your own learning journey that includes one
paraphrased idea (restating something you noted earlier in different
words) using `in other words`.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill natural stress and pacing
for summarising aloud — slightly slower, more clearly stressed key
words than casual conversation, appropriate to explaining something
someone else hasn't heard yet.

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** A note-taking abbreviation/symbol
matching game (&, w/, ->, e.g., i.e., etc.) tied directly to this
lesson's practical technique.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks that summaries are
genuinely shorter and in the learner's own words (not copied phrases)
and that paraphrases preserve meaning, during independent practice.

**Homework.** Learners draft their reflective learning-journey
paragraph (from the writing task) into a fuller version for Module 2's
assignment.

**Revision.** This lesson opens with the Lesson 2.1 fact recap used as
note-taking material. Module 2's Quiz and Assignment draw on both
lessons.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners summarise the same talk in
exactly one sentence (a stricter constraint), forcing a sharper
judgement about what the single most essential point actually is.

## Module 2 Quiz (auto-graded, 10 questions)

*Seeded verbatim into `quiz_questions` — see
`sql/seed-curriculum-level-3.sql`. Answer key marked with ✓.*

1. "I've ___ studying English since 2022." — (a) been ✓ (b) be (c)
   was (d) being
2. "I'm taking a course ___ focuses on marketing." — (a) who (b) which
   ✓ (c) where (d) when
3. Which sentence uses a correct defining relative clause? — (a) I'm
   taking a course, that focuses on marketing. (b) I'm taking a course
   that focuses on marketing. ✓ (c) I'm taking a course which it
   focuses on marketing. (d) I'm taking a course focuses on marketing.
4. "___, time management is a common challenge for students." (a
   paraphrase signal) — (a) According to (b) In other words ✓ (c) As a
   matter of fact (d) Which is why
5. What is the main difference between summarising and paraphrasing? —
   (a) They are exactly the same thing. (b) Summarising shortens;
   paraphrasing keeps similar length but different words. ✓ (c)
   Paraphrasing always uses the exact same words. (d) Summarising is
   only for spoken language.
6. "I've been ___ this book for two weeks." — (a) read (b) reading ✓
   (c) reads (d) to read
7. In British English, a student who studies law at university would
   say: — (a) I major in law. (b) I read law. ✓ (c) I take law. (d) I
   grade in law.
8. Which word means "stay on schedule with ongoing coursework"? — (a)
   fall behind (b) keep up with ✓ (c) take in (d) cram
9. "The teacher ___ I mentioned earlier is running the seminar." (a
   person, defining clause) — (a) which (b) who ✓ (c) whose (d) when
10. Good note-taking generally means: — (a) writing down every word
    the speaker says (b) writing only headings and short phrases
    capturing main points ✓ (c) memorising instead of writing (d)
    copying the speaker's exact sentences

## Module 2 Assignment — "My Learning Journey — A Short Reflective Report"

**Instructions given to the learner:** Write (or record) a short
reflective report, 10-12 sentences, about your own experience learning
English (or another subject of your choice). Include: at least one
present perfect continuous sentence describing how long you've been
studying; at least two defining relative clauses specifying particular
courses, resources, or people; a short summary (2-3 sentences) of what
you find most useful about how you study; and at least one paraphrase
using `in other words` or `to put it another way`.

**Grading rubric (for the instructor):**
- **Grammatical accuracy** — correct present perfect continuous
  formation, correct defining relative clause formation.
- **Vocabulary range** — at least 4 distinct academic/study-habit
  words used correctly, plus one phrasal verb/collocation from this
  module.
- **Task completion** — duration statement, defining relative clauses,
  a genuine summary, and a paraphrase all present.
- **Communicative quality** — is the reflection genuinely specific
  about the writer's own real learning process, not generic?
- **Discourse coherence & register** — does the report read as one
  connected reflective piece with an appropriate semi-formal academic
  register, and is the summary genuinely condensed rather than just
  copied from earlier sentences?

A grade at or above the platform's pass threshold marks Module 2
completed for the learner.
