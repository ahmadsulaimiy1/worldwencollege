# WEC — Level III, Module 5: Environment, Ethics & Global Citizenship

*Part of `docs/curriculum-level-3-intermediate.md`'s Level III build-out.*

**Module learning objectives.** By the end of Module 5, the learner
can: describe general processes and facts about environmental and
global issues using the passive voice (present simple); report past
events using the passive voice (past simple), correctly choosing when
to de-emphasise or omit the agent; explain cause and effect using a
more formal register; weigh the different sides of an ethical dilemma
in a structured case-study discussion.

**Content map (5 learning items):** module overview & key phrases;
Lesson 5.1 ("Plastic Is Recycled... — Passive Voice, Present Simple");
Lesson 5.2 ("The Area Was Affected... — Passive Voice, Past Simple &
an Ethical Case Study"); Module 5 Quiz (10 questions); Module 5
Assignment ("A Global Issue — Report & Reflection").

## Reference reading: Module overview & key phrases

**Key phrases introduced this module:** [Something] is done/is made/
is used... — [Something] was affected/was caused/was reduced... — As a
consequence,.../This has led to... — Due to.../Owing to... — On the
one hand..., on the other hand...

**Discourse markers this module (functional set — formal cause and
consequence):** *as a consequence*, *this has led to*, *due to*, *owing
to* — a more formal register than Module 3's `as a result`, suited to
reporting on issues rather than describing personal or workplace
situations ("Deforestation has increased. This has led to significant
biodiversity loss.").

**Phrasal verbs & collocations this module:** *carry out [a study/
research]* (conduct it), *cut down [trees]* (a specific environmental
sense of "cut down," distinct from the general meaning "reduce"),
*give rise to [a problem]* (cause something to begin or develop),
*take action (on)* (do something in response to an issue), *phase out
[something]* (gradually stop using something over time).

**BrE / AmE note:** two further everyday vocabulary differences,
genuinely relevant to environment/transport topics: British
**rubbish**/**bin** vs. American **trash/garbage**/**trash can**; and
British **petrol** vs. American **gas/gasoline** for vehicle fuel — a
pair that can cause real confusion, since British "gas" usually means
a cooking/heating gas, not vehicle fuel.

**Key vocabulary previewed:** environmental vocabulary (emissions,
sustainability, renewable, biodiversity, deforestation, pollution),
ethics/global-citizenship vocabulary (dilemma, trade-off, responsible,
stakeholder, impact). Intercultural note: environmental priorities and
what counts as an urgent issue can differ significantly by country and
circumstance (a coastal nation and a landlocked one may reasonably
prioritise differently); this module's discussions deliberately invite
comparison of perspectives, not a single "correct" global priority
list.

## Lesson 5.1 — "Plastic Is Recycled... — Passive Voice, Present Simple"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) form
the present simple passive correctly (`is/are + past participle`); (2)
use it to describe general processes and facts where the action
matters more than who performs it; (3) choose actively vs. passively
depending on what should be emphasised; (4) use `as a consequence`/
`this has led to`/`due to` to explain cause and effect formally.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Level I, Module 3 (present simple); Level
II, Module 4 (opinion language, lightly recycled for the discussion
activity).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor shows two versions of the same
sentence — one active ("Factories produce a lot of waste"), one
passive ("A lot of waste is produced") — and asks learners what feels
different about each before formal presentation.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model: "Plastic is recycled in many
countries. Renewable energy is used more and more. Emissions are
monitored by environmental agencies." Highlight explicitly: the
passive voice shifts focus onto the action or the result, not the
doer — genuinely useful when the doer is unknown, unimportant, or
already obvious from context (a defining feature of report-style,
functional grammar, not just a grammar rule for its own sake). Model
converting one active sentence to passive as a class, discussing why
the passive version might be preferred in a report.

**Guided practice (10 min).** Learners convert 8 active sentences
about environmental processes into passive voice, discussing for each
one whether the passive genuinely improves the sentence (de-
emphasising an unknown/unimportant agent) or whether the active would
actually be clearer.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Learners write 5 present-simple
passive sentences describing a process or general fact related to the
environment or global systems (recycling, energy production, food
distribution — learner's choice), including at least one `as a
consequence`/`due to` sentence.

**Speaking activity.** Learners explain one of their passive-voice
process sentences to a partner, who asks "Why do you think the passive
is used here, rather than saying who does it?" — directly testing
whether learners understand the *function*, not just the form.

**Critical thinking / discussion prompt.** "Can you think of a global
issue where it matters a lot *who* is responsible for a problem, and
one where it matters less? What's the difference?" — connects the
grammar's function (emphasising action vs. agent) to a genuine
ethical/critical-thinking question about accountability.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to a short factual report (6-7
sentences, mostly passive voice) about an environmental process and
complete a simple process-sequence worksheet.

**Reading activity — extended reading & inference (8 min).** Read a
short factual article excerpt (150-180 words) about an environmental
or global issue, written largely in passive voice. Answer 2 literal
questions and 2 inference questions ("The text doesn't state this
directly, but what can you infer about who is most affected by this
issue?").

**Writing task (5 min).** Rewrite 4 active-voice sentences about an
environmental topic into passive voice, then explain in one sentence
why the passive suits a factual report better here.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill the natural stress pattern
of passive constructions ("Plastic is REcycled," stress on the past
participle, not the auxiliary `is`) and clear, level intonation
appropriate to reporting facts neutrally (as opposed to the more
varied intonation of personal opinion-giving from Module 4).

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** An environmental-vocabulary matching
game (emissions, sustainability, renewable, biodiversity,
deforestation, pollution) with simple definitions.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks correct passive formation
and, more importantly, a genuine understanding of *when* passive suits
the sentence better than active, during guided practice.

**Homework.** Learners choose one real global issue they're interested
in and jot down 3-4 facts about it (in any form, active or passive),
ready for Lesson 5.2's case-study discussion.

**Revision.** Lesson 5.2 opens with learners briefly naming their
chosen issue.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners add one present-continuous
passive sentence as a recognition-level preview ("Renewable energy is
being adopted more widely"), formally taught at a later level.

## Lesson 5.2 — "The Area Was Affected... — Passive Voice, Past Simple & an Ethical Case Study"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson you can: (1) form
the past simple passive correctly (`was/were + past participle`); (2)
use it to report a past event where the result matters more than the
agent; (3) present both sides of an ethical dilemma using `on the one
hand.../on the other hand...`; (4) participate in a structured
case-study discussion, weighing trade-offs rather than giving a single
simple answer.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Lesson 5.1 (present simple passive), Level
I Module 7 (past simple, the past passive's grammatical foundation).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor states one real (generic, non-
identifying) past-event sentence in active voice and asks learners to
convert it to passive, focusing attention on the past participle form
before formal presentation.

**Presentation (10 min).** Model: "The area was affected by flooding
last year. A large amount of habitat was destroyed. As a consequence,
several species were displaced." Then introduce the case-study
structure: a short, realistic (generic, not tied to a real named
place, company, or event) ethical dilemma is presented — for example,
a coastal town must choose between a new factory (jobs, but pollution
risk) and no factory (fewer jobs, but a cleaner environment). Model
weighing it explicitly: "On the one hand, the factory would create
jobs. On the other hand, it could harm the local environment."

**Guided practice (10 min).** Learners convert 6 active past-tense
sentences about an environmental event into passive voice, checking
past participle accuracy (including irregular forms).

**Independent practice (10 min).** In small groups, learners are given
one of 3 short ethical case-study scenarios (a resource trade-off, a
technology-vs-privacy dilemma, a development-vs-conservation choice)
and discuss it, each person contributing at least one `on the one
hand/on the other hand` weighing statement and one past-passive
sentence reporting a relevant past fact from the scenario.

**Speaking activity — presentation/discussion task.** Each group
briefly presents their case study and their range of views to the
class (1-2 minutes per group) — this lesson's core task, recycling
Module 3's presentation skills in a collaborative, case-study format,
and the direct basis for Module 5's assignment.

**Critical thinking / discussion prompt.** "Is it possible to make a
decision that's completely good for everyone, or does almost every
real decision involve some kind of trade-off? Can you think of an
example from your own life?" — a genuinely mature B1-level ethical
question, connecting the module's global-scale case studies to
learners' own everyday decision-making.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Listen to a short case-study
discussion (2-3 speakers weighing a dilemma) and note each speaker's
"on the one hand/on the other hand" points.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Read a short written case study and
identify the trade-off being described and at least one passive-voice
sentence reporting a past fact within it.

**Writing task (5 min).** Write a short paragraph (5-6 sentences)
presenting both sides of an ethical dilemma of your choice, using `on
the one hand/on the other hand` and at least one past-simple passive
sentence.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill the weak-form pronunciation
of `was/were` in past passive constructions ("The area was afFECTed")
recycling Level II's weak-form work, now applied to a new, more formal
structure.

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** An ethics/global-citizenship vocabulary
matching game (dilemma, trade-off, responsible, stakeholder, impact)
with simple definitions.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor checks correct past-passive
formation and a genuine, balanced weighing of both sides (not a
one-sided answer) during the case-study discussion.

**Homework.** Learners choose one global issue (their own or their
group's case study) and draft a short factual report paragraph for
Module 5's assignment.

**Revision.** This lesson opens with the Lesson 5.1 issue-naming
recap. Module 5's Quiz and Assignment draw on both lessons.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners propose one possible
compromise or partial solution to their case study's dilemma, going
beyond simply describing the trade-off.

## Module 5 Quiz (auto-graded, 10 questions)

*Seeded verbatim into `quiz_questions` — see
`sql/seed-curriculum-level-3.sql`. Answer key marked with ✓.*

1. "Plastic ___ in many countries." (present simple passive) — (a)
   recycles (b) is recycled ✓ (c) recycling (d) recycled
2. "Emissions ___ by environmental agencies." — (a) monitor (b) are
   monitored ✓ (c) monitoring (d) monitored by
3. "The area ___ by flooding last year." (past simple passive) — (a)
   affects (b) was affected ✓ (c) affected (d) is affected
4. "A large amount of habitat ___." — (a) destroyed (b) was destroyed
   ✓ (c) destroys (d) is destroying
5. "Deforestation has increased. ___, biodiversity loss has become a
   serious issue." — (a) Due to (b) This has led to ✓ (c) On the one
   hand (d) Which is why
6. In British English, rubbish is put in a: — (a) trash can (b) bin ✓
   (c) garbage can (d) waste zone
7. "___ hand, the factory would create jobs; on the other, it could
   harm the environment." — (a) In one (b) On the one ✓ (c) From one
   (d) At one
8. Which phrase means "gradually stop using something over time"? —
   (a) carry out (b) give rise to (c) phase out ✓ (d) cut down
9. Why might a writer choose the passive voice in a factual report? —
   (a) to make the sentence longer (b) to focus on the action/result
   rather than who did it ✓ (c) it is always required in reports (d)
   to avoid using verbs
10. "Several species ___ as a result of the flooding." — (a) displaced
    (b) were displaced ✓ (c) displace (d) are displacing

## Module 5 Assignment — "A Global Issue — Report & Reflection"

**Instructions given to the learner:** Write (or record) a short
factual report and reflection, 10-12 sentences, on one real global
issue you're interested in (environmental, ethical, or another
genuine global-citizenship topic). Include: at least 2 present-simple
passive sentences describing a general process or fact; at least 2
past-simple passive sentences reporting a specific past event; one
`as a consequence`/`due to`-style cause-effect sentence; and a short
"on the one hand/on the other hand" section weighing at least two
sides of the issue.

**Grading rubric (for the instructor):**
- **Grammatical accuracy** — correct present-simple and past-simple
  passive formation.
- **Vocabulary range** — at least 4 distinct environmental/ethics
  words used correctly, plus one phrasal verb/collocation from this
  module.
- **Task completion** — both passive tenses, a cause-effect sentence,
  and a genuinely balanced trade-off section all present.
- **Communicative quality** — is the issue described with genuine,
  specific detail (not vague generalities), and is the trade-off
  weighing genuinely balanced rather than one-sided?
- **Discourse coherence & register** — is the register appropriately
  formal and report-like throughout, and does the passive voice
  genuinely improve clarity where it's used (not overused where active
  voice would be more natural)?

A grade at or above the platform's pass threshold marks Module 5
completed for the learner.
