# Audit 03 — A Saudi family

**Who we are.** Riyadh. Two children we would enrol: a son of twenty-
four and a daughter of nineteen. English for both — his for work, hers
before a foreign degree. We can pay. What we are buying is not the price;
it is whether this is a place we are comfortable putting our daughter.

**What we checked.** The Arabic site first, because that is what we
read. Then the English site, because we suspected they would not say the
same things.

---

## What is right

**The Arabic edition is not a translation. It is written.**

We are used to Arabic pages produced by machine, with English word order
and terms nobody uses. This is not that. The register is right, the
numbers are isolated correctly so they do not break the line, and the
level names are given in Arabic — *مرحلة التأسيس*, *مرحلة الإتقان* —
rather than left in English for us to work out.

We noticed something the College may not have: **the Arabic navigation
was clearer than the English one.** The English menu used to give award
codes — ECIC, HCAEC — that mean nothing to a first reader. The Arabic
menu named the stage. Whoever wrote the Arabic understood the reader
better.

**Thirty-three Arabic pages against forty-six English ones**, and every
Arabic page has an English counterpart. Nothing important is
English-only on the public site. That is not a token Arabic page. That
is a second edition.

**The programme is online.** Our daughter studies at home. There is no
campus, no travel, no accommodation question, no mixed residence. For us
this removes the largest single obstacle to studying abroad, and the
College did not have to design it that way for us — it simply happens to
be right.

**Nothing on the site offends.** We looked. No imagery we would object
to, no assumptions about how a family decides, no examples that assume a
Western household. The FAQ answers a question about a family enquiring
on behalf of an applicant under eighteen and directs them to admissions
rather than the form. Somebody thought about this.

---

## What we would ask before enrolling our daughter

### 1. Who teaches her, and in what setting?

The College delivers "live and recorded instruction". We could not find,
on any page, whether live classes are mixed, whether a female instructor
can be requested, whether classes are recorded and who can see the
recording, or whether her camera must be on.

We are not asking the College to segregate anything. We are asking it to
**tell us what happens**, so we can decide. At the moment we would have
to email and hope.

This is the single largest gap in the site for a Gulf family, and it is
not a values question — it is a description question. The College
describes its assessment rubrics in more detail than it describes what a
class actually looks like.

### 2. Her voice is recorded. Where does it go?

The privacy page is good — better than most — and now says recordings
are deleted after 730 days and can be erased on request. Good.

It does not say **where the recordings are stored** or which country
they sit in. For a family recording a daughter's voice, that is not a
technical detail. The College's own Board Paper 02 lists data location
as an open decision, so we know the answer is not being withheld — it
has not been decided. We would wait for it.

### 3. The certificate is not a certificate

We understand the College holds no accreditation and says so. For our
son this does not matter; he wants the English, and his employer will
test him directly.

For our daughter it matters completely. She needs something a foreign
university will read. This College cannot give her that, and it says so.

---

## What we would do

**Enrol our son. Not our daughter.**

Our son needs command of English and this looks like a serious place to
get it. He is not buying a certificate.

Our daughter needs a credential, and the College is explicit that it has
none to give. That is not a failure of trust — it is the College being
honest, and we would rather be told.

---

## The one change that would matter most to us

Publish a page describing what a live class actually is. Who is in the
room. Whether it is recorded. Who can see the recording. Whether cameras
are required. Whether a female instructor can be requested.

Not a policy about values. A description of the room.

Every family we know would read that page before any other, and no
institution we have looked at has written it.
