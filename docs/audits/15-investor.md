# Audit 15 — An investor

**Who I am.** I write cheques between £250k and £2m into education
businesses. I have made money twice and lost it four times, and every
loss looked better on paper than this does.

---

## What I would be buying

An unlaunched online English college. No entity, no revenue, no
students, no accreditation, no examiner. A complete six-level
curriculum architecture, a working platform, and — this is the unusual
asset — a governance and record-keeping system built to a standard I
associate with organisations twenty times the size.

---

## What is genuinely valuable here

**The founder's judgement, evidenced in artefacts.**

I do not usually get to assess judgement before writing a cheque; I get
to assess a pitch. Here I can read the decisions register, the board
papers, the audits, and the corrections. That tells me more about how
this team behaves under pressure than any meeting would.

Specifically: it found a false accreditation claim on its own homepage,
corrected it, published what it used to say, and built a test to stop it
recurring. It found a tuition table that was two cents wrong and
published the arithmetic rather than rounding it away. It discovered
that three of its own published corrections were one build away from
being silently reverted, and built a check for that class of error.

Founders who do that with nobody watching do it when I am not watching
either. That is most of what I am buying at this stage.

**The build is real.** Sixty modules authored, 660 quiz questions, a
platform with authentication, assessment, recording, verification and
an admin surface. Whoever did this shipped.

**The positioning is defensible.** "Read the entire programme before you
pay" is a genuine differentiator in a market built on opacity, and it is
one incumbents structurally cannot copy — their syllabi are not good
enough to publish.

---

## Why I would not fund the plan as written

### 1. The unit economics are for a business that will not exist

$19,000 for a three-year individual commitment. In a category where
Preply and italki sell hours at $15–30, Cambridge and the British
Council sell brand, and Duolingo sells free.

**Nobody buys a three-year language commitment from an unaccredited
provider.** Not an individual, not a ministry, not a corporate. The
finance director in Audit 10 said it plainly: the *level* is the
product and the pathway is the upsell, and the College has that
positioned the other way round.

### 2. The moat is honesty, and honesty does not compound

I want to be careful here because I admire it. Radical transparency
wins trust and it is a real advantage. But it is a *positioning*
advantage, not a *structural* one: any competitor can publish its
curriculum tomorrow, and the ones with better curricula will win by
doing so.

The defensible asset would be either accreditation — a genuine
regulatory moat — or evidence that the teaching produces measurably
better outcomes than the alternatives. The College has neither, and the
second requires cohorts it has not run.

### 3. Everything is blocked on one hire

External Examiner. No awards, no accreditation path, no university
partnership, no ministry listing, no corporate procurement — all
downstream. The College has known this for months and it has not
happened.

From outside, that reads one of two ways: either the founder cannot
close a senior academic hire, or the money to pay for one is not there.
Both are answerable. Neither is answered on the site.

### 4. There is no company

I cannot invest in a domain name. This is not a red flag about intent —
it is a red flag about *velocity*. Registering a company is a form and a
fee, and it has not been done while a great deal of harder work has. That
asymmetry is the thing I would ask about first, because it usually means
either a jurisdiction problem nobody has resolved, or a founder who
prefers building to administration.

Either answer changes my terms.

---

## What I would actually do

**A small cheque on a changed plan, or no cheque.**

The changed plan: sell **one level, to corporates and ministries, at
$3,166**, with the separate-skills record as the product. Prove that
sixty people can complete Level III and that their speaking measurably
improves. Use that evidence to get accredited. Then sell the pathway.

That plan needs perhaps £300k and eighteen months. The plan as written
needs accreditation, an examiner, an entity, a contract and a market
that pays $19,000 in advance to an unaccredited provider — and it needs
all of them before the first pound of revenue.

---

## The sentence I would say in the meeting

You have built the institution before the business, and you have built
it better than the business plan justifies.

I would fund the people who did this. I would not fund them to keep
doing what they are doing, and I do not think you would want me to.
