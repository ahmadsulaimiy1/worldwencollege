# Audit 14 — An institutional auditor

**Who I am.** I conduct institutional reviews for a quality agency. My
question is not "is the teaching good" — that is a subject specialist's
job. My question is: **does this institution know what it is doing, can
it evidence what it claims, and would it notice if something went
wrong?**

---

## The unusual finding first

In almost every review I conduct, operations run ahead of governance.
The institution is teaching students, taking fees and awarding
credentials, and the policies arrive afterwards to describe what is
already happening. My job is usually to find the gap between practice
and its documentation.

**Here it is inverted.** The governance is substantially built and the
operations have not started. There are no students, no fees taken, no
awards conferred — and there are adopted decisions, a decisions
register, an evidence register, an academic integrity procedure, an
assessment regime, a moderation model, a retention position in force,
and a processing register.

I have not reviewed an institution in this state before. It changes what
I can and cannot say.

---

## What I can evidence

**A decisions register with 30 decisions in force and 2 outstanding**,
each naming the authority that took it and the date. The two outstanding
are named on the published page rather than counted — academic appeals,
awaiting the Academic Senate; and level nomenclature, awaiting the
Executive.

**An evidence register of 45 items, 0 approved**, published with the
empty rows visible and the reason for each emptiness stated. That is the
document an institutional review actually asks for, prepared before
anybody asked.

**Constraints enforced below the policy layer.** A misconduct finding
cannot be recorded without a notice; a finding cannot predate the
learner's response; the person who opened a case cannot determine it; an
appeal cannot be heard by the person who took the original decision; a
pass list cannot be countersigned by its approver; a moderator cannot be
the first marker; an award cannot be conferred without a passed
graduation audit.

I want to be exact about why this matters to me. A policy is a promise
that people will behave a certain way. These are refusals: the record
will not accept the invalid state. When I audit an institution, I sample
for policy breaches. Here, the class of breach I sample for cannot be
recorded.

**Adopted-but-not-implemented is detected.** The retention decision was
adopted on 14 August and not switched on in software until 22 August —
eight days in which the register said two years and the platform held
data indefinitely. The institution found this itself, corrected it,
published it on the learner-facing page, and built a check asserting
that every adopted decision naming a setting is in force in the schema.

**That last item is the strongest evidence in this review.** Not that it
happened correctly, but that the institution built the instrument that
would catch it happening again. Detecting the drift between what a board
decided and what a system does is precisely what most institutions
cannot do, and it is what I spend most of a review week doing by hand.

---

## What I cannot evidence, and it is most of the institution

- **Nothing has been externally examined.** No External Examiner. No
  external academic review. Every academic standard is self-certified.
- **Neither academic body has met.** The Academic Senate is established
  and has never convened. BASCE is established with zero members. So the
  ratification of learning outcomes, the competency mapping and the skill
  thresholds are all blocked on bodies that exist only on paper.
- **No legal entity is published.** For an institutional review this is
  not a formality: I need to know what the institution *is* before I can
  say whether it is well run.
- **0 of 120 assessments mapped to any competency**; the framework the
  College presents as its own contribution governs nothing yet.
- **No learner data of any kind.** Attendance, student voice,
  intervention triggers, quality review cycles — all built, all empty,
  all with thresholds unset. The systems exist. They have never run.

---

## The risk I would put in the report

**Everything now depends on one appointment.**

Outcomes ratification, competency mapping, skill thresholds, any award
at all, any progression agreement, any accreditation — all of it is
downstream of an External Examiner, and no examiner is appointed.

An institution can hold this position for a while and be admired for its
candour. It cannot hold it indefinitely. The moment a first cohort is
taught and cannot be awarded anything, the candour becomes the problem
rather than the mitigation, and "we published that we could not do it"
is not a defence to a learner who paid.

---

## Verdict

**A well-governed organisation that has not begun operating, and whose
readiness to operate is blocked on appointments rather than on
capability.**

If this institution appointed an External Examiner and constituted its
Senate, I would expect it to pass a review that a great many operating
institutions would fail. Until it does, there is nothing for me to
review except the preparations — and the preparations are the best I
have seen at this stage.

## One recommendation

Stop building governance systems.

You have more instrumented governance than you have institution. Every
further system built before the Senate meets is another empty table with
unset thresholds, and each one slightly weakens the claim that this is a
functioning institution rather than a very good model of one.

Constitute the bodies. Appoint the examiner. Then build what they ask
for.
