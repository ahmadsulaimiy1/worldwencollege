# Audit 01 — A prospective student

**Who I am.** Twenty-six, working in Cairo, B1-ish English learned at
school and never used properly. I want C1 because my employer promotes
people who can chair a meeting in English and I cannot. I have $19,000
of my own money over three years, or I have nothing, depending on
whether this is real.

**What I checked.** The homepage, the six level pages, the tuition
page, the FAQ, the assessment page, the awards page, and — because I am
the kind of person this College says it is written for — the governance
pages nobody is supposed to read.

---

## What made me want to enrol

**The curriculum is published, and it is genuinely detailed.** I read
Level IV's outcomes, its assessment, and the rubric my writing would be
marked against, before anyone asked me for money. I have shopped for
English courses. Nobody does this. Most providers will not tell you what
is in week three.

**The four skills are marked separately and stay separate.** I know my
reading is B2 and my speaking is A2. Every provider I have used gave me
one number that hid exactly the thing I needed to fix.

**It tells me what it cannot do.** The awards page says no qualification
can be conferred because no External Examiner is appointed. It did not
have to tell me that. A page that volunteers its worst fact is a page I
believe about everything else.

**The price is fixed and the arithmetic is shown.** $19,000, or
$3,166.67 a level, and a note explaining why six of those come to
$19,000.02 rather than $19,000. That is two cents. Somebody who explains
two cents is not going to surprise me with a fee later.

---

## What stopped me

### 1. I would be paying for a course that is not finished

The homepage told me sixty modules are written and published in full.
The academics page told me the lessons inside them are still being
written. Both are on the same site.

I counted what the record actually holds: **174 reading items against
720 designed lessons** — 120 per level, six levels. So roughly a quarter
of the teaching content exists.

The College now says this on the homepage too, which it did not until
this week. It is still the fact that stops me: I am being asked for
$3,166.67 for Level IV, and Level IV's lessons are being written while I
study them. That may be fine. It is not what I would have understood
from "published in full", and I would want it said in the same size type
as the price.

### 2. Nobody has finished this course

Not one person. The graduate register is empty, and it is empty
honestly. But it means every claim about what I will be able to do at
the end is a design intention, and I would be the experiment.

The College's answer — a founding cohort gets attention that scale makes
impossible — is a real answer and I half believe it. It is also what
every new provider says.

### 3. There is no contract

I looked for terms and conditions. There are none, anywhere on the site.
No governing law, no statement of what happens if the College closes,
nothing about what I am buying in a form a lawyer would recognise.

I would be sending $3,166.67 to an email address.

### 4. There is no refund policy, and the College says so

I respect that it says so. I still cannot plan around it. If I pay for
Level IV and lose my job in month two, I do not know what happens, and
neither does the College — the decision is recorded as outstanding.

### 5. I cannot phone anyone

One email address. No phone number, no address. The contact form opens
my own mail client, which the page admits: "WEC does not yet have a
backend form service connected."

For $19,000 I would like to be able to ring someone.

---

## What I would do

**Apply. Not pay.**

The placement assessment is free and tells me something useful about
myself. The application costs me nothing and does not commit me.

I would then wait for three things, and I would enrol the day all three
were true: an External Examiner appointed, terms and conditions
published, and Level I's lesson content finished so I could see what a
completed level actually looks like.

I would not wait for accreditation. I understand what the College is and
I am not buying a UK qualification. I am buying teaching, and the
teaching looks better than what I can get locally.

---

## The one thing I would tell the College

Your homepage is the weakest page on your site, and it is the only one
most people read. Everything behind it is more honest than it is. That
is an unusual problem to have and it is worth fixing, because the
readers you say you are writing for are the ones who will notice the gap
and conclude the rest is marketing too.
