# Audit 24 — Ten reasons not to enrol

> *"If Worldwide English College were launched tomorrow, what are the ten
> remaining reasons an exceptionally intelligent international applicant
> might still choose not to enrol? Do not defend the institution. Do not
> minimise weaknesses."*

The Board's instruction was that only when ten convincing reasons cannot
be produced should the institution be considered operationally mature.

**Ten were produced without difficulty.** They are ordered by how much
they would weigh with the applicant described — not by how hard they are
to fix, and not by how much credit the College deserves for having
disclosed most of them itself.

---

## 1 · You would receive nothing at the end

No External Examiner is appointed, so no award can be conferred. Not
"not yet accredited" — *not conferrable at all*.

An intelligent applicant reads this and understands the position
precisely: they would pay $19,000 to complete a programme whose
certificate does not currently exist as an object the institution is
able to issue. Every academic item on the College's own outstanding list
— outcome ratification, competency mapping, skill thresholds — sits
behind this one appointment, and it has been outstanding for months with
no date against it.

## 2 · There is no organisation to contract with

No registered company name. No registration number. No jurisdiction of
incorporation. No registered office — the College's own status page
lists the address as not yet confirmed.

The applicant would be sending money to a domain name and an email
address. Nothing else on this list matters if this one is unresolved,
and it is the cheapest item on it to fix.

## 3 · There is no contract, in any language

No terms and conditions on any page of the site. No governing law, no
jurisdiction for disputes, no statement of what happens if the College
ceases to operate mid-programme, no consumer cancellation position, no
complaints route that is not an academic appeal — and the appeals
procedure is itself recorded as **not adopted**.

Every commitment the College makes is a statement of intent. None is a
promise anyone could enforce.

## 4 · Roughly three-quarters of the teaching does not exist yet

Measured against the record: **174 reading items against 720 designed
lessons** — 120 per level, six levels.

The College publishes this. It did not publish it on the homepage until
this week, where it said instead that sixty modules were "written and
published in full" and invited the reader to "read the entire programme
before you pay for any of it".

The applicant would be paying full price for a programme being written
while they study it, and would have discovered this only by reading past
the front page.

## 5 · Nobody has ever completed it

Zero graduates. Zero enrolled learners. Zero marks awarded, moderated or
appealed.

Every claim about what the applicant would be able to do at the end is a
design intention. The College's counter-offer — a founding cohort
receives attention that scale makes impossible later — is real, and it
is also what every new provider says, and the applicant has heard it
before.

## 6 · No refund policy has been decided

Stated honestly, which does not help. The applicant cannot answer the
only financial question that matters: *what happens if I stop?*

An unfair refund policy can be planned around. An undecided one cannot.

## 7 · Nothing has ever been looked at by anyone outside the building

No External Examiner. No external academic review. No accreditation, and
none applied for. No moderation by a third party. Zero of 45 evidence
items approved.

The rubrics, the outcomes, the CEFR mapping, the pass marks — all
internally written, internally applied and internally judged. An
intelligent applicant knows that a standard is not a standard until
somebody outside the institution agrees that a mark against it means
what the institution says it means.

## 8 · The certificate would mean nothing to the people it is for

The College says plainly that its awards are its own, not regulated, and
carry no accreditation or transfer entitlement.

So: a university will not accept it for admission. A ministry cannot
place the College on an approved list. An employer will not recognise
the awarding body or the award title. The applicant's own audit of who
would honour this credential returns an empty set — and the College
agrees.

What is left is the teaching, which may well be worth $19,000. But the
applicant did not come here for teaching alone; they came for teaching
*that produces something*.

## 9 · The institution has almost no visible people

Twenty faculty are named with qualifications, attested by the College,
with the provenance of every field recorded honestly.

There are no photographs. No biographies beyond a line. No engagement
dates. No named principal, no academic director, no data protection
officer, no press contact — the privacy page states that the College
cannot yet name a person accountable for personal data.

There is one email address, no telephone, no postal address, and a
contact form that opens the reader's own mail client.

The applicant can answer "what is this institution?" in considerable
detail and cannot answer "**who** is this institution?" at all.

## 10 · Everything outstanding is outstanding without a date

This is the one the College will find hardest to hear, and it is the
reason the other nine compound rather than merely accumulate.

No External Examiner — no appointment date. No accreditation — no
application date. No refund policy — no decision date. No registered
address, no academic calendar, no first-cohort start date, nine of
eleven data retentions undetermined, both academic bodies established
and never convened, 0 of 120 assessments mapped to a competency.

The College's institutional strategy is disclosure, and it executes it
better than anyone in its sector. But **disclosure with no schedule is
indistinguishable, from outside, from a permanent condition.** An
intelligent applicant does not ask "are you hiding something" — the
College has answered that more convincingly than its competitors ever
will. They ask "*when*", and there is no page on the site that answers
it.

An applicant who returned in six months and found the same list, the
same length, with the same absence of dates, would draw the obvious
conclusion. And they would be right to.

---

## The Board's test

Ten convincing reasons were produced, and the constraint was space
rather than material. **The institution is not yet operationally
mature.**

Two observations about the ten, offered without defending anything.

**Only two are academic.** Reasons 1 and 7 concern the standard and its
external validation. The other eight are administrative, commercial or
communicative — a company registration, a contract, a refund decision, a
phone number, a set of dates. The College has done the hard part and not
the cheap part, which is an unusual and recoverable failure mode.

**The College disclosed eight of these ten itself**, before this audit,
on its own pages, unprompted. That fact does not remove a single reason
from the list — an applicant who declines because there is no contract
declines just as firmly whether or not the absence was admitted.

But it does say something about which direction this institution is
travelling in, and the Board should read the list with that in mind
without allowing it to soften a single item.
