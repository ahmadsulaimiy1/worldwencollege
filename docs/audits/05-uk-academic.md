# Audit 05 — A UK academic

**Who I am.** I direct a pre-sessional English programme at a British
university and I have sat on validation panels. I was asked to look at
this because a colleague forwarded it and said "this is either very good
or very strange".

---

## What is genuinely good, and I want to be precise about why

**The assessment architecture is better than most validated provision I
have examined.**

Sixty modules, each carrying exactly one assessed assignment and one
assessed quiz. Assignment marks recorded on a 0–1 scale by a named
marker with written feedback, never auto-generated. Rubrics published to
the learner before the assessment. A moderated mark and a first-marker
mark recorded distinctly so a board can tell them apart. Spoken work
captured as recordings and reviewed rather than judged on impression.

Any panel would accept that design. I have failed institutions that
could not describe theirs half so clearly.

**The database refuses invalid governance.** I was shown that the
misconduct record will not accept a finding without a notice, will not
accept a finding recorded before the learner's response, and will not
let the same person open and determine a case. That is a constraint
enforced by the system rather than by a policy nobody reads. I have
never seen it done and I have read a great many policies nobody reads.

**Learning outcomes are marked `interim`.** Four per level, stated
before the teaching was designed, and honestly flagged as not yet
ratified. That is the correct status and the correct word.

---

## Where I would fail it

### 1. Nothing has been externally moderated. Nothing at all.

No External Examiner. No external academic review. The standard is
internally defined, internally applied and internally judged. The
College knows this — it is the reason it will not confer awards — but I
want to name the consequence precisely: **every academic claim on this
site is currently self-certified.**

A rubric is not a standard until somebody outside the institution has
agreed that a mark against it means what the institution says it means.

### 2. The competency framework is defined and unmapped

Six competencies — Clarity, Command, Judgement, Reason, Bearing, Reach —
and **0 of 120 assessments mapped to any of them.** The College publishes
that figure itself.

So the framework it presents as its own intellectual contribution
currently governs nothing. It is a good framework. It is not in use.

### 3. The skill descriptors have bands and no thresholds

Five bands named — Emerging to Distinguished. Nobody has determined what
evidence earns Proficient rather than Developing. The College reserves
this to its Academic Senate, which has been established and has never
met.

A five-point scale without thresholds is not a scale. It is five words.

### 4. Speaking does not count toward certification

The College adopted this deliberately, because no moderated marking
standard for speech exists. I agree with the decision and I want to say
what it costs: this is an English *fluency* course whose certificate
currently rests on reading, writing and multiple-choice.

### 5. 1,200 hours from zero to C2

This is the claim I would press hardest at a panel, and the College
half-defends it already: it says the figure is a design workload, not a
measurement, and explicitly disclaims that C2 is reachable in 1,200
hours from no English.

Good. But the six-level table still presents 200 hours per level as the
route from A1 to C2, and the sector's working assumption is roughly
1,000–1,200 guided hours to reach *C1* from zero, not C2. The
disclaimer is doing a great deal of work.

I would want the College to say plainly, in the table and not beneath
it, that 1,200 hours is a lower bound that few learners will match.

---

## My verdict

**A serious academic design, entirely unvalidated, and honest about
being so.**

If a colleague asked whether to send students, I would say no — not
because the teaching looks poor, but because I cannot recommend
provision that nobody outside the provider has examined, and neither can
the College.

If I were asked to be its External Examiner, **I would accept.** There is
something real here to examine, the records are in a state where
examination is possible, and the institution has clearly been built by
someone expecting to be examined. That is rarer than it should be.

---

## The one sentence I would put to its Board

You have built the machinery of academic quality assurance to a standard
that would survive a validation panel, and you have not yet let a single
person outside the building look at anything.

Appoint your External Examiner. Everything else on your list is
downstream of that one appointment, and it has been the blocking item
for months.
