# Audit 02 — A parent funding a learner

**Who I am.** My daughter is twenty-two, finished her degree in Lagos,
and needs English good enough for a master's in the UK. I am paying. I
have paid for things that did not exist before and I am not doing it
again.

**What I checked.** Whether this is a company. What I would be signing.
What happens to my money if it stops. What my daughter gets at the end
that anyone else will accept.

---

## What I found in ten minutes

**There is no company.** I looked for a registered number, a registered
office, a legal name. There is none on the site. The College says its
"registered administrative headquarters address in London" is still to
be confirmed — its own words, on its own institutional status page.

So the entity I would be paying is: a domain name and an email address.

**There is no contract.** No terms and conditions on any page. I checked
all of them. For a $19,000 commitment there is nothing that says what
the College owes me, what I owe it, which country's law applies, or what
happens if it stops trading.

**There is no refund policy.** The College states this plainly and says
the decision has not been taken. I do not doubt its honesty. I cannot
give my daughter's fees to an organisation that has not yet decided what
happens if she withdraws.

**There is no phone number.** One email address, no telephone, no
address. If something went wrong I would have no second way to reach
anyone.

**My daughter would not get a qualification.** The awards page says no
award can be conferred because no External Examiner is appointed. The
British university she is applying to will not know what a "Certificate
of Applied English Communication" is in any case — the College says
itself these are its own awards, not regulated, not accredited.

---

## What I want to say for the College, because it earned it

I have read a lot of education marketing. This is the only site I have
seen that tells you what it has not got before you ask.

It says nobody has graduated. It says no examiner is appointed. It says
the lesson content is unfinished. It says its awards carry no
accreditation and no transfer entitlement. It publishes a register of 45
things an external reviewer would ask for and marks 0 of them approved.

That is not weakness. That is the first thing I have read in this sector
that I believed.

And the teaching, as far as I can judge it, looks serious. The rubrics
are real. The assessment policy is published before the assessment. My
daughter's speaking and writing would be marked separately by a person,
not scored by software.

---

## What I would do

**Not fund it. Not yet, and not because of the teaching.**

I would fund it the week three things exist, and none of them is
academic:

1. **A registered legal entity, published** — name, number, jurisdiction.
2. **Terms and conditions**, including what happens if the College
   ceases to operate mid-programme.
3. **A refund policy.** Any refund policy. Even a strict one. A stated
   position I can plan around beats a fair position nobody has decided.

I would add a fourth if I were being careful: **client-money protection**
— some assurance that fees paid in advance are not simply spent. A
sponsor's finance department will ask this and the College currently has
no answer.

---

## The thing that would change my mind fastest

Let me pay per level. The College already offers this — $3,166.67 rather
than $19,000. Say it louder.

My real fear is not that the teaching is bad. It is that I hand over
$19,000 and the organisation is gone in eight months. Paying level by
level caps that risk at $3,166.67, and the College's own pricing already
solves most of my objection. It is buried on the tuition page under a
headline figure of $19,000.

The College is frightening me with a number it does not need me to pay.
