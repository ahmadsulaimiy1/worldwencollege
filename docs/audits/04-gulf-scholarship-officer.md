# Audit 04 — A Gulf government scholarship officer

**Who I am.** I administer a ministry programme that sends citizens
abroad for language and academic preparation. I do not choose providers.
I check whether a provider can be placed on an approved list, and I sign
the memo that says so. If I approve a provider that later fails, that is
my name on the file.

**What I need, in order.** Legal entity. Regulatory standing.
Verifiable qualification. Financial standing. Duty of care. Invoice.

---

## The check, item by item

### Legal entity — FAIL

No registered company name, no registration number, no jurisdiction of
incorporation, no registered office address published anywhere on the
site. The College's own institutional status page lists "Registered
administrative headquarters address in London" as *not yet confirmed*.

I cannot open a supplier file. This is the first field on the form and
there is nothing to type in it.

### Regulatory standing — FAIL

No accreditation. None applied for. The College states this plainly and
I credit it for that, but my list requires a recognised accreditation —
in practice ASIC, BAC, Accreditation UK, or an equivalent national body.

The College publishes a route toward these and marks every stage
correctly as not begun. A route is not a standing.

### Verifiable qualification — FAIL

Our scholarships are conditional on a completion credential we can
verify. The College cannot confer one: no External Examiner is
appointed. It says so.

Its verification service exists and appears well built. It has nobody in
it.

### Financial standing — CANNOT ASSESS

No accounts, no entity, no bank details, no VAT position, no client-money
protection. We pay in advance and in tranches; there is no mechanism
here for either.

### Duty of care — PARTIAL

Good: a published academic integrity procedure, a published assessment
regime, an adopted data-retention position, an accessibility statement
that claims no conformance level it has not audited.

Missing: a complaints procedure that is not an academic appeal (and the
appeals procedure itself is recorded as *not adopted*), a safeguarding
position, and any statement of what happens to a sponsored learner who
withdraws.

### Invoice — FAIL

No entity, so no invoice. No currency but US dollars — SAR is present in
the record but inactive with no exchange rate. Our finance office pays in
SAR against a local or a UK entity.

---

## What I would write in the memo

> *Not eligible for listing. The provider does not meet the threshold
> requirements at any of the four gates and is not near them. It is not
> a marginal case and should not be reconsidered on representation.*
>
> *This is not a warning about the provider's conduct. Its published
> material is unusually candid — it states its own deficiencies without
> being asked, which is not typical. The deficiencies are simply
> disqualifying for our purposes.*
>
> *Recommend re-examination when the provider holds (a) a registered
> legal entity, (b) accreditation from a recognised body, and (c) an
> appointed External Examiner. Any two of the three would not suffice.*

---

## What I would say privately

The College is being told "no" by every institution like mine, and it is
being told no for entirely fixable reasons that have nothing to do with
the quality of its teaching.

An entity registration is a form and a fee. Terms and conditions are a
solicitor's afternoon. Those two would not get it onto my list — only
accreditation does that — but they would move it from *not a real
organisation* to *a real organisation without accreditation*, and those
are very different sentences.

I would also say this, because somebody should: the College's evidence
register — 45 items, 0 approved, published with the empty rows visible —
is closer to what an accreditation panel actually wants than most of the
folders I am sent by accredited providers. It is preparing for the right
examination. It just has not sat it.

---

## One thing I could not check, and it matters

I read Arabic. Our ministry's staff read Arabic. **The verification page
has no Arabic edition** — and 32 of the 33 Arabic pages link to it
from the header.

If we ever did list this College, the officer verifying a scholar's
completion would be sent from an Arabic site to an English-only page
with no way back. That is the single page where the language matters
most, and it is the one page that has no Arabic.
