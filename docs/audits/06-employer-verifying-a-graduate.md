# Audit 06 — An employer verifying a graduate

**Who I am.** HR at a logistics firm in Dubai. A candidate has put
"Higher Certificate in Applied English Communication, Worldwide English
College" on a CV. My job is to find out in under five minutes whether
that is true and whether it means anything.

---

## The five minutes

**Minute one.** I search the College. The site is real, substantial and
consistent. Good start — most fake credentials fall over here.

**Minute two.** I find `/verify/`. It asks for a verification code from
the certificate, needs no account, no sign-in, and states that it does
not record who performs a check. That last line is unusual and correct:
I check candidates confidentially and I do not want the institution
knowing I asked.

**Minute three.** I ask the candidate for the code.

**Minute four.** The College's own awards page tells me no award has
been conferred to anyone, because no External Examiner is appointed.

**Minute five.** So either my candidate's certificate does not exist, or
it was issued by something other than this College under a similar name.

---

## What this means for the candidate

Not fraud, necessarily — but a claim I cannot substantiate, which for my
purposes is the same outcome. The candidate goes into the "unverified
credential" pile and I test their English myself.

---

## What I would say about the verification service itself

**It is well built and I would use it.** Three standings — verified,
withdrawn, not found — and a withdrawn award says *withdrawn*, in words,
with a date, rather than being implied by absence. That distinction
matters more than people realise: a system that answers "not found" for
a revoked credential is a system that launders revocations.

No sign-in, no logging of the checker. Correct.

**One thing I could not do — since fixed.** The page was English only.
My colleague who handles Gulf-national candidates works in Arabic, and
there was no Arabic version of the verification page, although every
Arabic page but one linked to it. She would have arrived at an English
page from an Arabic site with no way back.

`/ar/verify/` now exists, and 1 of the 34 Arabic pages links to the
English one — that page’s own language switch. The record itself is
still shown as issued, in English, and the page explains why.

**One thing I would want.** A QR code on the certificate that lands
directly on the verified record. The College appears to have built QR
verification; it is not obvious to me as an employer that scanning the
certificate is the fast path. Say so on the verification page.

---

## What the qualification would mean to me if it existed

Honestly? Less than the College hopes, and more than nothing.

I do not recognise the awarding body. I do not recognise the award
title. The College tells me plainly that it is not regulated and carries
no accreditation, which I respect and which also settles the matter: I
would treat it as evidence that the candidate studied seriously, not as
a qualification.

**What I would actually value** is the thing the College has and does not
sell: the four skills marked separately. If a candidate showed me a
record saying *Speaking: Proficient, Writing: Developing*, marked by a
named person against a published rubric, that is more useful to me than
an IELTS band. I hire people to speak on the phone to Karachi and Rotterdam.

At present the College cannot give me that either — speaking is adopted
as not counting toward certification, and the skill thresholds have not
been set.

---

## Verdict

**Nothing to verify, because there is nobody to verify.**

The service is ready. The institution is not. Those are different
problems and the College has solved the harder one first, which is an
odd but defensible order.

## One thing I would fix today

~~Put a line on the verification page saying that no awards have yet
been conferred, so a code will not be found.~~ **Done, in both
editions.**

The page invited me to enter a code and told me "not found" — which,
from where I sit, is indistinguishable from a forged certificate. The
College knew the true reason and did not say it on the one page where it
changes my conclusion about a real person. It says it now, and the
Arabic error message says it too.
