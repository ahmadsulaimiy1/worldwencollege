# Audit 09 — A data-protection regulator

**Who I am.** A supervisory authority caseworker. I am not looking for
reasons to act; I am establishing whether this organisation understands
what it holds and can answer a subject-access request without
improvising.

---

## First impressions

**A published privacy notice exists**, at `/support/privacy/`, and it is
better than the median.

It opens by stating that the site runs no analytics, no advertising
pixel and no third-party measurement of any kind — and the build refuses
to publish the page if a tracker ever appears. It names its actual
processors rather than saying "service providers". It states a retention
period for voice recordings (730 days), an erasure position, and what is
*not* deleted on request and why.

That last item is the one most notices get wrong. This one says plainly
that a conferred qualification is not deleted on request because other
people rely on it, while the holder can be removed from the browsable
register immediately and unconditionally. That is a correct and
well-reasoned position and it is written in language a data subject can
follow.

**There is a processing register.** Eleven activities, recorded, with
higher-risk ones flagged.

---

## Where I would open a file

### 1. No controller is identified

I cannot tell who the controller is. No legal entity is published — no
company name, no number, no registered address. The organisation's own
status page lists its registered address as not yet confirmed.

**Without a controller I do not know who I would be corresponding
with**, and neither does a data subject. This is the finding that
matters; everything else is downstream of it.

### 2. No lawful basis has been determined — for anything

All eleven processing activities record their lawful basis as **NOT
DETERMINED**. The organisation's own schema forbids publishing an
activity in that state, which is a good control, and the consequence is
that the register is complete and entirely undetermined.

Processing personal data without having identified a lawful basis is not
a documentation gap. It is the thing itself.

I note in mitigation that the organisation appears to hold almost no
personal data yet — no learners, no recordings, no graduates. The
determination is therefore still ahead of the processing rather than
behind it, which is the right side of the line and unusual.

### 3. No data protection officer, and no assessment of whether one is
required

The privacy page says the College cannot yet name a person accountable
for personal data. Given voice recording of learners at scale, whether a
DPO is required is a question that needs answering rather than deferring.

### 4. No stated data location

Board Paper 02 records data location as an open decision. For voice
recordings of learners in multiple jurisdictions, this determines
transfer obligations. Data subjects are entitled to know where their
voice is.

### 5. Nine of the eleven retentions are undetermined

One is set (recordings, 730 days) and in force in software — I checked
that it is actually implemented, not merely written, and it is. The
other nine are open.

---

## What I would say about how this organisation behaves

Two things I would put in a file note, because they change how I would
handle it.

**It found and reported its own failure.** The retention decision was
adopted on 14 August and not implemented in software until 22 August.
For eight days the organisation held voice data indefinitely while its
own register said two years. It published that fact on its privacy page,
including that nobody was affected because nobody has recorded anything,
and stated that it wrote it down rather than quietly correcting because
"a privacy page that edits its own history is not one you should trust".

Organisations do not do that. I have never had one do it.

**The board paper is written for a decision, not for a file.** It sets
out options and consequences and recommends, and it refuses to fill
blanks with plausible text where determinations belong.

---

## Verdict

**A file, not a case.**

I would write asking for the controller's identity, the lawful basis for
each activity, the data location, and the DPO assessment. I would set a
generous deadline. I would not be expecting evasion.

If the organisation begins enrolling learners and recording voices
before those four are settled, the posture changes immediately — and it
should know that the sequence matters more than the speed. Determine
first, process second. It is currently, and to its credit, on the right
side of that line by accident of not having launched.
