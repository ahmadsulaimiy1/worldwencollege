# Audit 20 — The applicant journey

**Method.** Walked the route an applicant actually takes, from arriving
cold to holding a qualification, flagging every point at which a
thoughtful reader stops. Not a usability review — a hesitation review.

---

## Stage 1 · Arrival

**The homepage.**

Strengths: the proposition is stated in one sentence and it is an
unusual one. The four-part case for English is well argued and does not
flatter the reader. The level table is clear.

**Hesitation 1 — the strongest claim on the site is its weakest.**

Until this week the homepage said sixty modules were "written and
published in full" and invited the reader to "read the entire programme
before you pay for any of it", while the academics page one click away
said the lessons inside those modules were still being written. Measured:
**174 reading items against 720 designed lessons.**

Corrected now. The structural lesson stands: the homepage was the only
page on the site making a claim its neighbours contradicted, and it is
the page nine readers in ten never leave.

**Hesitation 2 — the fixed accreditation claim.** The same block asserted
an accreditation candidacy and retained legal counsel. Neither existed.
Corrected, with the retraction published in place. See governance F1.

---

## Stage 2 · Reading the programme

This is the strongest stage of the journey and the reason anyone would
continue.

The six level pages carry outcomes, CEFR band, module list, assessment
and workload. The assessment page publishes the rubric policy. The
integrity procedure is published. Nothing here required a login and
nothing was withheld.

**Hesitation 3 — the reader meets two names for the same six levels.**
The navigation says *Application Stage · B1*; the table on the same page
has a column headed **Programme** saying *Intermediate Programme*.
Nothing tells the reader they are the same thing. Board Paper 03 puts
the choice to the Executive; it is registered as C11 and undecided.

**Hesitation 4 — "interim" is honest and unexplained.** Every learning
outcome is marked `interim`. An applicant does not know that this means
"not yet ratified by a body that has never met". A one-line gloss where
the word first appears would cost nothing.

---

## Stage 3 · Price

**Hesitation 5 — the headline number is the one nobody should pay.**

The page leads with **$19,000**. Level-by-level payment at $3,166.67 —
which is how almost every reader would and should buy — is the secondary
presentation.

The College is frightening its own applicants with a figure it does not
need them to pay. Every one of the fifteen stakeholder audits that
looked at price reached this conclusion independently: the parent, the
finance director and the investor all said the same thing in different
words.

**Hesitation 6 — no refund policy.** Stated honestly. Still unplannable.
An applicant cannot answer "what happens if I lose my job in month two"
and neither can the College.

**Not a hesitation, and worth saying:** the two-total ledger. Six levels
at $3,166.67 come to $19,000.02, not $19,000, and the page publishes both
totals and explains the two cents. A reader who notices this trusts the
next page more, not less.

---

## Stage 4 · Deciding

**Hesitation 7 — there is no contract.** No terms and conditions
anywhere on the site. No governing law. No statement of what happens if
the College ceases to operate. This is the point at which the parent in
Audit 02 stopped and did not resume.

**Hesitation 8 — there is no entity.** No registered name, number or
address. The College's own status page lists its registered address as
not yet confirmed.

**Hesitation 9 — one email address, no phone.** The contact form opens
the reader's own mail client, which the page admits. For a $19,000
decision there is one channel.

---

## Stage 5 · Applying

The application is well built. It is free, it does not commit, and the
self-guided level quiz gives the applicant something useful before
asking for anything.

**Hesitation 10 — what happens next is not stated.** How long until a
reply. Who replies. What the placement assessment involves, how long it
takes, and whether it is live or asynchronous. An applicant sends a form
into silence.

---

## Stage 6 · Enrolling and studying

Cannot be walked. No cohort exists, no start date is set, and the
academic calendar is listed as not yet confirmed.

**Hesitation 11 — no start date.** An applicant who decides to enrol
cannot find out when they would begin. This is the single most ordinary
question in admissions and the site cannot answer it.

**Hesitation 12 — what a live class actually is.** Nowhere does the site
describe the room: how many learners, whether cameras are required,
whether sessions are recorded, who can see recordings, whether a
particular instructor can be requested. The Saudi family in Audit 03
made this their principal finding, and it is not only a Gulf question —
every applicant wants to know what they are walking into.

---

## Stage 7 · Being assessed

Well designed and unrunnable at present. Rubrics published in advance,
marks by a named person, four skills separate, moderation modelled.

**Hesitation 13 — speaking does not count.** Correctly adopted, because
no moderated speaking standard exists. An applicant buying an *English
fluency* course should meet this fact on the assessment page rather than
in the decisions register.

---

## Stage 8 · Graduating

**Hesitation 14 — nobody can graduate.** No External Examiner, so no
award can be conferred. Said plainly on the awards page.

Every stage before this is a route to a destination that is currently
closed.

---

## Stage 9 · Being verified afterwards

**Hesitation 15 — the verification page does not say why a code will not
be found.** An employer entering a code today gets "not found", which is
indistinguishable from a forged certificate. The page should say that no
awards have yet been conferred.

**Hesitation 16 — verification is English only.** 32 of the 33 Arabic
pages link to it from their header. An Arabic reader is deposited on an
English page with no way back, at the one moment the language matters
most.

---

## Summary

Sixteen hesitation points. Their distribution is the finding:

| Stage | Hesitations | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival | 2 | Overclaim (both now corrected) |
| Reading the programme | 2 | Naming and jargon |
| Price | 2 | Presentation and an undecided policy |
| Deciding | 3 | **Administrative — entity, contract, contact** |
| Applying | 1 | Missing information |
| Enrolling | 2 | **Missing information** |
| Assessment | 1 | Disclosure placement |
| Graduating | 1 | **Blocking — the examiner** |
| Verification | 2 | Missing information |

Only two are academic. Three are administrative and cheap. Six are
missing sentences that nobody has written — what happens after you
apply, when you would start, what a class is, why a code is not found.

**The journey does not fail on quality. It fails on the ordinary
paperwork of being an organisation, and on a handful of paragraphs
nobody has got round to writing.**
