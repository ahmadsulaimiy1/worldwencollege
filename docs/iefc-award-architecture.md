# The IEFC Award Architecture

**A proposal of the Academic Board to the Executive. Second reading.**

**Status: NOT ADOPTED.** No award below exists, none may be conferred,
and nothing here may appear on the public site until the Executive
approves it. Registered as governance **C4**.

---

## 0. What changed at second reading, and why

The first draft proposed a ladder of **capability** — Aspirant,
Practitioner, Communicator, Professional, Orator, Laureate. The
Executive returned it with a ladder of **standing**, and was right to.

The distinction matters more than the words. A capability ladder tells a
graduate what they can do — which the qualification descriptors already
say, at length, in the place designed for it. A standing ladder tells
them **what they are within the College**, and that is what a person
still identifies with twenty years later. "I am an Associate of
WorldWide English College" is an identity. "I am an English
Communicator" is a skill assessment wearing a title.

The test the Executive set — *what will still sound credible in fifty
years?* — is also the test that decides this. Capability language dates:
"Communicator" is a word of its decade. Standing language does not:
aspirant, candidate, associate, envoy, orator and laureate have meant the
same things for centuries and will still be legible when everyone
involved in founding this College is dead. That is the correct standard
and the Board adopts it.

**Adopted: the Executive's Option B**, with one structural fix (the
post-nominals, §III) and two objections the Board is keeping (§II).

---

## I. The three rules this architecture is built under

Not disclaimers. They determine the design, and an architecture that
ignored them would fail on its first serious reading by an admissions
officer or a competitor.

### 1. These are institutional awards, not qualifications

WEC confers these. They are not degrees, not regulated
qualifications, not professional-body grades, and not equivalent to any
of those. Any institution may confer its own awards. What it may not do
is dress them as something a regulator issued.

The architecture is designed so a graduate can display an award with
pride **and** with accuracy, and so a stranger can tell exactly what it
is. Those goals are not in tension: the awards are *more* impressive for
being unambiguous.

### 2. CEFR-aligned and extended — never "above"

The Executive's own formulation is adopted verbatim as house wording:

> **CEFR-aligned English mastery, enhanced by leadership, executive
> communication, intercultural competence, critical thinking,
> professional communication, and real-world performance.**

The CEFR ends at C2. There is no C3, and presenting one would be
inaccurate in a way that a single informed reader could destroy. Level VI
maps to C2; the *IEFC designation* carries the broader achievement.

| Strand | What the CEFR says about it |
|---|---|
| Leadership and executive communication | Nothing |
| Professional practice | Nothing |
| Critical thinking | Nothing |
| Intercultural competence | Touched only at the margins |
| Digital communication | Nothing — the framework predates it |
| Real-world performance | Nothing |

Never write: "above the CEFR", "higher than C2", "C3", "exceeds CEFR
level". Where more is wanted after C2, it is a **post-programme
fellowship** (§XII), not an invented rung.

### 3. No claim of accreditation or recognition

Nothing here is accredited, recognised, validated, or equivalent to
anything. Where standing must be described, the sentence is: *an award of
WorldWide English College.* That is complete and honourable.

## II. The Ascent — the six awards

| # | CEFR | Award | Post-nominal | Standing conferred |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | A1 | **English Aspirant of WorldWide English College** | ApWEC | Entry into the tradition |
| II | A2 | **English Candidate of WorldWide English College** | CnWEC | Recognised learner of the College |
| III | B1 | **English Associate of WorldWide English College** | AsWEC | Established member of the academic community |
| IV | B2 | **English Envoy of WorldWide English College** | EnWEC | Trusted representative and communicator |
| V | C1 | **English Orator of WorldWide English College** | OrWEC | High-level intellectual and professional communicator |
| VI | C2 | **English Laureate of WorldWide English College** | LrWEC | Distinguished master of the programme |

**Each award is complete in itself.** A learner who stops at Level III
has not failed to become something — they **are** an English Associate
of WorldWide English College, permanently, and the College says so in
those words. An award that only means something as a step toward the
next is not an award; it is a receipt. This remains the load-bearing
decision of the architecture.

### The two objections the Board is keeping

**Fellow is not available for Level IV.** In a college, a Fellow is
ordinarily *academic staff* — the governing academic body. "Fellow of
WorldWide English College" would tell a reader the holder teaches here.
It also borrows the senior grade of professional bodies, and placing it
at Level IV inverts every external expectation, since a Fellow elsewhere
outranks almost everything. The Executive's Option B already resolves
this with **Envoy**, which is adopted.

**Fellow is not discarded — it is relocated to where it is true.** The
post-programme specialist credentials (§XII) are exactly what a
fellowship is: advanced, specialist, held by someone who has already
completed the programme. "Fellow" belongs there and nowhere else.

**Candidate carries a residual risk, and here is the mitigation.**
Aspirant → Candidate is a genuine academic arc: one who aspires, then
one formally admitted to candidature. "Candidate" is a conferred
standing in several traditions, not a pending state, and the Board
accepts the Executive's preference.

The risk is real all the same: to a lay reader in Lagos, Dubai or São
Paulo, "English Candidate" can read as *applying for something*. On a CV
that is the opposite of what the award is for.

**Mitigation, mandatory in every issued artefact:** the qualification
line always appears beneath the award and always reads in the perfect
tense —

> **English Candidate of WorldWide English College**
> *International English Fluency Certificate — Level II (CEFR A2),
> conferred 14 March 2027*

Nothing reads as pending when the word *conferred* and a date sit under
it. The Board asks that this be reviewed after the first year of
conferrals against how graduates actually report it being received.

### Why each word survives the fifty-year test

**Aspirant** — honours the decision, which at A1 *is* the achievement.
Beginning a language in adulthood is harder than any later step and is
where most people stop. Overclaims nothing.

**Candidate** — formal admission to candidature. The moment the College
recognises someone as its own.

**Associate** — membership. Not "can do B1 things"; *belongs*. This is
the point on the Ascent where a learner stops being someone taking a
course and becomes a member of an academic community, and the word says
exactly that.

**Envoy** — one who can be *sent*: to the meeting, the client, the
interview, to speak for someone other than themselves. B2 is precisely
the representation threshold, and representation is what an employer is
buying.

**Orator** — composition *and* delivery: clear, structured argument on
complex subjects, delivered fluently. That is oratory in the classical
sense, and C1 is where the leadership and executive-communication
strands become the substance of the award rather than an addition to it.
Chosen over *Scholar*, which implies research and carries a funding
connotation ("a scholar" is often someone with a scholarship).

**Laureate** — the crown. Conferred rather than accumulated, associated
with distinction rather than administration, and — critically — **not
confusable with a degree**. *Master* was rejected outright: a reasonable
person reads it as a master's degree, and an architecture whose summit is
a misunderstanding is not an architecture.

### Rejected, with reasons, so the decisions are not relitigated

| Rejected | Reason |
|---|---|
| **Fellow** (at IV) | Implies academic staff; inverts professional-body seniority. Relocated to §XII where it is accurate |
| **Master** | Reads as a master's degree. Non-negotiable |
| **Advocate** | Protected legal title in South Africa, India, Scandinavia and elsewhere |
| **Professional** | Abbreviates to "Prof."; also flat beside the rest |
| **Scholar** | Implies research and funding; *Orator* names what C1 actually certifies here |
| **Practitioner / Communicator** | Capability words. Date badly; duplicate the descriptors |
| **Member** | The undifferentiated grade of professional bodies |
| **Apprentice / Journeyman** | Trade-guild register; diminishing for adult professionals |
| **Ambassador** | Diplomatic office, and debased by marketing use |

## III. Post-nominals — the structural fix

The Executive's ladder creates a collision the first draft did not have:
**Aspirant and Associate both begin with A.** Single-initial
post-nominals would give two different awards the same letters, which in
a credential system is not a stylistic problem but a defect — a verifier
could not tell them apart.

**House form: a two-letter award code, joined to WEC.**

| Award | Post-nominal |
|---|---|
| Aspirant | **ApWEC** |
| Candidate | **CnWEC** |
| Associate | **AsWEC** |
| Envoy | **EnWEC** |
| Orator | **OrWEC** |
| Laureate | **LrWEC** |

Two letters for the award, three for the College, six characters every
time. The consistent width is what makes it read as a system rather than
six abbreviations, and by the second time a reader meets one the pattern
is unmistakably WEC's. All six are distinct.

**Display forms, descending formality:**

```
Aisha Rahman, English Orator of WorldWide English College
Aisha Rahman, OrWEC (WorldWide English College)
Aisha Rahman, OrWEC
```

**Required of the College in every template it issues:** first use in
any document spells the award in full and names the issuer. A
post-nominal whose issuer nobody can identify is where a
misrepresentation begins, whoever commits it.

**Before publication:** a designation and trade-mark search on all six
strings. The Board is not aware of collisions; that is not the same as
having checked, and the difference matters before these are printed.

## IV. The Honours System

Five honours, as the Executive directed. The Board has set them so the
top of the scale cannot be reached by arithmetic alone — an honour that
is always awarded to whoever scores highest is a ranking, not an honour.

| Honour | Basis | Proposed criteria |
|---|---|---|
| **Pass** | Calculated | Meets the standard on every assessed criterion |
| **Merit** | Calculated | 80% overall, no criterion at bare Pass |
| **Distinction** | Calculated | 88% overall, no criterion below Merit, Level Address at Distinction |
| **High Distinction** | Calculated | 94% overall, no criterion below Distinction |
| **Chancellor's Distinction** | **Conferred** | Not calculated. Conferred by the Academic Board on work it wishes to hold up as exemplary |

**Proposed pass mark: 70%** — awaiting governance B1/B2. Until a pass
mark is adopted, **no honour can be conferred at all.**

**The floors matter more than the numbers.** An aggregate alone lets a
learner take a Distinction while failing outright on one dimension. A
graduate who writes brilliantly and cannot be understood aloud has not
mastered English, and a certificate saying otherwise is one the College
would eventually have to defend. The rubrics already score criteria
separately, so the floors cost nothing to enforce.

**On Chancellor's Distinction — one thing must be settled first.**
WorldWide English College has no Chancellor. It has, deliberately, no
named academic leadership at all, and the standing rule against
fabricating leadership is not suspended for a good idea.

Two honest paths:

1. **Constitute the office.** A Chancellor is appointed, and the honour
   is conferred in that office's name. This is a real appointment with
   real duties, not a title for a certificate.
2. **Name it for the institution until then** — **the Distinction of the
   College** — and rename it when a Chancellor exists.

**Board recommendation: (2) now, (1) when there is a Chancellor.** An
honour issued in the name of an office nobody holds is a fiction printed
on a certificate, and it is exactly the kind of small untruth that costs
an institution its credibility when someone asks who the Chancellor is.

**Whichever name it carries, three rules hold:** it is conferred, never
calculated; it is capable of being conferred in **no** cycle at all; and
it is capped at a small number per cycle. An honour that can be withheld
is an honour. One that cannot is a formality.

## V. Ceremonies and conferral

**The Quarterly Conferral** — four times a year, online, for everyone
whose award was confirmed since the last. Frequent enough that nobody
waits long; rare enough to be an occasion.

**Named ceremonies**, in one consistent form so the series is
recognisable:

| Level | Ceremony |
|---|---|
| I | **The Conferral of Aspirants** |
| II | **The Conferral of Candidates** |
| III | **The Conferral of Associates** |
| IV | **The Conferral of Envoys** |
| V | **The Conferral of Orators** |
| VI | **The Laureation** |

The Laureation stands apart by name as well as by rank — it is the
College's principal ceremony of the year, and it should not sound like
the other five.

## VI. The traditions of WorldWide English College

### 1. The First Word

The Executive identified this as the most valuable idea in the proposal,
and the Board agrees: institutions become memorable through traditions,
not certificates.

At orientation, before any teaching, every learner records one sentence
in English. It is not assessed, not corrected, not shown to anyone. It is
kept.

At every subsequent conferral, the graduate hears their own First Word
played back immediately before the award is conferred. At the Laureation
it is played — and then the Laureate speaks.

It is built on infrastructure that already exists: `learner_recordings`,
the Listening Lab, and the object storage are all in place.

**The obligation it creates, stated with it rather than after it:** a
recording kept for years and replayed in public is personal data held
for a specific purpose. The learner must consent to *that* purpose at
the moment of recording — not by a clause buried in enrolment terms.
Governance **D1 and D2 must cover it before the first First Word is
taken.** A tradition founded on a consent failure is not a tradition, it
is a liability with ceremony attached.

### 2. Conferral in one's own voice

No IEFC award is conferred on written work alone. Every level requires a
**Level Address** — a recorded spoken piece, assessed, appropriate to the
level: sixty seconds at Aspirant, a persuasive address at Orator, a full
public address at Laureate.

Not ceremony. A certificate in English that never required the holder to
speak is one people are right to distrust. Requiring voice at every level
is what makes the award mean what its name says.

**Dependency: governance C3** currently declines to count speaking toward
certification, because no moderated marking standard exists. **This
architecture cannot be adopted without resolving C3.** The Level Address
is not decoration; it is the spine.

### 3. The Register of the Ascent

The permanent roll of every conferral. See §IX.

### 4. The Laureate Address

Each Laureation, one Laureate is invited by the Academic Board to address
the College. The address enters the Register and is kept.

A College with a hundred Laureates has a hundred addresses. That archive,
in twenty years, is the institution's most valuable asset — it cannot be
bought, only accumulated, which is the argument for beginning it with the
first Laureate rather than when it seems worth doing.

## VII. Academic regalia

Online delivery makes regalia largely **digital**, which the Board treats
as an advantage: a digital stole appears on every credential, every
certificate and every verification page, where a physical one is seen
once.

**The Ascent stole** — one design in six colourways, deepening as the
Ascent rises:

| Level | Colourway | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| I Aspirant | Pale gold on ivory | First light |
| II Candidate | Deeper gold on ivory | Recognition |
| III Associate | Gold on college blue | Belonging |
| IV Envoy | College blue, gold-edged | Representation |
| V Orator | Deep blue, double gold edge | Authority |
| VI Laureate | Deep blue, full gold, with the College device | Command |

**The Laureate's device** — a medallion, physical, sent to every
Laureate. It is the only physical object in the architecture, and it sits
at the summit deliberately: scarcity is what makes an object mean
something.

Where physical ceremonies are held, the stole is made in the same six
colourways, so a photograph from a hall and a credential on a screen are
recognisably the same tradition.

## VIII. Qualification descriptors, attributes and outcomes

### Descriptors

Written to be quotable by an employer or admissions officer, and
defensible if quoted back.

**Aspirant (ApWEC)** — Understands and uses familiar everyday
expressions and basic phrases; introduces themselves and others; asks and
answers questions about personal detail; interacts simply where the other
speaker talks slowly. *Beyond CEFR A1:* has demonstrated the study
discipline of the programme and works independently in the College's
digital environment.

**Candidate (CnWEC)** — Understands sentences and frequent expressions in
areas of immediate relevance; communicates in routine tasks requiring
direct exchange of information; describes their background and immediate
environment. *Beyond CEFR A2:* handles routine written digital
correspondence to a professional standard of courtesy and clarity.

**Associate (AsWEC)** — Understands the main points of clear standard
input on familiar matters; deals with most situations arising while
travelling; produces connected text; gives brief reasons for opinions and
plans. *Beyond CEFR B1:* structures and defends a position, and
recognises when a message has not been understood and repairs it — the
skill that separates the independent user from the confident one.

**Envoy (EnWEC)** — Understands the main ideas of complex text on
concrete and abstract topics, including technical discussion in their
field; interacts with fluency and spontaneity such that conversation with
a proficient speaker is comfortable for both; produces clear, detailed
text and explains a viewpoint with advantages and disadvantages. *Beyond
CEFR B2:* represents an organisation or a case in meetings and
correspondence; adapts register to audience and culture; chairs a
discussion to an outcome.

**Orator (OrWEC)** — Understands demanding, longer text and recognises
implicit meaning; expresses ideas fluently and spontaneously without
obvious searching; uses English flexibly for social, academic and
professional purposes; produces clear, well-structured, detailed text on
complex subjects. *Beyond CEFR C1:* constructs and delivers persuasive
argument to a sceptical audience; leads a team in English; reasons
publicly under challenge; gives and receives difficult feedback across
cultures.

**Laureate (LrWEC)** — Understands with ease virtually everything heard
or read; summarises information from different sources, reconstructing
arguments coherently; expresses themselves spontaneously, very fluently
and precisely, differentiating finer shades of meaning. *Beyond CEFR C2:*
sets communication standards for others; mentors and assesses the
communication of colleagues; represents an institution in its most
consequential exchanges; and has delivered the Laureate Address.

### Graduate attributes

Six, developed across all levels, certified to the standard of the level
attained. They are the spine of every rubric and every citation.

1. **Clarity** — is understood the first time, by the audience present
2. **Command** — controls the language rather than being carried by it
3. **Judgement** — chooses register, channel and moment; knows what not to say
4. **Reason** — constructs an argument, tests it, concedes what should be conceded
5. **Bearing** — holds a room, a call, a difficult conversation
6. **Reach** — communicates across cultures, and across the distance between expert and layperson

Attributes 3–6 are the ones the CEFR does not measure, and they are the
reason the IEFC is a distinct award rather than a repackaged proficiency
test.

### Outcomes

Stated as what the level **enables**, never as a promise of employment
or admission.

| Award | Typically enables |
|---|---|
| **Aspirant** | Travel and daily life in English-speaking settings; foundation for further study |
| **Candidate** | Customer-facing and service roles with routine English; routine written correspondence |
| **Associate** | Independent work in an English-speaking team; vocational study taught in English |
| **Envoy** | Professional roles requiring client or cross-border contact; the band commonly expected for undergraduate study taught in English |
| **Orator** | Leadership and client-facing seniority; postgraduate study taught in English; roles where persuasion is the work |
| **Laureate** | Executive and public-facing communication; teaching, mentoring and standard-setting |

**Required wording everywhere:** *the IEFC is not an entrance test and is
not accepted in place of one. Institutions and employers set their own
requirements, and many require a specific external test. Check theirs.*

## IX. The Register of the Ascent and the Laureates Roll

One register, not two. The Executive asked for a Global Laureates
Register; the Board recommends a single **Register of the Ascent**
covering every conferral, with the **Laureates Roll** as a distinguished
section within it, listed by year.

The reason is not economy. A register that lists only Laureates tells
every other graduate that their award was not worth recording — which
contradicts the load-bearing decision that each award is complete in
itself.

**The portal does four things:**

| For | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Employers** | Verify a credential at `/verify/<code>` — no account, no sign-up. A credential that requires the checker to register is one nobody checks |
| **Graduates** | A shareable page for the award, with the citation and the stole |
| **The College** | Issues digital badges to **Open Badges 3.0 / W3C Verifiable Credentials**, the open standards that make a credential portable rather than a picture |
| **The public** | The Laureates Roll by year — the College's visible tradition |

**Four rules the Board sets:**

- **Publication only with consent.** A graduate chooses whether their
  name appears; verification by code works either way.
- **A revoked or corrected award shows as revoked**, not vanished. A
  verification page that 404s tells a checker nothing; one that says
  "withdrawn on this date" tells them the institution is honest.
- **Verification never identifies the checker.** The whole value is that
  a stranger can check.
- **No personal data beyond the name consented to.**

Standards named above are an implementation intention, not a claim of
certification against them.

## X. Alumni: one Society, six Chapters

The Executive asked for six societies. The Board recommends **one Alumni
Society of WorldWide English College with six Chapters**, one per award,
and the Laureates Society as a distinct body within it.

**Why not six independent societies.** Six societies means six
committees, six calendars, six mailing lists and six sets of minutes, for
an institution that currently has no staff. Founded and then neglected,
they would become six pieces of evidence that the College does not
finish what it starts. One Society with six Chapters gives a graduate
exactly the same identity — *"I am a member of the Envoy Chapter"* — at a
fraction of the administrative surface, and Chapters can be given their
own officers as numbers justify it rather than in advance of them.

| Body | Membership | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| **The Alumni Society** | Any conferred award | Register entry; alumni communications; retained access to completed levels |
| **The Aspirant Chapter** | ApWEC | The above, and the Chapter's own gatherings |
| **The Candidate Chapter** | CnWEC | ” |
| **The Associate Chapter** | AsWEC | ” |
| **The Envoy Chapter** | EnWEC | ” |
| **The Orator Chapter** | OrWEC | ” |
| **The Laureates Society** | LrWEC | The above, the Laureates Roll, invitation to the Laureation, and eligibility to be invited to give the Address |

**Alumni standing begins at Level I.** A college whose alumni body
excludes the people who found it hardest has misunderstood what an alumni
body is for. The Aspirant who began at fifty with no English is the
College's best argument for its own existence.

**Chapter membership is by highest award held**, and a graduate moves
Chapter as they ascend — one identity that grows, not a collection.

**Retained access.** A graduate keeps access to the levels they
completed, indefinitely. It costs almost nothing and makes an award a
door that stays open. *(Requires approval: it is a commitment with a
hosting cost.)*

## XI. Citations

Every conferral carries a citation naming what *this* graduate did — not
a template with a name dropped in.

> *WorldWide English College confers upon **[Name]** the award of
> **[Title]**, with [honour], in recognition of [the specific
> achievement], demonstrated in [the assessed work], on [date].*

**Illustrative only — the names and details below are invented to show
the form and are not real graduates:**

> *WorldWide English College confers upon Aisha Rahman the award of
> English Orator of WorldWide English College, with High Distinction, in
> recognition of an address on urban water policy delivered without notes
> and defended under questioning, and of written work of consistent
> precision across the level. 14 March 2027.*

> *WorldWide English College confers upon Daniel Okonkwo the award of
> English Aspirant of WorldWide English College, in recognition of ten
> modules completed from no prior English, and of a first spoken address
> delivered in a language he did not speak eleven months ago.
> 14 March 2027.*

The second is the more important. An architecture that can only sound
impressive at the top has failed at the bottom, where most learners are
and where encouragement decides whether they continue.

## XII. After the Laureate — the post-programme Fellowships

The Executive's solution to "what comes after C2" is the right one, and
it resolves the Fellow question at the same time: **Fellow belongs to
advanced specialist credentials held by graduates**, which is what a
fellowship actually is.

These are **not Level VII**. The Ascent has six steps. Extending it later
would retrospectively demote every Laureate, so the Fellowships are a
parallel structure entered *after* the Ascent, not a continuation of it.

| Fellowship | Entry | Standing |
|---|---|---|
| **Fellowship in Executive Communication** | Laureate | Fellow of WorldWide English College in Executive Communication |
| **Fellowship in Academic Writing** | Laureate | Fellow … in Academic Writing |
| **Fellowship in Business Communication** | Laureate | Fellow … in Business Communication |

**The Professional Interpreter Pathway is held back, and the Board is
explicit about why.** Interpreting is a field with established
certification regimes, and the consequences of under-qualified
interpreting are borne by third parties — a patient in a consultation, a
defendant in a hearing — who never chose the College and cannot assess
it. WEC should not offer an interpreter credential until it has
qualified interpreter trainers, an assessment model built with them, and
a considered view on liability. It is the one item in this entire
document capable of causing harm to someone who is not a student, and it
is therefore the one item that must not be launched on enthusiasm.

Everything else about the Fellowships — post-nominals, assessment,
duration — is deferred until the Ascent itself is adopted and running.
Designing a second structure before the first has conferred a single
award would be the wrong order of work.

## XIV. Build order

1. **The Register and verification portal** — needs only decision 1, and
   every other artefact derives its worth from being checkable. An award
   nobody can verify is a decoration.
2. **The First Word** — infrastructure exists; needs decision 6.
3. **Certificate and credential issuance** — needs 1, 3, 5.
4. **The Level Address into assessment** — needs 2; the largest piece of
   work here.
5. **Ceremonies** — needs 8, and needs staff.
6. **Fellowships** — not before the Ascent has conferred its first
   awards.

The Board's view on sequence is unchanged: **build the Register first.**
