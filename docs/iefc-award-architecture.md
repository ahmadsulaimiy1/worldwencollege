# The IEFC Award Architecture

**A proposal of the Academic Board to the Executive.**

**Status: NOT ADOPTED.** No award below exists, none may be conferred,
and nothing here may appear on the public site until the Executive
approves it. Governance items C1–C3 (certification) remain open; this
document is what they are waiting for. It is registered as **C4** in
`docs/governance-decisions.md`.

---

## I. The three rules this architecture is built under

Before any title, three constraints. They are not disclaimers bolted on
at the end — they determine the whole design, and an architecture that
ignored them would be worthless the first time an informed person read
it.

### 1. These are institutional awards, not qualifications

WEC-LC confers these. They are not degrees, not regulated
qualifications, not professional-body grades, and not equivalent to any
of those. Any institution may confer its own awards; what it may not do
is dress them as something a regulator issued.

Everything that follows is designed so a graduate can display an award
with pride **and** with accuracy, and so that a stranger reading it can
tell exactly what it is. Those two goals are not in tension — the
architecture is *more* impressive for being unambiguous, not less.

### 2. "Beyond CEFR" means broader, never higher

The instruction was that the IEFC should stand above the CEFR. Taken
literally that is not a claim anyone can make: the CEFR is a proficiency
scale, C2 is its ceiling, and asserting a level above it would be a
benchmarking claim with nothing behind it. An informed applicant, a
competitor, or a university admissions officer would see through it
immediately, and the whole architecture would lose credibility on its
first serious reading.

So the design takes the instruction's real intent and makes it
defensible. **The CEFR certifies what a person can do with the
language. The IEFC certifies that, and six further domains the CEFR
does not address at all:**

| Strand | What the CEFR says about it |
|---|---|
| Leadership and executive communication | Nothing |
| Professional practice | Nothing |
| Critical thinking | Nothing |
| Intercultural competence | Touched only at the margins |
| Digital communication | Nothing (the framework predates it) |
| Academic excellence and real-world application | Nothing |

That is a stronger position than "above C2", and it is true. An IEFC
award states **both**: the CEFR band demonstrated, and the WEC strands
evidenced. A C2 certificate says a person's English is excellent. A
**Laureate of Worldwide English College** says their English is
excellent *and* that they have been assessed leading, persuading,
reasoning and operating across cultures in it.

The house formulation, to be used verbatim wherever this is described:

> *The IEFC is aligned to the CEFR and extends beyond its scope. CEFR
> bands describe language proficiency; the IEFC additionally certifies
> applied communicative capability across six professional strands.*

Never: "above the CEFR", "higher than C2", "exceeds CEFR level".

### 3. No claim of accreditation or recognition

Nothing here is accredited, recognised, validated, or equivalent to
anything. Where a document must describe the award's standing it says
what it is: *an award of Worldwide English College*. That is a complete
and honourable sentence.

## II. The Ascent — the six awards

The programme's tradition is called **the Ascent**: six conferrals, each
a completed thing in itself, forming one arc from a first sentence to a
public voice.

Each award is complete on its own. A learner who stops at Level II has
not failed to become something — they **are** an English Practitioner of
Worldwide English College, permanently, and the College says so in those
words. This is the load-bearing decision of the entire architecture: an
award that only means something as a step toward the next one is not an
award, it is a receipt.

| # | CEFR | Award | Post-nominal | The one-line claim |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | A1 | **English Aspirant of Worldwide English College** | AWEC | Has begun, in earnest, and can be understood |
| II | A2 | **English Practitioner of Worldwide English College** | PWEC | Uses English for the ordinary business of life |
| III | B1 | **English Communicator of Worldwide English College** | CWEC | Independent — handles the unfamiliar without rescue |
| IV | B2 | **English Envoy of Worldwide English College** | EWEC | Represents an organisation, a case, or themselves |
| V | C1 | **English Orator of Worldwide English College** | OWEC | Persuades, reasons and leads in English |
| VI | C2 | **English Laureate of Worldwide English College** | LWEC | Command — and the standing to teach and set standards |

### Why these six words

**Aspirant** (A1) — the aspirant has *chosen*. It honours the decision,
which at A1 is the achievement: beginning a language in adulthood is
harder than any later step and is where most people stop. It does not
overclaim; nobody reading it thinks the holder is fluent.

**Practitioner** (A2) — the shift from studying English to *using* it.
A practitioner does the thing.

**Communicator** (B1) — B1 is the independence threshold: the learner
who can handle a conversation that leaves the script. The word names
exactly that.

**Envoy** (B2) — B2 is where a person can be *sent*: to the meeting, to
the client, to the interview, to speak for someone other than
themselves. "Envoy" carries representation, which is precisely the B2
capability and precisely what an employer is buying.

**Orator** (C1) — chosen deliberately over "Advocate", "Professional"
and "Scholar". C1 is composition *and* delivery — clear, well-structured
argument on complex subjects, delivered fluently — which is oratory in
the classical sense. It is also the level where the IEFC's leadership
and executive-communication strands become the substance of the award
rather than an addition to it. The word is justified by the programme's
extension beyond the CEFR, which is exactly what the extension is for.

**Laureate** (C2) — the crown. Conferred, not earned by attendance;
associated with distinction rather than administration; and — critically
— **not confusable with a degree**. "English Master" was considered and
rejected outright: a reasonable person would read it as a master's
degree, and an architecture whose summit is a misunderstanding is not an
architecture.

### Words the Board considered and rejected, and why

Recording these so the decisions are not relitigated from scratch, and
so the reasoning is auditable:

| Rejected | Reason |
|---|---|
| **Fellow** | In a college, a Fellow is ordinarily **academic staff**. "Fellow of Worldwide English College" would imply the holder teaches here. It also borrows the senior grade of professional bodies. Both are misleading, and the second is the kind of borrowed prestige that collapses under scrutiny |
| **Candidate** | A candidate is someone *in progress*. Naming a completed milestone "Candidate" tells a graduate they have not arrived — the opposite of the brief |
| **Master** | Reads as a master's degree. Non-negotiable rejection |
| **Advocate** | A protected professional title for lawyers in South Africa, India, Scandinavia and elsewhere. A WEC graduate writing "Advocate" could mislead materially |
| **Professional** | Abbreviates to "Prof." — collision with Professor. The full word is also flat beside the rest of the set |
| **Associate / Member** | The grade ladder of professional bodies. Imitation, and imitation of the least distinctive part |
| **Apprentice / Journeyman** | Trade-guild register. Diminishing for adult professionals |
| **Ambassador** | Diplomatic office; also now debased by marketing use ("brand ambassador") |

### The post-nominals

**House form: `[initial]WEC`** — AWEC, PWEC, CWEC, EWEC, OWEC, LWEC.

The formula is the tradition. One letter for the award, three for the
College, and by the sixth conferral the pattern is unmistakably
WEC's — the way a distinctive post-nominal ought to work. The six
initials are deliberately distinct, which is why **Envoy** occupies
Level IV: "Professional" would have collided with "Practitioner" on P.

**Correct display forms**, in descending formality:

```
Aisha Rahman, English Orator of Worldwide English College
Aisha Rahman, OWEC (Worldwide English College)
Aisha Rahman, OWEC
```

**Required of the College, in every template it issues:** the first use
in any document spells the award in full and names the issuer. A
post-nominal whose issuer nobody can identify is the beginning of a
misrepresentation, whoever commits it.

**Before publication:** a designation and trade-mark search on all six
strings. The Board is not aware of collisions; "not aware of" is not
"has checked", and the difference matters before these are printed.

## III. Prestige hierarchy

The awards rank in level order, I lowest to VI highest. Within a level,
honours rank Distinction above Merit above Pass.

Two rules keep the hierarchy honest:

**A higher award supersedes but does not erase.** A Laureate does not
stop having been an Aspirant; the Register records every conferral with
its date. The Ascent is a record of a journey, not a single current
status.

**Only the highest is displayed.** House guidance: use one post-nominal,
the highest held. Stacking them ("AWEC, PWEC, CWEC") reads as inflation
and diminishes all six.

## IV. Honours: Pass, Merit, Distinction

**Awaiting governance B1/B2** — the thresholds below are proposals, and
until a pass mark is adopted no honour can be conferred at all.

| Honour | Proposed criteria |
|---|---|
| **Pass** | Meets the level's assessed standard on every criterion |
| **Merit** | Meets the standard, and reaches the Merit threshold overall, with no criterion at bare Pass |
| **Distinction** | Meets the standard, reaches the Distinction threshold overall, no criterion below Merit, **and** the Level Address is assessed at Distinction |

**Proposed thresholds:** Pass 70%, Merit 80%, Distinction 90% overall,
with the floors above.

**The reasoning behind the floors, which matters more than the numbers:**
an aggregate alone lets a learner take a Distinction while failing
outright on one dimension. A graduate who writes brilliantly and cannot
be understood aloud has not mastered English, and a certificate saying
otherwise is one the College would eventually have to defend. The
rubrics already score criteria separately, so the floor costs nothing to
enforce.

**At Level VI only**, one further honour:

**Laureate with the Commendation of the College** — conferred by the
Academic Board, not calculated. Reserved for work of a standard the
College wishes to hold up as exemplary. Not automatic at any mark, and
capable of being conferred in no year at all. An honour that is always
awarded is a formality; one that can be withheld is an honour.

## V. Qualification descriptors

What a holder of each award can do. Written to be quotable by an
employer or an admissions officer, and to be defensible if quoted back.

**English Aspirant (AWEC)** — Understands and uses familiar everyday
expressions and basic phrases. Introduces themselves and others, asks
and answers questions about personal detail. Interacts simply where the
other speaker talks slowly and is willing to help. *Beyond CEFR A1:* has
demonstrated the study discipline of the programme and can use the
College's digital learning environment independently.

**English Practitioner (PWEC)** — Understands sentences and frequent
expressions in areas of immediate relevance. Communicates in simple,
routine tasks requiring direct exchange of information. Describes their
background, immediate environment and matters of immediate need.
*Beyond CEFR A2:* handles routine written digital correspondence to a
professional standard of courtesy and clarity.

**English Communicator (CWEC)** — Understands the main points of clear
standard input on familiar matters. Deals with most situations likely to
arise while travelling. Produces connected text on familiar topics,
describes experiences, and gives brief reasons for opinions and plans.
*Beyond CEFR B1:* structures and defends a position, and recognises when
a message has not been understood and repairs it — the skill that
separates the independent user from the confident one.

**English Envoy (EWEC)** — Understands the main ideas of complex text
on both concrete and abstract topics, including technical discussion in
their field. Interacts with fluency and spontaneity such that
conversation with a proficient speaker is comfortable for both. Produces
clear, detailed text and explains a viewpoint with advantages and
disadvantages. *Beyond CEFR B2:* represents an organisation or a case in
meetings and correspondence; adapts register to audience and culture;
chairs a discussion to an outcome.

**English Orator (OWEC)** — Understands demanding, longer text and
recognises implicit meaning. Expresses ideas fluently and spontaneously
without obvious searching. Uses English flexibly and effectively for
social, academic and professional purposes. Produces clear,
well-structured, detailed text on complex subjects. *Beyond CEFR C1:*
constructs and delivers persuasive argument to a hostile or sceptical
audience; leads a team in English; reasons publicly under challenge;
gives and receives difficult feedback across cultures.

**English Laureate (LWEC)** — Understands with ease virtually
everything heard or read. Summarises information from different spoken
and written sources, reconstructing arguments and accounts coherently.
Expresses themselves spontaneously, very fluently and precisely,
differentiating finer shades of meaning in complex situations. *Beyond
CEFR C2:* sets communication standards for others; mentors and assesses
the communication of colleagues; represents an institution in its most
consequential exchanges; and has delivered the Laureate Address to the
College.

## VI. Graduate attributes

Six attributes, developed across all levels, each certified to the
standard of the level attained. They are the spine of every rubric and
every citation, and they are what makes an IEFC award describe a person
rather than a score.

1. **Clarity** — is understood the first time, by the audience actually present
2. **Command** — controls the language rather than being carried by it
3. **Judgement** — chooses register, channel and moment; knows what not to say
4. **Reason** — constructs an argument, tests it, and concedes what should be conceded
5. **Bearing** — holds a room, a call, a difficult conversation
6. **Reach** — communicates across cultures, and across the distance between expert and layperson

The Board notes: attributes 3, 4, 5 and 6 are the ones the CEFR does not
measure, and they are the reason the IEFC is a distinct award rather
than a repackaged proficiency test.

## VII. Career and academic outcomes

Stated as **what the level enables**, never as a promise of employment
or admission. No institution can promise either, and a graduate who was
promised one will remember it.

| Award | Typically enables |
|---|---|
| **Aspirant** | Travel and daily life in English-speaking settings; foundation for further study |
| **Practitioner** | Customer-facing and service roles with routine English; routine written correspondence |
| **Communicator** | Independent work in an English-speaking team; vocational study taught in English; a threshold many employers set for general roles |
| **Envoy** | Professional roles requiring client or cross-border contact; the band commonly expected for undergraduate study taught in English |
| **Orator** | Leadership and client-facing seniority; postgraduate study taught in English; roles where persuasion is the work |
| **Laureate** | Executive and public-facing communication; teaching, mentoring and standard-setting; the highest band any framework describes |

**Required wording, everywhere:** *the IEFC is not an entrance test and
is not accepted in place of one. Institutions and employers set their
own requirements, and many require a specific external test. Check
theirs.* Anything short of that would let a graduate believe an award
opens a door it does not.

## VIII. Progression pathways

- **The Ascent** — I → VI in sequence. The intended path.
- **Entry by placement** — a learner may enter at the level their
  placement assessment supports, and receives every award from that
  level upward. The College does not confer awards for levels it has not
  taught and assessed. *(Placement policy: not yet drafted.)*
- **Pause and return** — an award already conferred is permanent. A
  learner who stops after Level III and returns three years later
  resumes at Level IV; nothing lapses. **Recommendation: awards do not
  expire.** An expiring award is a rental, and the Ascent's premise is
  that each conferral is a permanent fact about a person.
- **Beyond Level VI** — the Laureate is the summit of the IEFC. Any
  further offering (an instructor pathway, a specialist certificate)
  would be a *separate* award structure and must not be presented as
  Level VII. The Ascent has six steps; extending it later would
  retrospectively demote every Laureate.

## IX. Conferral, ceremonies and the College's traditions

The instruction was that every level should feel like a graduation. That
requires a *moment*, and a moment requires a fixed occasion, a form of
words, and something the graduate keeps.

### The conferral cycle

**Quarterly Conferral** — held four times a year, online, for everyone
whose award was confirmed since the last. Frequent enough that nobody
waits long; rare enough to be an occasion. *(Frequency requires
Executive approval — it has a staffing cost every quarter.)*

**Named ceremonies:**

| Levels | Ceremony |
|---|---|
| I–II | **The Threshold** — for those who have begun and are now using English |
| III–IV | **The Passage** — for independence and representation |
| V | **The Rostrum** — for those who now persuade |
| VI | **The Laureation** — the College's principal ceremony of the year |

### Four traditions particular to Worldwide English College

**1. The First Word.**

At orientation, before any teaching, every learner records one sentence
in English. It is not assessed, not corrected, and not shown to anyone.
It is kept.

At every subsequent conferral, the graduate hears their own First Word
played back immediately before the award is conferred. At the Laureation
it is played, and then the Laureate speaks.

This is the College's signature tradition, and it is deliberately built
on what the platform already does: `learner_recordings` and the
Listening Lab exist, the storage exists, and the retention question is
already open as governance D1. Nothing else in this document is as cheap
to implement or as impossible to forget.

*The Board notes the obligation this creates: a recording kept for years
and replayed in public is personal data with a specific purpose, and the
learner must consent to that purpose at the moment of recording, not by
a term buried in enrolment. Governance D1 and D2 must cover it before
the first First Word is taken.*

**2. Conferral in one's own voice.**

No IEFC award is conferred on written work alone. Every level requires a
**Level Address** — a recorded spoken piece, assessed, of a length and
kind appropriate to the level: sixty seconds at Aspirant, a persuasive
address at Orator, a full public address at Laureate.

The reason is not ceremony. It is that a certificate in English which
never required the holder to speak is a certificate people are right to
distrust. Requiring voice at every level is what makes the award mean
what its name says.

*Dependency: governance C3 currently declines to count speaking toward
certification because no moderated marking standard exists. **This
architecture cannot be adopted without resolving C3** — the Level
Address is not decoration, it is the spine.*

**3. The Register of the Ascent.**

A permanent roll of every conferral: name, award, honour, date,
verification code. Published only with consent, verifiable by anyone
holding a code. The Register is the institutional memory, and it is what
makes a WEC award checkable by a stranger — which is the difference
between a credential and a picture of one.

**4. The Laureate Address.**

Each Laureation, one Laureate is invited by the Academic Board to
address the College. The address enters the Register and is kept.

A College with a hundred Laureates has a hundred addresses. That
archive, in twenty years, is the institution's most valuable asset and
cannot be bought, only accumulated. It is the reason to start it now,
with the first Laureate, rather than when it seems worth doing.

### Academic regalia

Online delivery means regalia is largely **digital**, and the Board
treats that as a design opportunity rather than a compromise: a digital
stole appears on every credential, every certificate and every
verification page, where a physical one is seen once.

**The Ascent stole** — a single design in six colourways, deepening as
the Ascent rises:

| Level | Colourway | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| I Aspirant | Pale gold on ivory | First light |
| II Practitioner | Deeper gold on ivory | Warmth, use |
| III Communicator | Gold on college blue | The turn to independence |
| IV Envoy | College blue, gold-edged | Representation |
| V Orator | Deep blue, double gold edge | Authority |
| VI Laureate | Deep blue, full gold, with the College device | Command |

**The Laureate's device** — a medallion, physical, sent to every
Laureate. It is the one physical object in the architecture, and it is
at the summit deliberately: scarcity is what makes an object mean
something.

**Where physical ceremonies are held**, the stole is made in the same
six colourways, so that a photograph from a hall and a credential on a
screen are recognisably the same tradition.

## X. Award citations

Every conferral carries a citation naming what *this* graduate did. Not
a template with a name dropped in — a sentence a stranger could not
have written about anyone else.

**Form:**

> *Worldwide English College confers upon **[Name]** the award of
> **[Title]**, with [honour], in recognition of [the specific
> achievement], demonstrated in [the assessed work], on [date].*

**Illustrative — the names and details below are invented for the
purpose of showing the form, and are not real graduates:**

> *Worldwide English College confers upon Aisha Rahman the award of
> English Orator of Worldwide English College, with Distinction, in
> recognition of an address on urban water policy delivered without
> notes and defended under questioning, and of written work of
> consistent precision across the level. 14 March 2027.*

> *Worldwide English College confers upon Daniel Okonkwo the award of
> English Aspirant of Worldwide English College, in recognition of ten
> modules completed from no prior English, and of a first spoken address
> delivered in a language he did not speak eleven months ago.
> 14 March 2027.*

The second citation is the more important of the two. An architecture
that can only sound impressive at the top has failed at the bottom,
where most learners are and where encouragement decides whether they
continue.

## XI. Digital credentials and verification

**Every award issues four artefacts:**

1. **The Certificate** — PDF. Name, award, honour, CEFR band, strands,
   citation, date, issuer, verification code. Nothing else — every
   additional claim on a certificate is a claim somebody may one day
   have to substantiate.
2. **The Credential** — a shareable digital badge carrying the same
   data as structured, machine-readable metadata.
3. **The Register entry** — a public verification page at
   `/verify/<code>`, resolving to award, honour and date. **No personal
   data beyond the name the graduate consented to publish**, and
   verifiable without an account, because a credential that requires the
   checker to sign up is one nobody checks.
4. **The Transcript** — issued on request: every level, every honour,
   every date, and the CEFR band attained.

**Standards.** The credential should be issued to **Open Badges 3.0 /
W3C Verifiable Credentials**, which are the open standards for exactly
this and are what makes a credential portable rather than a picture.
This is an implementation intention, not a claim of certification
against those standards.

**Two rules the Board sets on verification:**

- **A revoked or corrected award must show as revoked**, not vanish. A
  verification page that 404s tells a checker nothing; one that says
  "withdrawn on this date" tells them the institution is honest.
- **Verification never requires the checker to identify themselves.**
  The whole value is that a stranger can check.

## XII. Alumni status

**Alumni standing begins at Level I.** Anyone who has been conferred any
IEFC award is an alumnus of Worldwide English College, permanently.

The Board considered restricting alumni standing to Level VI and rejected
it decisively. A college whose alumni body excludes the people who found
it hardest has misunderstood what an alumni body is for. The Aspirant who
began at fifty with no English is the College's best argument for its own
existence.

| Standing | From | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| **Alumnus of the College** | Any award | Register entry; alumni communications; the College's continued interest |
| **Alumnus in the Ascent** | Any award, still progressing | The above, and continued access to their completed levels |
| **Laureate of the College** | Level VI | The above, the Laureates' body, invitation to the Laureation, and eligibility to be invited to give the Address |

**Retained access.** A graduate keeps access to the levels they
completed, indefinitely. It costs the College almost nothing and it
means an award is a door that stays open. *(Requires Executive approval:
it is a commitment with a hosting cost.)*

## XIV. What the Board recommends is built first

Sequenced so that nothing is built before the decision it depends on:

1. **The Register and verification page** — needs only decision 1, and
   is what makes every later artefact checkable.
2. **The First Word** — the infrastructure exists; needs decision 5.
3. **Certificate and credential issuance** — needs 1, 3 and 4.
4. **The Level Address into assessment** — needs 2, and is the largest
   piece of work here.
5. **Ceremonies** — needs 6, and needs staff.

The Board's view on sequence: build the **Register first**. An award
nobody can verify is a decoration, and every other artefact in this
document derives its worth from being checkable.
