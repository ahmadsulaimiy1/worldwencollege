## Module 1: Meeting People — full build

### Module overview

Module 1 is the learner's first contact with the programme. It
establishes the two most foundational structures in English (`to be`
and basic question formation) through the single highest-utility
task a beginner has: introducing themselves and finding out about
someone else. Everything in this module is scaffolded — no
unsupported production is required until the module's final speaking
assignment.

**Module learning objectives.** By the end of Module 1, the learner
can: greet someone appropriately for the time of day; introduce
themselves (name, nationality, where they live); ask someone else the
same questions; spell their name aloud using the English alphabet;
use subject pronouns and `to be` correctly in simple statements and
questions.

**Content map (5 learning items):**
1. Module overview & key phrases (reference reading)
2. Lesson 1.1 — "Hello! Introducing Yourself" (full lesson)
3. Lesson 1.2 — "Where Are You From?" (full lesson)
4. Module 1 Quiz — 8 questions, auto-graded
5. Module 1 Speaking Assignment — "Introduce Yourself" (staff-graded)

### Reference reading: Module overview & key phrases

**Key phrases introduced this module:**
Hello / Hi / Good morning / Good afternoon / Good evening / Goodbye /
See you later — What's your name? — My name is... / I'm... — Nice to
meet you. — Where are you from? — I'm from... — I live in... — How do
you spell that? — Can you repeat that, please?

**Alphabet note:** learners are expected to recite the English
alphabet and spell their own name aloud by the end of this module —
this is drilled explicitly in Lesson 1.1's pronunciation segment.

### Lesson 1.1 — "Hello! Introducing Yourself"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson, the learner can:
(1) greet someone appropriately using a time-of-day-appropriate
greeting; (2) say their own name using `My name is.../I'm...`; (3) ask
someone else's name using `What's your name?`; (4) spell their name
aloud using the English alphabet.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** None — this is the learner's first lesson.
No prior English is assumed.

**Warm-up (5 min).** Instructor greets each learner individually at
the door with "Hello! Good morning!" (or time-appropriate greeting),
modelling the response before class begins. Open class by writing
today's date and greeting time-of-day on the board; elicit which
greeting fits (Good morning / Good afternoon / Good evening).

**Presentation (10 min).** Introduce the target language via a simple
dialogue between two puppets/pictures ("A" and "B"):
> A: Hello! My name is Sofia.
> B: Hi, Sofia! I'm Daniel. Nice to meet you.
> A: Nice to meet you too!

Drill each line chorally, then in pairs. Write `My name is ___` / `I'm
___` on the board, highlighting they mean the same thing. Introduce
`What's your name?` as the question that gets this answer, with rising
intonation modelled clearly.

**Guided practice (10 min).** Chain drill around the room: each
learner asks the person next to them "What's your name?", that person
answers and then asks the next person. Instructor monitors and
corrects pronunciation/word order only (not fluency) at this stage.

**Independent practice (10 min).** "Find Someone Who" mingling
activity: learners stand and greet 5 different classmates, exchanging
name and a greeting, with no instructor modelling mid-activity.

**Speaking activity.** The mingling activity above **is** this
lesson's speaking activity — fully unscripted after the guided-practice
stage, the first genuinely independent speaking of the programme.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Play (or read aloud, varying voice) 3
short recorded-style greeting exchanges at different times of day;
learners circle the correct time-of-day greeting used (morning/
afternoon/evening) on a simple worksheet.

**Reading activity (5 min).** Learners read a very short 4-line
dialogue (same structure as the presentation dialogue, new names) and
underline every greeting phrase and every `to be` form.

**Writing task (5 min).** Learners complete a 3-line gapped
self-introduction template: `Hello! My ___ is ___. I'm ___.` — filling
in their own real name twice, correctly.

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Choral and individual drilling of
the English alphabet, then pair work: each learner spells their own
first name aloud to a partner, who writes it down and checks it back.
Focus sounds: the vowel letters (A, E, I, O, U said as letter-names,
not phonemes) and commonly confused consonant letters (G/J, S/Z).

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** Flashcard drill (or realia/mime) for:
hello, hi, goodbye, good morning/afternoon/evening, name, nice to meet
you.

**Formative assessment.** Instructor observation checklist during
guided/independent practice: can the learner (a) produce a greeting
unprompted, (b) state their name using either target form, (c) ask
"What's your name?" with correct word order and intonation. No formal
grade — feeds into the module's overall progress tracking.

**Homework.** Learners practise introducing themselves to one person
outside class (a family member, in English if possible, or by writing
the introduction if no English speaker is available) and bring one
new self-chosen "nice to meet you" partner name to share next lesson.

**Revision.** First 5 minutes of Lesson 1.2 recap this lesson's target
language before new material is presented (see Lesson 1.2's warm-up).

**Extension activity (for early finishers / stronger learners).**
Learners write and act out a 4-line dialogue introducing two
puppets/toys to each other, using both `My name is` and `I'm`.

### Lesson 1.2 — "Where Are You From?"

**Learning objectives.** By the end of this lesson, the learner can:
(1) ask and answer `Where are you from?`; (2) name their own country
correctly in English; (3) use `I'm from ___` and `I live in ___`
correctly and distinguish the two; (4) recognise and produce at least
6 country/nationality word pairs.

**Prerequisite knowledge.** Lesson 1.1 (greetings, `My name is/I'm`,
`What's your name?`).

**Warm-up (5 min).** Rapid-fire recap chain drill of Lesson 1.1's
question ("What's your name?") around the room, then instructor asks
"Where am I from?" pointing to a labelled world map with their own
home country marked, modelling "I'm from ___" before learners have
seen the target language formally — pure noticing, not yet production.

**Presentation (10 min).** Extend the Lesson 1.1 dialogue:
> A: Hi, I'm Sofia. Where are you from?
> B: I'm from Brazil. I live in São Paulo. Where are you from?
> A: I'm from Italy.

Highlight on the board: `I'm from + country` vs. `I live in + city`
are different and not interchangeable. Introduce 6-8 country/
nationality word pairs relevant to the class's actual nationalities
plus 2-3 common ones not represented (e.g. Brazil/Brazilian, Italy/
Italian, Japan/Japanese, Nigeria/Nigerian, Saudi Arabia/Saudi, the
United Kingdom/British) — country names only are required for
production at this level; the nationality-adjective forms are
receptive (recognition) only, not yet tested for production.

**Guided practice (10 min).** Pair work with a simple country-name
card each: Learner A asks "Where are you from?", Learner B answers
using the country on their card ("I'm from ___"), then swap cards and
repeat with 3 different cards each.

**Independent practice (10 min).** Whole-class mingling: learners ask
and answer both "What's your name?" AND "Where are you from?" with 5
different classmates, this time using their own real information for
both questions — integrating both lessons' language for the first
time.

**Speaking activity.** The independent-practice mingling above.

**Listening activity (5 min).** Instructor reads 4 short
self-introductions aloud (name + country + city); learners complete a
simple matching worksheet (name → country → city).

**Reading activity (5 min).** Learners read 4 short profile cards
(name, country, city, one extra fact) and answer 3 simple
comprehension questions ("Where is Daniel from?").

**Writing task (5 min).** Learners write a 4-line self-introduction
combining both lessons: greeting + name + country + city, using the
model: `Hello! My name is ___. I'm from ___. I live in ___.`

**Pronunciation practice (5 min).** Drill the specific stress pattern
of country names that differ from learners' L1 pronunciation (teacher
selects 3-4 relevant to the actual class); drill rising intonation on
`Where are you from?` vs. falling intonation on the statement answer.

**Vocabulary reinforcement.** Country/nationality pair matching game
(cards or simple worksheet) using the 6-8 pairs introduced above.

**Formative assessment.** Exit ticket: each learner says one sentence
(their own name + country + city) to the instructor individually on
the way out — a 10-second spoken check, not a formal grade.

**Homework.** Learners look up and write down (in English) the
capital city of one country introduced in class that isn't their own,
for a brief class share at the start of Module 1's next lesson.

**Revision.** This lesson opens with recap of Lesson 1.1 (see Lesson
1.1's Revision note above) and closes Module 1's core input — Module 1
Quiz and the Speaking Assignment (below) both draw only on Lessons 1.1
and 1.2's content.

**Extension activity.** Stronger learners produce a short 4-line
dialogue for two new (invented) people meeting for the first time,
using all four target questions/statements from both lessons.

### Module 1 Quiz (auto-graded, 8 questions)

*Seeded verbatim into `quiz_questions` — see
`sql/seed-curriculum-level-1.sql`. Answer key marked with ✓.*

1. "___ name is Sofia." — (a) I (b) My ✓ (c) Me (d) Mine
2. "___ you from Brazil?" — (a) Are ✓ (b) Is (c) Am (d) Be
3. "I ___ from Nigeria." — (a) is (b) are (c) am ✓ (d) be
4. "Nice to ___ you." — (a) meet ✓ (b) meeting (c) met (d) meets
5. It is 8 o'clock in the evening. What do you say? — (a) Good morning
   (b) Good afternoon (c) Good evening ✓ (d) Good night-time
6. "What's your ___?" (asking for someone's name) — (a) from (b) name
   ✓ (c) live (d) meet
7. "I'm from Italy. I ___ in Rome." — (a) am (b) live ✓ (c) from
   (d) is
8. Which question asks about someone's country? — (a) What's your
   name? (b) How are you? (c) Where are you from? ✓ (d) Nice to meet
   you?

### Module 1 Speaking Assignment — "Introduce Yourself"

*Staff-graded via `POST /api/lms/grade-assignment`, matching the
framework's "1-minute prepared self-introduction speech" element.*

**Instructions given to the learner:** Record yourself (or perform
live for your instructor) a short spoken self-introduction, 30-60
seconds long. Include: a greeting appropriate to the time of day, your
name, the country you are from, and the city you live in. Speak
clearly and try to use full sentences from this module.

**Grading rubric (for the instructor):**
- **Content completeness** (all 4 required elements present)
- **Grammatical accuracy** (`to be` forms, `I'm from`/`I live in`
  distinction)
- **Pronunciation & intelligibility** (greeting stress, country-name
  pronunciation drilled in Lesson 1.2)
- **Delivery** (audible, reasonably fluent for A1, not read
  word-by-word from a script)

A grade at or above `platform_config.lms_pass_threshold` marks Module
1 as completed for that learner, per `functions/_lib/lms/content.js`'s
existing, already-tested grading mechanism (see
`docs/lms-architecture.md`) — this assignment is real, gradable content
running on real platform logic, not a design mock-up.

## Scope and next steps — stated honestly

**Built to full depth this milestone:** Module 1 ("Meeting People") of
Level I — two complete lesson plans, one real answer-keyed quiz, one
real staff-gradable assignment, all seeded as actual rows in
`sql/seed-curriculum-level-1.sql` and verified to work end-to-end
through the live LMS endpoints (`tests/curriculum-level-1.test.mjs` —
see that file for exactly what was checked: the quiz scores correctly
against the real answer key, the assignment can be submitted and
graded, and a passing grade marks the unit complete).

**Framework-level only, not yet lesson-by-lesson:** Modules 2-10 of
Level I (table above) have their can-do focus, grammar, and vocabulary
targets defined, but not yet full lesson plans, quizzes, or seeded
content — that is real, substantial authorial work, and doing it
honestly (to the standard Module 1 sets) takes real time per module. I
have not seeded placeholder or lower-quality content for modules 2-10
to create an appearance of completeness; they stay genuinely
unbuilt until authored to the same standard.

**Not yet started:** Levels II-VI's lesson-by-lesson content
(framework-level detail for all six levels exists in
`curriculum-framework.md`).

**Recommended next step:** continue authoring Level I's remaining nine
modules to this same depth before moving to Level II — finishing one
level completely is more valuable than thin coverage across several,
both pedagogically (a real, usable Foundation-level course) and for
proving the authoring-and-seeding pipeline at scale before repeating it
five more times.
