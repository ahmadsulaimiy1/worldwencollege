# IEFC Recording Pack

**For the person recording the audio. Everything here is generated from
the curriculum database, so it matches exactly what the platform will
look for.**

---

## What this job actually is

Read scripts aloud into a microphone, in a quiet room. That is the whole
job. There is no filming, no music, no sound design, and no studio
booking required.

| | |
|---|---|
| Clips to record | **120** |
| Total words | ~7,534 |
| Reading time at the specified rates | ~60 minutes |
| Realistic session time, including retakes and direction | **~3 hours** |
| Clips needing two or more voices | 54 |
| Language varieties required | British English |

Two people, two sittings. That is the honest size of the thing that has
been described as "needing a studio" — and the description was mine, and
it was wrong enough to make the job look bigger than it is.

## What you need

- **A quiet room.** Soft furnishings, no air conditioning running, no
  traffic. A bedroom with curtains and a wardrobe beats an office.
- **A decent microphone.** A USB condenser microphone is sufficient. A
  phone held close is better than an expensive microphone across a
  reverberant room, because the enemy here is echo, not fidelity.
- **A pop filter**, or a sock over the microphone. Plosives on `p` and
  `b` ruin an otherwise good take, and learners are listening to
  exactly those sounds.
- **Free recording software.** Audacity or Ocenaudio will do all of it.

## What the recordings must be

These are teaching materials, not a podcast. The requirements are
unusual and each has a reason:

- **Clean, not produced.** No music, no reverb, no compression that
  smooths consonants. A learner at A1 is straining to hear the
  difference between two similar sounds; anything smeared over them
  defeats the lesson.
- **At the stated speed, not slower.** Each clip carries a target
  words-per-minute figure. Do not slow down to be kind — the whole
  point of the listening strand is coping with real pace. If a clip
  says 90 wpm, 90 wpm is the lesson.
- **Natural, not over-enunciated.** Say "wanna" if that is what a
  speaker says. Several lessons specifically teach connected speech,
  and a careful reading destroys the feature being taught.
- **One take per clip, one file per clip.** Do not concatenate.
- **WAV, 48 kHz, 16-bit or better, mono** unless the clip specifies
  more than one speaker, in which case still mono — the voices should
  be distinguishable by voice, not by ear position.
- **Half a second of silence** at the start and end of every file. Not
  more; the platform handles the pause.

## Filenames

One file per clip, named exactly with the clip ID given in the tables
below, `.wav` appended. So `aud_l1_m1_listen` becomes:

    aud_l1_m1_listen.wav

Nothing else — no spaces, no level prefix, no version number. The
platform matches on the ID.

## About the `|` marks in the scripts

A vertical bar separates **speaker turns**, not sentences. In a
two-voice clip the turns alternate: first turn voice A, second voice B,
and so on. In a one-voice clip the bar marks a distinct item that needs
a clear gap after it — leave about a second.
