# abisulaimiycollege.com.ng — Content Audit

Every problem found in the 16 August 2026 capture, with its location and
what it would take to fix. Ordered by consequence: the things that could
harm the College first, the things that merely look unfinished last.

**Headline.** The writing is strong — the certification framework, the
admissions rules and the founder's welcome are substantial, coherent
documents. What undermines them is that the site cannot be *acted on*: a
prospective student cannot find a price, cannot start an application,
cannot phone anyone, and cannot find a working address for any of it. On
top of that sit a small number of claims — about affiliation, faculty
credentials, and legal rights — that carry real risk if republished
unchecked.

| Severity | Count | Nature |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Critical** | 6 | Unverified claims and legal exposure |
| **High** | 8 | The site cannot be acted on |
| **Medium** | 11 | Contradictions and structural gaps |
| **Low** | 9 | Typos, wrong headings, style |

## Critical

### C1. The affiliation claim is unsubstantiated

*Home › Why Choose Us, and Home › Affiliations.*

> "We are affiliated with institutions such as **Haramain International
> University**, with more partnerships in progress, allowing students to
> pursue further education."

A public search returns no university of that name — the nearest matches
are Haramain Academy (an Islamic sciences academy in India) and Haramaya
University (Ethiopia). This does not prove the partner does not exist, but
it does mean a prospective student cannot verify the claim, which is the
whole point of publishing it.

This is the most consequential claim on the site. A student may choose the
College, and pay, because they believe a credential will be accepted
elsewhere. If that acceptance is not documented, the College has induced a
decision it cannot honour — and it has done so on the same site that
promises, in § 3.1, not to issue certificates that aren't real.

**Fix.** Do not republish until there is a signed partnership agreement on
file naming the partner, and stating precisely what it recognises, for whom
and for what purpose. If no such agreement exists yet, say so plainly:
"partnerships in progress" alone, with no institution named.

### C2. Certificate recognition is claimed two incompatible ways

The site says all three of the following:

| Where | Claim |
| --- | --- |
| Home › Why Choose Us | Certificates are "**to be** recognized by respected Islamic educational institutions" — a future intention |
| EDIC key features | "**Internationally recognised** certificates" — a present fact |
| FAQ 15 | "Our certificates and diplomas **are recognised** by relevant institutions and organisations. We ensure that our qualifications meet international standards" |

One of these is true. Publishing all three means the strongest one is what a
reader takes away, and the weakest one is what the College can actually
defend.

**Fix.** Pick the claim that documentary evidence supports and use its exact
wording everywhere. If recognition is aspirational, the FAQ and the EDIC
page must be rewritten, not just the home page.

### C3. Faculty credentials include claims that do not survive a basic check

*Faculty & Staff.* These are specific, checkable assertions made against
named universities. Several have problems visible without any
investigation:

| Person | Claim | Problem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founder | ESL professional, "**University of Millerville, USA**" | No institution of that name exists. Millersville University of Pennsylvania is presumably meant |
| Dr. Yusuf Abdul-Malik | "PhD in English Linguistics from **Oxford University**" | The institution is the University of Oxford, and it awards a **DPhil**, not a PhD. Someone who holds the degree does not describe it this way |
| Shaykh Talal Al-Farsi | MA from "**University of Kuwait**" | The institution is **Kuwait University** |
| Dr. Ahmed Al-Farsi | "**Islamic University of Madinah**" in the credential list, "**the University of Medina**" in the prose | Two names, one paragraph apart |
| Shaykh Abdur Rohim | "**Qur'an International University**" | Not identifiable; verify it exists and is degree-granting |
| Registrar and Director of Students Affairs | Both studying at the "**Islamic University of Minnesota, USA**" | Verify its accreditation status before citing it as a credential |
| Dr. Hussain Al-Najdi | BA from "a reputable university in the UAE" | Unnamed. A degree with no institution is not a credential |

**Why this is critical rather than cosmetic.** The College's central promise
is that its certificates are *real* — § 3.1 opens "We do not issue fake
certificates for financial gain," and § 3.3 disqualifies candidates for
dishonesty. Faculty credentials that cannot be verified attack that promise
at its root, and they are exactly what a sceptical journalist, regulator or
partner institution checks first.

**Fix.** For every person, hold a copy of the certificate on file, or remove
the line. Use each institution's own legal name and each award's correct
title. Where a degree is in progress, say "in progress" with the expected
completion year.

### C4. Named guest speakers may not have agreed to appear

*News & Events › Webinars & Lectures.* Three named academics — Shaykh Dr.
Abdulla al-Qari, Professor Maryam al-Ghazzali, Dr. Yousuf Khalifa — are
listed as "invited," each with a described session.

An invitation is not an acceptance. Publishing a named scholar as a speaker
before they have agreed misrepresents them and can sour the relationship
permanently.

**Fix.** Written confirmation from each before the name is published, and a
date attached to each session.

### C5. The "No Right to Prosecution" clause is unenforceable and damaging

*Terms of Services, clause 9.* Students are made to waive "any right to
prosecute, litigate, or take legal action against the College or its
personnel," while the College asserts the right to expel "without liability
or legal consequence," with the governing body's decision "final and
binding."

A blanket waiver of the right to bring legal action is not enforceable under
Nigerian law, nor under the consumer law of the UK, EU or Gulf states where
the College's students live. Beyond being ineffective, it is *actively
harmful*: it advertises that the institution intends its students to have no
recourse, which is the opposite of the trustworthiness the rest of the site
is trying to establish. It also sits badly beside § 3.3's careful appeal
procedures and § 10.2's promise to honour data rights.

The clause additionally invokes "the laws of the governing jurisdiction"
without naming one, which leaves the whole Terms unanchored.

**Fix.** Have a lawyer redraft clause 9 and the governing-law clause. The
rest of the Terms are reasonable and can stand. Name the jurisdiction.

### C6. The donations page solicits money through channels that do not work

*Support Us › Donations.* Every route is a placeholder:

- Online: "our secure donation platform at
  `[www.imamsulaimiycollege.edu/donate]`" — a domain the College does not
  appear to own, on a TLD it cannot register (see C6b).
- Bank transfer: contact `[donations@imamsulaimiycollege.edu]` — same
  domain.
- Post: cheques "to our administrative office" — no address is published
  anywhere on the site.

The page explicitly invites **Zakat**, an obligatory religious payment. A
page that solicits Zakat and then provides no functioning way to pay it —
and whose only stated channels point at a domain that isn't the College's —
is the sort of thing that gets read as a scam even when it is only
unfinished.

**C6b. The `.edu` addresses cannot exist.** Four of the site's contact
addresses sit on `.edu` domains (`imamsulaimiycollege.edu`, `imamcol.edu`).
`.edu` is administered by EDUCAUSE under contract to the US Department of
Commerce and is restricted to US postsecondary institutions holding
institutional accreditation from a US Department of Education–recognised
accrediting agency. A Nigerian college cannot register one. Nigerian
institutions use `.edu.ng`, which is itself restricted to institutions
approved by the relevant Nigerian regulator.

**Fix.** Take the donations page down until at least one channel works.
Then publish real channels on the College's own domain, with the receiving
account name shown, and a stated policy on how Zakat is segregated from
general funds.

## High — the site cannot be acted on

### H1. There is no way to apply

`/apply-now` is **completely empty**. No form, no email address, no phone
number, no downloadable document. Yet the application process is described
in detail on the Application Process page and again across the FAQ.

This is the site's single biggest functional failure: every other page
funnels toward an action that does not exist. The Application Process page
opens "Complete the online application form"; FAQ 3 repeats it; FAQ 6 makes
a registration fee "required upon submission of the application form."

**Fix (cheapest working version).** Until a real form exists, publish a
`mailto:` link to a monitored address, listing what to include. That is
honest and it works.

### H2. No fee is published anywhere

All four tuition figures are published as blank rules:

```
Ibtida'iyyah (Primary):    [---------------] per term.
I'dadiyyah (Intermediate): [---------------] per term.
Thanawiyyah (Secondary):   [----------------] per term.
Diploma:                   [----------------] per semester.
```

The dedicated **Tuitions and Fees** page is an empty stub carrying the
wrong heading. The registration fee "varies by programme" with no figures.
The certification commitment fee has no amount. The College describes its
fees as "affordable" and a testimonial praises its "affordable price," but
no number appears on the site.

**Fix.** Publish figures with currency, or state plainly that fees are
quoted on application and give the route to ask.

### H3. No usable contact details

| Field | Published |
| --- | --- |
| Address | `[Insert College Address]` |
| General phone | `[Insert Phone Number]` |
| Admissions phone | `[Insert Admissions Phone Number]` |
| DPO phone | `+[insert number]` |

The Contact page is titled **"Cntact Us"**. Both its sub-pages — Contact
Information and Make an Enquiry — are completely empty. The enquiry form
the page points to ("please fill out our inquiry form [here]") does not
exist.

The only geographic fact anywhere is "Lagos Island" — and it appears in the
FAQ, not the Contact page.

### H4. Five email addresses across four domains, none of them the site's

| Address | Domain | Owned by the College? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| info@**ibrahimsulaimiycollege.com** | #1 | Unknown |
| admissions@**ibrahimsulaimiycollege.com** | #1 | Unknown |
| donations@**imamsulaimiycollege.edu** | #2 | Impossible — see C6b |
| support@**imamsulaimiycollege.edu** | #2 | Impossible |
| privacy@**imamcol.edu** | #3 | Impossible |
| abisulaimiycollege@**gmail.com** | #4 | Yes |

The live site is `abisulaimiycollege.com.ng` — **domain #5**, which appears
in no email address at all. The one address on a domain the College
demonstrably controls is a free Gmail account, published on the
volunteering page.

**Fix.** Consolidate everything onto `@abisulaimiycollege.com.ng`:
`info@`, `admissions@`, `donations@`, `privacy@`. Set them up before
publishing them.

### H5. Two advertised programmes have no content at all

**Islamic Studies** and **Humanitarian & Professional Development** are in
the main navigation and on the Programmes hub. Both pages render nothing.

Both are load-bearing. The entire certification framework (§ 3) awards
*Islamic studies* qualifications. The Humanitarian programme has its own
admission route, its own per-module fee model, its own scholarship category
and its own webinar series — all described elsewhere, none on its own page.

| Scholarship application form | Application Process § Scholarships |

The Student Life page is the clearest case: three resources, each below a
button captioned **"Click the button above,"** and not one of the buttons
links anywhere.

### H7. The learning platform is never named

"Online learning platform" and "virtual classrooms" appear throughout —
grades are on it, materials are on it, classes happen in it, the Privacy
Policy governs data collected by it. It is never named, linked or
described. Students cannot know what software they need before enrolling,
despite being told to have a webcam and microphone ready.

### H8. Broken link on the Programmes hub

The Humanitarian & Professional Development card links to `http://hum` — an
unfinished URL that resolves nowhere.

## Medium — contradictions and structural gaps

### M1. Eight different names for the institution

| Name | Occurrences | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Imam Ahmad **Bin** Ibrohim Sulaimiy College | 16 | Testimonials, Faculty |
| Imam Ahmad **bin** Ibrohim Sulaimiy College | 15 | Most pages |
| Imam Ahmad Bin **Ibrahim** Sulaimiy College | 5 | Faculty |
| Imam **Ahmed** bin Ibrahim **Suleimani** College | 4 | Terms of Services, Privacy Policy |
| Imam **Ahmed** bin Ibrahim **Suleimi** College | 3 | Earn Professional Certifications |
| Imam Ahmad bin Ibrohim College | 3 | Home |
| Imam Ahmad bin Ibrohim **Centre for Islamic Sciences and Humanitarian Aid** | 1 | Home hero |
| Abisulaimiy College | 1 | Earn Professional Certifications |

Plus **"abisulaimiy college"** as the site title and logo.

The variation is not stylistic — the founder's name changes (Ahmad/Ahmed),
his father's name changes (Ibrohim/Ibrahim), the family name changes
(Sulaimiy/Suleimi/Suleimani), and the institution type changes
(College/Centre). The two documents that must be legally precise — the
Terms of Service and the Privacy Policy — use a name that appears nowhere
else.

**Fix.** One registered legal name on every certificate, policy and
contract; one short form for everyday use.

### M2. Is it a College or a Centre?

The home page opens "Welcome to Imam Ahmad bin Ibrohim **Centre for Islamic
Sciences and Humanitarian Aid**" — a differently-scoped body with a
different name. Every other page says College. A visitor's first impression
is of an organisation that is unsure what it is.

### M3. EPCD or EDIC?

The English Language hub introduces "the Advanced English for Islamic
Propagation and Da'wah Communication Programme (**EPCD**)". Its own detail
page is titled "English for Da'wah and Islamic Communication (**EDIC**)"
and uses EDIC throughout. Same programme, two acronyms.

### M4. A programme named only in a testimonial

A home-page testimonial from "Yasir Imani, Jeddah" describes the
**Intensive English Fluency Course (IEFC)** in detail. No programme of that
name exists anywhere else on the site.

### M5. Contradictory age rule for Ibtida'iyyah

Earn Professional Certifications § 7 (*Age Requirements*): "**No age
limit**, typically designed for younger learners."
Earn Professional Certifications § 1 (*Sequential Pathway*): "available to
students **above 10 years of age**."

The same page says both. Note also that the Privacy Policy sets its
children's threshold at 13 with no parental-consent mechanism, while the
College offers a certificate aimed at under-13s.

### M6. Two people hold the same post

Dr. Abdullahi Al-Hilwan is captioned "Vice Director" but described as
"**Head of Quranic Sciences Department**." Dr. Hussain Al-Najdi is
"**Director of Quranic Studies**," with the same described duties —
receiving faculty reports, approving curriculum plans.

Dr. Dhikrullahi Ibrahim has the same problem alone: headed "IT Support
Manager," described as "Director, Information Technology Department."

### M7. A duplicated block of text

Earn Professional Certifications § 4 repeats a full four-paragraph passage
verbatim — from "…qualify for a Thanawiyyah Certificate, candidates must
first have successfully completed…" through "…before being allowed to
progress to the final stage." It appears twice in immediate succession.

### M8. Terms of Service skips clause 6

Numbered 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, **7**, 8, 9, 10, 11. Either a clause was deleted
without renumbering, or one is missing.

### M9. The Arabic programme's timezone claim only holds half the year

IAC class times are given as Nigeria 8:30–9:30 p.m. and UK 7:30–8:30 p.m.
Nigeria (WAT) is UTC+1 year-round; the UK is UTC+0 in winter but UTC+1
during British Summer Time, when the two times would be **identical**. As
written, the schedule is wrong for roughly seven months of the year.

**Fix.** Publish one time with an explicit UTC offset.

### M10. Only one of three IAC stages is published

The IAC is described as a three-month course, one stage per month, covering
Nahw, Sarf and Balagha. Stage 1 (Nahw) has a detailed 15-topic syllabus.
Stages 2 and 3 do not exist on the site. Two other Arabic programmes —
**Diploma in Arabic** and **Regular Arabic Programme** — are headings with
no body.

Similarly, the **Diction Mastery Programme** has a heading and no content,
and the **ITTC** section of EDIC has no duration where the other three
sections do.

### M11. Programmes referenced but never catalogued

Named in passing, with no page of their own: Adhan and Nasheed Voice
Training; the **Accelerated 150% Qur'anic Memorization Program**; the
**Muraja'ah Program**; **Tajweed Mastery Certification**; the **Shahada of
Hifz**; **Document Translation** services; **Humanitarian Aid Training**;
and the **Diploma in Islamic Studies**. Durations are missing for the
Intensive Qur'an Course and for the Regular Qur'an Program (the 24-month
figure is attributed only to "Complete Qur'anic Memorization" on the home
page).

## Infrastructure

### I1. The apex domain does not work

| Host | Result |
| --- | --- |
| `https://abisulaimiycollege.com.ng` | **TLS failure** — the server does not speak HTTPS on the apex |
| `http://abisulaimiycollege.com.ng` | **Redirects to a registrar parking page** (`dns3.qservers.net/domainnotknown.html`, HTTP 404) |
| `https://www.abisulaimiycollege.com.ng` | Works (HTTP 200) |

Anyone who types the domain without `www.` — which is most people — lands
on a registrar error page. This is a DNS/hosting configuration fix at the
registrar (qservers), not a content change, and it is probably the single
highest-impact item on this list relative to effort.

### I2. Platform

The site is built on **Google Sites**. Its footer carries Google Sites
branding and a "Report abuse" link on every page, which reads as informal
for an institution issuing accredited-equivalent certificates. Google Sites
also cannot host the forms, payment flows, student records or learning
platform the content repeatedly promises.

## Suggested order of work

Grouped so that each block delivers something a visitor can actually use.

**1 — Stop the bleeding.** Take down or rewrite the three items that carry
real risk: the affiliation claim (C1), the unverified faculty credentials
(C3), and the donations page (C6). Get clause 9 of the Terms to a lawyer
(C5).

**2 — Make the site actionable.** Fix the apex domain (I1). Set up
`@abisulaimiycollege.com.ng` email and replace all five addresses (H4).
Publish a real address and phone (H3). Put a working application route on
`/apply-now`, even if it is only a `mailto:` (H1).

**3 — Fill the holes.** Publish fees, or state how to obtain them (H2).
Write the Islamic Studies and Humanitarian programme pages (H5). Either
publish the promised forms and resources or remove the references to them
(H6). Name the learning platform (H7).

**4 — Make it consistent.** Settle the legal name and apply it everywhere
(M1, M2). Resolve EPCD/EDIC (M3), the age rule (M5), the duplicated
directorships (M6), and the timezone (M9). Delete the duplicated block
(M7).

**5 — Polish.** Correct the headings (L1), the typos (L2), and the
transliteration and spelling (L3, L4). Add dates (L8).

## What is genuinely good

Worth stating, because the list above is one-sided by design:

- **The certification framework (§ 3)** is a serious piece of policy
  writing. The sequential pathway, the 70% threshold, the paired
  written/oral examination, identity verification before the final exam,
  and the transcript issued alongside the certificate together describe a
  credential that has been thought about properly.
- **The premise is distinctive and well argued.** Serving people who hold
  real knowledge but no paperwork is a clear, defensible position, and the
  founder's welcome makes the case for it well.
- **The FAQ is unusually thorough** — 40 questions covering nearly every
  practical concern a prospective student would raise.
- **The admissions requirements are specific**, per programme, with named
  documents. Most institutions this size publish far less.
- **The Privacy Policy is substantive** and its data-sharing commitments
  are stronger than most.
- **The IAC Stage 1 syllabus** is a real curriculum with 15 named topics,
  not a marketing summary.

The problem is not the thinking. It is that a genuinely considered
institution is presented through a site that reads as unfinished, and that
a handful of unverified claims put the credible parts at risk.
