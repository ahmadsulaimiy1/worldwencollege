### 1.2 What the College is

An **online-only** institution offering Islamic sciences, Qur'anic studies,
Arabic language, English language and humanitarian training to students
worldwide. Administration is based in **Lagos Island, Nigeria**; no
physical attendance is required for any programme.

The home page also describes the body as the *Imam Ahmad bin Ibrohim
Centre for Islamic Sciences and Humanitarian Aid*, which reads as a
second, differently-scoped entity — see `audit.md`.

### 1.3 Positioning

- Comprehensive, accessible Islamic education delivered worldwide, rooted
  in the Qur'an and the Islamic sciences, taught in a modern, self-paced,
  fully online format.
- Explicitly **not driven by financial gain**; the stated purpose is
  spiritual and humanitarian.
- Aimed particularly at people who hold real knowledge but were denied
  formal recognition of it — through poverty, geography, or lack of access
  to conventional schooling — and who need a credential to progress.
- Paired with humanitarian work: crisis assistance, masjid construction,
  translation, aid training, and help applying for overseas scholarships.

## 3. Certification framework

The College's flagship proposition: legitimate, examined credentials for
people whose knowledge is real but unrecognised.

### 3.1 The pledge

> We uphold the highest standards of academic integrity and ensure that our
> certifications are earned through genuine effort and knowledge. **We do
> not issue fake certificates for financial gain.**

The stated purpose is to serve people who hold strong educational
backgrounds but have been held back by societal or systemic barriers — who
gained substantial knowledge but, through the limits of their environment
or the absence of formal recognition, cannot continue their studies or
advance their careers. Each student receives a syllabus tailored to their
level; on completing it and passing the examinations, they are awarded a
certificate, together with a transcript, that opens routes to higher
education or professional work.

### 3.2 The three awards

| Award | Equivalent to | Minimum age | Prerequisite |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ibtida'iyyah** | Islamic Primary Certificate | None stated *(see conflict below)* | None |
| **I'dadiyyah** | Islamic Junior Secondary Certificate | 12 | Ibtida'iyyah |
| **Thanawiyyah** | Islamic Senior Secondary Certificate | 15 | I'dadiyyah |

> **Conflict.** One section states Ibtida'iyyah has "no age limit"; another
> states it "is available to students above 10 years of age."
> **`[TO CONFIRM]`** — one of these has to be withdrawn.

Progression is **sequential**: Thanawiyyah requires I'dadiyyah, which
requires Ibtida'iyyah. A candidate who studied elsewhere without gaining
formal certification may be issued the Ibtida'iyyah directly once their
educational background is verified, and can then progress. A candidate with
the knowledge but no paperwork may likewise be awarded the I'dadiyyah after
passing both examinations and a background verification of their academic
history and ethical standing.

### 3.3 Eligibility conditions

**1. Adherence to the correct Islamic creed.** Candidates must adhere to
the creed (Aqidah) of Ahlus Sunnah wal Jama'ah. A certificate will not be
awarded to anyone known to deviate from it, or involved in actions strongly
opposing the Sunnah — specifically anyone who abuses or speaks ill of the
Companions, propagates deviant sects or ideologies, or acts in ways
contradicting clear principles of the Qur'an and Sunnah.

*Reformation:* someone who previously engaged in such behaviour, has
repented, and wishes to be certified must supply a formal attestation
letter from a notable, recognised Islamic scholar confirming that they have
reformed. Certification is reconsidered only once that letter is received
and verified.

**2. Free of disciplinary expulsion.** Anyone expelled from a Madrasah or
other formal Islamic institution for disciplinary reasons is ineligible —
this prevents the certification being used to bypass an expulsion.
Exceptions require proof beyond reasonable doubt that the expulsion was
unjust, established by a College investigation. Where an expulsion is found,
certification is halted pending that investigation; if the expulsion was for
misconduct or academic dishonesty, the candidate is **permanently
ineligible**. A back-up letter from a notable individual or recognised
authority is required, attesting to a strong personal relationship with the
candidate and to their character. The College verifies the letter and may
reject the application if the relationship or circumstances lack
credibility.

**3. Curriculum completion.** Candidates complete the prescribed
curriculum for their level. Study is self-paced and material can be
accessed at any time before the assessments.

**4. Written examination** — pass mark **70%**.

**5. Oral examination** — an interview assessing the candidate's ability to
articulate their knowledge verbally, so that proficiency is proved in
speech as well as in writing.

**6. Purpose statement.** An official statement of how the qualification
will be used — further education, employment, or community service.
Required for Thanawiyyah and above, and it should state whether the
certificate is intended for further study in Arab countries or for
professional purposes.

**7. Attestation letter.** Thanawiyyah candidates may be required to submit
an attestation letter from a notable individual or recognised authority —
a community leader, scholar or employer — endorsing their background,
verifying identity, and confirming they meet the educational and ethical
standards.

**8. Identity verification.** Every candidate submits a government-issued
identity document: **national identity card, passport, or driver's
licence**. Before the final examination, all candidates must present a
valid passport or other recognised identity document, so that the person
sitting the exam is demonstrably the person who studied. Proof of age —
birth certificate or identity card — is required at registration.

**9. Flexibility for the non-privileged.** The programme is crafted for
people who gained strong Islamic knowledge informally. Curriculum access
and assessment arrangements flex to their circumstances; the **standard for
passing does not**.

### 3.4 Examinations

All certification examinations are conducted **online**. The Examination
Committee assigns each candidate to a batch and issues a schedule.
Candidates may be required to sit a **live video session** during the
examination for quality control and integrity. This lets candidates sit the
examination from any location without weakening the credential.

Candidates who have followed the curriculum closely and engaged with the
material will be equipped to pass. The process is designed to be strict but
fair.

### 3.5 Issuance

- Certificates are issued **3–7 working days** after successful completion.
- A **digital copy** is emailed first. A **hard copy** is posted on
  request, with **shipping paid by the candidate**.
- Every certificate is accompanied by a **transcript** detailing
  performance at each level, so employers and academic institutions can see
  a complete record.
- Certificates **do not state that the course was completed online.**
- The **Council of Scholars** oversees examination and certification.

> **`[TO CONFIRM]`** — omitting the delivery mode from the certificate is a
> deliberate policy, but many receiving institutions and employers treat an
> undisclosed delivery mode as a misrepresentation. This decision should be
> taken with legal advice, not on the website's own authority.

### 3.6 Fees

A **commitment fee** is charged by the Registry and Examination Office at
the start of the application, covering the administrative costs of
examination and certification. Candidates receive a **cost card** listing
total fees and the necessary personal information. The fee must be paid
before results are processed and the certificate issued.
**`[TO CONFIRM]`** — the amount.

### 3.7 Curriculum integrity

The certification is backed by a comprehensive curriculum vetted by the
College's **academic council**, aligning with both traditional and modern
Islamic educational standards.

### 10.1 Terms of Service

Governs use of the College's online learning platforms, programmes and

**Acceptance.** On enrolment or participation you agree to abide by the
Terms and any amendments, to uphold Islamic values and professional
conduct, and to show respect for the rules, the faculty and fellow
participants.

**Islamic core values.**

- **Modesty and dress code** — students and faculty dress according to
  Islamic guidelines for modesty. Any video participation must reflect
  appropriate Islamic attire (hijab for women, modest clothing for men).
- **Respectful interactions** — all communication must reflect mutual
  respect and integrity. Personal attacks, slander and inappropriate speech
  are prohibited.
- **Gender interaction** — classes and forums maintain Islamic norms of
  gender interaction. Dating, private chatting and personal relationships
  between male and female students beyond academic interaction are
  prohibited, in group forums, breakout rooms and private messages alike.

**Professional conduct.** Stay focused on academic activity during class;
avoid disruptive behaviour such as speaking out of turn, off-topic texting
or offensive language; keep personal conversation out of class; observe the
no-dating policy online and offline.

**Time and attendance.** Attend scheduled classes on time and stay for the
full session; complete assignments, exams and projects by their deadlines;
review material independently if you miss a class. Failure to meet
attendance or submission standards may lead to suspension or termination
from the programme.

**Prohibited.** Sharing login credentials; conversations unrelated to the
programme; using the platform for non-educational purposes; any conduct
violating Islamic principles or compromising academic integrity.

**Academic integrity.** Plagiarism — passing off another's work as your own
— brings immediate disciplinary action up to expulsion. Cheating — sharing
exam questions, unpermitted collaboration, unauthorised resources in exams
— is forbidden. All submissions must be the student's original work.

**Limitation of liability.** Services are provided "as is." The College
cannot guarantee uninterrupted access and is not liable for technical
difficulties or service interruptions beyond its control, for direct or
indirect losses arising from use of the services, or for legal actions
arising from disputes outside the agreement.

governing body's decision final and binding.
