# Appointments schedule — which posts need a real person

Not an org chart. `docs/org-chart-placeholders.md` already holds an
eighteen-row operational chart of fictional placeholders for interface
design, and none of those names may ever be published.

This is the narrower and more useful question: **which posts are
currently blocking real work, or would be legally or structurally
required before a real learner enrols.** Every entry names what it
unblocks, measured, and what it costs to leave vacant.

Ranked. If only one appointment is made this year, make the first one.

---

## The structural constraint that sets the minimum

Three of the posts below exist **only** to be independent of the person
who wrote the material. An author cannot review their own work; a
reviewer cannot examine their own review. That is not bureaucracy, it is
the entire reason those roles produce evidence at all.

So the minimum credible academic staffing is **two qualified people who
are not each other**, plus one external. Below that, no amount of
appointing changes what the College can honestly claim.

Everything else on this list can be doubled up, fractional, or bought
in.

---

## Tier 1 — blocking measured work *right now*

These three are the reason 43 % of Stage 1 cannot be completed by any
amount of further effort from the Press.

### 1. Academic Reviewer — *the single highest-value appointment*

**Unblocks:** every publication in the Press. All of them currently stop
at step 3 of a six-step approval pathway (`lifecycle.mjs` PATHWAY), and
step 4 is "read by a qualified academic who did not write it."

**Currently discharged by:** nobody. The review register is empty.

**Cost of vacancy:** 114 self-checks carrying 366 items, 49 exercise
sets, 199 vocabulary entries, 36 competency mappings and 13 published
volumes are all `press_drafted`. Not one word has been read by a
qualified reader who did not write it. Everything the College publishes
carries that qualification, correctly.

**Minimum credible profile:** MA TESOL / Applied Linguistics or higher,
with assessment experience. DELTA-level practitioners are appropriate
reviewers for the teaching material; the assessment mapping wants
someone who has worked on language testing.

**Can be fractional?** Yes. This is consultancy work — a defined number
of days against a defined reading list. It does not need a salary.

### 2. Board of Academic Standards & Curriculum Excellence — members

**Unblocks:** approval of the competency framework, the learning
outcomes, and the pass thresholds that are currently `NULL`.

**Currently:** the body exists in the database with
`members_appointed = 0`. Every mapping is recorded `interim`, never
`approved`, and a test fails the build if that ever changes while the
board has no members.

**Cost of vacancy:** the awards rest on marks with no approved statement
of what they are marks *in*. Level I's twenty assessments are mapped to
four competencies under delegated authority — good work, and not
approved work.

**Minimum:** three members, at least one external to the College. One
may be the Academic Reviewer above; they cannot all be.

### 3. Practising Teacher (any level, any hours)

**Unblocks:** the Classroom Management Notes, and the entire
`observed_in_teaching` evidence class — 1,533 pedagogy fields that are
honestly empty because this College has taught nobody.

**Cost of vacancy:** the Teacher's Companion, Intervention Guide and
Differentiation Guide can now be written from educational expertise
(and have been, for Level I), but nothing in them is evidence of what
actually happens in a room. The annual improvement cycle cannot run,
because there is no year of teaching to feed back.

**Note:** this is the one post where the *smallest possible* version
still works. One teacher, one cohort, one term generates real
observational data. It does not need a department.

## Tier 2 — required before the first real learner

Not blocking authoring. Blocking *operating*.

### 4. Data Protection / Compliance owner

**Trigger:** the first real applicant's data. **Not yet reached** — and
this is worth stating precisely, because it is easy to over- or
under-state.

The public site as it stands **stores no personal data from visitors**.
The contact form is a `mailto:` form: it opens the sender's own email
client and posts nothing to any server. `info@worldwencollege.co.uk`
forwards to a personal inbox, which is ordinary correspondence.

The moment someone **registers or enrols**, that changes: identity data
via Clerk, payment data via the gateways, and learner voice recordings
in R2 — the last of which is the most sensitive thing the platform
handles.

**Required then:** ICO registration, a lawful basis for each processing
purpose, a retention policy, and a named contact. Whether a formal DPO
is *required* is a legal question that depends on scale and processing
type — take advice rather than assuming either way.

**Can be fractional?** Yes. Fractional DPO services exist precisely for
this stage.

### 5. Safeguarding Lead — *if, and only if, under-18s are admitted*

**Currently undetermined.** The programme states no minimum age, and no
admissions decision has been recorded either way.

**This is a decision to make before marketing, not after.** If the
College admits under-18s, safeguarding is not optional and is not
something to retrofit — it changes the admissions flow, the recording
policy, the instructor vetting requirement and the insurance position.
If it admits adults only, say so on the admissions page and the question
closes.

**Recommendation:** decide first, appoint second.

### 6. Named officer for the imprint

**Unblocks:** the Foreword, any signed institutional message, and the
ISBN application.

**Currently:** no President or principal officer is appointed, which is
why every volume's preface is issued by the publisher rather than
signed. That is the honest form and it reads as what it is.

## Tier 3 — at first cohort, or at scale

| Post | Trigger | Fractional? |
|---|---|---|
| **External Examiner** | Before any award is conferred | Yes — standard sector practice, per-cycle engagement |
| **Registrar** | First cohort with real enrolment records | No — needs continuity |
| **Second instructor** | Second cohort, or any cover requirement | Yes |
| **Assessment / Examinations Coordinator** | First live examination series | Can be the Registrar at this size |
| **Finance owner** | First real payment taken | Yes — bookkeeper suffices initially |

The **External Examiner** deserves its own line: it is the post whose
absence most limits what the awards can claim, and it is the one that
cannot be doubled with anything else, because its entire function is
being outside.

---

## Who can be one person, at this size

Honest consolidation, not padding:

- Registrar + Assessment Coordinator + Student Records — **one person**
- Finance + Procurement — **one person, or outsourced**
- Marketing + Admissions enquiries — **one person**
- IT + Learning Technologies — **one person**, and note that neither
  needs learner-record access (see the access rationale in
  `org-chart-placeholders.md`)

What **cannot** be consolidated:

- Author ≠ Academic Reviewer
- Academic Reviewer ≠ External Examiner
- Whoever confers an award ≠ whoever examines it

Those three separations are the ones an accreditation panel will look
for first, and the ones that cost nothing to preserve now and are
expensive to unpick later.

---

## If you make exactly one appointment

**The Academic Reviewer.** One qualified reader, engaged for a defined
number of days, moves the entire Press from step 3 to step 4 of the
approval pathway and converts a body of `press_drafted` material into
reviewed material. Nothing else on this list changes as much per pound.

**If you make two:** add a practising teacher on any hours. The
combination unblocks both the review gate and the observation gate, and
between them those two gates account for every remaining academic
blocker in Stage 1.
