# Dār al-Rusūkh — Founding Plan

**A board-and-executive planning document.**
Drafted August 2026. Supersedes the content of `abisulaimiycollege.com.ng`,
which is archived and audited in `docs/abisulaimiy-college/`.

---

## Preface — what this document is for

The instruction was to plan properly before building, to act as both board
and chief executive, and to be hard on myself. So this document makes the
decisions rather than listing options. Where a decision is mine rather than
the founder's, it is marked **[BOARD DECISION]** and can be reversed by the
founder in one line. Where something is genuinely unknowable until the
institution operates, it says so instead of inventing a number.

The predecessor institution failed for one reason worth naming plainly: it
described a college it had not built. It published a Tuition page with
blank rules, an Apply page with nothing on it, a Donations page whose every
channel was a placeholder, and faculty credentials that dissolve on
contact. The thinking behind it was serious — the certification framework
was real policy work — but nothing could be *acted on*, and an institution
that cannot be acted on is not an institution.

**The governing rule of this build, therefore: nothing is published that
cannot be done on the day it is published.** A programme goes on the site
when it has a syllabus, a price, a start date and someone to teach it. Not
before. A short honest site beats a large aspirational one, and the site we
build will be large *because* the plan behind it is large — not the other
way round.

## Part I — Identity

### 1.1 The name **[BOARD DECISION]**

> ## دار الرسوخ
> ## DĀR AL-RUSŪKH
> **The College of Qur'ānic and Islamic Sciences**
> *Distance Learning · Founded 1441 / 2020 · Re-established 1448 / 2026*

**Why this name.** *Rusūkh* (رسوخ) is the verbal noun of *rasakha* — to be
firmly rooted, deeply established, immovable. Its most famous use in
Arabic is Qur'ānic:

> **وَالرَّاسِخُونَ فِي الْعِلْمِ**
> *"…and those firmly rooted in knowledge."* — Āl 'Imrān 3:7

That is precisely the thing a rigorous college claims to produce. The word
carries depth, permanence and scholarly seriousness in one breath, and it
is not in common institutional use — unlike *al-Nūr*, *al-Hudā*,
*al-Furqān*, *Bayt al-Ḥikmah* or *al-Manār*, each of which names several
hundred existing schools and none of which would be ownable.

**Why not the alternatives I considered.** *Dār al-Itqān* was the strongest
rival — *itqān* is exactly "doing a thing to the standard it deserves" —
but it collides directly with Itqān Academy, whose programme architecture
we are drawing on, and a college cannot open by taking the name of an
institution it is learning from. *Al-Rāsikhūn* as a standalone was rejected
as a claim about our graduates rather than a name for the house that forms
them; *Dār al-Sanad* was too narrow, naming only the Qur'ān faculty's work.

**Forms of the name.**

| Use | Form |
| --- | --- |
| Full legal | Dār al-Rusūkh College of Qur'ānic and Islamic Sciences |
| Display lockup | DĀR AL-RUSŪKH · *College of Qur'ānic and Islamic Sciences* |
| Arabic | دار الرسوخ — كلية القرآن والعلوم الإسلامية |
| Short / conversational | Dār al-Rusūkh |
| Abbreviation (documents, transcripts) | **DAR** |
| Adjectival, for members | *Rusūkhī* (pl. *Rusūkhiyyūn*) — an alumnus of the College |
| Domain | `daralrusukh.com` — with `.ng` and `.org` defensively held |

**Motto [BOARD DECISION]**

> **الراسخون في العلم**
> **"Rooted in revelation. Rigorous in method."**

**The founder's place in the name.** The predecessor carried the founder's
own name in eight inconsistent spellings. That is dropped from the
institutional name and moved where it belongs — the founder is named as
Founder, and the *foundation* (the charitable arm) carries the family name:
the **Ibn Ibrāhīm Sulaimiy Foundation**. This separates the school from the
man, which is what allows the school to outlive him. It also ends the
eight-variant problem at a stroke.

### 1.2 Positioning **[BOARD DECISION]**

Dār al-Rusūkh is a **distance-learning college**, not an "online school."
The distinction is the whole strategy and it must be defended in every line
of copy:

| An online school | A distance-learning college |
| --- | --- |
| Sells access to video | Enrols students into a programme |
| Course-by-course, transactional | Laddered, with progression and standing |
| Certificate on completion | Award on examination |
| Teacher is a content provider | Teacher holds a chain and confers one |
| You finish a course | You are *placed*, you *progress*, you *graduate* |

We take the register of a serious residential institution and apply it to
a body that has no campus by design — the way the University of London
External Programme, or Al-Azhar's *intisāb* system, have always done. The
absence of a campus is stated as a *method*, never apologised for.

**Our market position, stated bluntly:** most online Islamic education is
either free and unstructured (excellent scholars, no progression, no
standing) or cheap and transactional (a tutor on a video call, no
institution behind them). Almost nothing occupies the position of
*rigorous, laddered, examined and prestigious at a price a Nigerian family
can actually pay monthly*. That is the gap, and it is where we build.

### 1.3 Who we serve

Ranked by priority, because a college that serves everyone serves no one:

1. **Nigerian learners** — Lagos and the South-West first, then nationally.
   Working adults, undergraduates, secondary-age students, and imams and
   teachers seeking formal standing. Fees quoted in ₦, payable monthly,
   over data-light delivery.
2. **People with knowledge but no paper** — the founding mission. Adults
   who studied in traditional *madāris* and hold real learning but no
   certificate that any institution will accept. Served by the
   Certification Ladder (Part III).
3. **The diaspora and the Gulf** — Nigerians and West Africans in the UK,
   USA, Gulf states; and non-Arabic-speaking Muslims globally. Fees in
   USD/GBP at a different tier. This is where the margin comes from that
   subsidises (1) and (2).
4. **Women learners** — explicitly, with women teachers, women-only
   *ḥalaqāt*, and timetables that fit domestic responsibility. Under-served
   everywhere and a decisive advantage where it is done properly.
5. **Children (7–15)** — a junior stream, weekend and after-school,
   parent-portal supervised.

### 1.4 The standard **[BOARD DECISION]**

Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools has the **CLEVER** standard, published as an
operational commitment rather than a wall slogan. We need our own, and it
must be ours rather than a rewording of theirs. Ours is built from the word
that names us:

> ## The RUSŪKH Standard

| | | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **R** | **Rooted** | Everything taught is traced to a source a student can open — a *matn*, an *āyah*, a *sanad*. No teaching without a text behind it. |
| **U** | **Upright** | Character is assessed, not assumed. Conduct is a condition of award, and it is the same condition for the teacher as the student. |
| **S** | **Sequenced** | Nothing is taught out of order. Every subject is a ladder with a named rung below it and above it, and no student stands on a rung they have not earned. |
| **Ū** | **Unbroken** | Where a chain exists, we transmit it. Where it does not, we say so. An *ijāzah* from this house names its chain or it is not issued. |
| **K** | **Known** | Every student is known by a teacher by name. Class sizes are capped so this is true rather than claimed. |
| **H** | **Held** | Every award is held to an examination, and every examination to a published standard. A certificate that anyone can obtain is worth what anyone paid for it. |

This is not decoration. It generates the class-size cap (K), the
matn-based curriculum (R), the sanad policy (Ū), the examination
architecture (H), and the conduct condition (U). Each is a testable
commitment and each appears in Part III as a mechanism.

## Part II — What the market taught us

I looked at how serious institutions actually operate — the free-scholarly
model (SeekersGuidance), the accredited-degree model (International Open
University), the *ḥalaqah* model (Itqān Academy, Riwāq), the *ijāzah*
houses, and the local reference (Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools). What follows
is what we take, what we reject, and why.

### 2.1 What we take

**From SeekersGuidance — the ladder.** Their five-level structure
(Essentials → Foundations → Intermediate → Advanced → Specialisation),
built on *farḍ 'ayn* first, is the single best piece of curriculum
architecture in online Islamic education. It answers the question every
adult learner actually has — *where do I start and what comes next* — which
almost nothing else does. **We adopt the laddered structure wholesale and
build every faculty on it.**

**From the *ijāzah* houses — the chain as the product.** The market
standard is a three-stage route: Tajwīd correction → full recitation to
standard → *ijāzah bi-sanad muttaṣil* in Ḥafṣ 'an 'Āṣim, with the ten
*qirā'āt* (*ṣughrā* via al-Shāṭibiyyah and al-Durrah, *kubrā* via Ṭayyibat
al-Nashr) above it. This is rigorous, verifiable, and it is the most
prestigious thing an Islamic institution can confer. **The founder already
holds the ten *qirā'āt* and *ijāzāt* in the six books — this is our single
strongest asset and the plan is built around it.**

**From Itqān Academy — age-banded *ḥalaqāt* and the schedule library.**
Their division by life-stage (3–6, 7–12, 13–18, 19–40, 40+, special needs)
rather than by course is right: a 45-year-old beginner and a 9-year-old
beginner need different rooms, not the same room. Their downloadable
memorisation schedules (5, 6, 10, 14-month plans) are a genuinely
excellent, cheap acquisition tool. **We adopt both.** We also adopt their
separate enrolment pathways for men, women, children and non-Arabic
speakers — routing at the door, not after.

**From International Open University — the semester spine and the
progression from certificate to diploma to degree.** IOU proved that a
low-cost, high-volume, properly-structured Islamic distance programme works
at global scale. **We adopt the semester structure and the
certificate→diploma progression.** We do not attempt degrees (see 2.2).

**From Sultan Hanafi Royal Schools — the institutional register.** Their
site is the best in this market for one reason: *it publishes rather than
promises*. Governance papers, the notice archive, verification by reference
number, offices listed by name, "a school should be judged on what it can
show, not on what it can say." **We adopt that discipline entirely** — it
is the exact antidote to the predecessor site's failure mode. We also adopt
the five-stage admission journey with published time expectations, and
public credential verification.

**From the traditional *madrasah* — the *matn*.** The classical ladder
(al-Ājurrūmiyyah → Qaṭr al-Nadā → Alfiyyah in *naḥw*; al-Arba'īn →
'Umdat al-Aḥkām → Bulūgh al-Marām in *ḥadīth*) is the most rigorously
tested curriculum sequence in human history. It is *why* traditional
students are formidable. Almost no online institution uses it properly.
**We build every faculty on named *mutūn*, and students memorise them.**
This is the single decision that will most distinguish us.

### 2.2 What we reject, and why

**Degrees.** BA and MA awards require accreditation we do not have and
should not claim. The predecessor's collapse began with an unverifiable
affiliation. **We award certificates, diplomas and *ijāzāt* — all of which
we can confer honestly on our own authority — and nothing else.** An
*ijāzah bi-sanad muttaṣil* needs no state's permission to be prestigious;
it has been prestigious for fourteen centuries. This is the strongest
position available to us and it happens to be the honest one.

**Claimed recognition.** No line on the site will say our awards are
"internationally recognised" or "recognised by relevant institutions." We
say exactly what an award *is*, who examined it, and what chain it carries,
and we let receiving institutions make their own judgement. **Where a
partnership exists it is published with the agreement behind it; where none
exists, nothing is said.**

**Fabricated faculty.** The predecessor listed eleven directors with
doctorates from Oxford, Cairo, Manitoba and a university that does not
exist. We open with the faculty we actually have, named, with credentials
we can evidence, and with vacancies shown as vacancies. **A small real
faculty is an asset; a large fictional one is a liability that detonates
later.**

**Free.** SeekersGuidance can be free because it is donor-funded at scale.
We cannot, and a college that cannot pay its teachers cannot hold them to
the RUSŪKH standard. **We charge, we charge modestly, and we fund the
students who cannot pay through *zakāh* and *kafālah* rather than by
pretending the teaching is free.**

**Unlimited intake.** Standard K (Known) caps *ḥalaqah* size. That caps
revenue per teacher and it is the correct trade: the thing we are selling
is being known by a teacher.

### 2.3 The honest competitive risk

We are entering a crowded market with a strong asset (the founder's
*qirā'āt* and *ijāzāt*), a real mission (recognition for the unrecognised),
and two serious weaknesses: **no accreditation** and **a predecessor
institution that went dormant for two years**. Both must be addressed in
copy rather than hidden. The dormancy is stated as history in the founder's
message — it already is, and the honesty of that passage is one of the best
things on the old site. The accreditation gap is answered by making our
awards the kind that do not require it.

### 3.4 Faculty III — Kulliyyat al-Sharī'ah

Streamed by *madhhab* where the science requires it. **Mālikī is the
default stream** — it is the *madhhab* of the overwhelming majority of
Nigerian Muslims, and no online institution serves it properly. Shāfi'ī and
Ḥanafī streams open when a qualified teacher for each is appointed, and not
before.

| Science | Ladder |
| --- | --- |
| **'Aqīdah** | Thalāthat al-Uṣūl → Kitāb al-Tawḥīd → al-'Aqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah |
| **Fiqh (Mālikī)** | Matn al-Akhḍarī → al-'Ashmāwiyyah → Risālat Ibn Abī Zayd al-Qayrawānī → Mukhtaṣar Khalīl |
| **Uṣūl al-Fiqh** | al-Waraqāt → Uṣūl min 'Ilm al-Uṣūl |
| **Ḥadīth (text)** | al-Arba'īn al-Nawawiyyah → 'Umdat al-Aḥkām → Bulūgh al-Marām |
| **Muṣṭalaḥ al-Ḥadīth** | al-Manẓūmah al-Bayqūniyyah → Nukhbat al-Fikar |
| **Tafsīr** | Uṣūl al-Tafsīr → Tafsīr al-Sa'dī, juz' by juz' |
| **Sīrah & Tārīkh** | al-Raḥīq al-Makhtūm → the Rightly-Guided Caliphs |

### 3.5 Faculty IV — Kulliyyat al-Taṭbīq (Applied & Professional)

### 3.6 The Certification Ladder

The predecessor's best idea, kept and made rigorous. This is the mission —
**recognition for people who hold knowledge and no paper.**

| Award | Level | Min. age | Prerequisite | Examination |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ibtidā'iyyah** | Primary | 10 | Placement | Written + oral |
| **I'dādiyyah** | Junior secondary | 12 | Ibtidā'iyyah *or* verified prior learning | Written + oral |
| **Thānawiyyah** | Senior secondary | 15 | I'dādiyyah | Written + oral + *purpose statement* |
| **Diploma ('Āliyah)** | Post-secondary | 17 | Thānawiyyah or WAEC/NECO equivalent | Written + oral + long essay |

**Prior-learning recognition.** A candidate who studied in a traditional
madrasah may enter at I'dādiyyah or Thānawiyyah directly by examination
plus verification of their study history. This is the door the mission
exists to open, and it stays open.

**The standards that keep it honest:**

- Pass mark **70%**, written and oral both.
- Oral examination is **compulsory at every level** — it is what makes the
  award un-buyable.
- Identity verified against government ID before the final examination.
- Every award issues **with a transcript**.
- Examinations overseen by the **Majlis al-'Ilmī** (Academic Council).
- **The delivery mode is stated on the certificate.** *This reverses the
  predecessor's policy.* It said certificates "will not specify that the
  course was completed online" — that is a misrepresentation risk that
  would destroy the credential the moment one receiving institution noticed.
  We state it, and we make the examination rigorous enough that stating it
  costs us nothing.
- **The conduct condition is rewritten.** The predecessor barred candidates
  by creed and required attestation letters certifying doctrinal
  reformation. That is unenforceable at distance, impossible to
  administer fairly, and it turns a registry clerk into an inquisitor. It
  is replaced by an **academic integrity and conduct undertaking** — the
  standard every serious institution uses — covering honesty in
  examination, respect in the *ḥalaqah*, and truthfulness of submitted
  documents. Teaching remains grounded in Ahl al-Sunnah wa'l-Jamā'ah; that
  is a statement about *what we teach*, not a test we administer at the door.

### 3.7 Ijāzah policy **[BOARD DECISION]**

The most valuable thing we issue. Therefore the most tightly governed.

1. An *ijāzah* is issued **only** by a teacher who himself holds a
   connected chain in what he is conferring.
2. Every *ijāzah* **names its full sanad** on the face of the document.
3. It is conferred **only after complete recitation** to the teacher, in
   person or by live video — never on the basis of recordings.
4. Every *ijāzah* is **publicly verifiable** by reference number.
5. The **Register of Chains** is published: which chains this College
   holds, through whom, and which teachers may confer them.

Point 5 is unusual and it is deliberate. It converts our central claim from
an assertion into a document a sceptic can check — the Sultan Hanafi
discipline applied to the thing that matters most.

### 4.5 Flexibility mechanisms

Flexibility was named as critical. Concretely:

- **Pause, don't drop.** A student may suspend enrolment for up to two
  terms and return to the same rung. Ramaḍān, childbirth, exams,
  bereavement, cash-flow. No penalty, no re-application.
- **Switch tier at any month boundary**, up or down.
- **Per-*juz'* payment** on the modular Ḥifẓ track — the lowest-commitment
  entry into the College.
- **Family rate**: second and subsequent members of a household, −25%.
- **Teacher rate**: practising imams and madrasah teachers, −40%, because
  they multiply everything we teach them.
- **No fee is ever a barrier to placement.** Assessment and placement are
  free and happen before any payment is requested.

### 4.6 Kafālah — the scholarship system

| Scheme | Covers | Funded by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Kafālat Ṭālib** | Full fees, one student, one year | A named sponsor |
| **Zakāh seats** | Full fees for eligible students | The *zakāh* fund, disbursed only to eligible categories |
| **Ḥāfiẓ bursary** | Q3–Q5 for students of exceptional ability | General fund |
| **Teacher bursary** | I'dād al-Mu'allim, free to serving madrasah teachers | Foundation |

**Target: 20% of every cohort on full or partial *kafālah*, published as a
real figure each term.** *Zakāh* funds are segregated from general funds
and separately accounted — this is a religious obligation and it will be
stated on the site.

## Part V — Operating model

### 5.1 The academic year

Three terms, aligned to the Hijrī calendar with Gregorian dates published
alongside:

| Term | Runs | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Term I — Muḥarram** | Muḥarram → Rabī' al-Ākhir | 16 teaching weeks |

it.

### 5.2 Delivery

- **Live *ḥalaqāt*** — the core. Recorded for review, never as a substitute.
- **Data-light by design.** Audio-first, video optional. This is a Nigerian
  institution and bandwidth is a real constraint, not a footnote.
- **WhatsApp as an official channel**, not an afterthought — it is how
  Nigeria actually communicates.
- **Class times published in WAT with an explicit UTC offset.** *The
  predecessor published "Nigeria 8:30pm / UK 7:30pm", which is only correct
  for the five months the UK is on GMT.* One timezone, one offset, computed
  client-side for the reader.

### 6.1 Design direction

Built on this repository's existing system — `css/brand.css` already
carries the exact palette required: `--sapphire`, `--navy`, `--oxford`,
`--midnight`, `--royal-blue` against five golds from `--gold-royal` to
`--gold-champagne`. Display type is Bodoni Moda (variable optical size),
ceremonial capitals Cinzel, body Inter, with Amiri and Cairo for Arabic.

Against Sultan Hanafi's coffee-brown house livery, ours is **sapphire and
navy under gold** — deeper, cooler, more scholarly. Where SHRS reads as a
*royal school*, we read as a *college*: less ornament, more rule and
hairline, more white space, more typography doing the work.

**Imagery.** No campus exists, so no campus photography can honestly be
shown, and stock photographs of other people's classrooms are exactly the
dishonesty this plan exists to avoid. The site is carried instead by
**calligraphy, the crest, geometric *girih* ornament, manuscript rules and
generated SVG diagrams** — the visual language of the book rather than the
building. This is more prestigious than stock photography, not less, and it
is the correct answer for a house whose subject is a text.

## Part VII — Self-criticism

The instruction was to be sensitive and critical of myself, so:

**The plan's biggest weakness is teacher supply.** Every commitment here —
class caps, oral examinations at every level, one-to-one *ijāzah* work,
*matn*-based teaching — is teacher-intensive. The College can be founded on
one exceptional scholar; it cannot be *run* on one. If Phase I opens wider
than three or four teachers can hold, the RUSŪKH standard becomes exactly
the wall slogan this document promised it would not be. **I'dād al-Mu'allim
is therefore not one programme among five; it is the constraint on
everything, and it should arguably open in Phase I rather than Phase II.**
I have left it in Phase II because it needs the founder's time and the
founder's time is the same constraint — but the board should revisit this
first.

**The second weakness is that the fee schedule is a judgement, not a
finding.** The numbers in Part IV are calibrated to Nigerian household
income, competitor pricing and the cost of a capped *ḥalaqah*, but they
have not been tested against a single real applicant. They should be
treated as an opening position and reviewed after the first cohort.

**The third is that "prestige" and "accessible to Nigerian families" are in
real tension.** Every prestige signal — small classes, oral examinations,
calligraphed *ijāzāt*, chain-holding teachers — raises unit cost. The
resolution here is cross-subsidy from the international band, which works
only if the diaspora actually enrols. If it does not, either the price
rises or the standard falls. **This is the single commercial assumption the
whole model rests on and it should be tested early and deliberately.**

**Fourth, I have made the founder the institution's principal asset while
also arguing the institution must outlive him.** Both are true and they
pull against each other. The Register of Chains and I'dād al-Mu'allim are
the mechanisms that resolve it — chains held by more than one person, and
teachers formed rather than hired. Until those exist, the College is one
person's health away from dormancy, which is precisely what happened in
2022.
