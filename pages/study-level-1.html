IEFC · Level I

Foundation Programme

Foundation is the hardest level to begin and the easiest to underestimate. It assumes no English at all and ends with a learner who can introduce themselves, handle a shop, a street and a clinic, and write a few true sentences about their own life. The work is not complexity; it is building the first automatic responses, so that greeting someone or saying where you live no longer requires assembling the sentence first.

Why these are marked interim

The Board of Academic Standards & Curriculum Excellence has no appointed members yet, and an outcome cannot be approved by a body that does not sit. These were written under authority delegated to the Press and are recorded as interim until the Board is constituted and reviews them. They are used, and they are not yet ratified — and we would rather say so than describe them as settled.

Assessment

How the level is judged.

Every instrument is written before the teaching it tests, and the criteria are published to the learner in advance.

Continuous

10 module quizzes

One at the end of each module, testing the language that module taught and nothing else. Marked automatically, with the correct answer and the reason shown.

Productive

10 assignments

Speaking and writing tasks marked by an instructor against a published rubric. These are where the productive skills are actually assessed — a quiz cannot judge whether someone can hold a conversation.

Self-check

Before every assessment

Each lesson carries a self-check with traps aimed at the mistakes learners at this level really make, so a learner discovers a gap before an examiner does.

Teaching Methods

How it is taught.

Speaking-first

Practice before polish

Guided speaking is built into every lesson, not offered as an extra. Learners speak from the first lesson, before their grammar is ready, because waiting for accuracy is how people stay silent for years.

Structured

A shared standard, not a personal style

Every instructor teaches to the same mapped curriculum, so a learner who changes class or cohort does not lose their place, and two learners at the same level have covered the same ground.

Supported

A teacher who knows what goes wrong

Instructors work from the Teacher's Companion, which sets out for each lesson what commonly goes wrong, a second way to explain it, and what to do for the learner who is behind and the one who is ahead.

Measured

Adjusted to the cohort

Quiz and assignment results are visible to the instructor as the level runs, so teaching responds to how this group is actually performing rather than to how the plan assumed they would.

honours the decision, which at A1 is the achievement. Beginning a language in adulthood is harder than any later step and is where most people stop. Overclaims nothing.

Graduate profile

What the holder can do

The learner arrives with little or no English. By the end of Foundation, they can introduce themselves, handle short everyday exchanges (shopping, ordering food, asking directions, basic scheduling), read and write short simple texts, and understand slow, clear speech on familiar topics.

What this award is not

WEC-LC holds no accreditation, and the College has not appointed an External Examiner — the independent post whose whole function is to confirm, from outside, that this level sits where the College says it sits. This award is defined, its criteria are published, and every one conferred so far was set, marked and second-marked inside the College. See About · Institutional Status.

Questions

What people ask about this level.

Duration

How long does Level I take?

4 months of study by design, covering 120 designed lessons across 10 modules. Learners who need longer are not penalised; the level is a body of work, not a race.

Entry

Do I need any English to start?

No. Foundation assumes none, and the first lesson teaches the alphabet and how to say your own name.

Assessment

What happens if I fail an assessment?

Assessments can be resat. The purpose is to establish what you can do, not to record a single bad afternoon — the appeals and resit procedure is published in full.

Recognition

Is the award recognised?

The award is defined and its criteria published, but WEC-LC holds no accreditation and has appointed no External Examiner. We state this plainly rather than implying recognition the College has not obtained.
