What the College may claim to have found.

An institution that measures its own teaching and publishes the result is marking its own work. These regulations are what stop a flattering internal number being published as a finding.

The College teaches, and it studies its own teaching. This instrument governs that study. It sets what counts as evidence here, who may evaluate work they did not run, how a learner’s data enters a study and on what consent, and when a result is published whether or not it flatters the College. It also settles how a published claim is corrected or withdrawn.

I Scope

The Press governs the volume. This governs the claim inside it.

What this instrument governs.

The College operates an imprint, and the imprint carries its own instrument. The Publishing Constitution settles what WEC Press may issue, how a volume is made, and the separation of an author from a reviewer. These regulations sit one step earlier, on the claim itself.

What research means here

Any systematic attempt to find out whether something the College does works, and to say so in public. A comparison between cohorts, an analysis of examination items, a trial of a teaching method, a survey of learners.

Why it is held this tightly

A claim about a college’s own effectiveness is the hardest claim it makes, because the institution making it holds all the data behind it. It is held to the standard in Leaf II rather than to the confidence of its prose.

Where the instrument sits

The Executive holds these regulations. The academic standard they serve is owned by the Board of Governors, and nothing here alters a rubric, a mark or an award — see Governance.

One sentence, and the rest of the page enforces it

A claim may not be stated more strongly than the class of evidence beneath it. Every rule that follows is a way of holding that sentence in place at the moment it becomes inconvenient, which is the only moment a rule of this kind is worth anything.

II Evidence

Four classes, and the class travels with the claim.

Four classes of evidence, ranked.

The College already marks the provenance of every statement a teacher will act on inside its own volumes, and this instrument takes the same four classes rather than inventing a second vocabulary. The class is written beside the claim and travels wherever the claim goes.

ClassWhat it isWhat may be said on it
Designed A decision the College took while building the curriculum. It may be described as designed, with the reasoning that produced it. It is never described as a result.
Derived Read off the curriculum, the register or the platform’s own data. It may be stated as a count, carrying the date it was taken and the query that produced it.
Established Attested in the field the College teaches in, with the source named. It may be cited, and the College’s own practice may be described as following it.
Observed Seen in the College’s own teaching, under a method fixed before the looking. It may be published as a finding, with its method, its figures and the limits the College can see in it.

A denominator, or no percentage

A proportion is published with the figure it is a proportion of. “Most learners” without a count is a sentence rather than a measurement, and it is precisely the sentence that survives longest once it is in circulation.

A comparison, or no claim of effect

Saying that something works is saying it works better than something else. The comparison is named and its measure is stated, or the claim is left unmade and the underlying figures are published on their own.

Where a numeral comes from

Every figure about cohorts, learners, awards or countries is drawn from the College’s record of standing, which holds who attested it and on what date. A number that reaches a page by any other route is a number nobody can be held to, and the build refuses it.

III Separation

The person who ran the thing does not get to mark it.

Whoever ran it does not evaluate it.

This is the rule the College already applies to appeals and to marking, turned on the College itself. An evaluation carried out by the person whose work is being evaluated reports that person’s confidence, which is a fact about them rather than about the teaching.

  • The officer who designed or delivered an intervention takes no part in evaluating it.
  • Every evaluation is commissioned by the Director of Quality Assurance, whose remit is programme evaluation and institutional review.
  • The question, the measure and the analysis are fixed and dated before a single figure is collected.
  • The analysis is repeated by a second person against the registered method before the result leaves the College.
  • The finished paper is read before publication by somebody who took no part in the work it reports.
The register

A study is written down before it starts

The Registrar holds the study register. A study is entered in it before any data is collected: the question, the population, the measure, the analysis and the date. The register is what makes a question changed halfway visible afterwards.

The limit on a commissioner

What commissioning a study does not buy

Whoever commissions an evaluation may set the question and may not set the answer. They may not edit a finding, and they may not decide after seeing the figures which measure counts. A measure chosen afterwards is an argument wearing a method’s clothes.

IV Consent

Teaching a learner and studying them are two different permissions.

Learner data, and the consent that releases it.

Most of what this College could study is material written about learners by the College: attempts, marks, recordings and feedback. Using that to teach somebody is one act. Using it to publish something about them, or about people like them, is a second act and takes a second permission.

How it is asked for

Opt-in, specific, and dated

Consent is asked for by name, for one study and its stated question, and it is recorded with the date it was given. A permission granted so that the College could teach somebody is not a permission to publish about them.

Withdrawal

It can be taken back

Up to the moment of publication a learner may withdraw consent, and their data leaves the analysis. Where a withdrawal after publication changes the result, the College issues a correction under Leaf VI rather than leaving the figure standing.

The guarantee

Never a condition of anything

Consent is never a condition of enrolment, of teaching, of assessment or of an award. A learner who refuses every request is taught, marked, moderated and awarded exactly as one who agrees to all of them, and the refusal is recorded so nobody asks twice.

Anonymisation

The floor under a published table

Published data carries no name, no address and no identifier that can be matched back to a person. No group smaller than five learners is reported, and a free-text answer is quoted only with the writer’s separate written consent.

Two instruments, read together

What the College collects, where it is stored and who may reach it are questions of data policy — see Privacy and your data. Who owns a learner’s work and what licence the College takes over it is a question of ownership — see Intellectual property. This leaf governs the further step of publishing something drawn from either.

V Unfavourable Results

The obligation the rest of this instrument exists to protect.

A result is published whether or not it flatters the College.

An institution that publishes its successes and files its disappointments has a marketing department rather than a research record. It also devalues every figure it does publish, because a reader who knows the losing results were filed has to discount the winning ones.

What “in full” covers

The question as registered, the method, the figures with their denominators, the analysis, and the limits the College can see in its own work. A finding published without its method is an assertion with a table beside it.

A result showing nothing is a result

A study that finds no difference is published on the same terms as one that finds a difference, in the same place and to the same timetable. Publishing only what moved is how a whole field fills up with effects nobody can reproduce.

VI Correction

Four notices, and a silent edit is none of them.

Correcting a claim already published.

A published claim that turns out to be wrong is corrected at the address where it was published, in a form the reader who saw the original will recognise. Nothing is quietly rewritten, and no correction is made in a place the original reader would have to go looking for.

NoticeWhen it is issuedWhat happens to the original
ClarificationThe finding stands; the wording misled a reader.Amended, carrying a dated note of what changed.
CorrectionA figure, a method or a limit was stated wrongly.Amended, with the error named and dated.
Expression of concernA doubt has been raised that the College cannot settle at once.Left standing, with the notice attached to it.
RetractionThe finding does not survive examination.Left at its address, marked retracted, with the reason.
The clock

Twenty working days from the error being established

Where the error sits in a figure a reader may already have acted on, the notice goes out first and the fuller analysis follows it. Getting the correction exactly right is worth less than getting it in front of the person who relied on it.

Permanence

A retracted finding stays where it was

It keeps its address and its mark for as long as this site exists. Deleting it would leave every citation of it pointing at nothing, and every reader who acted on it with no way of discovering that it fell.

VII Review

A word that carries borrowed authority, used exactly.

Review, described as it is.

A college of this size has to be exact about what review means here. The word carries a great deal of authority earned by other institutions over a long time, and borrowing it is the easiest overstatement in academic writing.

What the College itself does

Internal checking, and a great deal of it: the arithmetic, the method against the register, the terminology, and the claim against its class of evidence. Every paper is read before publication by someone who took no part in the work.

Why the data travels with the finding

The method and the figures are published alongside the claim so a reader can check it rather than take it on trust. A finding that can only be believed is a finding nobody outside the institution is able to test.

The standing invitation

Any suitably qualified reader who offers to read a paper is sent it, with the data behind it. Write to info@worldwencollege.co.uk. Criticism is the point of the exercise — see On review.

What the College says about its own findings

Every finding published here is a finding about this College, made by this College, on its own learners. It is offered as evidence a reader may inspect rather than as a conclusion a reader should accept, and the class of evidence is printed beside it so the weight of the claim is visible at a glance.

VIII Claims Refused

A prohibition is shorter than a principle and harder to argue with.

The claims these regulations refuse.

Each line on the left is a sentence the College declines to publish, whatever the internal figures happen to look like on the day. Each line on the right is what goes out in its place, which is why the refusals cost the reader nothing.

Refused, whatever the figures show

6
  • A ranking, a league position, or a comparison against a named institution.
  • A claim that something works, offered without a stated comparison and a stated measure.
  • A testimonial, a quotation or a single case offered as evidence that a method works.
  • A percentage published without the figure it is a percentage of.
  • A figure about learners, awards or countries that the record of standing did not supply.
  • A finding drawn from a study whose question was settled after the data had been seen.

Published in their place

4
  • The question as it was registered, and the date the register received it.
  • The method, the measure and the analysis, in enough detail for somebody else to repeat them.
  • The figures, with their denominators and the date they were taken from the record.
  • The class of evidence the claim rests on, printed beside the claim itself.
Why a refusal list rather than a principle

A principle is agreed with by everybody and applied by nobody at the moment it costs something. A list of sentences the College will not publish can be checked against a draft in a minute, by anybody, including a reader holding the College to it.
