The case for English

English is the room you are allowed into.

It is the working language of the university lecture hall, the tender document, the board paper, the cockpit and the arbitration. Command of it does not make a person capable. It makes a capable person legible — and without it the ability is there and the door is not.

This page makes no claim about the language's superiority. It sets out where the decision is actually taken in English, and the rule in each case that makes it so.

The domains

Eight rooms, and the rule that governs each.

Each of these is checkable. None of them is an opinion about English, and none of them is a claim about this College.

Diplomacy

English is one of the six official languages of the United Nations and the language most of its day-to-day work is conducted in. A delegation that cannot argue in it argues through someone else.

Aviation

The International Civil Aviation Organization requires demonstrated English proficiency of every pilot and every controller working international routes. It is not a preference there; it is a licensing condition.

International law

Cross-border contracts name a governing language, and it is overwhelmingly English. Arbitration before the major institutions is pleaded in it, and a party who cannot follow the hearing is represented rather than heard.

Science and research

The journals that confer standing on a finding publish in English, and so do the conferences that test it. A result written in another language is not less true; it is less read, and in research those are close to the same thing.

Higher education

Universities across every continent teach graduate programmes in English and admit on evidence of it. The offer is decided on academic English long before it is decided on ambition.

Commerce and capital

Tenders, term sheets, listings and board papers are drafted in English across most of the world's markets. The price of a deal is often settled by whoever writes the clause, and the clause is written in English.

Technology

The documentation, the standards, the specifications and the arguments that settle them are in English. An engineer without it works from translations of decisions taken without them.

Government and leadership

A minister, a regulator or a chief executive who can put their own case in English puts it themselves. Everyone else's position reaches the room through an interpreter, one remove from the person who holds it.

The consequence

What follows from that, for a person.

A capable engineer who cannot chair the meeting is chaired for. A researcher whose finding is sound but unpublished in the journals that confer standing is cited by nobody. A minister who briefs through an interpreter is one remove from the room, every time, on every question.

None of that is a failure of intelligence and it is not corrected by time spent abroad. It is corrected by instruction: a structure that names what is to be learnt, in what order, marked against criteria published before the work is attempted.

The commonest outcome

Not failure. A decade with nothing to show.

The usual result of language study is neither a pass nor a fail. It is years of lessons that never produced a sentence anybody would use, and no record of what was actually reached. A learner leaves knowing they studied and unable to say what they now hold.

That is what a published standard exists to prevent. Every level of this programme states its entry standard, its exit standard and its criteria before you begin, and confers an award with a title and a transcript when you meet them.
