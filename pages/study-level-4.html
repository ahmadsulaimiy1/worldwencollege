Assessment

How the level is judged.

Every instrument is written before the teaching it tests, and the criteria are published to the learner in advance.

Continuous

10 module quizzes

One at the end of each module, testing the language that module taught and nothing else. Marked automatically, with the correct answer and the reason shown.

Productive

10 assignments

Speaking and writing tasks marked by an instructor against a published rubric. These are where the productive skills are actually assessed — a quiz cannot judge whether someone can hold a conversation.

Self-check

Before every assessment

Each lesson carries a self-check with traps aimed at the mistakes learners at this level really make, so a learner discovers a gap before an examiner does.

Teaching Methods

How it is taught.

Speaking-first

Practice before polish

Guided speaking is built into every lesson, not offered as an extra. Learners speak from the first lesson, before their grammar is ready, because waiting for accuracy is how people stay silent for years.

Structured

A shared standard, not a personal style

Every instructor teaches to the same mapped curriculum, so a learner who changes class or cohort does not lose their place, and two learners at the same level have covered the same ground.

Supported

A teacher who knows what goes wrong

Instructors work from the Teacher's Companion, which sets out for each lesson what commonly goes wrong, a second way to explain it, and what to do for the learner who is behind and the one who is ahead.

Measured

Adjusted to the cohort

Quiz and assignment results are visible to the instructor as the level runs, so teaching responds to how this group is actually performing rather than to how the plan assumed they would.

A Complete Qualification

Level IV is a qualification, not a stage of one.

Six levels form one pathway, and every one of them is also a finished award with its own entry standard, its own exit standard and its own uses. A learner who completes this level and stops has completed something.

Who it is for

The reader this level was written for

A professional or a university-bound student who is already functional and is being held back by precision: register, structure, and the difference between being understood and being persuasive.

To enter

What is required to start

The Level&nbsp;III award, or B2 attainment demonstrated through the placement assessment or a recognised external qualification.

To be awarded

What is required to finish

70% overall with no skill below 50%, ten assignments, and a spoken paper at B2 requiring a technical topic discussed in the candidate&rsquo;s own field.

What the award is used for

At work

What a holder can do professionally

The level at which you can represent a position rather than only state one: chair a routine meeting, write a document that goes out under your name, negotiate a detail. Most international employers treat B2 as the working standard for professional English.

In study

Where it is accepted academically

Satisfies the entry standard for Level&nbsp;V. B2 is the band most universities require for admission to an English-taught programme, and this is where the College&rsquo;s examination preparation is at its most direct.

If you stop at Level IV

A complete B2 qualification &mdash; the most commonly required level in the world of work and study, and a finish line rather than a way station for most of the people who reach it. The College recommends the full pathway to anyone whose purpose needs it, and recommends stopping to anyone whose purpose does not. A provider that treats every exit as a failure is selling levels rather than teaching English.

Graduate profile

What the holder can do

The learner becomes fluent enough to follow extended discourse, argue a position in depth, and produce genuinely structured academic and professional writing. This level carries the heaviest step-up in register and complexity in the programme.

Two ways to take this level

Most candidates enrol: $3,166.67 for the level, four instalments, a named instructor and written feedback on every piece of produced work. A candidate who wants the qualification without the teaching may take the same level independently — access to the level, the examination and the award, bought separately — and sits the same examination for the same award. How this award is moderated

Every award at this level is set, marked and second-marked inside the College, against the criteria published above and before the work is attempted. That moderation is internal: no External Examiner is appointed, and that is the post which would confirm from outside that this level sits where the College says it sits. Questions

What people ask about this level.

Duration

How long does Level IV take?

4 months of study by design, covering 120 designed lessons across 10 modules. Learners who need longer are not penalised; the level is a body of work, not a race.

Entry

Do I need Level III first?

Not necessarily. You need the language Intermediate Programme teaches, however you acquired it. A placement assessment establishes that.

Assessment

What happens if I fail an assessment?

Assessments can be resat. The purpose is to establish what you can do, not to record a single bad afternoon — the appeals and resit procedure is published in full.

Commitment

Can I study this alongside a job?

Yes, and it is designed to be. Level IV is 200 designed hours across 4 months — about 12 hours a week, spent when you choose to spend them. Nothing opens on a fixed date and nothing closes if a week goes badly.
