Support · Complaints

Complaints, and what they are for.

An appeal contests a decision about you. A complaint raises the conduct of the College itself, and this is the instrument for the second one.

It sets out what may be complained about, who investigates, how long each step takes, and what an upheld complaint gets you. It ends on the clause that makes the rest credible: complaining costs you nothing.

I The Boundary

What this instrument hears, what the appeal route hears, and the window for either.

A complaint and an appeal are different instruments.

A complaint says the College fell short of what it published. An appeal says a decision about you was wrong. Sending one down the other’s route costs weeks.

One

Teaching and supervision

A lesson promised and not delivered. Feedback later than the published ten working days. A tutor silent for a fortnight. A module taught off the published curriculum.

Two

Administration, records and money

A transcript naming the wrong level. An enrolment processed twice. A refund handled outside the terms at Tuition. A correction left unanswered.

Three

The platform and the materials

A level that stayed shut after the one before it was completed. Audio that plays for nobody. A page missing from the volume you were told to use.

Four

How somebody treated you

Rudeness, or a remark about your country, your faith or your accent. Harassment and discrimination are investigated here and sanctioned under the code of conduct.

What belongs on the appeal route instead

A mark, a resit, a progression decision, a conduct finding or an admissions decision is contested by appeal, down the three stages in the academic regulations. A misdirected complaint is passed across within three working days, and its clock runs from the day you wrote.

Bring a complaint within three months of the thing complained of, or of the day you learned of it. The Registrar accepts a later one where the delay has a reason.

II Informally

The stage most matters end at, and why it takes nothing from you.

Say it to somebody first.

Much of what a formal procedure receives was a broken link or a week nobody explained. This stage exists so those are fixed in days rather than documented for a month.

How

Raise it with whoever is nearest

Your tutor, the Registrar, or info@worldwencollege.co.uk. A message in the student portal counts. Write in English or Arabic; the answer comes in that language.

When

An answer inside five working days

The deadline the College holds for any ordinary written question. Where it needs longer, you are told inside the five days, with the date it will come.

What it costs

Nothing, and no right is spent

Days spent here are added to your three months rather than taken from them. You may skip this stage, and nobody is asked to justify that.

III Formally

What the College asks for, what it sends back, and who it hands the matter to.

One form, and a named investigator.

A formal complaint is made in writing to the Registrar. There is no fee, no prescribed wording, and no requirement to have tried the informal stage.

The form

Four things, in up to a thousand words

What happened, when, who was involved, and what you want done. Attach what you hold. The fourth matters most: a complaint naming its remedy is answered faster.

Day three

An acknowledgement with a name on it

Within three working days you receive a reference, the investigator’s name and office, the date the decision is due, and what the College may do.

The appointment

Chosen by office, not by convenience

The Registrar appoints the investigator. Where the complaint concerns the Registrar, the Provost appoints; where it concerns the Executive, the Governor for Ethics and Institutional Values does.

The bar

Never the person complained about

Anyone named in a complaint is a witness to it and decides no part of it. They learn of it from the investigator, and are heard before it is decided.

The separation rule, written out

Four people are barred from investigating: the subject of the complaint, anybody in their reporting line, anybody who took a decision it questions, and anybody who heard it informally. Where those exclusions empty the office, the appointment rises until somebody unconflicted holds it. The chain of offices is published under Governance.

IV The Finding

The deadline, the one extension it allows, and the four things a decision may say.

Twenty working days, and one extension at most.

The investigator reads what you sent, asks the people involved, examines what the College holds, and writes a decision. It reaches you within twenty working days.

Where the matter genuinely needs longer, the College writes before day twenty with the reason and a fixed new date. That extension may be taken once and adds no more than fifteen working days.

The decision saysWhat follows from it
UpheldThe College fell short. The decision names the remedy, the office responsible and the date it takes effect.
Upheld in partSome of what you raised is made out and some is not. The decision separates the two and says which evidence carried which.
Not upheldThe College acted as it published. The decision sets out what was examined and how it concluded, so the reasoning can be argued with.
Already decidedThese facts have been decided before. The decision encloses the earlier one; a fresh fact reopens it.
  • Every decision arrives in writing, with its reasons and its evidence.
  • Anybody criticised has read the criticism and answered it first.
  • The College decides on the balance of probabilities and carries the burden.
  • A complaint may be withdrawn at any point, confirmed in writing.
  • Where a withdrawn matter bears on safety, it is finished anyway.
V The Review

Three grounds, a reader who took no part, and fifteen working days.

One review, read by somebody new.

Ask within ten working days of the decision, naming the ground you rely on. A review tests whether the decision was properly reached, rather than running the investigation again.

Ground one

The procedure was not followed

A deadline passed without an extension. The investigator was conflicted. An allegation shaped the finding and you never saw it.

Ground two

Something material was missed

Evidence you supplied went unaddressed, or a fact you could not have produced earlier has surfaced. Say why it changes the finding.

Ground three

The remedy does not match the finding

The complaint was upheld and what followed did not answer it — an apology where a mark had to be set aside, or better practice promised where something owed went undone.

Who reads it, and what they may do

A review is decided by an officer senior to the decision-maker who took no part in it: the Provost where the Registrar decided, the Governor for Ethics and Institutional Values where the complaint concerned the Executive. It is answered within fifteen working days. A reviewer may confirm the decision, substitute another, increase the remedy, or send it to a fresh investigator, and never reduces a remedy already granted.

VI The Clock

Every deadline in this procedure, laid end to end at its worst.

Sixty working days, at the outside.

Twenty to decide, fifteen more if the College extends once, ten for you to ask for a review, and fifteen to decide it. Nothing here runs longer than the line below.

What stops the clock

One thing does: a written extension, taken once, with a reason and a fixed new date. A complaint waiting on something the College asked you for pauses until you answer. Everything else runs against the College rather than against you.

VII The Remedies

Six things the College can put right, each within its own gift.

What an upheld complaint actually gets you.

Every remedy below is one the College delivers on its own authority. The decision names which applies, who is responsible, and when it takes effect.

One

An apology that says what for

Specific, in writing, from the office responsible. An apology regretting your feelings rather than the College’s conduct is not one.

Two

A correction of the record

An entry amended, a wrong standing lifted, a note removed. Anything sent out on the old record is reissued. What is held is listed at Your academic record.

Three

A fresh mark by a different marker

Where a complaint about the handling of an assessment is upheld, the work is marked again by somebody who took no part in the first marking.

Four

The teaching restored

A lesson delivered again, supervision replaced, or access extended by the time you lost. Where teaching was owed, the College gives it rather than pays for it.

Five

A refund or a credit of fees

Up to the fees paid for the level affected, returned to the account they came from or credited to the next level. The ordinary refund window is at Tuition.

Six

A change of practice

The rule, the form or the deadline that produced the complaint is altered, so the next learner meets a better one. Such changes are reported.

A remedy carries a date

Every remedy is delivered within twenty working days of the decision, or by the date the decision names where the thing takes longer. A date that passes undelivered is heard at the review stage rather than starting again.

VIII Standing

Complaints that arrive unsigned, brought by somebody else, or about another learner.

Anonymously, on somebody’s behalf, or about another learner.

Current learners, applicants, and former students within twelve months of leaving may complain. Three cases need saying plainly, because a procedure silent on them loses them.

Unsigned

An anonymous complaint

Recorded, given a reference, and investigated on whatever can be established without you. It counts in the yearly return, and a complainant who later gives a name receives the decision.

On behalf

Brought by somebody else

A parent, a sponsor, an employer or a fellow learner may complain. Where it concerns one learner’s record, the College acts on that learner’s written consent.

About a learner

Where the conduct code takes over

A complaint about another learner is investigated here and decided under the code of conduct, which holds the sanctions. You are told the outcome as far as it affects you.

A complaint is seen by the people who need it to decide it, and by nobody else. Your name reaches the person complained about, because answering an allegation requires knowing it. Where being named puts you at risk, the matter goes to the Registrar first and proceeds on the facts alone.

IX Protection

The clause that makes every other clause worth reading.

Complaining costs you nothing, and the College writes that down.

A learner who thinks a complaint will follow them into their marks keeps quiet, and a College that hears nothing believes it has no problems. So this protection is a rule with a sanction behind it.

What is barred

Detriment, named rather than gestured at

A harsher mark, a slower answer, a withdrawn reference, a lost cohort place, or a conduct case built on your having complained.

What follows

It is a breach in its own right

Detriment for having complained breaches the code of conduct, is investigated under it by somebody who took no part in your complaint, and carries the same tariff.

  • Marks are set against the published rubric, and a complaint is invisible to a marker.
  • Access to teaching continues throughout, as the regulations provide.
  • A complaint appears on no transcript and no reference.
  • Complaining twice, or ten times, is treated as complaining once.
  • A mistaken complaint carries nothing, provided it was made honestly.
X The Record

What is written down, how long it is held, and who may read it.

What is kept, and what is published.

A complaints procedure with no register learns nothing from itself. The College keeps one, apart from the academic record, and reports from it yearly.

The remedy and the date it was deliveredSix yearsThe office responsible for it
Your own fileOn request, at any timeYou, in full, within ten working days
The yearly returnHeld permanentlyPublished, and laid before the Board

The complaint file sits outside the academic record. It reaches no marker, no reference and no transcript, and goes to nobody outside the College without your written consent. What is held about you is set out at Privacy.

Once a year the Executive lays a return before the Board of Governors and publishes it under the decisions register. It gives the complaints received by category, how many were upheld, the median working days to a decision, and every change of practice made as a remedy. It names nobody.
