I The Commitment

Three operating rules, each a thing that happens rather than a thing that is believed.

What the College binds itself to.

Equality at this College is a set of arrangements rather than a sentiment. Each of the three below can be tested against what actually happened to a learner.

One

One standard, applied to everybody

Every learner is taught the published curriculum and marked against the rubric published before the task. That rubric carries no criterion for background, accent, nationality or belief, and a mark that cannot be justified against it is overturned.

Two

The route in is the same for everyone

There is one entry route, one published fee schedule and no qualification requirement. Nothing about an applicant’s country, name, gender or means changes what they are offered or what they pay.

Three

The barrier moves, and the standard stays

An adjustment changes how a learner demonstrates a competency. It never removes the competency from the assessment, and it never lowers what an award means. Those two sentences are the whole adjustment policy in principle.

What makes this enforceable

Every clause on this page names the office that owns it and the working days it runs on. Where the College misses one, that is a matter for the code of conduct, which entitles a learner to a written answer with reasons. An obligation without a deadline cannot be enforced in either direction.

II Admission

Four mechanics that make one entry route the same route for everybody.

Admission on the same terms, and how that is made real.

Most unequal admission is not decided; it accumulates, in the documents a form demands and the impressions an interview leaves. The four rules below remove the places where it collects.

The form

It asks for what a decision needs

The application asks no photograph, no identity papers and no fee. A field earns its place only where the College would otherwise have to write and ask for it.

The placement

A level is decided by an assessment

Placement rests on the placement assessment, marked against a published descriptor — not on where somebody was schooled, which passport they hold, or how confident they sounded.

No quota

No country is capped, and none is preferred

The College sets no ceiling and no floor on admissions from any country, and operates no agent network that would give one market a different price. What is published is what everyone pays.

A refusal

Comes with its reason, in writing

An applicant who is declined receives the ground for it, in writing, against the published admissions rules. A decision that need not be explained is the one that carries a prejudice unnoticed.

What the College asks about a person, and what it does not

Admission requires a name, a way of reaching somebody, where they are, and confirmation that they are an adult. Faith, ethnicity and health are asked at no stage. A learner who wants an adjustment tells the Registrar what they need — a different question, put by the learner, at a moment of their choosing.

III Adjustments

How to ask, what is asked of you, who decides, by when, and what is on offer.

Reasonable adjustments, and the procedure that grants them.

An adjustment is a change to how a learner takes part, so that an assessment measures their English rather than their circumstances. Asking for one is a right under these regulations rather than a favour.

How to ask

One line to the Registrar

Write to the Registrar at any point, before enrolling or mid-level. Say what you need rather than why you need it. Where an adjustment affects an assessment, ask at least ten working days before it.

Who decides

The Registrar, with the Dean where a mark turns on it

The Registrar decides. Where the adjustment changes a condition of an assessment the Dean of Academic Affairs decides it jointly, so the academic standard is weighed by the officer who owns it.

By when

Ten working days, and it then stands

A decision arrives in writing within ten working days, with its reason. A granted adjustment stands for the rest of the programme until the learner asks to change it, so nobody explains themselves again at every level.

What evidence is asked for

For an adjustment that changes nothing about an assessment — a different format, a written channel, a change of hour — the College asks for nothing but the request. Where an assessment condition changes, it asks for one document naming the barrier: a letter from a qualified professional, or an adjustment a school, university or employer already granted. It need not be recent and it need not name a diagnosis. Where no such document can be obtained, the Registrar decides on the learner’s own written account and says so.

Three rules that travel with every adjustment

An adjustment carries no charge, at any level, ever. It is held on the student record and appears on no transcript, in no reference and in no mark: a marker is told the condition of the task, never the reason for it. And a refusal is appealable under the academic regulations, which put the decision to somebody who took no part in the first one.

IV The Classroom

What a classroom spanning four regions obliges the teaching to do.

Language and respect, in a classroom that spans four regions.

One cohort of this College spans the Gulf, Europe, Africa and Asia. A programme drawn that widely cannot treat one region’s habits as the neutral case, and the four rules below are how the teaching answers it.

Accent

Intelligibility is the criterion, and accent is not

Speaking is marked on whether a listener understands, not on whether a learner sounds British. Every standard English is accepted — British, American, Australian, Indian or another national standard — and a learner is asked only to hold one consistently within a piece of work.

Names

Your name, spelled and said as you give it

The College records a learner’s name as they write it, in the script they write it in where the record allows, and asks how it is said. Nobody is given a shorter name for an instructor’s convenience.

Topics

Argument is taught without conscripting belief

The course teaches how to concede a point and hold a position under question. Where a task would require a learner to argue against their own religion, the task is changed rather than the learner — see Equity and Welcome.

Time

Nothing is scheduled against an obligation

The programme is self-paced, which is the accommodation that comes before all the others: a learner fits it around prayer, fasting, a night shift or a family. No fixed deadline exists to choose against.

What an instructor may not introduce

The published rubric is the whole of what is marked. An instructor may not add a criterion to it — not for register, not for cultural reference, not for how close an answer sits to their own assumptions. A learner who believes a mark carried one says so, and the work is marked again by somebody else under the assessment scheme.

V Materials

The form the teaching comes in, and what it costs to have it another way.

Materials, in more than one form.

How this site is built for a disabled reader is set out at Accessibility. What belongs on this page is the rule about the teaching material itself.

Composed, not enlarged

A large-print edition of its own

The Large Print curriculum is set at its own size, because enlarging a page designed for another size breaks its line lengths and tables. It is issued at the price of the ordinary edition.

Listening

Every recording has its script

Listening work is published as full scripts alongside the audio, so a deaf or hard-of-hearing learner reaches the same material rather than a summary. The scripts are open to everyone, at no charge.

Two languages

The whole site in Arabic and English

Every page of this site ships in both languages, and neither edition is a summary of the other. A reader weighing a year of fees reads the same argument in the language they think in.

If something is unusable for you

Write to the Registrar and describe what happened rather than what you think should be built. A report of a real barrier is answered within ten working days, and where the fix is small it is made first and reported afterwards. An unreported barrier is one the College has no way of finding.

VI Fee Support

The largest barrier on this list, and the three instruments aimed at it.

Means, treated as an equality question.

For most applicants the barrier is money rather than attitude, and an equality policy silent on price has left out the thing that decides who comes.

Remission

A scholarship funded by a rule

The Foundation Remission takes a fixed share of every dollar of tuition received, so the fund exists whenever tuition does. Need is weighed more heavily than promise, and the criteria and panel are published at Tuition.

Instalments

Offered to everyone, at no charge

A level’s fee divides into equal instalments on request rather than on application, and the plan costs nothing to use. A plan priced above the lump sum charges the poorest learner most for being poorest.

One level at a time

Nobody commits to six years of anything

A learner may buy one level and decide again afterwards, and paying for the whole programme buys no discount. The College declines to price a larger commitment as a bargain, because the person least able to make it pays the most.

What hardship is asked to prove

The remission asks for one supporting document where one exists, and the College requires no document a candidate in hardship cannot obtain. An applicant who is not awarded is told so, with the score against each published criterion, rather than left to infer it from silence.

VII Learner Voice

Where a learner’s view enters a decision, and what comes back.

How a learner reaches a decision that affects them.

Consultation with no seat and no answer is a suggestion box. Each of the three routes below ends somewhere a decision is taken.

Two seats

Learners sit on the annual programme review

Two places on the Student Experience Committee’s annual review of the programme are held for learners, filled from the current cohort for a term of one year. They read the same papers as everybody else there.

Every level

A review at the end of each level

Every learner is invited to review the level they have just finished. It is optional, never attached to a mark, and may be given under a name or without one.

Any day

A letter to the Registrar

Anything about how the College treats people can be written to the Registrar. It is answered by a person within five working days, and where it asks for a change it goes to the officer who can make one.

  • Every submission is answered, including the ones the College declines to act on.
  • A learner review is never linked to a mark, a standing or a reference.
  • What a review changed is published each year in the evidence record.
  • A learner holding a seat may be accompanied, and may speak to a paper in writing.

Why the College counts itself and not its learners

An equality report built on identity data requires an institution to ask every learner their faith, their ethnicity and their health, and to hold those answers. This College asks none of them, and declines to begin asking in order to produce a statistic about its own fairness. The rows above measure the College’s conduct instead, which is the half of the question it controls.

How to hold the College to this page

Every clause here names an office and a number of working days. Where one is missed, quote the clause back to the Registrar and it is answered in writing with a reason. Where that does not satisfy, the appeal route in the academic regulations puts it to somebody who took no part in the first decision, and then to the Board.
