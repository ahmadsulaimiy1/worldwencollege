      This statement covers your account and nobody else’s. It is produced from the ledger itself — the same rows the College’s own revenue report reads — so a figure here and a figure there cannot disagree. Where the platform does not hold a fact, it says so rather than estimating one.

      I The ledger

      Four rules the College holds itself to about money, each one checkable on the page above.

      A balance you can check.

      The workings are published

      Assessed, less relief, less what you have paid, plus anything refunded. Every term is on the page with its own figure, and the page says whether they add up. A balance nobody can check is a balance you are asked to trust.

      Two currencies, never one

      Every figure is shown in the College’s ledger currency and, where the rate is known, in yours — with the date that rate was true carried on the figure itself, so a screenshot of it stays honest.

      What you were charged, as charged

      An invoice shows the amount that actually reached the card, in the currency it was taken in, beside the ledger figure. Converting what you paid at today’s rate would overwrite a fact with an estimate.

      Relief is measured, not claimed

      A discount appears here once it has actually reduced a charge, and it names the authority that granted it. A scholarship you hold but have not yet applied is listed with nothing attributed to it, which is the truthful figure.

      If a figure looks wrong

      Say so, and quote the reference beside it. A query about a charge is answered by the Registry under the complaints procedure, in writing, within the published clock — and the full fee schedule the College may charge against is closed and published at Tuition & Fees. If a cost is not on that list, it does not exist.
