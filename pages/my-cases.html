          I The instrument

          Four things Decision E2 settles, each of them a constraint on the College rather than on the person appealing.

          An appeal reconsidered by whoever decided it first is not an appeal.

          Nobody hears a case they were part of

          Stage one goes to a member of academic staff senior to, and other than, whoever took the decision. Stage two goes to the Senate, which sets standards and marks nothing. Stage three goes to a Governor, and no member of the Executive sits on the Board.

          The clock is published and it is shown running

          Three working days to acknowledge, ten to answer at stage one, twenty at stage two. Where the College publishes no interval it says so on your case and names what it holds itself to instead, which is the longest interval it does publish.

          No stage moves without a reason on the record

          Every change of stage is written down with the post that made it and the reason it gave. A case moved silently is an institution moving a person’s file without saying why, which is the thing the procedure exists to prevent.

          The vocabulary follows what you asked for

          An appeal and a complaint are upheld or they are not. A withdrawal, a deferral and a transfer are granted or refused. Nobody is told their request to pause was “not upheld”, which is an answer to an argument they never made.

          Where the chain ends, and the College says so

          The Board of Governors is independent of the Executive; it is not independent of the institution, and there is nobody outside the College in the chain. Anyone who has exhausted stage three and remains dissatisfied is entitled to say so publicly, and the College will not treat having done so as a matter of conduct.
