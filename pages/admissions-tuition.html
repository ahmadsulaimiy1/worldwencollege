Currency

One settled currency, and six that are not.

The College charges in US dollars. Other currencies exist in the system as recognised codes, but a currency is only settled once a rate policy stands behind it, and none of them has one yet.

Settled

The US dollar

Every fee is set and charged in dollars. $19,000 means $19,000, and $791.67 means $791.67 — on the day you pay it and on any other day.

Recognised, not settled

Sterling, naira, riyal, dirham, dinar

Your bank or card issuer converts at its own rate on the day. The College publishes no local-currency price, because a price with no fixed rate behind it is a number that changes without notice — and it would change most for the students furthest from a dollar economy.

Recognised in the system as codes: AED, GBP, KWD, NGN, QAR, SAR. A code is not a price. A currency becomes settled here on the day a rate policy stands behind it, and that policy will be published rather than applied quietly.

Where a bank converts, the conversion is between you and your bank. The College sees only the dollar amount and cannot quote or guarantee the local figure.

Ways to Pay

Three arrangements, smallest first.

Instalments · the default

Four payments of $791.67

A level’s fee in four equal parts, offered to everyone rather than granted on request, and carrying no charge for using it. The College absorbs the rounding rather than handing you a fraction of a cent. The split is equal because no evidence-based cadence policy has been adopted; when one is, it will be published here rather than changed quietly.

Per level

$3,166.67 at a time

One level paid at its start, and the next decided only when you reach it. This is what most people choose, and it is what the College would choose in your position: it commits you to ten modules and to nothing after them.

Full programme

$19,000 once

One payment covers all six levels. It enrols you in Level I immediately; Levels II to VI are added one at a time as each preceding level is completed, so a paid-for level is a level you are ready to study. It buys no discount, and the College will not present it as the better choice: it is the larger exposure, and the section below says so.

Refunds

No refund policy yet — so the College limits what it can hold.

What is true

The College can process a refund technically — the mechanism is built and tested against the payment gateways. What does not exist is a policy: who authorises a refund, on what grounds, within what window, and at what proportion. Those are executive decisions and none of them has been taken. Until they are, a refund request is decided case by case by the founding team, in writing, and the decision is recorded against the payment. That is a weaker guarantee than a published policy and it is stated as one.

If you want certainty first

Pay by level, not in full

Your exposure is whatever you have paid and not yet studied. On instalments that is $791.67; on a level, $3,166.67; on the full programme, $19,000. Until a policy exists the College will not recommend the largest of those, and it does not price it to tempt you into it.

What the College commits to now

One instalment, and never more, before you have studied

Until a refund policy is adopted, the College will not hold more than a single instalment from anyone who has not yet begun studying. That is a commitment it can keep without anybody’s permission, which is the only kind this College makes. Every refund request is also answered in writing with a reason, and decisions taken before a policy exists will stay on the record rather than be quietly reinterpreted once one does.

The Position

A mechanism exists. A scheme does not.

The distinction matters, so it is drawn plainly rather than blurred.

Exists

The award mechanism

The College can record a scholarship against a named person, as a percentage, a fixed sum or a full remission, with the approving officer recorded alongside it. It applies automatically at checkout for the person it was awarded to, and for nobody else. This is built and tested.

Does not exist

Eligibility, criteria, a fund, a deadline

No criteria have been adopted, no budget has been allocated, no round has opened and no scholarship has been awarded to anyone. Publishing criteria the College could not fund would be worse than publishing nothing.

Why this is not simply written and published

A scholarship scheme is a spending commitment and an equity commitment at once. Deciding it requires a fund, a set of criteria that can be applied consistently to strangers, and someone accountable for applying them — the same three things that are missing from every other unfilled post at the College. It is an executive decision, and it has not been taken.

What You Can Do Today

Three routes that are real.

One

Pay level by level

The programme is deliberately divisible. $3,166.67 for one level, decided one level at a time, is a materially different commitment from $19,000, and no explanation is required to choose it.

Two

Spread a level across 4 payments

An instalment plan divides a single level’s fee into equal parts. It is available on request rather than by application, and carries no charge for using it.

Three

Write and ask

If the fee is the only thing standing between you and the programme, say so to Admissions in your own words. There is no fund to draw on and no promise attached to this, but a request that is never made cannot be considered when there is one.

Sponsors and Employers

If someone else is paying.

Possible

A third party paying for a named learner

An employer, a ministry or a family member can pay for a named learner today. The payment is recorded against the learner’s enrolment, and the learner — not the payer — controls their own academic record.

Not built

Organisational invoicing and seats

A corporate account with purchase-order invoicing and assignable seats exists in the data model and has no working process behind it. It will be built against a real organisation’s requirements rather than guessed at in advance. If that is you, write to Admissions.

You are buying a level, and a level is a published quantity.
