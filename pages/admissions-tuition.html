Currency

One settled currency, and six that are not.

The College charges in US dollars. Other currencies exist in the system as recognised codes, but a currency is only settled once a rate policy stands behind it, and none of them has one yet.

Settled

The US dollar

Every fee is set and charged in dollars. $19,000 means $19,000, and $791.67 means $791.67 — on the day you pay it and on any other day.

Recognised, not settled

Sterling, naira, riyal, dirham, dinar

Your bank or card issuer converts at its own rate on the day. The College publishes no local-currency price, because a price with no fixed rate behind it is a number that changes without notice — and it would change most for the students furthest from a dollar economy.

Recognised in the system as codes: AED, GBP, KWD, NGN, QAR, SAR. A code is not a price. A currency becomes settled here on the day a rate policy stands behind it, and that policy will be published rather than applied quietly.

Where a bank converts, the conversion is between you and your bank. The College sees only the dollar amount and cannot quote or guarantee the local figure.

Ways to Pay

Three arrangements, smallest first.

Instalments · the default

Four payments of $791.67

A level’s fee in four equal parts, offered to everyone rather than granted on request, and carrying no charge for using it. The College absorbs the rounding rather than handing you a fraction of a cent. The split is equal because no evidence-based cadence policy has been adopted; when one is, it will be published here rather than changed quietly.

Per level

$3,166.67 at a time

One level paid at its start, and the next decided only when you reach it. This is what most people choose, and it is what the College would choose in your position: it commits you to ten modules and to nothing after them.

Full programme

$19,000 once

One payment covers all six levels. It enrols you in Level I immediately; Levels II to VI are added one at a time as each preceding level is completed, so a paid-for level is a level you are ready to study. It buys no discount, and the College will not present it as the better choice: it is the larger exposure, and the section below says so.

Refunds

The refund policy, adopted 17 August 2026.

Decision E1, taken by the Executive and in force for every payment made from that date. The rule is simple on purpose: a window in which a payment is always returned in full, and a clear line after which a level is treated as delivered.

The rule

Any payment — an instalment, a full level, or the full programme — is refunded in full, no reason required, if you ask within 14 days of making it and before you have opened any assessed work in the level it paid for. Once either of those has passed, the fee for that level is earned: a level is priced and delivered as one qualification, not as a sequence of lessons you can return unopened. A level you have not yet paid for is never at risk, whatever happens to one you have.

This policy does not replace your rights at law

Wherever the law of your own country gives you a stronger right than this policy does, that law applies and this policy gives way to it. Nothing here asks you to waive anything, and the College will not cite its own policy against a statutory right. This is stated because a published refund policy tends to be read as the whole of a buyer’s protection, and a document that left that impression uncorrected would be the thing that misled you.

Paying by instalment

Each instalment carries its own window

The 14-day, work-not-opened test applies to every instalment on its own. Withdraw after paying the first of four and before the level’s work is opened, and that $791.67 is returned; the remaining three are simply never collected. Nothing is owed for an instalment that was never charged.

Paying by level or in full

The same test, on the larger sum

A level paid in one instalment of $3,166.67, or a full programme paid at $19,000, is refunded in full under the identical rule — 14 days, work not opened. Paying in full buys no certainty the level price does not already give you; it only puts a larger sum behind the same 14-day window, which is why the College continues to recommend paying level by level.

Outside the standard window

Considered individually, not refused automatically

Documented exceptional circumstances — serious illness, bereavement, or an error the College itself made — are considered by the Executive on their own facts, in writing, whether or not the 14 days or the first assessed item has passed. There is no promise of a particular outcome; there is a promise that the request is read by a person and answered with a reason, not dismissed on a date alone.

How it is paid

Written within five working days, paid within ten

Every request receives a written decision within five working days. An approved refund is returned to the original payment method within ten working days of that decision. Nothing is deducted from it: no application fee exists to withhold, and this policy names no administrative charge of its own.

Payments made before this policy

The policy applies to every payment from 17 August 2026 onward. A request about a payment made before that date is decided under this policy too, as if it had already been in force. The one exception runs in the payer’s favour: where the case-by-case standard the College promised at the time would give them more, that earlier standard applies instead. Nobody is worse off for having paid early.

Receipts

Issued

On request

What a band does not buy
  • The band applies to tuition. It never applies to an assessment standard, a pass mark or a moderation step.

III The Foundation Remission

A scholarship scheme funded by a rule rather than announced as a figure.

The Foundation Remission.

Five per cent of every dollar of tuition the College receives goes into the remission fund. The fund therefore exists exactly when tuition does, cannot be overspent, and needs no capital the College does not have. The consequence is stated rather than hidden: in a term with little tuition there is little remission.

What is weighed, and how much

60%

Financial need

Household circumstances as stated by the applicant, with one supporting document where one exists. The College does not require documents a candidate in hardship cannot obtain, and says so rather than quietly rejecting them.

40%

Academic promise

The placement assessment already taken, and a written statement of 300 words on what command of English would change for the applicant. Marked against a published descriptor, not an impression.

Who decides, and who can audit it
  1. 1Applications are received by the Registrar, who holds student records and confirms eligibility.
  2. 2A panel of three assesses them: the Registrar, the Director of Student Success on need, and the Dean of Academic Affairs on promise.
  3. 3The panel's decisions are minuted with the score against each criterion. Every applicant is told the outcome, including those who are not awarded.
  4. 4The Governor for Finance and Audit receives the round's report and may audit any decision in it.
When a round opens

A round opens with each cohort, closes fourteen days before that cohort begins, and is decided within fourteen days of closing.

IV Referral

A remission against your own next fee. Never cash, and never to anyone outside the College.

A credit, not a commission.

The moment a person is paid cash for bringing the College a student, that person has an interest in what they say about it, and the College has bought a salesperson it cannot supervise. So the benefit is a credit against the referrer's own next fee, it is capped, and it settles only after the referred student's refund window has closed.

V Alumni

What an award-holder has that a stranger does not.

Standing, not a discount.

Almost everything a college normally sells its alumni, this one already gives away: the Library is free to anyone, verification is free to anyone, and certificates and transcripts are reissued free for life to the person they belong to. There is no discount left to invent that would not be an admission the first fee was too high. What an award-holder has instead is standing.

01

The independent route, without re-enrolling

An award-holder may take any later level by the independent route at the published step fees, without applying again. The placement already stands.

02

The referral credit, usable either way

An award-holder who refers a candidate receives the same $250 credit, and may set it against an independent-route fee rather than tuition.

03

The register, by choice

A public roll of award-holders, for anyone wanting to confirm a person rather than a document. Appearing in it is the graduate's decision, not a consequence of graduating.

04

Everything else, already free

Every Library volume, every verification check, and every reissue of a certificate or transcript — free to alumni because they are free to everyone.

What You Can Do Today
