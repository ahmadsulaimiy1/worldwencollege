About WEC

An institution built on one conviction: English fluency changes what's possible.

Worldwide English College — London Campus is being built as an international English-language institution: one programme, six CEFR-aligned levels, taught and assessed online, for students wherever they are in the world.

Our Operating Model

Online-first by design. London-based by administration.

"London Campus" identifies WEC's administrative and management headquarters — it is not a claim of a physical teaching campus, and it never referred to more than one. Our educational delivery is intentionally online-first: every level of the IEFC is taught through live and recorded instruction inside our digital campus, so a student in Lagos, Riyadh, or Manila studies on equal footing with one in London. This is a deliberate strategic choice for global accessibility and flexibility, not a stand-in for premises we don't yet have.

What "London Campus" means

The institution's administrative and management headquarters, and the seat of its governance — the brand and legal home of WEC.

What "London Campus" doesn't mean

A physical building where classes are taught, or a network of regional campuses. Every student, everywhere, studies through the same online digital campus.

Our Commitment

We would rather tell you plainly what isn't finished yet than dress up an incomplete claim as a settled fact. Every section on this site follows that rule — including this one.

The Vision

An ambition, stated as an ambition.

That sentence is a destination, not a description. WEC is a new institution: it holds no accreditation, has conferred no award, and has taught no cohort. Publishing a vision does not change any of that, and this page does not pretend otherwise.

Recognised

Recognition has to be earned from outside

Recognition means external bodies attesting to quality — accreditation, external examining, professional endorsement. None of it can be self-awarded, and none of it has been obtained. The College is building the evidence such bodies ask for rather than describing itself as though they had already asked.

Excellent

Excellence is a standard, not a claim

What can be shown today is the standard the work is held to: a mapped curriculum, published rubrics, assessments written before the teaching they test, and a policy of recording what is missing. That is measurable now; graduate success is not.

Honest

The vision includes how it is pursued

An institution that overstates itself while becoming excellent is not becoming excellent. The refusal to publish unearned claims is part of the vision, not a constraint on it.

What This Means In Practice

Three commitments that follow from it.

One

Publish the standard before the result

Rubrics, pass criteria and learning outcomes are published to learners before assessment, not explained afterwards. A standard revealed after the fact is not a standard.

Two

Record the gap rather than fill it

Where the College lacks something — an external examiner, appointed board members, classroom evidence — the record says so. Every published volume states what it does not yet rest on.

Three

Let the curriculum carry the claim

The strongest thing WEC can show a prospective student is the programme itself: six levels, sixty modules, every lesson planned and every assessment mapped. That is inspectable today.

The Mission

What the College undertakes to do.

One

Teach English to a standard the College can evidence

Delivered as 6 CEFR-aligned levels, 60 modules, and 720 designed lessons — every one planned stage by stage rather than left to the room.

Two

Develop confident, fluent and academically competent speakers

Guided speaking is built into every lesson from the first, and the four skills are assessed separately so a learner strong in reading and weak in speaking is not described as simply "intermediate".

Three

Prepare learners for study, employment and professional communication

The upper levels teach academic writing, meetings, negotiation, advocacy and research presentation — the registers that decide outcomes rather than the ones that pass a test.

Four

Integrate modern educational technology with expert instruction

A learning platform carrying lessons, quizzes, self-checks and progress, plus a Listening Lab that records the learner’s own speech for pronunciation feedback. Technology serving instruction, not replacing it.

Five

Provide accessible, high-quality English education worldwide

Taught online from a London administrative base, with multi-currency pricing and payment routing built for learners in Africa, the Gulf, Europe and Asia rather than retrofitted for them.

Five Positions

The beliefs that shape every lesson.

One

Speaking comes before readiness

Learners are asked to speak from the first lesson, before their grammar can support it. Waiting until you are accurate enough to speak is how people study a language for years and cannot use it. Accuracy is built through use, not before it.

Two

A curriculum is a promise, not a suggestion

Every lesson states its objectives, its prerequisites and the timing of each stage, and every instructor teaches to it. This is not distrust of teachers; it is what lets a learner change class, pause and return, or be taught by two people, without losing their place.

Three

Assessment criteria belong to the learner

Rubrics and pass criteria are published before the assessment. A learner who does not know what is being judged is being tested on guessing the examiner, and the least advantaged learners guess worst.

Four

Four skills, four measurements

Listening, reading, speaking and writing are recorded separately. A single overall grade averages away exactly the information a learner needs — which skill is holding them back.

Five

Error is data, not failure

Each lesson carries a self-check built around the mistakes learners at that level actually make, so a learner meets their own error privately before an examiner meets it. The traps are deliberate.

The Competency Framework

Beyond proficiency: what fluency is for.

IEFC is an advanced academic qualification built on CEFR proficiency, extending it through competency verification, leadership, professional communication, critical thinking, authentic assessment, and independently verifiable digital credentials.

CompetencyWhat it means
ClarityUnderstood the first time, by the audience actually present
CommandControls the language rather than being carried by it
JudgementChooses register, channel and moment; knows what not to say
ReasonConstructs an argument, tests it, concedes what should be conceded
BearingHolds a room, a call, a difficult conversation
ReachCommunicates across cultures, and across the distance between expert and layperson
The status of this framework

The competencies are defined and every Level I assessment is mapped to them. Those mappings are recorded as interim, approved under authority delegated to the Press, because the Board that would ratify them has not been constituted. They are used and they are not yet ratified, and the difference is recorded in the database rather than smoothed over.

What Follows

How belief becomes practice.

In the lesson

A plan with timings

Warm-up, presentation, guided practice, independent practice, listening, reading, writing, pronunciation, homework — each with stated minutes, so the speaking stage is not the one that gets cut when time runs short.

In the room

A teacher who knows what fails

Instructors work from a Companion that names, for each lesson, what commonly goes wrong, a second way to explain it, and what to do for the learner who is behind and the one who finished early.

After the lesson

Evidence that accumulates

Quiz and assignment results, time on task and pronunciation attempts build a record the learner owns and can show — not a certificate asserting a level, but evidence of what was done.

Separation of Duties

Three separations that are not negotiable.

These exist only to keep certain people independent of each other. They are the first thing an accreditation panel looks for, they cost nothing to preserve now, and they are expensive to unpick later.

First

Author &ne; Academic Reviewer

Nobody reviews their own work. A publication reviewed by its author has not been reviewed.

Second

Reviewer &ne; External Examiner

The examiner’s whole function is being outside the College. It cannot be doubled with an internal role.

Third

Conferring &ne; examining

Whoever confers an award is not whoever examined it.

What this means for staffing

The minimum credible academic staffing is two qualified people who are not each other, plus one external. Everything else at this size can legitimately be combined — records with assessment administration, finance with procurement, IT with learning technologies.
