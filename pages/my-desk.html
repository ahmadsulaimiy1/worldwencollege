        This desk shows what was addressed to you and what you are party to. A notice sent to another learner is never read out of the database on your request, and a thread you were never added to cannot be reached by guessing its address.

        I The rules

        Four decisions about correspondence, each of them made in the database rather than in the page that draws it.

        Who a notice is for is a question the database answers, not a filter the page applies.

        A notice you may not see is never loaded

        The audience test is part of the query, so a notice addressed to one learner is not read into memory on another learner’s request. Nothing is filtered out afterwards, because a filter is a line of code that happens to still be there.

        The watermark follows the page, not the clock

        Opening a thread marks read exactly the messages you were shown. A message that arrives while the thread is loading stays unread and keeps its badge, so nothing is ever marked read on your behalf and then lost.

        An untranslated notice says so

        A notice is published in one language or in two. Where yours is not among them the College names the edition you are being shown, because a notice in the wrong language that does not announce itself is indistinguishable from a broken one.

        You write to an office, and the office answers

        A learner addresses the tutors of their level or the Registrar’s desk. Who staffs that desk is the College’s to publish on its own pages; here you are told the desk is reachable, which is what you need to write to it.

        Reading a notice is not the cost of losing it

        Marking a notice read and dismissing it are two separate acts, and the record keeps them apart. A notice you have read stays on this page until you dismiss it, and the first time you opened it is kept as the fact it is rather than being overwritten on every visit.
