What the College owes you after the award.

A qualification is worth what a stranger can still check about it in twenty years. Every line on this page is an undertaking the College gives for the life of the award, and each of them is exercised by writing one email to the Registry.

Alumni standing at this College is a standing, not a subscription. It begins on the day an award is entered in the Register of Awards, and it runs from there without renewal, grade or fee. This page states what a graduate is owed for life, what a chapter is and how one is formed, how graduates contribute to teaching and to admissions, and what the College may and may not do with a graduate’s name.

I Standing

When it begins, what it costs, and what cannot take it away.

A standing that begins at conferral.

Most institutions make alumni status an afterthought of graduation. Here it is a consequence of it, and it is the same act: an award entered in the Register creates a graduate, and a graduate holds everything set out below from that moment.

01

It begins on the date of conferral

The date on the certificate is the date the standing starts. Leaving the programme afterwards has no bearing on it: an award already made stands on its own, and a graduate who never returns for a further level is a graduate on the same terms as one who takes all six.

02

It carries no term and no renewal

Standing runs for as long as the award stands. The College charges nothing for it, issues no renewal notice, and operates no membership grade above or below it. There is one class of graduate and every graduate is in it.

03

It attaches to the person, not the payer

Where an employer, a ministry or a family paid the fees, the standing is still the graduate’s alone. A sponsor buys a place in a cohort. Nothing in this page is owed to a sponsor, and nothing here can be exercised on a graduate’s behalf.

The single condition on all six

A document is issued to the person it belongs to and to nobody else. Where a graduate wants a transcript sent directly to a third party, the direction is given in writing by the graduate, names the recipient, and is acted on once. The College declines a request made by an employer, a relative or an agent for a document about someone else, however the request is framed. That refusal is the reason the undertakings above are worth anything.

III The Chapters

One per award, and a rule for forming a regional one.

The Society, in six chapters.

The Worldwide English College Alumni Society is constituted in six chapters, one for each award. A graduate belongs to the chapter of their highest standing award. Membership is derived from the Register rather than kept as a second list, so a graduate who takes a further level changes chapter on the day the new award is entered, without applying to anybody.

Formation

How a regional chapter is formed

Twelve award-holders in one country, or in one metropolitan area, may petition the Registrar to form a regional chapter. The Registrar answers within 20 working days. Recognition runs for three years and is renewed on a petition from the chapter’s own members.

Office

How a chapter is led

Officers are elected by the members of the chapter, one member one vote, for a term of two years. The Registrar records the result and holds the correspondence address. The College appoints nobody to a chapter office, which is what keeps a chapter the graduates’ and not the institution’s.

What a petition must carry

  • The names and register numbers of at least twelve award-holders who support it.
  • The country or metropolitan area the chapter is to serve, stated precisely enough that two chapters cannot claim the same ground.
  • A named convenor who holds an award and who will hold the correspondence until the first election.
  • The Society’s model constitution, adopted whole, with any local amendment listed separately for the Registrar to consider.
  • An undertaking that membership of the chapter is free to every award-holder it serves.
IV Contribution

Three doors back in, and the limit on each of them.

Three ways back in, all of them optional.

A graduate has something a tutor cannot supply: they have been through the level as a learner, recently, in a second language. The College uses that in three defined places, and stops using it at the point where it would touch an academic decision about somebody else.

Teaching

As a reader and a respondent

A graduate may record a passage for the Listening Lab, sit in on a tutorial as a second voice, or write commentary on a piece of student work under a tutor’s supervision. Marking stays with staff. A graduate’s comment reaches the learner as commentary and never as a mark.

Admissions

As an interviewer

Trained alumni interviewers conduct the applicant conversation and write it up against the published criteria. The decision belongs to Admissions. An interviewer who knows the applicant says so and withdraws before the conversation, and the declaration is kept on the applicant’s file.

Quality

As a witness in the annual review

The annual review takes evidence from graduates on how the award was read outside the College, and on what the level failed to prepare them for. Evidence goes to the Director of Quality in writing and is quoted in the review with the graduate’s consent. See the quality architecture.

The bounds on all three

A contribution is unpaid unless the graduate is engaged as staff under a contract, in which case they are staff and the contract governs. A contribution buys no standing in any academic decision, changes nothing about the contributor’s own award, and is never a condition of anything else on this page. A graduate who declines is asked once and left alone.

V Register & Consent

Two registers, one of them public, and the switch between them.

Two registers, and only one of them is public.

The distinction matters more than any other on this page, because confusing the two is how institutions end up publishing people who never agreed to be published.

Public

The Graduate Register

A roll for anyone wanting to confirm a person rather than a document. It shows the name, the award, the year, the register number and the chapter. Appearing in it is the graduate’s decision, taken after conferral and reversible afterwards.

Internal

The alumni contact record

An address, a country and a set of correspondence preferences, held so that the College can reach the person whose academic record it keeps. It is never published, and consent to the public register has no effect on it either way.

What the College undertakes about a graduate’s name

  • The alumni list is never sold, rented, exchanged or handed to a third party, on any terms.
  • A graduate’s name is never used to endorse a product, an employer, an agent or another institution.
  • A portrait appears only where the graduate submitted one, and only after the Registry has reviewed it.
  • Withdrawal from the public register takes effect within five working days of the request.
  • Consent is set from the graduate’s own record, in one place, and each part of the profile is switched separately.
Verification survives withdrawal

Leaving the public register closes a profile. It does nothing to the award. A code entered at Verification still resolves, because the award is a fact of the Register of Awards while the profile is a publication the graduate controls. The two are deliberately separate, and your academic record sets out which fields sit on which side of the line.

VI Keeping It Current

A name changes, a career moves, and the record follows.

A record a graduate can keep true.

A credential decays when the name on it stops matching the person holding it. The Registry treats a change of name or of country as ordinary business rather than as an exception, and charges for none of it.

Name

A change of name

Send the Registry evidence of the change. A certificate and a transcript are reissued in the new name within 20 working days and at no charge. The Register keeps both entries, marks the first as replaced and dates it, so a check made against an older document still resolves and reports the replacement.

After

What a graduate adds later

A graduate may enter continuing professional development against their record. The profile marks which entries the College has seen evidence for and which are the graduate’s own declaration. The two are shown differently on purpose: a self-declared line rendered as a verified one would be the graduate’s word in the College’s typeface.

The annual confirmation

  • The Registry writes once a year, each September, asking the graduate to confirm the contact record.
  • A confirmed record carries the date it was confirmed, so a stale address is visible as stale.
  • A record that passes two annual notices without an answer is marked dormant, which stops the correspondence and changes nothing else.
  • One email restores a dormant record the same day, and the award, the transcript and the verification code were untouched throughout.
