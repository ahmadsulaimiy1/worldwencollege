IEFC · Level II

Elementary Programme

Elementary is where isolated sentences become connected speech. The learner already has the present and the past; this level teaches them to join, sequence and qualify. That is telling a story rather than listing events, giving an opinion rather than a fact, and keeping a conversation going when the other person says something unexpected.

Assessment

How the level is judged.

Every instrument is written before the teaching it tests, and the criteria are published to the learner in advance.

Continuous

10 module quizzes

One at the end of each module, testing the language that module taught and nothing else. Marked automatically, with the correct answer and the reason shown.

Productive

10 assignments

Speaking and writing tasks marked by an instructor against a published rubric. These are where the productive skills are actually assessed — a quiz cannot judge whether someone can hold a conversation.

Self-check

Before every assessment

Each lesson carries a self-check with traps aimed at the mistakes learners at this level really make, so a learner discovers a gap before an examiner does.

Teaching Methods

How it is taught.

Speaking-first

Practice before polish

Guided speaking is built into every lesson, not offered as an extra. Learners speak from the first lesson, before their grammar is ready, because waiting for accuracy is how people stay silent for years.

Structured

A shared standard, not a personal style

Every instructor teaches to the same mapped curriculum, so a learner who changes class or cohort does not lose their place, and two learners at the same level have covered the same ground.

Supported

A teacher who knows what goes wrong

Instructors work from the Teacher's Companion, which sets out for each lesson what commonly goes wrong, a second way to explain it, and what to do for the learner who is behind and the one who is ahead.

Measured

Adjusted to the cohort

Quiz and assignment results are visible to the instructor as the level runs, so teaching responds to how this group is actually performing rather than to how the plan assumed they would.

A Complete Qualification

Level II is a qualification, not a stage of one.

Six levels form one pathway, and every one of them is also a finished award with its own entry standard, its own exit standard and its own uses. A learner who completes this level and stops has completed something.

Who it is for

The reader this level was written for

A learner who can already handle single sentences and needs connected speech: the person who is understood word by word and wants to be understood in paragraphs.

To enter

What is required to start

The Level&nbsp;I award, or A2 attainment demonstrated through the placement assessment or a recognised external qualification. See the recognition table on Admissions.

To be awarded

What is required to finish

70% overall with no skill below 50%, ten assignments marked, and a spoken paper at A2 &mdash; where the standard becomes sustained speech rather than accurate fragments.

What the award is used for

At work

What a holder can do professionally

Enough English to hold a service, retail, hospitality or administrative role conducted partly in English: taking an instruction, describing a problem, writing a short message that reads as intended.

In study

Where it is accepted academically

Satisfies the entry standard for Level&nbsp;III. The last level at which a learner is usually described as a beginner, and the first at which they are not.

If you stop at Level II

A complete A2 qualification. Most language learning in the world stops somewhere around here without anything to show for it; this stops with an award and a transcript naming every skill separately. The College recommends the full pathway to anyone whose purpose needs it, and recommends stopping to anyone whose purpose does not. A provider that treats every exit as a failure is selling levels rather than teaching English.

Questions

What people ask about this level.

Duration

How long does Level II take?

4 months of study by design, covering 120 designed lessons across 10 modules. Learners who need longer are not penalised; the level is a body of work, not a race.

Entry

Do I need Level I first?

Not necessarily. You need the language Foundation Programme teaches, however you acquired it. A placement assessment establishes that.

Assessment

What happens if I fail an assessment?

Assessments can be resat. The purpose is to establish what you can do, not to record a single bad afternoon — the appeals and resit procedure is published in full.

Commitment

Can I study this alongside a job?

Yes. That is the shape it was built to. Level II is 200 designed hours across 4 months — about 12 hours a week, spent when you choose to spend them. Nothing opens on a fixed date and nothing closes if a week goes badly.
