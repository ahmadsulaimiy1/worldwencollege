Teaching

What a teacher is given.

17 fields against every lesson, and — the part that matters — every entry declaring where its knowledge came from. Most institutions do not separate these. Separating them is the reason this record can be trusted.

Four Kinds of Knowledge

Not all teaching knowledge is the same kind of thing.

Blurring these is how a designed judgement comes to be cited as evidence. Each entry in the record is marked with exactly one.

Derived

359 entries

Read off the programme itself: the prerequisite a lesson names, the minutes its stages declare, the confusion its own self-check was written to trap. Not an opinion — a fact about the curriculum, and checkable against it.

Established

19 entries

Attested in the international teaching of English and not particular to this College — that the third-person -s is among the most persistent errors for learners of every first language, that countability in English is arbitrary and must be memorised. Synthesis, and marked as synthesis.

Designed

152 entries

An authored judgement by the people who wrote the curriculum: how else to explain this, which analogy holds, how to stretch a learner who has finished early. Defensible, and improvable by anyone who teaches it and finds better.

Observed

0 entries

What actually happened in a room with real learners. This is empty, and it is empty because the College has taught nobody. It is not filled with the other three.

Why this record was rebuilt

It began with three states and most of it empty, on the reasoning that a teacher’s knowledge comes from teaching and this College has taught no one. That was right about one kind of knowledge and wrong about three others — and the conflation left the Teacher’s Companion unwritable for a reason that did not actually apply to most of what it would contain.
