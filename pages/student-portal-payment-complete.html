I The undertaking

Four things the College owes you from the moment a charge clears, each of them checkable on your own statement.

A payment buys a place, a receipt, and fourteen days in which to change your mind.

A numbered receipt, issued once

Every payment the College receives is receipted, and a receipt number is issued exactly once against it. It appears here, on your statement of account, and in the ledger the College’s own revenue report reads — three views of one record rather than three records.

The level opens, and the whole of it opens

A cleared payment enrols you at the level it was struck against: ten modules, the Listening Lab, and marking by a person. A payment for the whole programme opens Level I now and adds each level after it as the one before is completed, so a paid-for level is a level you are ready to study.

Fourteen days, running from the charge

Ask within fourteen days of paying, before any assessed work in that level has been opened, and the whole sum is returned. No reason is required and none is asked for. The clock starts when the money moves, not when the enrolment is made.

Two currencies on every figure

The amount that reached your card is shown in the currency it was taken in, beside the same amount in the College’s ledger currency. Neither is re-converted afterwards: a recorded charge is a fact, and quoting it at today’s rate would turn it into an estimate.

If a figure here is not the figure on your card

Quote the reference above and write to the Registry. A query about a charge is answered in writing under the complaints procedure, within the published clock, and the closed list of every fee the College may charge against is at Tuition & Fees. If a cost is not on that list, it does not exist.
