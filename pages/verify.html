        This page confirms what the College’s Graduate Register records. It states the award, the holder as named on the certificate, and the award’s current standing — and nothing further. Marks, progress and contact details are not published here.

        I The register

        What it holds, and what a check can be relied on to tell you.

        Four properties, and a check depends on all four.

        Every award, not only the highest

        The Register records every award of the International English Fluency Course, from English Aspirant to English Laureate. Each level is a complete academic achievement of the College and is verifiable in its own right.

        Withdrawn awards stay visible

        An award that has been withdrawn or replaced is never removed from the Register. Its page says so, with the date. A verification that simply failed would tell you nothing.

        Tamper-evident records

        Each entry carries a cryptographic digest computed over its own contents and the entry before it. Altering a record after conferral breaks that sequence and is detectable. This is a hash chain in the College’s register; it is not a blockchain, and the College does not describe it as one.

        Verification exists before the first award

        This register was built and opened ahead of conferral, so that the first person to hold a WEC award can have it checked on the day it is issued rather than months afterwards. An employer who arrives here with a code gets an answer from the record, not from a mailbox.

        If a check does not resolve

        Read the code again from the certificate — capitals, dashes and spacing do not matter, and the register is tolerant of all three. If it still does not resolve, write to the College with the code and the name on the certificate. A code that names no award is answered as such, plainly, rather than by a blank page.
