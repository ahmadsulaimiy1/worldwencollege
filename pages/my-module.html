    Live hours for this level

      A module opens only for a level you are enrolled on. Everything you submit here is marked against the rubric published before the task, and every mark on it is second-marked.

      I The allowance

      Four rules about sitting an assessment again, each of them published before you sit it the first time.

      You should not have to spend an attempt to find out what an attempt costs.

      Three sittings, and the third is not a cliff

      A first sitting and two resits. Where all three are spent and the assessment still stands unpassed, the level is repeated and the assessment is set afresh — the regulations say so rather than leaving a learner at a wall with no door in it.

      A fortnight between sittings, and the reason for it

      Fourteen days is not an administrative delay. It is the interval in which the thing that went wrong the first time can actually be worked on, and the page names the day the next sitting opens rather than refusing one and leaving you to guess.

      A resit is capped, and the cap is published

      A mark earned at a resit counts to seventy. That is a decision about comparability between a first sitting and a fourth month of preparation, and it is printed on the paper rather than applied quietly afterwards.

      A resit carries no fee

      Nothing is charged for sitting again, and nothing is charged for repeating a level. A College that billed for a second attempt would be selling the outcome rather than teaching towards it.

      A number already spent is never reissued

      Each sitting takes the next number and keeps it. A sitting struck out on appeal gives the attempt back and does not give the number back, so the record of what was sat, and in what order, stays legible however the marks on it change afterwards.
