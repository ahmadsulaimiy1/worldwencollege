Governance

The quality architecture, with the state of every office in it.

Twelve standing bodies and three frameworks. Each one carries what it decides, who it answers to, and whether it has ever met — because a committee nobody can tell the state of is a name on a chart.

Adopted 18 August 2026

I The Commission

A permanent body, because the work does not stop between meetings.

Institutional Quality Commission

A permanent commission rather than a standing committee, because the work it owns does not stop between meetings. It holds academic quality, institutional effectiveness, educational innovation, policy review, curriculum monitoring, learner outcomes and strategic benchmarking, and it is the body every subcommittee below reports through.

IQC

Institutional Quality Commission

A permanent commission rather than a standing committee, because the work it owns does not stop between meetings. It holds academic quality, institutional effectiveness, educational innovation, policy review, curriculum monitoring, learner outcomes and strategic benchmarking, and it is the body every subcommittee below reports through.

Reports to: Board of Governors

Established · no members appointed
Why it is set up this way

A committee is convened for a question and dissolved when the question is answered. Quality is not a question; it is a condition that decays the moment nobody is responsible for it. The Commission is permanent so that responsibility has somewhere to sit in the months when nothing is being reviewed.

II Subcommittees

Seven, each owning one question and reporting through the Commission.

Subcommittees

Seven, each owning one question and reporting through the Commission.

IQC-CURRICULUM

Curriculum Standards Committee

Holds the curriculum against its own published structure: that every module states its prerequisites, that every lesson carries an outcome, and that the sequence of a level is an argument rather than an ordering.

Reports to: Institutional Quality Commission

Established · no members appointed
IQC-ASSESSMENT

Assessment Integrity Committee

Owns the distance between a rubric and a mark. It reviews marking consistency, second-marking, the operation of the skill floors, and every case where a published criterion and an awarded grade could be read as disagreeing.

Reports to: Institutional Quality Commission

Established · no members appointed
IQC-EXPERIENCE

Student Experience Committee

Reads the programme from the learner's side: what a student actually meets, in what order, with what support, and where the programme as published and the programme as met come apart.

Reports to: Institutional Quality Commission

Established · no members appointed
IQC-DIGITAL

Digital Learning Committee

Owns the platform as an academic instrument rather than as software: whether the staging of a lesson teaches, whether the Listening Lab measures what it claims, and whether a learner's record is legible to the learner.

Reports to: Institutional Quality Commission

Established · no members appointed
IQC-TEACHING

Teaching Excellence Committee

Runs the observation cycle and the development scheme that follows from it, and holds the Teaching Excellence Framework's measures against what teaching actually produced.

Reports to: Institutional Quality Commission

Established · no members appointed
IQC-PUBLICATIONS

Academic Publications Committee

The review gate in front of WEC Press. No volume is described as reviewed until a qualified reader who did not write it has read it and said so on the record.

Reports to: Institutional Quality Commission

Established · no members appointed
IQC-INNOVATION

Strategic Innovation Committee

Decides which proposed change to the programme is worth the disruption it costs, and records the ones it declines with the reason — so a rejected idea is a decision rather than a silence.

Reports to: Institutional Quality Commission

Established · no members appointed

Councils and Offices

Advises on global education trends, curriculum development, employability, technology, international partnerships, research and educational innovation. It advises; it does not approve. Nothing it says becomes policy without a decision of the Senate or the Commission, and the record names which.

Established · no members appointed

Owns AI integration, immersive learning, virtual classrooms, digital laboratories, learning analytics, adaptive assessment and future pedagogy — and owns the obligation to say which of them the College has actually built and which it has only considered.

Established · no members appointed

Coordinates academic advising, learning support, wellbeing, mentoring, careers guidance and alumni support. An office rather than a committee, because a student in difficulty needs a door, not a quorum.

An institutional innovation centre for AI-assisted learning, multilingual education, digital assessment, educational technology, immersive learning, virtual reality and next-generation language pedagogy. Its output is evidence about teaching, not features.

IV External Examiner

The one office that cannot be filled from inside.

Independent External Examiner

Provides independent oversight of academic standards, moderation procedures, assessment quality and award integrity.

What the External Examiner independently reviews

  • Assessment frameworks
  • Examination standards
  • Moderation processes
  • Award classifications
  • Curriculum alignment
  • Academic regulations
  • Student achievement evidence

Reporting. Reports annually to the Board of Governors and to the Academic Senate. Recommendations are published through the College's quality assurance cycle rather than held internally, because a recommendation nobody outside the College can read is not oversight.

State

The office is vacant. No External Examiner has been appointed, so every award conferred to date was set, marked and moderated inside the College and by nobody outside it.

Why it is set up this way

This is the one office in the whole instrument that cannot be filled from inside. Every other body here is the College checking its own work; this one is the only thing that can establish that a level sits where the College says it sits.

V The Annual Cycle

Seven reviews a year, each producing the same four things.

Academic Quality Review Framework

The College operates a continuous quality assurance model. Every academic year runs the seven reviews below, and each one produces the same four things, in the same order, on the record.

The seven reviewsRemit
Curriculum ReviewWhether the programme still teaches what it says it teaches, in the order it says.
Assessment ReviewWhether the assessments still measure the outcomes they were written to test.
Student Experience ReviewWhat a learner actually met, against what the pages promised they would meet.
Graduate Outcome ReviewWhat holders of the award went on to do — reported when there is something to report.
Academic Standards ReviewWhether the standard applied in marking is the standard published in the rubric.
External ModerationThe one station that requires somebody the College cannot appoint to itself.
Governance ReviewWhether the bodies on this page did what this page says they do.

Every review produces four things, in this order

  1. 1Findings
  2. 2Recommendations
  3. 3Implementation plan
  4. 4Follow-up review

A review that produces findings and stops is an opinion. The fourth output is what makes the first three binding: the follow-up asks whether the implementation plan was implemented, and its answer is minuted whether or not it flatters anyone.

State

The framework is adopted. No annual cycle has completed a turn against it, so it has produced no findings, no recommendations and no implementation plan yet.

VI Observation

How teaching is reviewed, and what an observation must produce.

Teaching Observation Framework

Teaching quality is reviewed in structured observation cycles rather than by impression. An observation covers the eight areas below and produces the five outputs beside them.

8

What an observation covers

  • 1Lesson planning
  • 2Classroom management
  • 3Language accuracy
  • 4Student engagement
  • 5Assessment practice
  • 6Inclusive teaching
  • 7Digital teaching
  • 8Professional conduct
5

What every observation produces

  • 1Strengths
  • 2Recommendations
  • 3Action points
  • 4Mentoring schedule
  • 5Follow-up observation
State

The framework is adopted. No lesson has been observed under it, so the observed column of the teaching support record stands empty and is marked empty.

Why it is set up this way

The support record behind the Teacher's Companion has four kinds of knowledge and the fourth — what actually happened in a room with real learners — is empty. This framework is the only instrument that can fill it. Until an observation is written up under it, the column stays empty and the site says so.

VII Teaching Excellence

Measured, or marked as not yet measured. Never estimated.

Teaching Excellence Framework

Every module is evaluated against the measures below. Each is either measured or it is marked as not yet measured; none is estimated.

The measuresRemit
Learner achievementMarks against the published rubric, per outcome.
Learner satisfactionCollected from students, reported unedited.
Assessment reliabilityAgreement between first and second marker.
Completion ratePublished when a cohort is large enough for the figure to mean something.
EngagementTime on task and return rate, from the platform record.
Instructional qualityFrom the observation cycle, once observations exist.
Continuous improvementWhat the last review changed, and whether it worked.
