III The Argument

Why recording is not optional.

Three reasons, in the order they matter.

One

You cannot hear yourself while speaking

Every learner is a poor judge of their own pronunciation in the moment, because producing a sound and evaluating it compete for the same attention. Recorded, the same sentence is suddenly assessable — by you, before anyone else hears it.

Two

Improvement is invisible without a baseline

Progress in pronunciation is slow and continuous, which makes it exactly the kind of change nobody notices in themselves. A recording from month one played against month six settles the question in thirty seconds.

Three

Speaking cannot be assessed any other way

A programme that marks speaking from written work is not marking speaking. Recording is not an enhancement to the assessment; it is the assessment.

IV How It Works

What you actually do.

  1. 01Listen to the set. Each listening set carries its own audio, with the specific features the lesson is targeting marked in it — a linking sound, a stressed syllable, a contraction — rather than a general instruction to listen carefully.
  2. 02Record yourself against a target. Pronunciation targets are specific and named. “Sound more natural” is not a target; the vowel in a particular word is.
  3. 03Listen back before submitting. This step is where most of the learning happens, and it is the step learners skip. It is built into the flow deliberately.
  4. 04Receive feedback from a person. Pronunciation feedback is written by a teacher against the target, not generated by a score. No automated pronunciation scoring is used, and none is claimed.
  5. 05Keep the recording. It stays in your record. In six months it is evidence; deleted, it is nothing.
V Practicalities

What it needs and what it survives.

Any microphone

A phone is enough

Studio quality is not required and would not improve the assessment. The features being marked survive an ordinary microphone.

An unreliable connection

Recording continues offline

Recording does not require a live connection. A recording made while offline is held and uploaded when the connection returns, in parts, so that a drop does not lose the whole file. This was built because the College expects students in places where connections drop.

Your own ears first

Nothing is submitted automatically

A recording is submitted when you decide it is. Nothing is captured or sent in the background.

V The Residency in Britain

The residency, taken in the country the language belongs to — thirty to sixty days.

Every award-holder may take the residency in Britain.

The residency is the assessed component that finishes a level, and it may be completed in your own city under College supervision or in the United Kingdom. This is the British route: thirty to sixty days of using English for everything, which is a different order of practice from any classroom. The standard is identical on both routes — where you practise changes the logistics, never the mark.

30–60

An assessed component

Not a tour: a timetabled programme of tutorials, seminars, assessed presentations and supervised placement in a working English environment. It is marked against the same rubric as the supervised route at home, and entered on your academic record as the residency for that level.

Who

Every holder of an award

Open to every student who holds an IEFC award at any level, in any cohort, with tuition for that level settled. The British route is offered, never required: a student who completes the residency at home under supervision holds exactly the same award, marked to exactly the same standard.

Funded

Places awarded on merit

A number of British residencies each year are funded by the College in full — programme, accommodation and travel. They are awarded on a cumulative grade point average of 3.70 or above and on the same published criteria the Foundation Remission uses, by the same panel, with the score against each criterion minuted.

How a British residency is arranged

  1. 1You declare a preference at registration. The application form asks where you would complete the residency, with the British locations named. Choosing one commits you to nothing; it tells the Registrar to prepare your documentation early, which is the single largest saving of time later.
  2. 2You are invited when an award is conferred. The invitation names the dates of the next two British residencies and the closing date for each.
  3. 3The College issues your documentation. A letter of enrolment, a letter of invitation naming the academic programme and its dates, confirmation of your award, and evidence of any funding awarded to you.
  4. 4You apply for a UK Standard Visitor visa yourself. The visa is granted or refused by UK Visas and Immigration on their criteria. The College supplies documentation and answers any question the caseworker puts to it; it does not sponsor, guarantee or influence a decision, and no institution can.
  5. 5Your place is held until the decision. If entry permission is refused, any fee you have paid for the British residency is refunded in full, your place carries to the next one without a second application, and you may instead complete the residency at home under supervision for the same award.
What the College does not do, said plainly

It does not issue visas, sponsor them, or promise that one will be granted — that decision belongs entirely to UK Visas and Immigration. It does not offer the residency as a route to work or settlement in the United Kingdom, and it will not support an application presented as one. The residency is an assessed academic component of an English programme, and the College keeps it exactly that.

VII Stuck On The Material

Built into the teaching before you have to ask.

In the lesson

A second explanation, already written

Every lesson carries an alternative explanation of its hardest point, written in advance for the learner who did not follow the first one. Teaching that has only one route through it fails everyone who needs a different one.

In the lesson

The mistakes people actually make

Each lesson names the common errors for that point and what causes them, so that a mistake is recognisable rather than mysterious. These are written from the language itself rather than gathered from student data, and the College says which, because “the mistakes our students make” would be a stronger claim than it can evidence.

On demand

A tutorial with a teacher

Where the written support does not resolve it, a tutorial does. Ask; it is not rationed.

VIII Falling Behind

Noticed early, and answered with help.

Why it is measured

To reach you, never to penalise you

Engagement is tracked so that a learner who has gone quiet is contacted in month two rather than discovered in month eleven. It never produces a penalty, and there is no attendance requirement to fail — the programme is asynchronous, so attendance would be the wrong measure of anything.

What happens

A message, an offer, a conversation

Falling behind produces contact, not consequence. Someone who is behind can still finish; the entire value of noticing is reaching them while that is true.

IX Unhappy With Something

Who answers, and how honestly.

Who

The founding team, by name

Every message to the College is answered by a member of the founding team. There is no ticket queue and no first-line script, which is a genuine advantage of the College’s size and will not survive growth — so it is described as it is now.

What is missing

A formal complaints procedure

There is no independent stage to escalate to, because there is no appointed body to escalate to. That is stated on Academic regulations as well, because it is the kind of gap that should not be findable in only one place.

X What The College Does Not Provide

Named, so that nobody relies on it.

No

Counselling or wellbeing services

WEC-LC has no counselling provision, no wellbeing service and no qualified staff for either. If you need that kind of support, it must come from services where you live. Implying otherwise on a website is how people in difficulty get let down.

No

Careers or immigration advice

No careers service, and no immigration advice of any kind — see Visas and study permits.

No

Disability assessment or formal adjustments

The College has no process for assessing a need or granting a formal adjustment. What it can do is arrange practical accommodations informally — audio-only participation, extended time, alternative formats — on request. That is a smaller offer than a policy, and it is described as the smaller thing it is.
