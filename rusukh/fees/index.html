The four tiers

What you pay for is how you are taught.

Not how much content you unlock. Every tier gives access to the same syllabus; what changes is the size of the room and how often a teacher hears you.

Why the two bands differ

Not because the teaching differs — it is the same syllabus, the same examination and often the same teacher. The international band is higher because it carries the sponsored seats in Nigeria. A diaspora student on the Standard tier funds roughly two-thirds of a Nigerian student’s year at Foundation. We would rather say that plainly than dress a cross-subsidy up as a premium product.

Flexibility

Life interrupts study. The fee structure assumes it will.

These are not concessions granted on appeal. They are standing rights of every enrolled student, and the Registry applies them without asking why.

Pause, don’t drop

Suspend enrolment for up to two consecutive terms and return to the same rung, with the same teacher where possible. No penalty, no re-application, no re-payment of the application fee. Ramaḍān, childbirth, university examinations, bereavement, a bad month for cash — the Registry does not require a reason.

Switch tier at any month

Move up or down between Foundation, Standard and Private at any monthly boundary. Fees are pro-rated to the day. A student who takes a private tier through a hard passage and drops back afterwards has done exactly what the structure is for.

Pay per juz’

On the modular Ḥifẓ track you pay for one juz’ at a time and stop whenever you choose, with your progress recorded against your name. It is the lowest-commitment door into the College and it leads to exactly the same award.

Family and teacher rates

25% off for every household member after the first, on every tier. 40% off for serving imams and madrasah teachers on any programme — they carry what we teach them to more people than we will ever reach directly, so the discount is an investment rather than a kindness.

On zakāh

Zakāh given to this College is held in a segregated fund. It is not mixed with tuition income, it is not spent on salaries, buildings or administration, and it is disbursed only as fees for students who fall within the categories of eligible recipients. It is accounted separately and reported separately. This is a religious obligation on us before it is an administrative preference, and a College that solicits zakāh without honouring it has committed something worse than an accounting error.
