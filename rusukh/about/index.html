The College

Dār al-Rusūkh

A distance-learning college of the Qur’ānic and Islamic sciences, admitting students in Nigeria and internationally to structured programmes of study leading to the Certificate, the Diploma, the Higher Diploma and ijāzah.

The name

وَالرَّاسِخُونَ فِي الْعِلْمِ

Rusūkh is the verbal noun of rasakha, to be firmly rooted or deeply established. The College takes its name from the Qur’ānic description of those firmly rooted in knowledge (Āl ‘Imrān 3:7).

The College is styled Dār al-Rusūkh (دار الرسوخ); its full title is the Dār al-Rusūkh College of Qur’ānic and Islamic Sciences, abbreviated in documents to DAR.

Method of instruction

Instruction at a distance.

The College maintains no campus. Instruction is delivered by scheduled live class throughout the session, in the manner of the affiliated and external programmes long established at Al-Azhar and at the University of London.

Students are admitted to a named programme and placed at a level determined by assessment. Classes are capped at twelve, and every student is assigned a member of faculty responsible for their supervision and their record. Sessions are recorded and made available to enrolled students for revision.

Programmes are taught to prescribed texts against published syllabi, which are issued to students on matriculation and are available to applicants from the Registry. Assessment is by written paper and oral examination, and awards are conferred by the Academic Council on the recommendation of the examining board.

Instruction is given in Arabic and in English. Classes are conducted with regard to the customary separation of men and women, and women students are taught by women members of faculty where the programme requires individual supervision.

Delivery

Teaching is conducted audio-first, with video at the student’s election, so that classes remain accessible where bandwidth is limited. Class times are published in West Africa Time with the offset from Coordinated Universal Time stated. The academic year comprises three semesters kept against the Hijrī calendar, with the general timetable suspended in Ramaḍān.

Foundation

Origin of the College.

The College originates in a programme of instruction in the Qur’ān and the Arabic language established on 22 February 2020, which admitted more than 1,550 students from 22 countries, among them students resident in Nigeria, the United Kingdom, the United States, Qatar, the United Arab Emirates and Saudi Arabia.

Teaching was suspended in 2022 and 2023. The College was constituted in its present form in 1448 / 2026, with a faculty structure, an Academic Council and a published scheme of awards.

The Founder

Ahmad Sulaimiy read the Qur’ānic sciences at Taif University, having begun his studies in Nigeria, and studied further with scholars in Madinah and in Egypt. He holds ijāzah in the ten canonical readings and in the six principal collections of ḥadīth, including Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim.

He is the author of Grammar Planet, علمني ربي, كيف عاش الرسول؟ and Islamic Law and Contemporary View.
