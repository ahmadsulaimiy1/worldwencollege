The College

Dār al-Rusūkh — the house of firm rooting.

A distance-learning college of Qur’ānic and Islamic sciences. Founded 1441 / 2020, suspended through 2022 and 2023, and re-established in 1448 / 2026 on a structure built to outlast any one teacher — including the one who founded it.

The name

وَالرَّاسِخُونَ فِي الْعِلْمِ

Rusūkh is the verbal noun of rasakha — to be firmly rooted, deeply established, immovable. Its most famous use in the language is Qur’ānic: “…and those firmly rooted in knowledge” (Āl ‘Imrān 3:7).

That is precisely what a rigorous college claims to produce, and it is what this one is named for. Not light, not guidance, not wisdom — rootedness. The condition of a person who has read the books in order, been examined on them aloud, and cannot be shaken loose from what they know.

The College is Dār al-Rusūkh; a graduate of it is a Rusūkhī; and its documents abbreviate to DAR.

Why distance

We have no campus. That is the method, not a shortage.

What a campus gives a student is three things: a teacher, a text and an examination. All three travel. What a campus adds beyond them is rent, and rent is paid by the student.

Al-Azhar has examined intisāban — by affiliation, at a distance — for the better part of a century. The University of London has examined externally since 1858, and produced Nelson Mandela and five Nobel laureates doing it. Distance is not the compromise position in education; it is one of its oldest and most tested forms.

What distance does demand is that everything a campus conveys by atmosphere must instead be written down and published: the syllabus, the standard, the fee, the chain, the name of the teacher. A student who cannot walk through the gate and look must be able to read everything instead.

So this College publishes rather than promises. Every syllabus, every fee, every examination standard and every chain its teachers hold is on this site before you pay anything — and where something is not yet open, it says when it opens rather than implying it is available now.

Built for the constraint we actually have

This is a Nigerian institution and bandwidth is a real cost, not a footnote. Teaching is therefore audio-first with video optional, sessions are recorded for review, and WhatsApp is an official channel of the Registry rather than an afterthought — because that is how the people we serve actually communicate. Class times are published in West Africa Time with an explicit UTC offset, computed for your own clock.

The Founder

Ahmad Sulaimiy.

The Founder’s study began in Nigeria and continued in Saudi Arabia, where he read Qur’ānic sciences at Taif University, and with scholars in Madinah and Egypt. He is proficient in the ten recognised qirā’āt and holds ijāzah in the six major books of the Sunnah, including Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim.

He is the author of Grammar Planet, علمني ربي, كيف عاش الرسول؟ and Islamic Law and Contemporary View.

The programme that became this College was his, begun on 22 February 2020 during the pandemic, and it taught more than 1,550 students across 22 countries — among them students in the USA, the United Kingdom, Qatar, the UAE and Saudi Arabia — before he suspended it through 2022 and 2023.

From the Founder’s address

“Many individuals have been historically disadvantaged by a lack of formal recognition for their knowledge and skills. This programme was designed to address that — so that students who missed out on formal education may finally receive the recognition they deserve.”

“Because of increasingly busy schedules, we were unable to continue the programme in 2022 and 2023. Despite this, I have now committed to re-establishing the initiative.”

That suspension is published here rather than tidied away. It is the single most important fact about how this College has been rebuilt: around teachers it can form, and chains held by more than one person, so that the institution is not one man’s health away from dormancy again. I’dād al-Mu’allim — the formation of teachers — is the answer to it, and it is why that programme matters more than any other in the College.

Why the Foundation carries the family name and the College does not

The predecessor institution carried the Founder’s own name — in eight inconsistent spellings across its own website, which is its own small lesson. Here the school is named for what it produces and the foundation carries the family name. That separation is what allows the College to outlive the man who founded it, which is the whole purpose of building an institution rather than running a programme.
