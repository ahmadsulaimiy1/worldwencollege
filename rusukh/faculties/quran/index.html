Kulliyyat al-Qur’ān — كلية القرآن

From the articulation of one letter to a chain that reaches the Prophet ﷺ.

Eleven rungs. You are placed on them by reciting for five minutes to a teacher — never by telling us where you think you are, and never by paying for a level you have not reached.

Foundation · Q0–Q2

Before memorisation, the reading must be clean.

The most common failure in Qur’ān study is memorising on top of a faulty reading. Errors set into the memory at this stage take years to lift out, so this College will not let a student begin Ḥifẓ until Q2 is passed.

Q0

Al-Qā’idah al-Nūrāniyyah

Letters, their points of articulation (makhārij), their qualities (ṣifāt), and joining. Assumes no Arabic whatsoever. Suitable for children and for adults beginning at fifty.

10 weeks · Open now

Q1

Tilāwah wa Taṣḥīḥ

Reading the muṣḥaf aloud under continuous correction until the reading is clean. Every session is recitation; the teacher listens more than they speak.

12 weeks · Open now

Q2

Tajwīd, naẓarī wa ‘amalī

Tuḥfat al-Aṭfāl memorised in full, then al-Muqaddimah al-Jazariyyah memorised in full — each applied in recitation as it is learned, never studied as theory alone.

2 terms · Open now

Memorisation · Q3–Q4

Three tracks, because three kinds of life ask for three schedules.

Entry to any track requires Q2. Entry to the intensive track requires an additional assessment, and the College refuses it where the schedule is not realistic — a student set up to fail is worse served than one told the truth.

On murāja‘ah

Murāja’ah begins in week one, not at the end. The commonest way a memorisation is lost is that revision is treated as a phase that follows memorisation rather than a discipline that runs beside it from the first page. Every track on this faculty carries a revision schedule from its first week, and the schedule is part of the syllabus rather than advice.

The chain · Q5–Q6

Ijāzah, and the ten qirā’āt.

The most valuable thing this College confers, and the most tightly governed. A full statement of policy is published on the Awards page; what follows is the study route to it.

Q5 — Ijāzah in Ḥafṣ ‘an ‘Āṣim

The complete Qur’ān recited to a chain-holder, live, from memory. The document names every teacher in the chain, in order. Entry requires a complete memorisation and a reading assessed as clean.

6–18 months
Open now

Q6a — Al-Qirā’āt al-‘Ashr al-Ṣughrā

The ten readings by the shorter route: al-Shāṭibiyyah for the seven, al-Durrah for the remaining three. Both memorised.

18–24 months
Year 2

Q6b — Al-Qirā’āt al-‘Ashr al-Kubrā

The ten readings by the fuller route, via Ṭayyibat al-Nashr. The summit of this faculty, and of the discipline.

24–36 months
Year 2

The qirā’āt rungs are marked Year 2 because they require a second chain-holding teacher, so that no student is ever examined for a chain by the only person who could confer it. The Founder holds the ten qirā’āt; appointing the second holder is a stated condition of opening Q6, and it is published here rather than discovered later.

Ṣawtiyyāt · QS

The voice itself.

A separate eight-week course, taken alongside any rung from Q2 upward. It treats the reciting voice as a discipline with its own technique — breath, register, placement, and the maqāmāt that carry classical recitation.

Two schools are taught side by side: the Makkan and the Madinan styles, with the differences named rather than blurred. The course concludes with Adhān to professional standard, which is the most publicly consequential thing most graduates of this faculty will ever do with their voices.
