Awards

What this house confers, and on what authority.

Two kinds of standing: a ladder of examined certificates for people whose knowledge has never been recognised, and ijāzah with a named, unbroken chain. Both are conferred on the College’s own authority, and both are publicly verifiable.

The ladder

Four awards, examined in writing and aloud.

The founding mission of this College, stated plainly: there are people who studied for years in a traditional madrasah, who hold real learning, and who cannot produce one piece of paper any institution will accept. This ladder is for them.

Recognition of prior learning

A candidate who studied elsewhere without gaining certification may enter the ladder at I’dādiyyah or Thānawiyyah directly, by sitting the full examination for that level and submitting a verifiable account of where and with whom they studied. They do not begin again at the bottom. That door is the reason this College exists, and it stays open.

Examination standards

What makes an award un-buyable.

70%, on both papers

The pass mark is seventy per cent in the written examination and seventy per cent in the oral. A strong performance in one does not carry a weak one in the other.

The oral is compulsory at every level

Including Ibtidā’iyyah. It is the single mechanism that cannot be bought, delegated, borrowed or generated — a candidate either can explain what they have learned aloud, under questioning, or they cannot.

Identity verified before the final paper

Every candidate presents a government-issued document — national ID, passport or driving licence — so that the person examined is demonstrably the person who studied. Age is verified at registration.

Every award issues with a transcript

Not a certificate alone. A receiving institution can see what was studied, what was examined and what was scored, which is the only thing that makes a credential useful to anyone.

Overseen by the Majlis al-‘Ilmī

The Academic Council owns the syllabi, sets the papers, and holds the Register of Chains. No single teacher both teaches and finally examines the same candidate for an award.

The delivery mode is stated on the certificate

Our awards say that they were earned by distance study, because they were. See below — this reverses our predecessor’s policy deliberately.

A policy we reversed, and why

The institution that preceded this College published a rule that its certificates “will not specify that the course was completed online.” We have reversed it. Concealing the delivery mode is a misrepresentation that would destroy the credential the first time a receiving institution noticed — and it would do so at the expense of the student holding it, not the College that issued it. We state it, and we make the examination rigorous enough that stating it costs nothing.

Ijāzah policy

The most valuable thing we issue, and the most tightly governed.

An ijāzah bi-sanad muttaṣil is a licence to transmit, carrying an unbroken chain of teachers back to the Prophet ﷺ. It needs no state’s permission to be prestigious. It has been the credential of this tradition for fourteen centuries.

Only a chain-holder may confer one

An ijāzah is issued only by a teacher who himself holds a connected chain in precisely what he is conferring. Not a colleague’s chain, not the College’s in the abstract — his own.

Absolute

The document names its full sanad

Every name in the chain, in order, on the face of the certificate. A chain that is claimed but not written is not a chain.

On the document

Conferred only after complete recitation, live

The whole Qur’ān, recited to the teacher, in person or by live video. Never on the basis of recordings — a recording proves that a recitation happened, not that this candidate performed it.

No exceptions

Publicly verifiable by reference number

Anyone — a mosque committee, an employer, a university — can check an ijāzah issued by this College against its register, free, without asking us.

Free, instant

The Register of Chains is published

Which chains this College holds, through whom they came, and which teachers may confer them. Published, not furnished on request.

Open
Why the register is public

Because it converts our central claim from an assertion into a document a sceptic can check. Any institution can say its teachers hold chains. Very few will write down which ones, through whom, and invite you to verify it. That is the difference between a claim and a credential, and it is the whole argument of this College in one page.

The Register opens with the chains held by the Founder — the ten qirā’āt, and ijāzāt in the six major books of the Sunnah — and grows as chain-holding teachers are appointed. It names no chain the College cannot document.

Conduct

The condition every student undertakes.

At enrolment every student signs one undertaking: honesty in examination, truthfulness in the documents they submit, and respect in the ḥalaqah toward the teacher and toward every other student in it. The same undertaking binds every teacher. Breach of it is grounds for withdrawal of a place and, where an award was obtained by dishonesty, for revocation of the award.

A second policy we rewrote

Our predecessor barred candidates from certification on grounds of creed, and required those it judged to have deviated to obtain an attestation letter from a recognised scholar certifying that they had reformed before it would reconsider them. We have replaced that entirely. It was unenforceable at a distance, impossible to administer fairly or consistently, and it made a registry clerk into an examiner of other people’s hearts — which is not an office this College wishes to hold.

What replaces it is the academic integrity and conduct undertaking above: the standard every serious institution uses, testable against conduct rather than conscience. Our teaching remains grounded in the way of Ahl al-Sunnah wa’l-Jamā‘ah. That is a statement about what is taught in this house — which every prospective student is entitled to know before they enrol — and not a test administered at its door.
