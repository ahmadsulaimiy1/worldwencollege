The Register of Chains

Which chains this house holds, and through whom.

Published rather than furnished on request. Any institution can say its teachers hold ijāzāt; very few will write down which ones, name the transmitters, and invite you to check. That difference is the argument of this College in a single page.

The register

As it stands today.

The register opens with the chains held by the Founder and grows as chain-holding teachers are appointed. It names no chain the College cannot document.

Why Q6 is not open although the Founder holds the ten

Because no student should be examined for a chain by the only person in the institution who could confer it. The Founder holds the ten qirā’āt; that makes him able to teach Q6, and it does not by itself make the College able to examine it safely. A second holder is required before that rung opens, and the condition is written here so that it can be held against us rather than quietly dropped.

This is also why the register lists a vacancy. A register that shows only what an institution has, and never what it lacks, is a prospectus.

What is written on an ijāzah from this house

Every name in the chain, in order, from the holder back to the Prophet ﷺ. The date and place of the final recitation. The riwāyah conferred. The reference number by which anyone may verify it. A chain that is claimed but not written is not a chain, and a document that omits its sanad is not an ijāzah — whatever it says across the top.
