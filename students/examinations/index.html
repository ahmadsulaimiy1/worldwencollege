Students · Examinations

How an assessment is conducted.

An examination regulation is read once, usually by somebody already having a bad week. This one is written for the worst day of your year.

This instrument governs the conduct of an assessment: who is entered, how the College establishes who is sitting, what a dropped connection or a late day costs, who decides a claim of mitigating circumstances, and how a result is released and challenged. What is assessed, and against which criteria, is published at How you are assessed. Adopted by the Executive on 20 August 2026.

An extension
10 working days, on request
A mitigating claim
20 working days to make it
A level result
Released on working day 15
Resits
Two, and neither is charged

“A deadline with no remedy behind it is a trap.”

I Entry

Who is entered, when the window opens, and what ends an attempt.

You are entered by finishing the teaching.

There is no entry form, no examination fee and no closing date. An assessment opens when the teaching it tests is complete.

Opened by completion

A module first, then the level

A module assessment opens when its lessons are marked complete. A level examination opens when all ten modules are complete and a member of academic staff confirms it.

Ten working days

A window, not an hour

A level examination window stays open for ten working days and you choose your hour inside it. A single fixed hour is the middle of somebody’s night.

Three things, and only three

What ends an attempt

Work that is not yours, another person sitting in your place, and a breach of the conditions printed on the paper. Each is referred under academic integrity.

What does not end an attempt

Lateness does not: it is capped on the scale below and the work is still marked. A dropped connection does not: the attempt is set aside in your favour. A disputed mark does not: it stands while it is checked.

II Identity

The hardest question an online college answers, answered in four mechanisms.

Who is sitting is established by the work.

Nothing is installed on your machine and no software watches your screen. The price of that decision is paid below, in four mechanisms that each require a person rather than a licence.

One identity

The name on the document you enrolled with

Every assessment is sat under the identity the College holds from admission, and that is the name that goes on the award.

A voice on file

Recorded speech from your first module

Speaking is assessed by recording you speaking, so the College holds your voice from the first level. A recording that does not match it is referred to a person rather than marked.

Fifteen minutes

A live defence of your own submission

Every level examination carries a spoken component of at least fifteen minutes in which an examiner questions you on your own submission. Video where your connection carries it, voice where it does not.

Read aloud, at the start

A reference issued for that sitting

Each attempt is issued a reference you speak before a recorded task begins. It ties that recording to that attempt on that date.

What this establishes, and what it does not

It establishes that the voice submitted is the voice enrolled, and that whoever submitted the work can be questioned on it by an examiner who read it. It does not put an invigilator in your room. A person who can defend the work did the work.

III Conduct

Open book by default, closed where it must be, and a clock that starts when you do.

Every paper states its own conditions first.

The conditions are published with the task. A condition announced afterwards is a rule invented to explain a mark.

  • The time limit, and the moment it starts running
  • What you may consult, named rather than left to inference
  • The word count or recording length, and the tolerance on it
  • The rubric it is marked against — see the published criteria
The default

Open book, and deliberately so

Every written assessment may be taken with a dictionary, your notes and the course materials beside you. A competent user of English looks words up.

The exception

The spoken components are closed

A live defence and a recorded speaking task are done without a script, without a prompt read from a screen and without another person in the room.

Three hours

The clock starts when you open the paper

A level examination runs for three hours from the moment you open it, rather than from an hour the College picked for a continent it is not on.

IV Interruption

An attempt cut short, and which side of the connection bears it.

A failed connection is the College’s problem.

An assessment sat at home on an ordinary domestic line will sometimes be interrupted. The remedy is the same whether the fault was the platform’s or the power in your street.

1

Sixty minutes, and the paper resumes

Your work is written to the server as you produce it. A paper reopened within sixty minutes of a break resumes with the time that was left on it.

Resume
2

Beyond that, the attempt is set aside

A break longer than an hour sets the attempt aside at your election. It leaves no mark on the record, it is struck from the count of resits, and a fresh window opens in three working days.

Set aside
3

Twice a level, on your word alone

Two attempts a level are set aside this way without evidence. A third in the same level goes to the mitigating circumstances panel.

Twice
Who bears it, and why the College chose to

Nobody can produce a document proving a power cut. A rule demanding one would ask a learner to evidence their own electricity supply, and would favour whoever lives where the lights stay on.

V Deadlines

What a late day costs, and the extension granted for the asking.

Late work is marked. It is capped, never refused.

A deadline that destroyed the work behind it would teach a learner to submit nothing rather than something late.

SubmittedWhat the College does
Within the deadlineMarked in full against the published rubric.
Under 24 hours lateMarked in full. One day is the allowance the College makes for an ordinary bad day, made without being asked for.
One to five working days lateMarked, with the mark capped at the pass threshold. You keep the level; you lose the honour on that piece of work.
More than five working days lateRead and returned with full feedback, and recorded as incomplete. You sit it again in the next window, and that attempt is the first of your two resits.

A cap is lifted in full where an extension was granted or a mitigating claim is upheld. A capped mark goes on the transcript as the figure it is, with the reason beside it.

Ten working days

An extension, granted on request

Write to the Registrar before the deadline and an extension of up to ten working days is granted, once per module. No reason is asked for and no document is required.

Two working days

Answered before you need the answer

A request is answered within two working days, and at once where the deadline falls inside that period. Your marking clock then runs from the new date.

One exception

A fixed sitting is moved, not extended

A live spoken assessment holds an appointment with an examiner, so it is rescheduled rather than extended. A new appointment is offered within ten working days.

When a deadline falls

Every deadline falls at 23:59 in the time zone recorded on your account. A learner in Jakarta does not meet an earlier deadline than a learner in Casablanca.

The line between an extension and a claim

An extension is asked for before the deadline. Anything asked for after it is a claim of mitigating circumstances, decided by the panel below rather than by the Registrar.

VI Mitigation

The deadline, the evidence, and the one thing a panel may never do.

Illness, bereavement, and the week nobody plans for.

A claim says that something outside your control stopped you doing yourself justice. It is a claim about the conditions of an attempt, never about the standard.

Twenty working days

Counted from the release of the mark

A claim reaches the Registrar within twenty working days of the mark being released. The window runs from the mark, because a bad week often shows only in the result.

The evidence

Something a third party wrote

A medical note, a discharge summary, a death certificate, an employer’s letter, an official notice. It is read inside the Registrar’s office and never travels to the person who marked your work.

Where no document exists

A signed account of it is enough

A power cut, an unsafe situation at home, an emergency abroad: these leave no paperwork. A claim of that kind is decided on your signed account, weighed on what is likely.

Ten working days

Three people, none of whom marked it

A panel of three convened by the Registrar decides every claim, and no member of it marked the work. It answers within ten working days, in writing, with reasons.

What a panel may do, and what it may never do

It may set the attempt aside, lift a late cap, move a deadline that has already passed, or open a fresh window. It may never raise a mark. The remedy for a bad week is another attempt at the same standard.

VII Results

Who reads your work before you see the mark, and the day it arrives.

Two readers, one rubric, one release day.

Every judged mark is read by a second marker before it reaches you, and every level examination cohort is moderated as a batch.

The assessmentReturned to you
Module quizThe same day, marked by machine against a fixed key
Recorded speechWorking day 5 — short on purpose, while the attempt is fresh
Module assignmentWorking day 10
Level examinationWorking day 15
CapstoneWorking day 15

Every clock starts at the submission deadline rather than when a marker opens the script, so an extension carries your return date with it.

12:00 UTC, day fifteen

A cohort is released together

Level examination marks go out at one hour rather than as each marker finishes. Staggered release announces who was marked last, and announces it to everybody.

What arrives with it

The rubric, scored line by line

A mark arrives attached to the criteria that produced it, the second marker’s confirmation, and the single change that would raise it most.

Never held back

A result is not leverage over a debt

An outstanding instalment is a matter for the finance office and never for the academic record. A mark you have earned is released on the academic timetable.

Provisional, and then confirmed

A level examination mark is provisional until moderation closes on the batch, within five working days of release. Moderation may return a whole batch for re-marking; it may not move one learner’s mark on its own.

VIII Resits

Two further attempts, and a wrong mark told apart from a wrong procedure.

A second attempt, and how to challenge a first.

The rest of this leaf is the distinction on which every challenge to a result turns.

1

Two resits for every summative assessment

A third failure means the level is repeated rather than the assessment retaken — see the academic regulations.

How many
2

Fourteen days between attempts

No resit is sat sooner than fourteen days after the attempt before it, so that a second attempt follows some teaching rather than following a disappointment by an hour.

When
3

Capped at the pass threshold

A resit mark is capped at the pass standard, and the mark that stands is the one that counts rather than an average of the two.

Capped
4

No closing date, and no charge

There is no last date for a resit and no fee for one. Charging for a second attempt would turn an assessment into a toll gate.

Open

A mark you dispute, and a procedure that went wrong

Free · answered in 5 working days

A mark check

Ask within ten working days of release and the Registrar confirms that the published rubric was applied, that every criterion carries a score, and that the second marker read it.

Three stages · 20 working days to bring it

An appeal

An appeal is against the way a decision was reached rather than the academic judgement inside it. The stages and deadlines are at the appeals procedure.

  • The rubric applied was other than the one published with the task
  • Evidence you submitted was kept from the person deciding
  • Somebody with an interest in the outcome took part in the decision
  • The conditions of the sitting differed from those published
Why disagreement on its own is not a ground

Two examiners reading one script against one rubric may differ by a point, and a body reading it a third time would substitute an opinion for an opinion. So a disputed mark is answered with a check, and a disputed procedure with an appeal. Neither can lower your mark.

IX Retention

How long the College holds your script, your recording and the papers behind a decision.

An award that cannot be re-examined is an award nobody can defend.

Six years is set so that an award can still be tested against the actual script long after a cohort disperses, and bounded so the College is not holding a voice for ever.

What is heldFor how long
The submitted work, the recording and the scored rubricSix years from the release of the mark
The mark, the honour and the awardPermanently, as part of your academic record
The moderation sample and the panel’s minutesSix years
Appeal papers and mark checksSix years from the final decision
Medical and personal evidence behind a claimDestroyed twelve months after the claim is decided; the decision is kept without it
An attempt set asideNothing is recorded against you
Your own copy of any of it

You may ask for a copy of anything above that concerns you, at any time and at no charge, and it is sent within ten working days. What the College holds is listed at Privacy.
