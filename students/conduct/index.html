Students · Conduct

Conduct, and the rights it sits beside.

A code of conduct published on its own is a list of ways to be expelled. This one opens with what the College owes you, and holds both halves to the same deadlines.

Part one is the College’s obligation to the learner: what it teaches, how it marks, how quickly it answers, and what it does when somebody is treated badly. Part two is the learner’s obligation to the College and to the people in it. Part three is what follows when either half is broken — who decides, on what evidence, and how a decision is appealed.

“Obligation that runs one way is not obligation.”

I The Charter

Eight duties the College takes on, each carrying the measure by which a learner can tell it was kept.

Eight things the College owes every learner.

Each of these is written as a duty on the institution rather than as an intention. Where one carries a number of working days, that number is the College’s own commitment and a learner is entitled to hold it to the day.

One

To teach the curriculum it published

The College teaches to the curriculum it has already published, and publishes any revision before the teaching that follows it. A learner who read the programme before enrolling is entitled to be taught that programme.

Two

To mark against a rubric you saw first

Every assessment is marked against criteria published before the task is set. A mark reaches you attached to the rubric it was given under, so you can see which criterion cost you what. The scheme is set out at How you are assessed.

Three

To answer within a stated time

An ordinary written question is answered within five working days. A formal request — a correction, a complaint, an appeal — is acknowledged within three and answered within twenty. Marked work returns within ten working days of submission, and a capstone within fifteen.

Four

To give reasons for every decision

A decision that changes your mark, your standing, your record or your registration reaches you in writing, with the reason and the evidence it rests on. Reasons are part of the decision rather than an attachment to it, because a decision whose reasoning stays unrecorded cannot be argued with.

Five

To correct its own record on request

You may ask for anything the College holds about you to be corrected, and it answers within ten working days. Where it declines, it gives its reason and attaches your own statement to the entry permanently, so the disagreement travels with the record. What is held is listed at Your academic record.

Six

To have an appeal heard by somebody else

Every decision under this code may be appealed, and at each stage it passes to a person who took no part in the stage before. The full route, and who sits at each stage of it, is published in the academic regulations.

Seven

To protect you from harassment

A report of harassment, intimidation or discrimination reaches the Director of Student Success and is answered within two working days. You are never required to deal directly with the person you have named. Retaliation against somebody who reports in good faith is itself a breach of this code.

Eight

To teach in the language and at the pace you were admitted on

Teaching is in English; administration answers in English or in Arabic. The programme is self-paced, and no completion deadline was attached to it at admission. The College does not impose one afterwards on a cohort already studying.

II Your Obligations

Five, drawn as narrowly as the conduct they govern allows.

Five obligations, and the reason each is narrow.

A rule wide enough to cover everything is a rule that can be used for anything. Each clause below names the conduct it governs and stops there, so that a learner reading it knows in advance what it reaches.

1

Honesty in assessment

Work submitted for assessment is your own production, and a spoken assessment is your own voice. The College’s whole position on this, generative tools included, is set out at Academic integrity and is not restated here. What this code adds is the consequence: a finding made under that procedure is a conduct finding, and it draws on the sanctions in Part Three.

Assessment
2

Conduct toward tutors and other learners

Courtesy is the standard, and it is owed to staff and to other learners on identical terms. Harassment, intimidation and abuse directed at a person or at a group breach this code wherever in the College’s channels they occur. Disagreeing with a mark is legitimate and belongs in an appeal, never in a message to the person who set it.

People
3

Conduct in live teaching and in writing

A tutorial, a capstone defence and a spoken assessment are attended as yourself, under the identity the College holds. Recording another person requires their agreement, given before the recording begins. Anything you write in a College channel is read by a named member of staff, and the register the College asks for is the one you would use in a letter.

Channels
4

Respect for the materials and for other people’s work

The College publishes its curriculum and grants wide terms for reading, printing and quoting it; those terms are set out at the Library. What they do not grant is standing the curriculum up as another provider’s programme. Your account is yours alone, and passing its credentials to somebody else passes your access to a person the College has not admitted.

Materials
5

Accurate personal information

The name on your record is the name that goes on the award, so it is the name on the identity document you enrolled with. A change of name, country of residence or contact address is notified within ten working days. A material false statement made at admission breaches this code whenever it comes to light.

Record
III Procedure

Three stages, each with its own decider, its own deadline and its own right of appeal.

Three stages, and nobody decides twice.

The stages run in order, and the College may not skip one to reach a faster conclusion. At every stage the allegation is put in writing first, and no finding is made until the reply has been read.

Stage one · 5 working days

The tutor, and no record

Most of what this code covers is a misunderstanding, a hard week, or a message sent too quickly. A tutor may raise it with you directly, and where it settles within five working days nothing is entered on your conduct record. A matter closed here cannot be reopened at stage two on the same facts.

Stage two · 20 working days

The determination

What stage one does not settle is put to you in writing by the Registrar, with the allegation and the material it rests on. You have ten working days to answer, in writing or in a conversation you may bring one person to. The decision follows within twenty working days of your answer and names the sanction and its reason.

Stage three · 20 working days

The review

A review is heard by the Provost, who takes no part in any determination. It may uphold the finding, reduce the sanction, or set the finding aside entirely, and it may never increase what stage two imposed. Beyond it lies the Board of Governors, by the route published in the academic regulations.

The standard of proof

The College decides on the balance of probabilities: whether the thing alleged is more likely than not to have happened. The burden sits with the College throughout, and you are never asked to prove that you did not do it. The graver the allegation and the heavier the sanction, the more cogent the evidence has to be before that standard is met.

While a matter is open

Where an integrity matter is unresolved the Registrar may pause progression — the standing published in the academic regulations as Suspended Progression. It is the narrowest measure that answers the situation, and it is bounded in three ways. Your access to teaching, to the library and to support does not change; learning is never the thing withdrawn. A first-instance decision is reached within twenty working days of the matter opening, and where it is not, the suspension lapses of its own accord rather than waiting on anyone to lift it. And a matter that closes without a finding leaves nothing behind on your record.

  • The allegation is put in writing before any finding is made.
  • Your reply is read before the decision rather than after it.
  • Every stage answers in writing, with its reasons and its evidence.
  • Nobody decides a stage who decided the stage before it.
  • A review may reduce a sanction and may never increase one.
  • A sanction takes effect when the review closes, unless you ask otherwise.
IV The Tariff

Eight sanctions in order of severity, the office that may impose each, and how long each is held.

Eight sanctions, in order, and how long each is held.

A code with no published tariff lets identical conduct cost two learners different amounts. A sanction two steps above the conduct it answers is appealable on that ground by itself, without arguing the finding.

What a sanction does not touch

No sanction removes a mark you have earned or a level you have completed. Academic standing and conduct are separate registers, and the rule that no standing removes your access to learning is published in the academic regulations and is untouched by this code. Where a registration is withdrawn, levels you have paid for and have not begun are returned to you under the published refund policy.

A record that clears itself

A sanction is spent once its period has run, counted from the date of the decision. A spent sanction is disclosed in no reference, weighed in no later case, and printed on no transcript. The College clears it without being asked, because a record that clears only on request is a record most people never clear.
