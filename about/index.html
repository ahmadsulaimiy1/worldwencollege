WorldWide English College · London Campus

One conviction, and one programme built to it: command of English decides who is heard.

Every year, capable people lose places, promotions and hearings they were qualified for — not for want of the knowledge, but because they could not command the language it had to be argued in. That is a solvable problem, and it is the only one this College works on.

An international English-language institution with a single programme — the International English Fluency Course, six CEFR-aligned levels, taught and assessed online, for students wherever they are in the world.

I The Operating Model

What “London Campus” names, and what it does not.

Online-first by design. London-based by administration.

“London Campus” identifies WEC-LC’s administrative and management headquarters. It is not a claim of a physical teaching campus, and it never referred to more than one. The College is online-first by design: every level of the IEFC is taught and assessed inside the digital campus, so a student in Lagos, Riyadh or Manila studies on the same terms as one in London.

That is the architecture the College chose, and it is the reason a learner anywhere is taught to one standard rather than to whichever standard a local franchise happens to hold.

What it means

The institution’s administrative and management headquarters, and the seat of its governance — the brand and legal home of WEC-LC.

≠

What it does not mean

A physical building where classes are taught, or a network of regional campuses. Every student, everywhere, studies through the same online digital campus.

Who this page is written for

Families, professionals and institutions across the Gulf, Europe, Africa and Asia — readers who check what they are buying before they buy it. The College writes for that reader: the full syllabus, the pricing, the policies and the current institutional status are published before enrolment, in English and in Arabic.

II Institutional Status

Two columns, two marks. Nothing outstanding wears a tick.

What is confirmed, and what is still being built.

Trust is earned through precision, not promises. This is the current state of WEC-LC, and the second column is published with the same care as the first.

Held today

  • Institutional name, motto, vision, mission and core values
  • The full International English Fluency Course structure — six levels, CEFR alignment, curriculum and methodology
  • Tuition structure and per-level pricing
  • Admissions process and target learner profile
  • Named academic leadership and the faculty roster (see Faculty)
  • Operating model — online-first delivery worldwide, with a London administrative headquarters

Outstanding

4

  • Formal accreditation and external quality-assurance affiliations
  • Academic calendar and first-cohort start date
  • Completion of the full lesson content within each module — all sixty modules are live; the lessons inside them are still being written (see Curriculum status)

Each of these is published here before it is settled, and moves column only when it is. The mark on this side is an open ring rather than a tick, because a tick against unfinished work is the smallest possible lie and the easiest one to tell by accident.

The Commitment

The College states what is unfinished rather than presenting an incomplete claim as a settled fact. Every section of this site is written under that rule, including this one.

III Vision

A destination, published as a destination and not as a description.

An ambition, stated as an ambition.

To become one of the world’s leading English language institutions, recognised for excellence in English language education, innovation, academic integrity, and graduate success.

That sentence is a destination, not a description. WEC-LC is a new institution: it holds no accreditation, has conferred no award, and has taught no cohort. Publishing a vision does not change any of that, and this page does not pretend otherwise.

i

Recognition has to be earned from outside

Recognition means external bodies attesting to quality — accreditation, external examining, professional endorsement. None of it can be self-awarded, and none of it has been obtained. The College is building the evidence such bodies ask for rather than describing itself as though they had already asked.

ii

Excellence is a standard, not a claim

What can be shown today is the standard the work is held to: a mapped curriculum, published rubrics, assessments written before the teaching they test, and a policy of recording what is missing. That is measurable now; graduate success is not.

iii

The vision includes how it is pursued

An institution that overstates itself while becoming excellent is not becoming excellent. The refusal to publish unearned claims is part of the vision, not a constraint on it.

IV Mission

Five undertakings, each with the mechanism that delivers it.

What the College undertakes to do.

Five undertakings. Each is stated with the thing that actually delivers it, because an undertaking with no mechanism attached is a sentiment.

i

Teach English to a published standard, level by level, from A1 to C2

Delivered as 6 CEFR-aligned levels, 60 modules, and 720 designed lessons — every one planned stage by stage rather than left to the room.

ii

Develop confident, fluent and academically competent speakers

Guided speaking is built into every lesson from the first, and the four skills are assessed separately so a learner strong in reading and weak in speaking is not described as simply “intermediate”.

iii

Prepare learners for study, employment and professional communication

The upper levels teach academic writing, meetings, negotiation, advocacy and research presentation — the registers that decide outcomes rather than the ones that pass a test.

iv

Build the instrument the programme needs

A learning platform carrying lessons, quizzes, self-checks and progress, plus a Listening Lab that records the learner’s own speech for pronunciation feedback. Technology serving instruction, not replacing it — and built for this programme rather than licensed from one built for something else.

v

Reach learners worldwide on the same terms

Taught online from a London administrative base, with multi-currency pricing and payment routing built for learners in Africa, the Gulf, Europe and Asia rather than retrofitted for them.

V Core Values

Eight ordinary words. What matters is what each costs.

What the College holds itself to.

Eight values. They are ordinary words, and what makes them meaningful is whether an institution can be shown to act on them when it costs something. Each is published here with the cost attached.

Academic Excellence

The standard is written before the teaching that meets it, and published before the assessment that tests it.

Integrity

What is unfinished is named as unfinished, on the page where a reader would otherwise assume otherwise.

Innovation

An instrument built for this programme rather than a licensed player with a crest applied to it.

Professionalism

Every lesson carries timings, prerequisites and objectives, so a learner is taught the same way by two different people.

Inclusiveness

One standard and one price list, published in the learner’s own currency wherever they are.

Lifelong Learning

Workload is published in hours rather than months, because how long the pathway takes belongs to the learner.

Global Citizenship

English taught as the language people negotiate, study and are heard in — not as a subject that ends at the exam.

Student-Centred Education

The record belongs to the learner, accumulates as it is written, and is theirs to share or withhold.

§ Integrity, Specifically

Three occasions the value above cost the College something.

The value that costs something.

Integrity is the easiest value to list and the hardest to demonstrate. At WEC-LC it has a concrete meaning, and it has repeatedly cost the College the right to say flattering things.

i

Eighteen names that will never be published

A realistic staff chart exists for designing the administration screens against. Every name on it is fictional, and an automated check fails the build if any of them reaches a page the public can load.

ii

Interim means interim

The Level I learning outcomes and every competency mapping are recorded as interim, not approved, because the board that would approve them has 0 appointed members. The pages say interim.

iii

Books that state what they do not rest on

The Teacher’s Companion marks every one of its 245 panels with where it came from, and states in its front matter that it contains no classroom observation, because the College has taught nobody.

VI Educational Philosophy

Five positions, and what each one does to a lesson.

The positions every lesson is built on.

i

Speaking comes before readiness

Learners are asked to speak from the first lesson, before their grammar can support it. Waiting until you are accurate enough to speak is how people study a language for years and cannot use it. Accuracy is built through use, not before it.

ii

A curriculum is a promise, not a suggestion

Every lesson states its objectives, its prerequisites and the timing of each stage, and every instructor teaches to it. This is not distrust of teachers; it is what lets a learner change class, pause and return, or be taught by two people, without losing their place.

iii

Assessment criteria belong to the learner

Rubrics and pass criteria are published before the assessment. A learner who does not know what is being judged is being tested on guessing the examiner, and the least advantaged learners guess worst.

iv

Four skills, four measurements

Listening, reading, speaking and writing are recorded separately. A single overall grade averages away exactly the information a learner needs — which skill is holding them back.

v

Error is data, not failure

Each lesson carries a self-check built around the mistakes learners at that level actually make, so a learner meets their own error privately before an examiner meets it. The traps are deliberate.

How a position becomes practice

In the lesson

A plan with timings

Warm-up, presentation, guided practice, independent practice, listening, reading, writing, pronunciation, homework — each with stated minutes, so the speaking stage is not the one that gets cut when time runs short.

In the room

A teacher who knows what fails

Instructors work from a Companion that names, for each lesson, what commonly goes wrong, a second way to explain it, and what to do for the learner who is behind and the one who finished early.

After the lesson

Evidence that accumulates

Quiz and assignment results, time on task and pronunciation attempts build a record the learner owns and can show — not a certificate asserting a level, but evidence of what was done.

VII Competencies

Six competencies, defined and mapped — and recorded as interim.

Beyond proficiency: what fluency is for.

IEFC is an advanced academic qualification built on CEFR proficiency, extending it through competency verification, leadership, professional communication, critical thinking, authentic assessment, and independently verifiable digital credentials.

CompetencyWhat it means
ClarityUnderstood the first time, by the audience actually present
CommandControls the language rather than being carried by it
JudgementChooses register, channel and moment; knows what not to say
ReasonConstructs an argument, tests it, concedes what should be conceded
BearingHolds a room, a call, a difficult conversation
ReachCommunicates across cultures, and across the distance between expert and layperson
The status of this framework

The competencies are defined and every Level I assessment is mapped to them. Those mappings are recorded as interim, approved under authority delegated to the Press, because the Board that would ratify them has not been constituted. They are used and they are not yet ratified, and the difference is recorded in the database rather than smoothed over.

VIII How It Is Organised

Three functions, and three separations that hold them apart.

Academic, administrative, publishing.

Academic

Teaching and standards

Academic staff and tutors deliver the programme; the two academic bodies — BASCE and SENATE — hold standards and the competency framework. The roster is published on the Faculty page.

Administrative

Admissions, records, finance

Admissions, student records, assessment administration and finance. At the College’s current size these are held by few people rather than many, and the platform’s access levels enforce it.

Publishing

WEC Press

The College’s imprint publishes the curriculum, assessment and teaching volumes. It has no separate legal personality and no staff of its own, and every volume says so.

Three separations that are not negotiable

These exist only to keep certain people independent of each other. They are the first thing an accreditation panel looks for, they cost nothing to preserve now, and they are expensive to unpick later.

Author

Nobody reviews their own work. A publication reviewed by its author has not been reviewed.

≠

Academic Reviewer

Internal, and independent of whoever wrote the volume under review.

≠

External Examiner

The examiner’s whole function is being outside the College. It cannot be doubled with an internal role, and whoever confers an award is not whoever examined it.

What this means for staffing

The minimum credible academic staffing is two qualified people who are not each other, plus one external. Everything else at this size can legitimately be combined — records with assessment administration, finance with procurement, IT with learning technologies.

Posts the College has not filled.

Each of these blocks something specific, and the blocked thing is named rather than left to be discovered. Two of them can be fractional or consultancy engagements — see Careers.

VIII Equity & Welcome

Who is welcome, what is protected, and the mechanism behind each — not the sentiment.

English belongs to whoever learns it.

English is not the property of one country, one culture or one faith. It is the language in which a scholarship is applied for, a diagnosis is explained, a contract is argued and a case is made — and the person who commands it is heard, while the person who does not is spoken about. That is the whole reason this College exists, and it is why the door is the width of the world.

I

Muslim and Christian students, and students of no faith, are taught the same programme

There is one curriculum, one set of assessment criteria and one standard of marking. No learner is offered a lesser version of the course on any ground, and no learner is asked what they believe to enrol — the application form does not ask, because the College does not need to know.

II

The teaching does not require anyone to argue against their own faith

A language course teaches how to disagree, how to concede a point and how to hold a position under question. Those are transferable skills, and the College teaches them on subjects nobody has to be uncomfortable about. Where a discussion topic would put a learner in the position of arguing against their own religion, the topic is changed — not the learner.

III

Assessment is blind to everything except the English

Rubrics are published before the work and mark the four skills apart. They contain no criterion for background, accent, nationality or belief, and an instructor may not introduce one — a mark that cannot be justified against the published rubric is not a mark, it is an opinion, and it is overturned.

IV

Where a learner needs a particular instructor, that is arranged

A learner who would rather be taught and assessed by an instructor of the same gender may ask, and the College arranges it where staffing allows. This is treated as a scheduling matter and never as a concession: no reason has to be given, and asking is not recorded against anyone.

V

Nothing here is scheduled against anyone’s obligations

The programme is self-paced, and that is the accommodation before any other: a learner fits it around prayer, fasting, a night shift or a family. Where live sessions are introduced they will be offered at more than one hour, and the College says plainly that this has not yet been tested — no cohort has been taught.

What this is not

It is not a claim to have done it yet. The College has taught no cohort, so it has no record of practice to point at, and none of the above should be read as one. What it is: the standard the College has written for itself, published before the first student arrives so that the first student can hold it to them — and so that anyone who finds the College falling short of it can quote this page back.

This page is maintained as a record rather than as a brochure. Where a fact on it changes, it changes here first and on the evidence record beside it — and where something is not yet true, it is published in the outstanding column above rather than left out.
