The College

Al-Madinah International College

A distance-learning college of the Qur’ānic and Islamic sciences, admitting students in Nigeria and internationally to structured programmes of study leading to the Certificate, the Diploma, the Higher Diploma and ijāzah.

The name

وَالرَّاسِخُونَ فِي الْعِلْمِ

The College is named for al-Madīnah, the city to which the Prophet ﷺ emigrated and in which the first community of learning was formed. It is not a claim of location: this College keeps its registry in Lagos and teaches wholly at a distance. It is a statement of where its subject comes from, and of the standard the name obliges it to keep.

The College is styled Al-Madinah International College (كلية المدينة العالمية), abbreviated in documents to AMC. International is a description of whom it serves — students in Nigeria and in twenty-two other countries — and not a claim of accreditation in any of them (Standing, Recognition and Disclosure).

Its motto is retained from the verse the College was founded on: وَالرَّاسِخُونَ فِي الْعِلْمِ — and those firmly rooted in knowledge (Āl ‘Imrān 3:7). The name says where the tradition comes from; the motto says what the College is for.

Method of instruction

Instruction at a distance.

The College maintains no campus. Instruction is delivered by scheduled live class throughout the session, in the manner of the affiliated and external programmes long established at Al-Azhar and at the University of London.

Students are admitted to a named programme and placed at a level determined by assessment. Classes are capped at twelve, and every student is assigned a member of faculty responsible for their supervision and their record. Sessions are recorded and made available to enrolled students for revision.

Programmes are taught to prescribed texts against published syllabi, which are issued to students on matriculation and are available to applicants from the Registry. Assessment is by written paper and oral examination, and awards are conferred by the Academic Council on the recommendation of the examining board.

Instruction is given in Arabic and in English. Classes are conducted with regard to the customary separation of men and women, and women students are taught by women members of faculty where the programme requires individual supervision.

What is done live, and what is not

The College does not film a classroom. The two modes are designed against one another rather than stacked. Live time is scarce, is the most expensive thing the College has, and is reserved for what only a teacher can do — hear the error and name it. Recitation and its correction, speaking, oral examination, the diagnosis of a difficulty and the tutorial are live. Recorded lessons, reading, drill, vocabulary, murājaʿah and written work are not.

The rule the College applies is short: where a capable adult could acquire it alone with good materials, it is not given live time.

The undertaking on bandwidth

The students this College was built for pay for their own data, on inexpensive devices, on connections that fail. That is a fact of the curriculum and not merely of the interface. Audio is therefore authored to stand alone rather than offered as a fallback; every lesson has a form that works with the connection off; no examination requires sustained live video, and where video is genuinely needed it is short and has an audio route recorded as an approved adjustment.

The weight of a lesson’s required materials is stated to the student in megabytes before they open it, and a dropped connection never costs submitted work.

The whole of the College’s academic method is set out in the Academic Regulations: a fixed standard, a pace the student can keep, progression on what has been demonstrated, and a service that differs where the standard does not.

Delivery

Teaching is conducted audio-first, with video at the student’s election, so that classes remain accessible where bandwidth is limited. Class times are published in West Africa Time with the offset from Coordinated Universal Time stated. The academic year comprises three semesters kept against the Hijrī calendar, with the general timetable suspended in Ramaḍān.

Foundation

Origin of the College.

The College originates in a programme of instruction in the Qur’ān and the Arabic language established on 22 February 2020, which admitted more than 1,550 students from 22 countries, among them students resident in Nigeria, the United Kingdom, the United States, Qatar, the United Arab Emirates and Saudi Arabia.

Teaching was suspended in 2022 and 2023. The College was constituted in its present form in 1448 / 2026, with a faculty structure, an Academic Council and a published scheme of awards.

The Founder

Ahmad Sulaimiy read the Qur’ānic sciences at Taif University, having begun his studies in Nigeria, and studied further with scholars in Madinah and in Egypt. He holds ijāzah in the ten canonical readings and in the six principal collections of ḥadīth, including Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim.

He is the author of Grammar Planet, علمني ربي, كيف عاش الرسول؟ and Islamic Law and Contemporary View.

Constituted

Founded 1441 / 2020 as a programme of instruction in the Qur’ān and the Arabic language; constituted as a College in 1448 / 2026 with a faculty structure, an Academic Council and a published scheme of awards.

Authority

Governed by a Board of Trustees. Academic authority is vested in the Academic Council, which approves the syllabi, appoints the examiners and confirms every result. No commercial officer may alter an academic outcome.

Standing

Awards are conferred on the College’s own authority. It confers no degree and holds no external accreditation, and says so wherever it describes itself — Standing, Recognition and Disclosure.

Undertakings

No tuition fee and no examination fee. Verification free to every enquirer, for ever. The register of instruments is the last thing that would be switched off — Charges.

Set in Bodoni Moda, Cinzel, Inter and Amiri · kept against the Hijrī calendar · written in English and in Arabic, each authored rather than translated
