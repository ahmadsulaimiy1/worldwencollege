About

Who decides what, and on what authority.

The College separates academic judgement from institutional governance, from quality assurance, from finance, and from day-to-day administration. This page names who holds each of those and states plainly which posts are still unfilled — because a governance page that reads as though every board sits would be the most consequential untruth on this website.

Leadership

Leadership in service of scholarship.

The College is governed through a framework that separates academic judgement, institutional governance, quality assurance, finance and day-to-day administration. That separation is not administrative tidiness. It is what protects every learner, every assessment, every publication and every award the College issues.

The constraint that matters

No individual, including the Founder, may independently determine academic standards, approve their own work, or override established governance procedure. The College is built to outlast the people currently running it.

Board of Governors

Strategic oversight, held apart from management.

The Board preserves the College’s mission, safeguards its academic independence, oversees financial sustainability, and appoints senior officers. Its test for any decision is the long-term interest of learners rather than short-term convenience.

The College’s highest academic authority.

The Senate oversees academic standards, curriculum development, assessment integrity, research quality and academic regulations. It operates independently of operational management and advises the Board on academic matters.

College Executive

Daily operation, under policy set by the Board.

The Executive runs the College. It does not set the standards it operates under, and it does not review its own work — those sit with the Senate and with the External Examiner respectively.

Independent External Examiner

The post that cannot be filled from inside.

The External Examiner reviews assessments, academic standards, marking consistency and the integrity of awards, and reports directly to the Board of Governors rather than through the Executive. The whole function of the post is to sit outside the College, which is why no appointment made within it can substitute.

Currently vacant

The post is not filled. Until it is, awards are defined and published but cannot properly be conferred on anyone, and the College says so on every page where it bears on a decision rather than once in a footnote.

Academic Bodies

The two bodies and their remits.

BASCE

Board of Academic Standards and Curriculum Excellence

Defines institutional competencies; maps every assessment to one or more competencies; ensures each competency is assessed multiple times across each level; approves competency descriptors; reviews mappings annually; maintains the integrity of the competency framework.

Established 2026-08-04 · Members appointed 0

SENATE

Academic Senate

Approves the mapping between assessments and the four language skills, and the descriptor thresholds that turn assessed evidence into a skill descriptor.

Established 2026-08-04 · Members appointed 3

What these two counts mean

BASCE has no members, and a body with no members cannot approve anything. Every competency decision that would properly belong to it is recorded as interim, taken under authority delegated to the Press, and marked in the database with the body it awaits.

The Senate has 3, and has not yet convened. That is a different position from BASCE’s and it produces the same outcome for now: a body that can approve but has not met has not approved anything, so skill mappings and descriptor thresholds also remain interim. They change status when a minuted decision exists, not when an appointment letter does.

How Decisions Are Recorded

Every academic decision carries its authority.

Who decided

A named authority

Each mapping, outcome and threshold records the body it was decided under — BASCE or Senate — and its status. Nothing is recorded as simply true.

On what basis

A stated rationale

Every competency mapping carries a rationale explaining why that assessment bears on that competency. A mapping without one is an opinion that has acquired the authority of a database row.

Reviewed when

An annual cycle

The framework is reviewed annually against the evidence teaching produces. The first cycle cannot run until a cohort has been taught, and the record says so rather than describing a cycle that has never turned.

Posts Not Yet Filled

What the College does not have.

Listed rather than omitted. Each of these blocks something specific, and naming what it blocks is more useful than a page that simply stops short.

External Examiner

Required before any award is conferred

The independent post whose entire function is to sit outside the College. Until it is filled, awards are defined and published but cannot properly be conferred on anyone. No internal appointment substitutes for it, which is why filling the Board, the Senate and the Executive has not moved this one.

BASCE members

Required before competencies are approved

The Board of Academic Standards and Curriculum Excellence stands at 0 appointed members. Approval, as opposed to interim adoption, waits on appointment. The Board of Governors has a Governor for Academic Affairs; that is a different body with a different remit, and it is not read as BASCE membership.

A convened Senate

Required before skill mappings are approved

The Senate has 3 appointed members and has not yet met. Constituting a body and convening it are two events, and the record holds them separately so that neither can quietly stand in for the other.

Academic Reviewer

Required before publications are reviewed

Every published volume is authored by the Press and has not been read by a qualified reader who did not write it. Each volume states this on its own imprint page. Any of the ten academic staff may now take it; the assignment simply has to be recorded.

Governance Principles

Six principles, and every decision is held to them.

  1. 1

    Academic Integrity

    Scholarship before convenience.

  2. 2

    Independent Review

    No person approves their own work.

  3. 3

    Transparency

    Policies, standards and decisions are documented.

  4. 4

    Accountability

    Authority carries measurable responsibility.

  5. 5

    Service

    Leadership exists to support learning.

  6. 6

    Stewardship

    The College is built for future generations, not present personalities.
