The Standard for English. Nothing Hidden Until You Enrol.

WorldWide English College teaches one thing, and teaches it completely: the International English Fluency Course, carrying a learner from their first word to command of the language. Six levels. Sixty modules. Every one of them written and published in full — you read the entire programme before you pay for any of it.

Founding Cohort — now enrolling. Those who join now are taught with an attention the College’s own growth will make impossible later.

I The Case

English is the room you are allowed into.

It is the working language of the university lecture hall, the tender document, the board paper and the visa interview. Command of it does not make a person capable. It makes a capable person legible — and without it, the ability is there and the door is not.

The university place

Admissions offices worldwide read one language. A degree offer is decided on academic English long before it is decided on ambition, and the gap is closed by instruction, not by time spent abroad.

The professional ceiling

Careers stop at the level of English their holder can perform under pressure. The brief that must be written, the meeting that must be chaired, the client who must be persuaded — each is an English task before it is a professional one.

The standing of a nation

Delegations, contracts and scholarship are all negotiated in English. A country's people speaking it well is national capacity, and it is built one properly taught learner at a time.

What poor teaching costs

Years of lessons that never produced a sentence anyone would use. The commonest outcome in language education is not failure — it is a decade of study and no command. That is the outcome this College was built to end.

The question is never whether English is worth learning. It is whether it is being taught by an institution that will show you its standard, mark you against it, and put the evidence in your hands.

II The College

Read the entire programme before you pay for any of it.

Most language providers sell a syllabus you are not permitted to see. Every level here is published in full — its modules, its outcomes, its assessments, and the criteria they are marked against. That is the standard this College holds itself to, and it is published because it can be.

  • The standard is published first

    Outcomes, rubrics and pass criteria reach you before the assessment, never after it. A standard revealed afterwards is not a standard — it is an explanation of a verdict.

  • Four skills, marked apart

    Listening, reading, speaking and writing carry separate marks, and stay separate on your record. One averaged number conceals precisely what a language learner most needs to know.

  • The record is yours

    Every attempt, mark and piece of feedback accumulates in one place, visible to you as it is written. You decide what is shared, and with whom.

  • Nothing is taught that is not written

    Sixty modules exist as published documents before a single learner is enrolled against them. The College does not improvise a curriculum around the students who arrive.

III Academics

The International English Fluency Course

Six CEFR-aligned levels. Ten modules in each. One published assessment policy, from the first lesson to the last.

The pathway does not branch. A learner entering at their measured level walks the same road as everyone above and below them, and the four skills are carried through every turn of it rather than taught in sequence and then assumed.

The College publishes its workload in hours rather than in months, because how long the pathway takes is set by the hours you can give it each week — and that is yours to decide, not ours to promise. The 1,200-hour figure is the workload the curriculum was built to: a design figure, not a measurement, and it will be replaced by hours measured from real time-on-task once the founding cohort has generated them. The WEC Credit is an internal measure of academic weight; it is not ECTS or CATS and carries no transfer entitlement.

IV The Residency

Language is finished in use, not in a syllabus.

The pathway closes with a supervised practice period of between thirty and sixty days, in which a learner stops studying English and starts working in it — in their own city, or in the United Kingdom, where the language is not a subject but the weather, the ticket office and the seminar room.

  • Thirty to sixty days

    Long enough for the language to stop being translated in your head, short enough to take without leaving a career or a degree behind. The exact length follows the entry permission a learner holds and the law applying at the time, which is why the College publishes a range rather than a number it cannot promise every passport.

  • Your city, or the United Kingdom

    Complete the residency at home under College supervision, or in the United Kingdom — London and Westminster, Manchester, or another selected British location. Both routes are examined against the same criteria: where you practise changes the logistics, never the standard.

  • Why Britain

    English is not taught in the United Kingdom; it is simply used there. A month in London, Westminster or Manchester puts a learner in the register no classroom reproduces — the pace of a real conversation, the accent that does not slow down, the vocabulary of a city going about its business.

  • Assessed on performance

    Speaking under real conditions, writing to a real brief, listening in a room where nobody slows down for you. The residency is marked on what the language does, not on what is remembered about it.

A residency taken in the United Kingdom requires the appropriate entry permission, which is granted by the Home Office and not by the College. Admissions advises on which route fits a learner's circumstances and issues the documentation an application needs; the decision is the Home Office's alone.

V Admissions

Ambitious learners, at every stage.

The College teaches across the Gulf, Europe, Africa and Asia, in a single cohort and to a single standard.

One pathway serves all of them, because the language does not change according to who is learning it. What changes is the reason: a place at a university, a brief that has to be delivered in English, a board that has to be chaired in it. The programme is the same; the strand a learner leans on is not.

The Gulf

Professionals and postgraduate-bound students, held to one exacting standard.

Europe

Bridging into English-medium study or international careers across the continent.

Africa

Preparing for English-medium universities and the professional world beyond them.

Asia

Building the English a competitive university or employer expects.

Secondary students

Preparing for study in English before it becomes the thing standing in the way.

University students

Academic English written for seminars, essays and vivas rather than for exercises.

Working professionals

Business English and the register a room full of senior people actually uses.

International applicants

Study-abroad candidates

VI Student Life

An instrument, built for one programme.

The platform was written for the IEFC and for nothing else. It is not a licensed course player with a crest applied to it.

  • My Programme

    Your level, module by module, showing what is complete and what comes next. Progression is per learner — nothing unlocks on a date someone else chose.

  • The Listening Lab

    You record yourself against named pronunciation targets, and every recording is kept — so six months of change is something you can hear rather than something you are told about. It records offline when a connection drops.

  • A record that can be checked

    Results carry a verification reference, so a university or an employer can confirm what you hold without taking your word or ours for it.

Recorded listening audio and seminar sessions are written and timetabled against the founding cohort, and both are published on the platform roadmap with the state they are in.

VII Governance

Four commitments this College will not trade.

Each of them costs the College something. That is precisely why they are worth publishing.

01

An award worth conferring

The College confers its award only under external examination. A certificate issued without one is worth less, not more — and it would be the easiest thing on this site to fake.

02

Evidence, not assertion

Every institutional claim made here is published alongside the record that supports it, in a register open to anyone who wants to check it. The College argues from documents.

03

A founding cohort

Those who enrol now are the first, and they are taught with an attention that scale makes impossible later. A founding cohort is the one advantage a new institution can offer that an old one cannot.

04

Level by level, at your pace

Tuition is payable one level at a time. You commit to the next ten modules — never to the whole pathway at once, and never to a stretch of your life you have not yet lived.

WEC-LC delivers the IEFC online worldwide from a London administrative headquarters, and operates under UK law with professional counsel retained. Accreditation candidacy is underway; its current stage, and the governance appointments and academic calendar supporting it, are published in full and kept current — see institutional status and the evidence record.

VIII The Pathway

External validation, published stage by stage.

The College publishes the route it is taking to external validation before it arrives at any point on it. Every stage is named, sequenced and reported against — the same discipline the College applies to a learner's progress, applied to itself.

Stage What it establishes Position
I Internal quality assurance Written standards, moderation and a documented assessment regime — the foundation every external reviewer examines first. Well advanced
II External academic review Independent academics examining the curriculum, the marking and the standard the College says it holds. In preparation
III ASIC accreditation Accreditation Service for International Schools, Colleges and Universities — recognised internationally and by UK Visas and Immigration. In preparation
IV Accreditation UK The British Council scheme for English language provision, run with English UK — the sector benchmark in this discipline. In preparation
V BAC accreditation The British Accreditation Council, for independent further and higher education. In preparation
VI Awarding-organisation partnership Working with recognised awarding organisations so a learner's achievement carries a qualification issued outside the College. In preparation
VII ISO 21001 The international management-system standard for educational organisations, certified by external audit. In preparation
VIII University partnerships Articulation and progression agreements with universities in the United Kingdom, the United States and beyond. In preparation
IX Regulated awarding status The College’s own regulated awarding powers, pursued only where they serve the learner rather than the letterhead. Under long-term consideration

Each stage is reported in the evidence record as it moves, with the documents behind it. The College does not display a mark it has not been granted, and the position column above is the whole of its claim — see the evidence record.
