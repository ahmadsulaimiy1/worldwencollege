Faculty of the Qur’ān and its Sciences

Kulliyyat al-Qur’ān al-Karīm wa ‘Ulūmih

Six programmes in recitation, Tajwīd, memorisation and the readings. Admission to every programme in this faculty is determined by recitation before a member of faculty.

Certificate programmes

Foundation and recitation.

Certificate in Qur’ānic Foundation

One semester · Open entry · Admitting

For students beginning with no knowledge of the Arabic script. The programme covers the letters and their forms, the points and manner of articulation, the short and long vowels, and the joining of letters into words, to the point at which a student can read a vowelled text unaided.

Assessment. Continuous oral assessment and a final oral examination.

Certificate in Qur’ānic Recitation

One semester · Entry by assessment · Admitting

Sustained recitation of the muṣḥaf under correction. The programme establishes accuracy of articulation and fluency of reading before any programme of memorisation is undertaken, and is a condition of entry to the Diploma in Tajwīd.

Assessment. Oral examination in recitation from an unseen portion.

Diploma programmes

Tajwīd and memorisation.

Diploma in Tajwīd and Recitation

Two semesters · Entry: Certificate in Recitation · Admitting

The science of Tajwīd in theory and in application. Courses cover the rules of nūn sākinah and tanwīn, mīm sākinah, the categories of madd, qalqalah, the characteristics of the letters, and the points of waqf and ibtidā’.

Prescribed texts. Tuḥfat al-Aṭfāl (first semester) and al-Muqaddimah al-Jazariyyah (second), both examined from memory.

Assessment. Written paper and oral viva, each passed independently.

Diploma in Qur’ānic Memorisation

Four to nine semesters · Entry: Diploma in Tajwīd · Semester II

Memorisation of the Qur’ān, in whole or in defined portions, with a structured programme of revision (murāja’ah) running from the first week of study. Three modes of study are available — standard, accelerated and modular by juz’ — and the mode is set at admission according to the hours a student can commit.

Assessment. Continuous oral assessment by portion, and a final oral examination before an examining board.

The method

How memorisation is measured, and how it is kept.

This faculty does not record what a student reached. It records what a student retains, and the two are not the same number.

Measured in portions held

Memorisation is not measured in hours. It is measured in portions retained, and a portion is entered as held only where it survives unannounced re-examination after an interval — the Rule of Retention (Reg. 3.2). Hours are recorded against a portion for the planning of a student’s week and for the volume column of the transcript; they are never the standard.

The College reckons a juz’ at sixty to eighty notional hours inclusive of revision to the criterion. That figure is an estimate, is described as one, and will be revised against the College’s own completion data as it accumulates rather than defended.

The Five Returns — المراجعات الخمس

The classical cycle distinguishes sabaq, sabqī and manzil. Its weakness in practice is that the middle stage is left to the student’s discretion, and that is precisely where memorisation is lost. This faculty fixes it to a schedule of expanding returns, generated by the College and not left to intention.

ReturnInterval after the portion is first heard sound
First1 day
Second3 days
Third7 days
Fourth21 days
Fifth60 days

A portion that survives all five joins the manzil, the perpetual cycle, and is thereafter sampled without notice. A portion that fails any return re-enters at the first. The intervals expand rather than repeat because recall that is just difficult enough is what consolidates a memory: a portion revised while it is still effortless teaches the student little.

The Five Returns — المراجعات الخمس

Kept in the Riwāq

Every student of this faculty carries a ledger of all 604 pages of the muṣḥaf in the College’s portal: what is sabaq, what is sabqī, what has entered the manzil, which return each page is due, and the band of the last hearing. The schedule is generated from the record, and a portion becomes due whether or not the student remembers that it is.

Higher Diploma programmes

Recitation with ijāzah, and the ten readings.

Higher Diploma in Qur’ānic Recitation

Two to four semesters · Entry: complete memorisation · Admitting

Recitation of the complete Qur’ān to a member of faculty holding a connected chain, in the narration of Ḥafṣ from ‘Āṣim. On satisfactory completion the candidate is awarded the Higher Diploma together with ijāzah bi-sanad muttaṣil, which states the chain of transmission in full.

Assessment. Complete recitation before the conferring teacher, conducted live.

Higher Diploma in the Ten Readings

Five to eight semesters · Entry: Higher Diploma with ijāzah · Year II

The ten canonical readings by the route of al-Shāṭibiyyah and al-Durrah, with provision for the extended route by Ṭayyibat al-Nashr. Both governing poems are committed to memory and examined.

Admission. This programme opens on the appointment of a second member of faculty holding the ten readings, so that no candidate is examined for a chain by the sole holder of it.

Conditions governing ijāzah

Ijāzah is conferred only by a member of faculty holding a connected chain in the narration conferred; only upon complete recitation conducted live, and not on the basis of recordings; and the instrument states the chain of transmission in full. The chains held by the faculty are published in the Register of Asānīd.

Register of Asānīd
Supplementary study

Recitation voice.

A supplementary course in the discipline of the reciting voice, taken alongside any programme in this faculty from the Diploma in Tajwīd onward. It treats breath, register and placement, and the maqāmāt employed in classical recitation, with instruction in both the Makkan and Madinan manners.

The course concludes with instruction in the adhān to the standard required of one appointed to call it.

Recitation voice

Eight weeks · two classes weekly
Entry: Diploma in Tajwīd, or concurrent enrolment

Constituted

Founded 1441 / 2020 as a programme of instruction in the Qur’ān and the Arabic language; constituted as a College in 1448 / 2026 with a faculty structure, an Academic Council and a published scheme of awards.

Authority

Governed by a Board of Trustees. Academic authority is vested in the Academic Council, which approves the syllabi, appoints the examiners and confirms every result. No commercial officer may alter an academic outcome.

Standing

Awards are conferred on the College’s own authority. It confers no degree and holds no external accreditation, and says so wherever it describes itself — Standing, Recognition and Disclosure.

Undertakings

No tuition fee and no examination fee. Verification free to every enquirer, for ever. The register of instruments is the last thing that would be switched off — Charges.

Set in Bodoni Moda, Cinzel, Inter and Amiri · kept against the Hijrī calendar · written in English and in Arabic, each authored rather than translated
