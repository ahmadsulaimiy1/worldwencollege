What this is

A distance-learning college, not an online school. The difference is the whole of our method.

An online school sells access to video. A college enrols you: it places you on a rung by assessment, teaches you a named text, examines you on it, and confers standing when you pass. You are not a subscriber. You are a student of a house, and the house is answerable for what it says you know.

We have no campus. That is a method, not a shortage — the same method by which Al-Azhar has taught intisāban for a century and the University of London has examined externally since 1858. What a campus gives a student is a teacher, a text and an examination. All three travel.

What does not travel is a claim nobody can check. So this College publishes its syllabi, its fees, its examination standards and the chains its teachers hold, before you pay anything — and we do not name an award we cannot confer, nor a partner we cannot evidence.

On what came before

This College is the successor to a Qur’ān and Arabic programme begun on 22 February 2020, which taught more than 1,550 students across 22 countries before it was suspended through 2022 and 2023. That suspension is stated here rather than tidied away: it is the reason the College has been rebuilt around teachers it can form rather than one teacher it cannot replace. The full audit of the predecessor site, including everything that was wrong with it, is kept on file.

The standard

The RUSŪKH Standard.

Six commitments, each of which generates a mechanism rather than a sentiment. Every one of them costs us something — that is how you can tell they are real.

R Rooted

Nothing is taught without a text behind it a student can open — a matn, an āyah, a sanad. Our syllabi name their books.

U Upright

Conduct is a condition of award, and the same condition binds the teacher as the student. Integrity is undertaken in writing at enrolment.

S Sequenced

Every subject is a ladder with a named rung below and above it. No student stands on a rung they have not earned, and placement is by assessment, never by self-declaration.

Ū Unbroken

Where a chain exists we transmit it and name it on the document. Where none exists, we say so plainly rather than implying one.

K Known

Every student is known by a teacher by name. Ḥalaqāt are capped at twelve so this is true rather than advertised.

H Held

Every award is held to an examination and every examination to a published standard. A certificate anyone can obtain is worth what anyone paid for it.

Why some rungs say Term II

Because they are not staffed yet, and we will not enrol a student into a ḥalaqah that does not exist. Nothing on this site is offered that cannot be begun on the day you read it — where a programme opens later, it says when, and you can register interest rather than pay for a place in a queue.

The certification ladder

Four awards, examined in writing and aloud, at a 70% pass mark on both. They exist for one purpose above all others: people who hold real knowledge and no paper that any institution will accept.

Prior learning is recognised. A student who read for years in a traditional madrasah may enter at I’dādiyyah or Thānawiyyah directly, by examination and verification of their study history. That door is the reason this College exists.

And what we will not say

We do not offer degrees. A BA or MA requires accreditation this College does not hold, and a claimed accreditation is how our predecessor lost its credibility.

We do not describe our awards as “internationally recognised.” We state precisely what an award is, who examined it, and what chain it carries — and we leave every receiving institution to make its own judgement, which it was always going to do anyway.

We name no partner university we cannot evidence with a signed agreement, and at the time of writing there is none to name.

An ijāzah bi-sanad muttaṣil needs no state’s permission to be prestigious. It has been the credential of this tradition for fourteen centuries, we can confer it honestly on our own authority, and it happens to be the most valuable thing we have.

Flexibility, as mechanisms

  • Pause, don’t drop. Suspend for up to two terms and return to the same rung — no penalty, no re-application.
  • Switch tier up or down at any month boundary.
  • Pay per juz’ on the modular Ḥifẓ track.
  • Family rate — 25% off every member after the first.
  • Teacher rate — 40% off for serving imams and madrasah teachers, because they multiply everything we teach them.

Kafālah — who pays for whom

Diaspora and Gulf students pay an international band. We say plainly what that money does: it carries the sponsored seats in Nigeria.

The College aims for one in five of every cohort on full or partial kafālah, and will publish the real figure each term rather than the target. Zakāh is held in a segregated fund, disbursed only to eligible categories, and accounted separately — that is a religious obligation before it is an administrative one.
