Charges

Instruction Without Fee

The College charges no tuition. It charges no one to sit an examination, to be re-examined, to appeal, or to obtain the record of what they attained. What it charges for is the engrossed instrument — the sealed certificate, the designed report sheet, the identity card, the instrument of ijāzah — and the schedule of those charges is published here in full.

What carries no charge The schedule of charges
The principle

The record is free. The artefact is bought.

A student is never charged to obtain the evidence of their own education. That is not a discount; it is a condition of the College being honest. Every graduate holds a verifiable digital instrument and a digital transcript, issued without charge, on request, always — including to a student whose registration lapsed, and including to a student who has never paid this College a naira.

What costs money is the object: paper of a particular weight, a seal, engrossing by hand, a photograph laminated into a card, a document couriered under cover to a registrar in another country. Those are real costs and the College declines to pretend otherwise, or to fold them into a tuition fee that would fall on people who never asked for them.

No instrument is withheld for non-payment of a charge. A student who cannot pay for an engrossed certificate still holds the digital one, still appears in the register, and is still verifiable by any enquirer for ever. Nothing about their standing changes.

The digital certificate and transcript are not lesser documents. Each bears a unique reference number and resolves against the College’s public register, which is the award itself; the engrossed paper expresses it. A registrar who needs to confirm a qualification needs the register, not the seal.

Why the two columns differ

Charges outside Nigeria are set above the naira equivalent, deliberately and openly. A graduate in London or Doha paying for an engrossed certificate is meeting part of the cost of one issued in Ilorin or Kano at no charge at all. The College would rather state that plainly than disguise it in an exchange rate.

Payment

Currencies, instalments and reductions.

Currencies accepted

Naira and United States dollars are the two quoted currencies. Payment is also accepted in pounds sterling, euro, Saudi riyal, UAE dirham, Canadian dollar and Ghanaian cedi at the prevailing rate on the date of payment, which is stated on the receipt with the rate applied. The College does not profit from a conversion and does not hold a spread.

Instalments

Any charge above ₦10,000 or $20 may be paid in up to three monthly instalments on request to the Bursary, without interest and without a fee for the arrangement. The instrument is engrossed on the final instalment; the digital instrument, as always, issues immediately and does not wait.

Reductions

Twenty-five per cent for each member of a household after the first. Forty per cent for imāms and for teachers serving in recognised institutions. The reductions apply to every charge in the schedule and are applied at the point of invoice, not on application.

Where a student cannot pay

The zakāh fund meets the charges of students falling within the eligible categories, in full. Application is to the Bursary and is decided by the Bursary alone, without reference to the faculty who teach or examine the student (Reg. 9).

No charge in this schedule is a condition of study, of examination, of progression, or of an award. Every one of them buys an object. A student who buys none of them graduates on exactly the same terms as a student who buys all of them.

How it is funded

A college that charges no tuition must say how it pays its teachers.

Anything else is a promise with no mechanism behind it, and the reader is entitled to ask.

The Foundation

The Ibn Ibrāhīm Sulaimiy Foundation is the charitable arm of the College and meets the greater part of the cost of teaching. It is accounted for separately from the College and publishes what it received and what it spent at the close of each session.

Principal source

Sponsorship — كفالة طالب

A sponsor meets the cost of a named student for a session and receives that student’s report at the close of each semester. Sponsorship funds teaching, and a sponsor acquires no standing whatever in an academic decision about the student they sponsor.

Endowment — وقف

Gifts held as capital, of which only the yield is spent. An endowment is the only source on this list that does not have to be raised again next year, and building one is the College’s standing financial objective.

Long-term
The risk, stated

A college funded by charity rather than by fees is exposed to a risk a fee-charging college is not: if the giving falls, the teaching is what is at stake. The College therefore does three things rather than assert good intentions. It publishes what it received and spent each session. It commits to teaching only what it can staff for a full session in advance — which is why programmes appear as forthcoming rather than opening on hope. And it holds the register of instruments under the continuity provisions at Standing and Disclosure, so that a graduate’s award survives the College’s worst year whatever else does not.
