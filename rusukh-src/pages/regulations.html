The Manual

Academic Regulations

The regulations in force for the session 1448 / 2026–27, governing the measure of study, the demonstration of attainment, examination, grading, and the record the College keeps of each student. They are published because a standard a student cannot read is a standard the College can move.

The standard of attainment Standing and disclosure
Regulation 1

The governing standard.

One sentence governs the whole of what follows, and each of its four clauses constrains the other three.

A fixed standard, a pace the student can keep, progression on what has been demonstrated, and a service that differs where the standard does not.

Reg. 1.1

The standard is fixed

The syllabus, the examination and the evidence required for an award are identical for every student of that programme, on every mode of instruction, and in every country. A mode of instruction may not be created by removing content. A route that cannot carry the whole syllabus is not a shorter route to the same award; it is a different programme, and is named and certificated as one.

Reg. 1.2

The pace is the student’s, within limits

Employment, family, travel, Ramaḍān and the cost of bandwidth are ordinary conditions of the students this College serves, and an institution that ignores them is not serious about reaching them. In the Individual mode and throughout the Faculty of the Qur’ān, progression is by attainment and the calendar is the student’s, subject to Regulation 4. In the Class and Supervised modes the calendar is the cohort’s, because a class of twelve cannot be at twelve different points; a student who needs an individual calendar transfers mode at any monthly boundary.

Reg. 1.3

Progression is on demonstrated attainment

No student advances to a subsequent level, portion or stage on the ground of time served, fees paid or attendance recorded. Advancement follows a demonstration made to a member of faculty under Regulation 3 and entered in the record.

Reg. 1.4

What money may and may not obtain

The College charges no tuition and no examination fee, so the ordinary answer to what money buys here is: nothing. Its charges are for engrossed instruments — a sealed certificate, a designed report sheet, an identity card, an instrument of ijāzah — and each of those buys an object and not a standing. Money does not obtain a mark, an examination outcome, a level, a stage, an award or an ijāzah, and the College is arranged so that it cannot: see Regulation 9.2 and the schedule at Charges.

Modes of instruction differ in the size of the class and the frequency of individual supervision. They are allocated by the faculty on the student’s attainment and need. They are not sold.

What the College does not claim

The College does not claim that instruction at a distance is the equivalent of sitting before a teacher. It is not. It is reachable, which for the great majority of those it teaches is the difference between this education and none at all. That claim is sufficient, and it has the advantage of being true.

Regulation 2

The measure of a programme.

A programme is defined by the body of work it requires, not by the number of months a particular student takes to complete it. The months are an outcome of pace; the body of work is the programme.

Notional hours of study

Every programme is stated in notional hours of study: one hour of the total effort of a typical student — live class, recorded lesson, reading, drill, memorisation, murājaʿah, written work and examination taken together. The figure is a description of the work, not a promise about any individual, who may need more or fewer.

The College states hours rather than credit units deliberately. Credit unit and credit hour carry defined regulatory meanings in Nigeria and elsewhere, and to use them would imply a recognition the College does not hold. The hour states the same information without borrowing an authority it has not earned.

How the figures are arrived at

A semester is sixteen teaching weeks. A programme in the Class mode is taught in three scheduled classes a week of ninety minutes, giving seventy-two hours of instruction, against which the syllabus sets independent study, written work and examination in the proportion published with it. One semester of full study is therefore reckoned at 200 notional hours, or about twelve to thirteen hours a week.

Memorisation is not measured in hours at all. It is measured in portions retained, under Regulation 3.2, and hours are recorded against it only for the planning of a student’s week and for the volume column of the transcript.

Regulation 3

Attainment, and what counts as demonstrating it.

Reg. 3.1

Attainment defined

A student has attained a competence when, examined by a qualified member of faculty, they perform it to the published criterion, on demand, without notice, and after an interval. Each of those four conditions excludes something: a criterion set after the fact; a rehearsed performance; a scheduled performance prepared for; and the cramming which is precisely what makes memorisation evaporate.

Reg. 3.2

The Rule of Retention

A portion of the Qur’ān, or a competence in Arabic, is recorded as held only where it survives unannounced re-examination after a defined interval. Most institutions record what a student reached; this College records what a student retains, and the two are not the same number.

The rule is the strongest academic claim the College makes and the easiest to fake, and it is therefore the most tightly evidenced: every retention examination is timed, recorded, and attributed in the record to the member of faculty who conducted it. The schedule of returns is set out under Regulation 3.3 and is generated by the College, not left to the student’s intention.

Reg. 3.3

The Five Returns — المراجعات الخمس

The classical cycle distinguishes sabaq, the new portion; sabqī, the recently memorised and still fragile; and manzil, the consolidated corpus. The weakness in practice is that the middle stage is left to the student’s discretion, and that is exactly where memorisation is lost. The College therefore fixes it to a schedule of expanding returns — at one day, three days, seven days, twenty-one days and sixty days after a portion is first heard sound.

A portion that survives all five returns joins the manzil and is thereafter sampled without notice under Regulation 3.2. A portion that fails any return re-enters at the first. The intervals expand rather than repeat because recall that is just difficult enough is what consolidates a memory; a portion revised while it is still effortless teaches the student very little.

Reg. 3.4

What a failed examination means

A failure is a diagnosis, not a verdict. It produces a written statement of what was missing and a plan directed at it, issued to the student and to the supervising member of faculty within five working days.

Re-examination is without charge, and the number of attempts is not limited by fee. A College that charges for a re-examination has made a student’s difficulty into a source of income and has given itself an interest in their failure. The permitted number of attempts at each stage is set in the regulations of the programme, is issued with the syllabus, and is an academic limit only.

Regulation 4

Registration, leave, dormancy and lapse.

A flexibility with no end is not flexibility; it is an abandoned registration the institution has not had the honesty to close. Every programme therefore carries the following limits, published before a student enrols.

ProvisionThe rule
Minimum sustained pace Three notional hours a week, averaged over any eight-week period.
Maximum period of registration Two and a half times the normal duration of the programme.
Approved leave Up to two consecutive semesters, in blocks, on application to the Registry and without penalty or fee. The clock stops; the student resumes at the same point of the programme and no fresh application is required.
Dormancy Sixteen weeks below the minimum pace without approved leave: the Registry writes to the student, and where there is no reply the registration is recorded dormant. Dormancy is a record of fact, not a penalty.
Re-entry Permitted. Attainment already demonstrated is not re-examined unless the interval makes it unsafe to rely on, in which case the College says which portions it must hear again and why.
Lapse At the maximum period the registration closes and a Statement of Results is issued for what was completed.

A student who runs out of time still receives a truthful record of what they attained. Nothing is confiscated for lateness, and no student is ever charged to obtain the evidence of their own education.

Regulation 5

Examination and its instruments.

Assessment and demonstration are separated on purpose. Assessment is frequent and cheap and tells a student where they stand. A demonstration is a gate: infrequent, human, unannounced, and entered in the record.

An automatic mark is never a gate

An instrument marked by machine may inform a decision on attainment. It may never constitute one. Every gate in the College is heard or read by a qualified human being who is named in the record against the outcome.

Identity at the gate

Identity is confirmed at every gate by a member of faculty who has taught or examined the candidate before, and by a government-issued document before the final examination of any programme.

No surveillance software

The College uses no webcam monitoring, no screen recording, no keystroke analysis and no browser lockdown. Such systems fail hardest on inexpensive devices and poor connections — that is, on precisely the students this College exists for — and they buy less integrity than five minutes of oral examination. Integrity here is a matter of design, not of surveillance.

Papers rotate

No gate uses a fixed paper. Questions are drawn from a bank revised each session, and the portions set in a recitation or retention examination are chosen by the examiner at the sitting.

Moderation

Every summative gate is sampled by a second qualified examiner — one in ten scripts or five scripts, whichever is the greater. A disagreement of more than one band requires the whole cohort to be marked again.

Appeal

A candidate may appeal the outcome of any gate within twenty-one days. The appeal is heard by a person who took no part in the original decision, and the outcome is written, reasoned and recorded. Appeals are free. Academic judgement alone cannot be appealed; procedure, evidence, partiality and the application of the published criteria can.

Examinations are conducted remotely under live supervision by a member of the College. The College does not require a candidate to travel to a physical centre for any examination, because a distance-learning college that did so would be misdescribing itself.

Regulation 6

Grading.

Marks are recorded against a criterion published before the examination, and never against the performance of the rest of the cohort.

Lam yablughلم يبلغ Below 70Not yet — issued with a diagnosis and a plan under Reg. 3.4.

The pass standard of the College is seventy per cent. Where a programme is examined by both written paper and oral viva, each must be passed independently, and a mark in one does not compensate for a failure in the other.

The certificate carries the band, not the percentage

The mark is recorded in full in the student’s record and on the transcript. It does not appear on the face of a certificate or an instrument of ijāzah, because a percentage claims a precision that the oral examination of recitation and of language does not possess, and because it invites a comparison between students which Regulation 1 exists to prevent.

Regulation 7

Adjustments for those who need them.

A student may ask for an adjustment at any point, before or during a programme, and need not have asked at admission. The request is decided by the Registry together with a member of the Academic Council, and never by the student’s own teacher — no one should have to explain their circumstances to the person who will examine them.

The governing distinction is short. An adjustment changes how a student reaches the standard. It never changes the standard. Additional time, an audio-only route, an alternative format, a rescheduled sitting, a longer maximum period of registration, or a different instrument measuring the same competence, are all adjustments. A lowered criterion is not an adjustment; it is a different qualification, and it would be dishonest to award the same certificate for it.

Nothing is recorded on the instrument

No adjustment appears on any certificate, on any transcript, or against any entry in the register of instruments. The award states what the holder demonstrated. How they were enabled to demonstrate it is their own affair.

Adjustments are without charge, are recorded in the student’s file with the reason and the decision, and are reviewed with the student at each semester boundary rather than assumed to be permanent or assumed to have ended.

Regulation 8

The record and the transcript.

A transcript is not a list of marks. It is a document a stranger in another country must be able to evaluate without telephoning the College.

What every transcript carries

  • The legal name of the College, its standing stated as it truly is, and the address of the verification service
  • The student’s name and reference number
  • The programme, the mode of instruction and the pace followed
  • For each course: its code, its title in Arabic and in English, the level, the notional hours, the band, the mark, the number of attempts and the date
  • Cumulative notional hours
  • The grading scale, printed on the document itself
  • Progression and completion status, the date of issue, the reference number of the instrument, and the verification address

The scale is printed on every transcript because a registrar in Riyadh or Toronto cannot look up a scale they have never met, and a transcript that assumes they can is a transcript that will be set aside.

What may never be printed on it

Attendance at live classes is recorded and printed, as sessions attended against sessions scheduled. Notional hours are accrued against submitted evidence of work, and are printed.

Logins, time spent on a page, the proportion of a recording watched and any similar measure are not attendance, are not learning, and appear on no certificate and no transcript. They are proxies, and a proxy printed on an academic record is an untruth with a number attached. Their only legitimate use is support: a student who has stopped is a student to write to, not a student to penalise.

The record is a trust

The record is held on the student’s behalf. It is not a marketing asset, it is not sold, and it is not mined. It is retained under the College’s provisions on information with one exception: the entry in the register of instruments is permanent, because an award that can be deleted cannot be verified.

Regulation 9

The independence of examiners.

The whole academic model rests on an examiner’s willingness to write lam yablugh against the attempt of a student the College would rather keep — a sponsored student, a founder’s relation, a name it would like on its register. Good intentions will not hold that. Structure must.

Reg. 9.1

Separation of the examiner from the teacher

The oral examination for every award of the College is conducted by a member of faculty other than the candidate’s own teacher. A dissertation is read by the supervisor and by a second examiner.

Reg. 9.2

The examiner cannot see who is paying, or who is sponsored

At the point of examination the examiner is not shown the candidate’s mode of instruction, sponsor, zakāh standing, outstanding charges or state of payment. Not ought not to be shown — is not shown: the examination record carries no such field. This matters more in a college funded by giving than in one funded by fees, because a sponsor is a person with a name and a fee is not.

Reg. 9.3

No target for results

No pass rate, completion rate or progression target exists anywhere in the College, in any plan, forecast, budget or report. A forecast that assumes a rate of passing has created a reason to produce one. Examination outcomes are not used in the review of a member of faculty, which draws on observation, agreement at moderation, the account of students, and professional development.

Reg. 9.4

No commercial authority over an academic outcome

The outcome of a gate may be altered only by moderation or by appeal, both of which are academic processes conducted by academic officers. Not by the Bursary, not by the Registry, not by the Rector, and not by the Founder. Any member of the College may report pressure on an academic outcome directly to the Academic Council, and the College may take no adverse action in consequence of a report made in good faith.

Regulation 10

Citation, amendment and the version in force.

Citation

These regulations are cited by number — Reg. 3.2 — in offers of admission, in syllabi, in decisions of the Registry, in the outcome of an appeal, and in correspondence with students. A decision that cites no regulation is a decision a student cannot examine.

Amendment

Amendment is by the Academic Council alone, in writing, with the date of the decision and the date from which it takes effect. No amendment operates against a student already registered where it would raise the standard of an award they are working toward.

The version in force

The version published here is the version in force. Superseded versions are retained by the Registry and are issued on request to any student whose programme was governed by one of them.

Where the regulations are silent

Where these regulations do not provide for a case, the Academic Council decides it, records the decision with its reasons, and considers at the following session whether the case is general enough to be written in.
