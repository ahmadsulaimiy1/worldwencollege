Faculties

Four faculties. Every one of them a ladder of named books.

This College teaches the classical matn sequence — the most rigorously tested curriculum in the history of this tradition — and it teaches it the way it was meant to be taught: memorised, recited back, and explained under a teacher who can tell when you are guessing.

The method

Why we teach texts rather than topics.

Most online Islamic education teaches subjects: a course on Tajwīd, a course on Fiqh, a course on the life of the Prophet ﷺ. You finish it and there is nothing above it, because a topic has no next rung.

The traditional madrasah teaches texts. You memorise al-Ājurrūmiyyah. Then you read Qaṭr al-Nadā, which assumes it. Then the Alfiyyah, which assumes both. Each book is a rung, each rung is public, and a student anywhere in the world can be asked which books they have read and answer in one sentence that means something precise.

This is why traditional students are formidable and why we build on it. It also solves the problem every adult learner actually has — where do I start and what comes next — because the answer is a shelf, in order.

What this means for you in practice

You will be asked to memorise. Not everything — but the mutūn are memorised, because a text you carry is a text you can think with and a text on a shelf is a text you will look up twice and forget. You will also be examined aloud, at every level, which is the part that cannot be bought, borrowed or generated.

Kulliyyah I

Kulliyyat al-Qur’ān — كلية القرآن

The flagship. Eleven rungs from the articulation of a single letter to an ijāzah in the ten qirā’āt with a named chain.

Foundation · Q0–Q2

Al-Qā’idah al-Nūrāniyyah, then correction of the reading, then Tajwīd on Tuḥfat al-Aṭfāl and al-Muqaddimah al-Jazariyyah — both memorised.

Memorisation · Q3–Q4

Three tracks — Regular, Intensive and Modular (per juz’) — then Murāja’ah wa Itqān to bring a complete memorisation to examinable standard.

The chain · Q5–Q6

Ijāzah in Ḥafṣ ‘an ‘Āṣim, then the ten qirā’āt — ṣughrā by al-Shāṭibiyyah and al-Durrah, kubrā by Ṭayyibat al-Nashr.

On the madhhab

Fiqh is taught in the Mālikī stream first, because that is the madhhab of the overwhelming majority of the people this College serves, and because almost no online institution serves it properly. Shāfi‘ī and Ḥanafī streams will open when a qualified teacher for each has been appointed — named on this site, with the books they will teach — and not one term earlier.

The most important programme here

I’dād al-Mu’allim is not one programme among five. Every commitment this College has made — twelve to a ḥalaqah, oral examinations at every level, one-to-one ijāzah work — is teacher-intensive, and a college founded on one exceptional scholar cannot be run on one. This is how one teacher becomes forty. It is the constraint on everything else we have promised, and the board has been told so in writing.
