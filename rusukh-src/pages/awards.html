Recognition of prior study

Candidates who have completed a course of study at a madrasah or other institution without receiving formal certification may be admitted directly to examination at the I’dādiyyah or Thānawiyyah stage. The Registry assesses such applications on a documented account of where, with whom and in which texts the candidate studied, together with the full written and oral examination for the stage sought. Admission under this provision confers no exemption from examination.

Whose certificate this is

These are awards of Al-Madinah International College, conferred on its own authority. They are not the certificates of the National Board for Arabic and Islamic Studies and are not represented as such. A candidate whose purpose is the external school-stage certificate should read the two routes before applying.

Candidates at these stages are ordinarily under eighteen. The conditions under which the College teaches a minor — no member of faculty alone with a child, sessions recorded and open to the guardian, vetting before first contact, and a Safeguarding Officer outside the teaching line — are published in full at Safeguarding and the Conduct of Teaching.

Examination

Regulations of examination.

Standard

The pass mark is seventy per cent. Where a programme is examined by both written paper and oral viva, each must be passed independently; a mark in one does not compensate for a failure in the other.

Oral examination

An oral examination is required for every award of the College without exception, and is conducted by a member of faculty other than the candidate’s own teacher.

Identification

Candidates present a government-issued identity document before the final examination of any programme. Proof of age is required at registration for the graded certificates.

Conduct of examinations

Examinations are conducted remotely under live supervision. The Examinations Office assigns candidates to sittings and issues the timetable in advance of each semester’s examinations.

Re-examination

A candidate who fails may be re-examined at the following sitting, without charge. A failure is issued with a written statement of what was missing and a plan directed at it. The permitted number of attempts is set in the regulations of each programme, is issued with the syllabus, and is an academic limit and not a financial one (Reg. 3.4).

Moderation and appeal

Every summative examination is sampled by a second qualified examiner — one in ten scripts or five, whichever is greater. A candidate may appeal an outcome within twenty-one days, to a person who took no part in the original decision, and appeals are free (Reg. 5). Moderation is presently internal to the College, which is stated at Standing and Disclosure.

Issue of instruments

Certificates are issued within seven working days of the confirmation of results, in digital form with the transcript, and in printed form on request. The mode of study is recorded on the instrument.

All examination is overseen by the Academic Council (المجلس العلمي), which approves the syllabi, appoints examiners and confirms results. The grading bands, the standard of attainment, the instruments which constitute a gate and the independence of examiners are set out in full in the Academic Regulations.

Ijāzah

Conditions governing the grant of ijāzah.

Ijāzah bi-sanad muttaṣil is a licence to transmit, carrying a chain of transmission from the conferring teacher to the Prophet ﷺ. The following conditions govern its grant by this College.

Conditions precedent — before a narration is offered at all

The authority of an ijāzah is the chain, not the institution, and a college cannot manufacture one. This College therefore opens no narration to candidates, and advertises none, until every one of the following holds and is evidenced: a member of faculty holding a connected chain in that narration is appointed; the instrument evidencing his chain has been examined by the Academic Council; the narration is declared by name; a written method of examination for it exists and is issued with the syllabus; and the chain is entered in the Register of Asānīd and published.

Where any of the five is not yet met, the narration is shown as forthcoming and no student is admitted to it, whatever they offer to pay. The narrations presently open are those listed in the Register.

Authority to confer

Ijāzah is conferred only by a member of faculty who himself holds a connected chain in the narration being conferred.

Regulation 1

Statement of the chain

The instrument states the chain of transmission in full, naming each transmitter in order, together with the narration conferred and the date and place of the final recitation.

Regulation 2

Complete recitation

The candidate recites the Qur’ān in its entirety to the conferring teacher, conducted live. Recordings are not accepted in fulfilment of this condition.

Regulation 3

Verification

Every instrument bears a reference number against which it may be verified by any party, without charge and without reference to the holder.

Regulation 4

Register of Asānīd

The chains held by the faculty, the transmitters through whom they came, and the members of faculty authorised to confer each, are entered in the Register of Asānīd and published.

The instruments

What an instrument of this College states.

A certificate that is silent about its limits invites the reader to assume the maximum. Every instrument issued here therefore states what it attests and what it does not.

On the face of every instrument

  • The name of the holder, and the name of the College
  • The award, the programme and the level
  • The notional hours of study the programme carries
  • The competences attested — what the holder was seen to do
  • The narration (riwāyah) where the award concerns recitation
  • The band, and the date of the award
  • A unique reference number and the address at which it may be verified
  • A statement of what the instrument does not certify

A Certificate of Attendance states on its face that it attests attendance and submission and makes no claim of attainment. A Qur’ānic memorisation certificate states the portion, the narration, the band and the date of the last retention examination — and states that it is not an ijāzah and carries no chain of transmission. Only the instrument of ijāzah carries a chain.

The register is the award

The paper expresses the award. The public register of instruments is the award. It takes a reference number and returns whether the instrument is valid, what it attests, to whom, when it was issued, and whether it has been revoked — and nothing else.

It carries no advertising, requires no account, and is free to every enquirer for ever. It is designed to outlive the rest of the College, and the provisions that make that possible are published at Continuity of the register.

The transcript

Every award is issued with a transcript recording each course, its notional hours, the band, the mark, the number of attempts and the date — with the grading scale printed on the document itself, because a registrar abroad cannot look up a scale they have never met. Transcripts and digital certificates are issued without charge, on request, always, including to a student whose registration lapsed.

Academic conduct

The academic undertaking.

Every student signs an undertaking at matriculation covering honesty in examination, the accuracy of documents submitted to the Registry, and proper conduct toward faculty and fellow students. Members of faculty are bound by the same undertaking.

Breach is a matter for the Academic Council. Where an award has been obtained by misrepresentation, the Council may revoke it and record the revocation against the reference number, which the verification service will report.
