Students under eighteen

Safeguarding and the Conduct of Teaching

The College teaches children as young as ten in the graded certificates. It therefore states, before any parent enrols one, the conditions under which a minor is taught here, who is accountable for them, and how a concern is raised.

How to raise a concern Arrangements between men and women
The position

Distance makes this harder, not easier.

In a school there is a corridor, an open door, and a colleague walking past. At a distance there is none of that, and an institution which puts an unvetted adult alone in a private call with a child has created the very conditions that safeguarding exists to prevent. The controls must therefore be written down and made structural rather than left to the good character of teachers, however good that character is.

The College holds that it may not serve children before it can protect them, whatever that costs it in enrolment. The provisions below are conditions of teaching a minor at Al-Madinah International College, not aspirations; where one of them cannot be met for a particular student, that student is not taught until it can.

The binding limitation

No member of faculty is ever alone with a student under eighteen in a private session. Minors are taught in class, or with a parent or guardian present, or with a second member of the College present. The Individual and Ijāzah modes are accordingly closed to students under eighteen except where a parent or guardian attends the session or the College appoints a second member of faculty to it, which it does without charge on request.

Provisions

The conditions under which a minor is taught.

ProvisionThe rule in force
No private session No adult is alone with a minor. Group class, a guardian present, or a second member of the College present. Without exception.
Every session recorded Every session in which a minor takes part is recorded and retained for twelve months, and is available to the parent or guardian on request without a reason being given.
No private channel No contact between a member of the College and a minor by personal telephone, personal electronic mail, or any messaging service outside the College’s own systems. Correspondence with a minor copies the guardian.
Vetting before first contact Anyone who teaches or examines a minor is identity-verified, referenced by two persons known to the College, and cleared to the standard of the jurisdiction in which they work. The clearance is recorded before the first session, not after.
Consent of the guardian Recorded at admission. The guardian has standing access to the student’s record, to the recordings, and to the supervising member of faculty.
Training No one teaches a minor without recorded training in these provisions, renewed each session.
Minimum information Information about a minor is collected to the minimum the teaching requires. It is never used for marketing and never used for analysis of any kind.
Proof of age Required at registration for the graded certificates. Where age is unknown it is not assumed: the student is treated as a minor until it is established.

These provisions bind members of faculty, examiners, the Registry and any person engaged by the College in any capacity, including on a temporary appointment. Breach is a matter for the Board of Trustees rather than for the academic line, and is not dealt with by the person’s own head of faculty.

Raising a concern

The route, and why it does not run through the faculty.

A concern about a teacher must never have to travel through that teacher’s colleague, or through the office that appointed and pays them. The College therefore appoints a Safeguarding Officer (مسؤول حماية الطلاب) who reports to the Board of Trustees and stands outside the teaching line altogether.

The Officer is reachable directly by a student, by a parent or guardian, or by any member of the College, in Arabic or in English, without going through the Registry and without telling anyone else first.

  • A concern is acknowledged within one working day
  • The person complained of is not told who raised it
  • Where a child may be at risk the College acts first and investigates second
  • The outcome is given in writing to the person who raised it
  • No adverse action is taken against anyone who raises a concern in good faith, including a member of faculty

In an emergency, contact the emergency services of your own country first. Tell the College afterwards. Nothing in these provisions asks a parent to wait.

A limit the College states plainly

The duty of this College is to refer competently, not to counsel. Its members are teachers, not clinicians, and an institution that pretends otherwise does harm while believing it is helping. Where a student needs care the College cannot give, it says so and helps them reach someone who can.

Arrangements

Arrangements between men and women.

For a large part of the student body this settles whether enrolment is possible at all. It is therefore published in advance rather than left to be discovered in the third week of a programme.

i

Women are taught by women where the programme requires supervision

Programmes for women are taught by women members of faculty wherever a qualified teacher is available, and widening that availability is a standing priority in appointment rather than an aspiration. Where no qualified woman holds the chain or the specialism sought, the College says so at the point of application instead of after admission.

ii

No private individual session between the sexes by default

An individual live session between a teacher and a student of the opposite sex is not scheduled as a matter of course. Where one is academically necessary the student chooses between a teacher of the same sex, the presence of a third person, or a recorded session. The choice belongs to the student and costs nothing.

iii

Separate classes where numbers allow

Group classes are offered in separate sections for men and for women wherever demand supports it, and the composition of the section is stated in the offer of a place, before matriculation.

iv

The camera is never required

Full participation, full examination and every gate of every programme are reachable by audio alone. A student who does not appear on video is not disadvantaged in any examination, and identity at the gate is confirmed by other means. This is also the correct decision for a college whose students pay for their own bandwidth; the two requirements happen to agree.

v

Adab is taught, not merely imposed

The bearing expected in a session is the bearing expected of a student of knowledge, and it is expected of the faculty first. It forms part of what is taught rather than a list of prohibitions posted at the students.

The College states its own arrangements and does not characterise the arrangements of any other institution.

Adult students

Protection does not end at eighteen.

A vulnerable adult, a student in distress and a student disclosing harm are all foreseeable in an institution of any size, and a college whose students are met only through a screen may be the last party to notice. The route at Raising a concern serves adult students on the same terms, and the same limit is stated: the College refers competently and does not counsel.

Where a member of faculty has reason to believe a student is at risk, the duty is to inform the Safeguarding Officer the same day. It is not for a teacher to decide alone whether a concern is serious enough to pass on.
