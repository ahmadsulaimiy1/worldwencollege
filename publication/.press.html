Preface

What this volume is, and what it is not

Worldwide English College Press is an imprint, not a company.

It has no separate legal personality, no appointed staff, no ISBN publisher prefix and no distribution agreement. It has published 9 artefacts, all of them derived from one academic programme. This volume states that in its first constitution rather than its last, because a publishing house that opens by describing its ambitions and closes by admitting its position has the two the wrong way round.

What the Press does have is a body of rules that can be checked. Of the 99 clauses in Part I, 24 are enforced by a named test that fails the build when they are broken. That number is not high because software cannot check taste; it is not low because the clauses that matter most — truth in public claims, contrast, structural accessibility, artefact integrity — are exactly the ones a machine can check.

ForceClauses What it means
Enforced24A named test fails the build if this clause is broken. The clause is a property of the artefact, not advice about it.
Observed20Followed, and the following is visible in the source. Nothing would stop a future editor departing from it.
Adopted45A decision that has been taken and applies, with no artefact yet to check it against.
For adoption10Drafted here and not yet decided by anyone with the standing to decide it. It binds nothing until it is.

The absolute rule, restated because it governs everything after it

Nothing is invented. Not accreditation, partnerships, rankings, statistics, history, governance, staff, competencies, student outcomes or institutional achievements. Where a decision belongs to the institution, it is recorded as an institutional decision and left undecided in print. Where a figure cannot be evidenced, it is not printed. Every claim in every publication of this Press is intended to survive being checked by someone who does not trust it.

I

Part One

The Constitutions

Twenty-three constitutions governing every publication the College will ever issue. Each clause declares how firmly it binds, and the clauses that claim enforcement name the test that enforces them.

Constitution 1 of 23

Institutional Publishing Constitution

What the Press is, and what it may say it is.

  1. Adopted Worldwide English College Press is an imprint of Worldwide English College, London Campus. It has no separate legal personality, no appointed staff, no ISBN publisher prefix and no distribution agreement. No publication may imply otherwise.
  2. Enforced No publication may state or imply that the College is accredited, recognised, validated or approved by any awarding body, government department or quality-assurance agency. Where the question could arise, the publication states plainly that it is not. tests/published-claims.test.mjs
  3. Enforced No publication may print a figure the academic database does not support unless the same publication also states, in the same place, what is designed and what is delivered. tests/published-claims.test.mjs
  4. Adopted The Press publishes in English and in Arabic. A claim corrected in one language is corrected in every language in the same revision — not the next one.
  5. Enforced Every language edition of a public claim is checked, not only the English. tests/published-claims.test.mjs
  6. For adoption An imprint page naming a publisher, a place of publication and a year is required for legal deposit in most jurisdictions. The Press states place and year and cannot state a registered publisher until one exists.

Constitution 2 of 23

Editorial Constitution

What may be written, and by whom.

  1. Adopted Curriculum is authored by qualified academics and is reproduced by the Press verbatim. The Press may organise, typeset, index and cross-reference it. The Press may not write it, extend it, summarise it in place of it, or improve its wording.
  2. Enforced Every authored lesson body reaches the page in full. A parsing or layout change that silently drops curriculum fails the build. tests/curriculum-publication.test.mjs
  3. Observed Apparatus is extracted, never composed. An index entry, a cross-reference, a pull quote or a route must be derivable from text the curriculum already contains, and the derivation must be re-runnable by a reader.
  4. Enforced A pull quote appears verbatim in the lesson it cites. tests/publication-apparatus.test.mjs
  5. Observed Where an apparatus cannot be built honestly, nothing is printed and the reason is printed instead. The absence of a grammar route is stated on the page it would have occupied.
  6. Observed A caption is a claim. Any sentence describing what a figure shows is written from the rendered figure, never from memory of the data — two captions have already been withdrawn for failing this.

Constitution 3 of 23

Design Constitution

The house design, and what governs a departure from it.

  1. Adopted One design system serves every publication of the Press. A new title inherits the type, colour, ornament and page architecture of the house; it does not receive a design of its own.
  2. Observed Restraint is the house manner. Ornament is permitted where it marks a structural event — a cover, a divider, a colophon — and nowhere else.
  3. Enforced No element may exceed the page content box. A single over-wide element makes the engine shrink the entire document and every specified measurement becomes false. tests/publication-craft.test.mjs
  4. Enforced The median printed page is substantially full, and few pages are left mostly empty. White space is a decision, and an unfinished page is not one. tests/publication-craft.test.mjs
  5. Observed A design decision that cannot survive the curriculum changing underneath it is not a design decision. Figures, indexes and counts are generated, never transcribed.

Constitution 4 of 23

Typography Constitution

Two families, one measure, and a floor nothing goes below.

  1. Adopted Two families and no more: a transitional serif for continuous reading, a humanist sans for apparatus. The distinction is functional — a reader scanning for a stage should find it by texture before reading a word.
  2. Adopted Font stacks name only faces present on the systems the publication is opened on, with metric-compatible fallbacks in order. A flagship that renders in Times because a licensed face was assumed is not a flagship.
  3. Enforced Nothing is set below 5.5 pt as printed. This includes type inside figures, which scales with its viewBox and is where every past violation has been. tests/publication-craft.test.mjs
  4. Enforced Tracking and rule weights form small declared scales; sprawl is counted and fails. tests/publication-craft.test.mjs
  5. Enforced Runts are measured against the rendered width of the final word and held below a stated floor. The floor is not zero: hand-binding words in curriculum text is not the Press’s text to edit. tests/publication-craft.test.mjs
  6. Observed Orphans and widows are set on body prose; a heading never breaks from what follows it; a table repeats its column headings on every page it continues onto.

Constitution 5 of 23

Colour Constitution

Fourteen roles, six generated identities, one contrast floor.

  1. Adopted Every colour carries a stated role. A palette without roles is a mood board, and a designer picking prettily loses the argument by chapter three.
  2. Enforced Every ink holds at least 4.5:1 against the paper it is specified on. Royal Gold reaches 2.82:1 on the text paper and may therefore never carry type there; Bronze is the reading-safe gold. tests/publication.test.mjs
  3. Adopted Level identities are generated in a perceptual space at fixed lightness and chroma so that only hue varies. Hand-picking six hexes produced a 2.24× luminance spread and an ascent that sagged in the middle.
  4. Adopted Colour must survive production: distinguishable in greyscale, because a curriculum gets photocopied, and clear of the out-of-gamut blues that shift purple in CMYK.
  5. For adoption CMYK separation values are not yet specified. They require a press profile and a stock decision, and until a printer is appointed the Press publishes in sRGB and says so.

Constitution 6 of 23

Photography Constitution

What a photograph is for, and what it may never imply.

  1. Adopted Photography is editorial illustration. No photograph in any publication is a record of this College, its students or its premises, and none is captioned as though it were.
  2. Adopted Every photograph must be fully Islamically appropriate, modest, respectful, professional, internationally credible and commercially licensed. An image failing any one of these is not used, and a spent licence is not a reason to use it.
  3. Adopted One photographic direction per publication family. Images are graded to a duotone drawn from the colour system so that images by different photographers read as one commissioned series and belong to the design rather than sit on top of it.
  4. Enforced Every placed photograph clears 300 dpi at its printed size. tests/publication-craft.test.mjs
  5. Enforced Every placed photograph is credited with a resolvable licence reference, and the credits are counted against the images the edition actually places. tests/publication-craft.test.mjs
  6. Adopted No publication ever prints the words "sample image" or "placeholder". An image is licensed and placed, or it is absent.

Constitution 7 of 23

Illustration Constitution

Drawn, not licensed.

  1. Adopted The ornamental language of the Press is computed rather than drawn: hypotrochoid guilloché, eight-fold girih construction, and a crest, border, corner and fleuron system generated from its own geometry at render time. Nothing is a stock asset, a traced image or a licensed illustration.
  2. Enforced No drawn line falls below 0.25 pt, the practical floor for fine line-work on uncoated stock. tests/publication-craft.test.mjs
  3. Observed Ornament is parametric. A curve that does not close, or a stroke that scales twice, is a defect and not a style.

Constitution 8 of 23

Infographic Constitution

A figure that cannot become wrong was never saying anything.

  1. Adopted Every figure is measured from the source data at build time. Bar lengths are proportional to counts; nothing is drawn to a shape that looked good and then labelled.
  2. Adopted A figure with no variance to show is deleted, not redrawn. Two have been: one plotting six identical columns, one plotting four coincident flat lines under a caption the numbers contradicted.
  3. Enforced No figure label is clipped by its own frame, and no two labels on the same line are printed through each other. SVG reports neither. tests/publication-craft.test.mjs
  4. Observed An unflattering measurement is printed as measured. The assessment map prints an empty competency column in every edition, including the one written for accreditation panels.

Constitution 9 of 23

Iconography Constitution

One grid, one weight, one meaning per mark.

  1. Adopted Every icon is drawn on one 24-unit grid at one stroke weight with round caps, and inherits its colour from its context rather than carrying one.
  2. Observed An icon is judged at its printed size, not at the size it was drawn. Five of the nineteen stage icons were redrawn after inspection at 13 px.
  3. Observed A row of identical marks is noise wearing the costume of information. The stage tape is suppressed unless the stages it summarises actually differ.

Constitution 10 of 23

Accessibility Constitution

A publication nobody can read is not published.

  1. Adopted Every PDF is tagged, carries a document language, and carries a navigable outline.
  2. Enforced No heading level is skipped anywhere in any publication. tests/publication-craft.test.mjs
  3. Enforced Every table declares header scope and carries header cells. tests/publication-craft.test.mjs
  4. Observed Every photograph carries descriptive alternative text; every decorative ornament is hidden from assistive technology; every figure carries a label.
  5. Adopted A large-print variant is producible from the same source at any type scale, and is a build target rather than a separate design.
  6. For adoption PDF/UA conformance has not been validated by an external checker and reading order has not been tested with a screen reader. Both are checks not yet run, not defects found.

Constitution 11 of 23

Print Production Constitution

What a printer receives.

  1. Enforced The text block and the cover are separate files. A cover that is really page one of the text block is the clearest sign nobody involved has sent a book to press. tests/publication-craft.test.mjs
  2. Enforced The spine is calculated from the bound extent at a stated caliper, per edition. A guessed spine is a cover that wraps. tests/publication-craft.test.mjs
  3. Enforced The cover carries bleed on all four edges. tests/publication-craft.test.mjs
  4. Enforced A gutter allowance exists in the page setup, and margins are mirrored so the two text blocks of a spread sit symmetrically about the fold. tests/publication-craft.test.mjs
  5. Adopted Creep compensation is documented rather than applied: it depends on the signature scheme the printer chooses.
  6. Adopted No edition goes to a full run without a wet proof. Ink coverage, show-through and paper interaction are design intentions until a proof says otherwise.

Constitution 12 of 23

Digital Publishing Constitution

The editable edition is not a lesser edition.

  1. Adopted Every print publication has an editable companion carrying the same curriculum and the same apparatus. What the companion may lack is typographic set — drop caps, drawn ornament, chapter openers — never information.
  2. Enforced No curriculum text is present in one edition and missing from another. tests/publication-editions.test.mjs
  3. Observed Figures are rasterised for the editable edition at a resolution above screen need, and photographs are graded through the print filter before embedding, so the companion belongs to the same series.
  4. Adopted The digital companion and the public platform must agree. Where they cannot yet, the disagreement is recorded in the Bible, not resolved by editing whichever is easier to change.

Constitution 13 of 23

Copyright Constitution

Who owns what, stated once.

  1. Adopted Copyright in the curriculum and in the publication design vests in Worldwide English College. The imprint line, the year and the reservation of rights appear on the imprint page of every publication.
  2. Adopted Licensed photography is licensed, not owned. It is credited by reference, and a licence permitting editorial use does not permit use as a record of the institution.
  3. For adoption A rights-and-permissions contact and a policy on third-party reproduction requests are required before any publication is distributed outside the College. Neither exists.

Constitution 14 of 23

Licensing Constitution

Every asset traceable to its licence.

  1. Enforced Every placed photograph carries a licence reference in the colophon, and no reference is reused for two images. tests/publication-craft.test.mjs
  2. Observed A licence is recorded when it is acquired, not when the image is placed. Three references were once recoverable only from a session transcript, which is the state a rights query cannot be answered from.
  3. Adopted Typefaces are used under the licences under which they ship with the systems the publication is opened on. No face is embedded that the Press has not the right to embed.
  4. For adoption ISBN, DOI and legal deposit are printed as "Not assigned" with the issuing authority named. No edition is distributed commercially until they exist.

Constitution 15 of 23

Revision Constitution

How a publication changes without losing its identity.

  1. Adopted Every publication carries an edition, revision, issue and impression code, a publication identifier and a content digest computed over the curriculum it contains. A change to the curriculum changes the digest.
  2. Adopted A revision that changes what the curriculum says is a new edition. A revision that changes only how it is set is a new impression.
  3. For adoption From the second edition onward an edition history is required, stating what changed. There is no previous edition to record a change from, so nothing is printed and nothing is promised.

Constitution 16 of 23

Translation Constitution

One claim, every language.

  1. Adopted A translation is an edition, not a derivative. It carries the same claims, the same disclosures and the same corrections as the language it was translated from.
  2. Enforced Every language edition of a public claim is checked. A guard that reads one language and reports on a bilingual publication is a reassurance, not a guard. tests/published-claims.test.mjs
  3. Observed A retired term is retired in every language in the same revision. The Arabic pages carried a superseded figure and a retired pricing basis for an entire revision cycle after the English pages had dropped both.
  4. For adoption Arabic typesetting of a full publication — right-to-left page architecture, mirrored gutters, an Arabic text face — has not been designed. The platform is bilingual; the books are not yet.

Constitution 17 of 23

Quality Assurance Constitution

What is checked, and the standing suspicion of what is not.

  1. Adopted Never trust an implementation because it passes its tests. Continuously verify that the tests measure the complete behaviour they claim to guarantee.
  2. Observed Every fix is confirmed by removing it and watching the test fail. A test that has never failed has never been shown to test anything.
  3. Observed Every check states what it does NOT measure, in the file, so that coverage is never inferred from a passing suite.
  4. Adopted A defect found by looking rather than by measuring leaves a standing assertion behind, not only a fix.

Constitution 18 of 23

Academic Integrity Constitution

The clause that outranks every other clause.

  1. Adopted Never fabricate accreditation, partnerships, rankings, statistics, history, governance, staff, competencies, student outcomes or institutional achievements. Where a matter requires institutional approval it is recorded, never invented.
  2. Adopted Where appearance and truth conflict, truth. Where decoration and learning conflict, learning. Where novelty and clarity conflict, clarity. Where quantity and craftsmanship conflict, craftsmanship.
  3. Observed An unflattering finding travels into every edition, including the executive volume written for accreditation panels. An edition that drops its unflattering figures is a prospectus, not an edition.
  4. Adopted A finding filed as requiring academic authoring or governance may never be satisfied by writing plausible text. Moving a finding does not repeal it.

Constitution 19 of 23

Editorial Review Constitution

Who reads before a publication is released.

  1. Adopted No publication is released without a Final Publication Audit under standing headings, each concluding in one of four statuses. A board that approves everything has audited nothing.
  2. Adopted A heading marked Requires Governance Decision blocks the claim it concerns, not the publication, provided the publication states the limitation.
  3. For adoption External academic review of the curriculum by a reader outside the College has not taken place. It is not an editorial matter and cannot be arranged from here.

Constitution 20 of 23

Visual Review Constitution

Judged as printed, not as coded.

  1. Adopted A publication is reviewed as spreads, from rasterised pages, not from source. Four defects in the current edition were invisible in the HTML and obvious on the page.
  2. Observed A figure is inspected at its printed size before it is approved.
  3. For adoption Review against a physical proof on the specified stock has not taken place for any publication of the Press.

Constitution 21 of 23

Production Workflow

The order things happen in, and why that order.

  1. Adopted The curriculum is built from the academic database, never transcribed. Text block first, then page count, then spine, then cover: the cover cannot be produced before the extent is known.
  2. Adopted Photographs are resampled to the size they are actually printed at before placement, and the untouched originals are preserved outside version control.
  3. Adopted Figures are rasterised for the editable edition from the same source that sets them in print, in the same run, so the two cannot diverge.
  4. Observed Every artefact is regenerated from source on every build. No file in the publication directory is hand-edited.

Constitution 22 of 23

Version Control Policy

What is kept, and what is rebuilt.

  1. Adopted Source is committed. Artefacts are committed when they are the deliverable and genuinely distinct. A near-duplicate of a file already in the history is built on demand by a named script rather than stored again.
  2. Adopted Licensed originals are preserved outside version control and their licence references are held in source.
  3. Observed A generated public page is never edited in place; the source page is edited and the site rebuilt, and the build fails if the two disagree.

Constitution 23 of 23

Publication Governance

Who may decide what.

  1. Adopted Within editorial authority — design, typography, colour, layout, illustration, photography, navigation, book architecture, information design, print preparation, reader experience — the Press acts without seeking approval.
  2. Adopted Outside it — accreditation, competency frameworks, academic authoring, legal registration, external licensing, government recognition, physical proofing — the Press records and escalates, and publishes the limitation rather than working around it.
  3. Adopted The Internal Editorial Bible is the register of everything escalated. It is marked internal on every page and is not a public document.
  4. For adoption No officer of the Press has been appointed. Until one is, no publication carries a signed foreword, a named editor or a message from a head of institution, because the Press does not compose words for people who do not hold office.

The Publication Architecture

Every publication the College should issue over ten years, with the status of each computed against the academic database rather than asserted. Where a title is short of material, the shortfall is printed as a number.

How to read Part Two

What a status of Derivable does not mean

It means the material exists, not that the book is written. A derivable title still needs editing, sequencing, design and proofing — the judgement that turns a database into something worth reading. What it does not need is new curriculum or a decision from anyone. Treating derivable as done is the single most likely way for this catalogue to be misused.

Part Three

The House Identity

What never varies, what varies by series and only by series, and every number derived from the system that sets the books rather than restated from memory.

The signature

Constants

A publication that departs from these is not a publication of this Press.

CrestThe College crest, drawn as vector, appears on every front cover, every spine wide enough to carry it, and the title page. It is never redrawn, recoloured outside the two permitted treatments (gold on deep ground, single-ink on light ground), stretched, outlined or set inside another shape.
Type familiesTwo, and never a third: Cambria for continuous reading and Calibri for everything scanned. A publication needing a third family has a structural problem the third family will not solve.
GroundDeep navy #0F1D38 for covers, spines and dividers; #FAFBFC for text pages. Every publication of the Press shares both.
MetalGold #B4933E is the institutional accent and is used once per surface — a rule, a crest, or a title, never all three. Restraint is what makes it read as metal rather than as yellow.
FolioOuter edge, foot, sans, small. Same position in every publication so a reader flicking a book they have never opened finds the page number where their hand already is.
Spine architectureTitle at the head reading downward, series mark at the foot, crest above it where width allows. Downward is the British and international convention; a shelf of our books reads consistently against neighbours.
VerificationEvery publication carrying credential or award content prints the verification QR generated by the same encoder as a graduate certificate, resolving to the College’s own domain. No publication prints an identifier issued by an authority that has not issued it.

Interior

Openers, dividers and endpapers

Part openerFull bleed, deep ground, the part numeral at display size, the part title below it, nothing else. Always recto; the verso before it is left blank rather than filled.

Information design

Figures and tables

Purpose testA figure must communicate faster than the sentence it replaces. If it does not, it is removed rather than improved — one figure has been, and the reason is recorded in the source where it stood.
MeasurementEvery figure is rasterised and its text extracted before approval. Four defects in the flagship — clipped labels, overprinted text, a label running through a line — were invisible in the source and obvious in the raster.
Type floorNo text in any figure below 6.6 pt at printed size.
ColourA figure must survive greyscale. Series and level colour may distinguish, but never alone: shape, position or label carries the same distinction.
GutterNo figure crosses the gutter in a publication over 400 pages, and none crosses it in any perfect-bound publication where the crossing element carries meaning.

Tables

RulesHorizontal only. A vertical rule in a table is a confession that the columns are not aligned.
Header repetitionTable headers repeat on every page a table continues onto, set as a table header group so the repetition is structural rather than pasted.
Row integrityNo row breaks across a page. A rubric criterion split over a spread is a criterion that will be misread.
NumeralsTabular lining figures in every column of numbers, aligned on the decimal.
TotalsA total row states what it totals. A column of four cells under a header declaring five is a defect, and it has occurred once and been corrected.

Image

Photography, illustration and iconography

LicenceCommercially licensed, licence reference held in source, credited in the publication. No image is ever labelled sample or placeholder, because a publication containing a placeholder is not finished.
ProprietyModest, respectful and appropriate for an international Muslim-majority readership as well as a Western one. This is a constraint on selection, not a style, and it is not negotiable per publication.
NarrativePhotography carries atmosphere and aspiration, never illustration of the obvious. A photograph of a person reading placed beside a passage about reading adds nothing and costs a licence.
TreatmentDuotone in the level or series colour for fields and dividers; untreated at full colour only in plates. One treatment per publication.

Iconography

Drawn as typeStage marks are typographic glyphs, not images. They survive greyscale, cost nothing to embed and cannot fail to load.
One per stageA mark means one thing across every publication of the Press. A mark reused for a second meaning in a second book destroys its value in the first.
Never aloneA mark always accompanies a word. An icon-only affordance excludes the reader who has not learnt the key, and the key is on a page they are not on.

The limitation that governs every specification above

No publication of this Press has ever been printed. Every stock, caliper, binding and finish here is specified from published trade data and has not been proofed on press. The spine widths are computed from a caliper that has not been verified against a physical sample. Nothing in Part Three should be sent to a full print run without a wet proof, and this paragraph exists so that no future editor can claim they were not told.

Closing

What is settled here, and what is not

Settled: the rules, the catalogue, the identity, and the arithmetic underneath all three. 24 clauses are enforced by tests. 14 titles can be produced with no new curriculum and no institutional decision. Every format, margin, spine and series colour in Part Three is derived from the system that sets the books rather than restated beside it.

Not settled, and not the Press's to settle: 10 titles wait on institutional decisions — appointed officers, a registered publisher prefix, a print budget, a completed academic year, a signed contract. 10 wait on academic authoring, and each one states how much. 10 clauses are drafted and bind nothing until somebody with the standing to adopt them does.

The distinction between those two paragraphs is the whole discipline of this volume. A publishing house that cannot say which of its plans are its own to execute will eventually execute one that was not.
