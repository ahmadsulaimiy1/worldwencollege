Governance · WorldWide English College

The quality assurance manual.

The architecture names which body owns which question. This manual states what is opened, in which month, by whom, and what the finding obliges — because a cycle without a calendar is a diagram.

Adopted by the Executive on the recommendation of the Institutional Quality Commission. Ten leaves, and every station in them carries a month, an owner and a deadline.

The quality year
1 Sept – 31 Aug
Module review
Every module, once a year
A corrected finding
20 working days
Documents produced
Seven, each dated

“A finding with no owner and no date is an opinion.”

The Quality Architecture The Evidence Record
I The Quality Year

Four quarters, one station apiece.

The year, quarter by quarter.

The quality year opens on 1 September and closes on 31 August. A station opens on its date whether or not the last one flattered anybody. {{S:INTAKES}}

Sept – Nov

Quarter I · Module review

In force

Every module taught in the year just closed, read against its own evidence, in an order circulated before the quarter opens.

Dec – Feb

Quarter II · The review of assessment

In force

Rubrics, mark distributions and marker agreement, criterion by criterion. The quarter in which the College marks its own marking.

Mar – May

Quarter III · Level review

In force

Two levels read whole, with the prerequisite chain running into and out of each.

Jun – Aug

Quarter IV · The account

In force

Findings consolidated, owners named, deadlines set, and the seven documents laid before the Commission and the Board.

What runs across all four

Moderation, the learner voice and peer observation run continuously rather than in a quarter of their own. Each feeds whichever station is open, on the deadlines set below.

II Module Review

The rotation, and the six triggers that override it.

Every module is read once a year, and some are pulled forward.

A rotation alone reaches a failing module eleven months late. A trigger alone reaches only what has already gone wrong. The College runs both.

The rotation

Published before the quarter opens

The order is fixed in August and circulated to every tutor. Nobody is reviewed by surprise, and no reviewer picks the module that suits an argument.

The evidence

Six things the reviewer reads

The stated outcomes; the lesson sequence; marks criterion by criterion; the agreement between first and second marker; the learner responses; every change since the last review.

The reviewer

Neither the author nor the teacher

Appointed by the Curriculum Standards Committee from staff who neither wrote the module nor taught it that year. A review by the author is a rereading.

The six triggers that pull a module forward

  • A first-attempt pass rate under 60 per cent, or over 95 — both say the assessment sits at the wrong height.
  • First and second marker apart by more than one band on over a tenth of the marks.
  • Three learner responses naming the same lesson inside one collection window.
  • A resit rate above a fifth of the learners assessed in the module.
  • Any change to the rubric, read against the outcomes before it is applied.
  • A complaint upheld on the module’s teaching or assessment.
The clock on a trigger

A triggered review opens within fifteen working days of the condition being recorded, and the module keeps its place in the rotation. A review held under pressure asks a different question.

III Level Review

A level is more than its modules.

A level is read whole, every third year.

Ten sound modules can still make an incoherent level. A level review asks whether the sequence is an argument, and whether the standard at the end is the standard the award claims.

Two a year

A three-year rotation across the six

Two levels are read in full each year, so none waits longer than three. The rotation is published with the calendar in August.

A panel of three

One who teaches it, two who do not

A chair from the Assessment Integrity Committee, one member teaching at the level, one teaching at neither level beside it. Two are a quorum.

What the panel reads

The chain, not only the links

The level’s outcomes; the prerequisite chain into it and out of it; every examination paper set at it; the marks awarded; two years of module notes, read together.

Out of turn

Two conditions bring a level forward

Two of its modules pulled forward by trigger in one year, or its examination rewritten. Either brings the level into the next Quarter III.

What a level review may require

A sequence restructured, an examination reset before it is next sat, or a rubric referred upward. It settles no individual mark: that route is the appeal, at academic regulations.

IV Reviewing Assessment

Four tests a criterion must pass.

The College checks that its own rubrics discriminate.

A criterion that awards nearly everybody the same band has stopped measuring, and it costs the strong learner the credit for being strong. Quarter II runs four tests.

The testIt fails whenWhat follows
SpreadOne band takes over four fifths of the marks given on that criterion across a level.The bands are redrawn, or the criterion is merged into the one beside it.
AgreementFirst and second marker sit within one band on fewer than nine marks in ten.The wording is the suspect before the markers are, and the batch is re-moderated.
DuplicationTwo criteria move together so closely that one predicts the other across a cohort.One is withdrawn. Marking a quality twice weights it twice without saying so.
Blind re-markScripts re-marked a year on, by a marker who has not seen them, agree on fewer than seventeen in twenty.The rubric reopens, and the level’s reliability statement records the figure.
Why the blind re-mark earns its cost

The other three read marks the College already holds. Only the re-mark asks whether the same work, put before the same standard a year later, comes back with the same mark.

V Sampling

Who draws it, how large, and when it is fixed.

The sample is drawn by somebody with nothing riding on it.

What a sample contains is published in the Tutor’s Handbook. This leaf sets the three things the institution controls: who draws it, how big it is, and when it stops being changeable.

Drawn by the Registrar

Never by the tutor whose marking it tests

The Registrar’s office draws every sample from the assessment record. A marker who picks the work read on their behalf grades their own homework.

The size

A tenth, with a floor of five

Module assignments are sampled at one in ten, and never fewer than five. A level examination is not sampled: every script in the batch is read.

Fixed before marking

Sealed when the batch opens

The sample is fixed before the first mark is entered, and is not adjusted afterwards. A sample chosen once the marks are known measures the person choosing it.

The moderating panel

  • Three members, of whom two are a quorum, convened by the Assessment Integrity Committee.
  • A member steps out of any script they marked, and the stepping out is minuted.
  • The minutes name the scripts read, the criteria discussed and the decision on the batch.
  • Sample and minutes are held six years, alongside the scripts themselves.
The one power moderation withholds

A panel confirms a marking standard, returns a batch for re-marking, or refers the rubric upward. It does not lift one learner’s mark inside an untouched batch, and the reasoning sits in the handbook.

VI The Learner Voice

Collected on a window, answered on a date.

A collection nobody answers is worse than none.

The routes by which a learner reaches a decision are published at Inclusion & Learner Voice. This leaf states what the cycle does with what was said.

Ten working days

The window opens when the work closes

Collection opens the day a module or a level closes and runs ten working days. A response arriving later is carried into the next quarter and read there.

Read in full

Every free-text response, unsummarised

The Student Experience Committee reads each response as written before any of it is counted. A tutor sees their own module’s responses with names removed.

Forty working days

The answer goes back to the cohort

Within forty working days of the window closing, the cohort receives what was said, what the College is changing, and what it declined to change with the reason.

Four rules that make the collection worth giving

  • Three responses naming the same lesson inside one window open a module trigger.
  • A response naming an individual leaves the collection for the complaints procedure, where the person named has a right of reply.
  • A response attaches to a module and a level, and to no learner’s record.
  • What the responses changed is published each year in the Learner Voice Account, refusals included.
VII Peer Observation

The scheme whose note leaves no record.

Twice a year, and the note belongs to the pair.

The formal scheme is the Teaching Observation Framework at the quality architecture, whose write-up enters the teaching support record. Peer observation works the other way round.

Twice a year

Paired across levels by the committee

Each tutor observes one colleague and is observed by one. The Teaching Excellence Committee pairs across levels, so neither is ever the other’s second marker.

Five working days

Written to one reader and no other

The note reaches the observed tutor within five working days and goes nowhere else. It carries no grade, enters no record, and is read in no appraisal.

  • The fact of each observation is recorded so the cycle can be evidenced; the content is not.
  • Where both tutors agree, a theme goes up unattributed to the Teaching Excellence Committee.
  • A tutor may decline one pairing a year without giving a reason, and is paired again.
Why the content stays with the pair

An observation that can be produced against a tutor buys a lesson performed for the observer. The College already holds a scheme that judges; this one exists so a colleague can say something awkward at no cost.

VIII Examiner’s Remit

What the office does inside this cycle.

The one station the College cannot staff from inside.

The office of Independent External Examiner is constituted at the quality architecture. This leaf is its working remit: what reaches it, what it may do, and what the College reserves to its signature.

Before the level board

The papers arrive first, not after

The moderation sample, the panel’s minutes and the rubric reach the office before the level board sits. Papers sent afterwards invite comment on a thing already done.

Any script, in or out

The right to call for what was not sent

The office may re-mark anything in the sample and call for any script outside it. Oversight confined to what it is shown is oversight of the choosing.

Confirm, or decline to

Two outcomes, both of them written

Either a written confirmation that the standard applied is the standard published, or a written statement of where the two came apart. The office is asked for one of the two.

Annually, in public

Reports over the Executive’s head

The report goes to the Board of Governors and the Academic Senate, and is published through this cycle. An oversight report routed through the people it examines is a draft.

Who may hold the office

Appointment is made from outside the College for a fixed term of four years, not renewable within four years of its ending. A holder may not have taught for the College, nor held a contract with it, in the five years before appointment.

What the College reserves to this signature

An award is described as externally moderated on the office’s own written confirmation and on nothing else. Moderation carried out inside the College is recorded as internally moderated, in those words.

IX Finding to Change

Four dispositions, and a close that is earned.

A finding closes when the evidence moves, and not before.

Every finding raised anywhere in this manual leaves its review under one of four dispositions. Each carries one named office as owner and a date, and both travel into the Implementation Register.

DispositionWhat it meansThe clock
ConfirmedThe practice matches the published standard.Closed at the review, with the evidence that closed it.
CorrectedThe reviewer can name the change, and it sits inside the module.Twenty working days, then verified at the next station.
ReferredThe fault is in a rubric, a regulation or a sequence, and belongs to a committee.Tabled at that committee’s next sitting, inside forty working days.
HeldThe finding turns on evidence the reviewer could not settle alone.To the Commission with the evidence and a return date, and named in the annual account.

How a change is verified

  • Verification reads the same evidence again. An assurance that the change was made is not verification.
  • A finding closes only when the measure that raised it has moved the way the finding required.
  • Where the measure stands still, the finding reopens with a different owner and a new date.
  • A finding may close as Confirmed on second reading, and the reversal is minuted with its reason.
The learner’s protection inside a change

No change to a rubric or a threshold applies to work already submitted against the version it replaces. A learner is marked by the criteria published before they wrote — see how you are assessed.

X The Documents

Seven documents, each with an owner and a retention.

Seven documents, and every one of them dated.

A cycle is only as real as what it leaves behind. These seven are what a reviewer, a panel or a learner may ask to see, and each joins the evidence record as it is produced.

DocumentProducedLaid beforeHeld for
Module Review NotePer module, each yearCurriculum Standards CommitteeSix years
Level Review ReportPer level, every third yearThe CommissionTen years
Assessment Reliability StatementPer level, each yearAssessment Integrity CommitteeTen years
Learner Voice AccountAnnualStudent Experience CommitteeSix years
Observation SummaryAnnual, naming nobodyTeaching Excellence CommitteeSix years

The Executive adopts amendments to this manual on the recommendation of the Commission, each carrying the date it took effect. A deadline is never shortened inside a station already open, and every amendment is entered in the decisions register.
