Governance · Worldwide English College

Who owns what, and what may be done with it.

A college that publishes its whole curriculum has to say on what terms, and a learner who hands over a recording of their own voice is owed the same clarity. This instrument names the holder of every class of material, and states exactly what each holder may do.

Four estates of material pass through this College: the volumes it publishes, the work a learner submits, the material a tutor produces while teaching, and the College’s own name and marks. Each is held by a different person on different terms. Every grant below is made under the College’s own authority, and every reservation is one the College enforces itself.

Work you submit
Yours, and it stays yours
The teaching volumes
Free to read, print and teach from
A takedown notice
Answered in 20 working days
Permission to reprint
Given in writing, without a fee

“A licence nobody reads is a licence nobody keeps.”

I Scope

Four estates of material, and one instrument over all of them.

Four estates, and one instrument.

Almost every argument about ownership inside a college starts by treating all of its material as one thing. It is four things, held by different people on different terms, and this instrument keeps them apart.

Estate one

The College’s own volumes

Written inside the College and published under its imprint. The College is the author and the holder, and it has granted a wide licence over them which it holds itself to.

Estate two

Work a learner submits

Essays, recordings, projects and examination scripts. The learner is the author and the holder. The College takes a stated licence to teach, mark and keep the record.

Estate three

Material a tutor produces

Lesson plans, model answers, slides and feedback made in the course of teaching. Who holds each depends on who commissioned it, and the table in Leaf IV settles that.

Estate four

The name and the marks

The College’s name, its crest, the programme title, the edition mark and the credential. These identify the institution, and the College holds them outright.

Where two estates meet

A tutor’s model answer built on a learner’s script, or a volume quoting a learner’s writing, engages two holders at once. The more protective term governs. In practice that means the learner’s written consent is required before the material travels beyond that learner’s own academic record.

II The Volumes

What the Library grants, and how a grant behaves over time.

The volumes are open, and the licence is one page.

The College publishes its curriculum in full, at no charge and without registration. The operative terms are set out in The Licence, which is the one authoritative text. This leaf answers the two questions that page leaves to this instrument: who holds the copyright, and what happens to a grant when an edition changes.

Held by the College

4
  • Authorship of every volume, and the copyright in its text, tables and composed pages.
  • The edition mark printed on every page, which identifies the volume, the edition and the page a passage came from.
  • The right to issue a further edition, and to state which edition any given passage belongs to.
  • The right to give written permission for a reserved use, which the College gives without charging for it.

Granted to every reader

4
  • Reading, downloading, keeping and printing a volume, with no account and no email address.
  • Photocopying it for a class you teach, quoting from it, and setting it on a reading list.
  • Teaching from it, including inside another institution’s classroom.
  • Translating a passage for a learner who needs it in their own language.
What the grant stops short of

Standing a volume up as another institution’s programme, selling it, mirroring it, producing a derivative curriculum from it, and feeding it to a machine-learning model are reserved to the College. Permission for any of them is given in writing on request, and the address is at the foot of this page.

A grant already taken is not withdrawn

The College may issue a new edition, and it may set different terms for editions published after it. A copy lawfully made under the terms in force at the time stays lawfully made. Nothing here reaches back into a class set already printed and sitting in a teacher’s cupboard.

The edition mark is how a passage is traced

Every page of every volume carries the volume title, the College’s name and a mark computed from that edition’s curriculum. Material taken from a volume stays identifiable as to volume, edition and page, whether it is reproduced, retyped or paraphrased around.

III Your Work

One sentence long, and the rest of the leaf explains it.

Your work is yours.

The College takes no ownership of anything a learner writes, records, designs or submits. Copyright belongs to the learner from the moment the work is made, and enrolment transfers none of it. What the College takes instead is a licence, and it is narrow enough to list in full.

  • Mark the work, and have it second-marked by another member of academic staff.
  • Moderate it against the rubric published to you before the assessment it governs.
  • Keep it in your academic record with its date and its mark, so the record can be verified later.
  • Produce it to an appeal, to an integrity investigation, or to an examiner reviewing the standard.
  • Store and process it on the College’s own systems for those purposes and for those alone.
The limit

What the licence stops at

The College does not publish a learner’s work, sell it, license it onward, or use it to advertise the College. It is never used as training data for a machine-learning model — the same use the Library licence reserves against everybody else.

Exemplars

Showing your work to the next cohort

A strong script or recording is worth putting in front of the learners who come after you. That takes separate written consent, asked for by name and for a named use. Consent may be withdrawn at any time, and the material leaves teaching use within 20 working days.

Attribution

Named only if you ask to be

Where consent is given, work is shown without the author’s name unless the author asks to be named. A recording used with a class is introduced as an exemplar rather than attributed to the learner who made it.

Commissions

Work you are paid to make

A learner commissioned and paid to write, record or illustrate for the College is a contributor in that transaction rather than a student. The commission states in writing what it buys, what the College may do with it, and what the contributor keeps.

Consent is never a condition

A learner who refuses every request of this kind is taught, marked, moderated and awarded exactly as one who agrees to all of them. The refusal is recorded so that nobody asks twice by accident, and the College asks nothing further about it. Academic judgement and permission to reuse work are separate matters and are kept separate — see Academic integrity.

IV Work by a Tutor

Three classes, decided by who commissioned the work.

What a tutor makes while teaching.

A tutor arrives carrying material of their own and produces a great deal more of it while teaching. Claiming all of it for the College would be a claim over somebody’s career; claiming none of it would leave a programme the College could not maintain once that tutor moved on. Three classes settle it.

Class of materialHeld byTerms
Material the tutor brought The tutor The College takes a licence to use it with the cohorts it was brought to, for the length of the engagement and for twelve months after it ends. The tutor may use it anywhere else without asking.
Material commissioned and paid for by the College The College Published under the College’s imprint with the author named in the colophon. The tutor keeps a perpetual licence to teach from it elsewhere, short of standing up a competing programme.
Feedback, marks and moderation notes The College These are part of a learner’s academic record. They are written for that record and stay with it, and a named learner’s feedback is never reused outside the College.

Attribution is not optional

Where a volume carries a tutor’s writing, that tutor is named in the colophon of the volume. A house style is not a reason to publish somebody’s work anonymously.

What the College declines to take

The College requires no assignment of copyright in work a tutor made before the engagement, and claims nothing a tutor produces for their own study or for another employer.

Leaving

A tutor who leaves keeps every licence granted here. Access to the platform ends with the engagement; the licence outlives it, and so does the name in the colophon.

V Recordings

A live session, a learner’s own voice, and who holds each.

Recordings, and who holds them.

The platform records speaking work, and it records live sessions. Every such recording carries two interests at once: the College’s, in a session it arranged and paid for, and the speaker’s, in their own voice. Both are recognised below, and neither cancels the other.

Your voice

A speaking recording is yours

The recording belongs to the learner who made it, and the College holds it under the licence set out in Leaf III. It is kept so that months of change can be heard rather than erased at the moment of marking — see Your academic record.

Reach

Who is able to hear it

A speaking recording is reachable by the learner who made it and by the staff marking or moderating it. It is not served to another learner, and it stays inside the College’s own storage: not for analysis, not for advertising, not for a third party of any kind.

Live sessions

A recorded session has two authors

The College is the author of the recording of a session it arranged, and every participant keeps the rights in their own contribution to it. A session recording is available to the learners enrolled in that session for twelve months, and it is used for teaching and moderation only.

Withdrawal

Taking your voice out of a session

A learner may require their voice to be removed from a session recording, or from any use reaching beyond their own record. The College acts on that within 20 working days. The assessment record survives the removal, because a mark and a recording are different things.

Ownership here, retention there

How long recordings are held, and what erasure means against an academic register meant to be permanent, are questions of data policy rather than of ownership — see Privacy and your data. This instrument governs the licence. That page governs the retention, and the two are written to be read together.

VI Name and Marks

What may be said about the College, and by whom.

The name, and what others may do with it.

The College’s name, its crest, the programme title and the credential identify this institution and nothing else. A reader who sees any of them is entitled to conclude that the College stands behind whatever carries them. That is the whole reason the reservations below exist.

Anyone may, without asking

5
  • Say truthfully that they studied here, and name the level reached and the award held.
  • Name the College as the source of a quotation, a volume or a published figure.
  • Link to any page of this site, the verification page included.
  • Report on the College, criticise it, and quote it in the course of doing so.
  • Show a credential the College issued to them, in full, to anyone they choose.

Reserved to the College

5
  • Using the name, the crest or the programme title so as to suggest that the College endorses, approves or is connected with another organisation.
  • Issuing any certificate, level statement or transcript bearing the College’s name or marks.
  • Describing another course as the College’s programme, or as leading to the College’s award.
  • Registering the name, the crest or a confusable variant of either as a mark or as a domain.
  • Altering a credential the College issued, or presenting as the College’s one it did not issue.
Why the verification page belongs in this leaf

Any credential the College issues can be checked at the verification page, with no account and no relationship with the College. That is what makes the column on the right enforceable rather than decorative: a claim made in somebody’s name is checkable in seconds by the person it was made to.

VII Takedown

Both directions, and the same clock on each.

Takedown, in both directions.

Two things can go wrong. Material belonging to somebody else can appear in a College volume, and College material can appear where the licence does not reach. One procedure covers both, on the same timetable whichever direction the claim arrives from.

StageWhat happensTime allowed
NoticeA written notice naming the material, the page it sits on, and the right claimed over it.—
AcknowledgementThe College confirms receipt and names the office handling the matter.5 working days
Interim withdrawalWhere the claim is arguable on its face, the material comes down while it is examined.5 working days
DecisionA written outcome with reasons: restored, removed permanently, or kept under a licence agreed with the holder.20 working days
Counter-statementWhoever supplied the material may answer in writing, and the matter is reopened on a fresh reading.20 working days of the decision
Entry in the registerThe notice, the outcome and both dates are entered in the College’s rights register.On decision

Sending a notice

Write to rights@worldwideenglishcollege.com with the address of the page, the material complained of, and the right claimed over it. A notice that names the page is acted on faster than one describing it.

When the College is the one claiming

Where the College finds a reserved use of its own volumes, it writes and asks before it does anything else. A teacher trying to do the work properly is a colleague, and permission costs nothing. Formal notice follows only a request that is refused.

Taking material down is not an admission

Withdrawing material while a claim is examined protects whoever turns out to be right, and the College does it before knowing which party that is. Restoration carries the same weight as removal, and the rights register records both outcomes with their dates.

Read the instrument that decides who may change any of this.
