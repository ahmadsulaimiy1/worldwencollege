Governance · WorldWide English College

Who decides what, and on what authority.

Any institution can publish a promise. What tells you whether it will be kept is who holds the power to break it, and what stops them — including the four powers here that have never yet been used.

The College separates academic judgement from institutional governance, from quality assurance, from finance, and from day-to-day administration. This page names who holds each of those, how the standard is set and how it is checked, and which posts remain unfilled. Every decision on it is dated, and every date is the date it took effect.

I The Instrument

Seven articles. Each states a power, who holds it, and whether it has ever been exercised.

The powers, stated before the people who hold them.

Everything below this leaf describes bodies, offices and cycles. This leaf states the powers those things exist to carry — and marks, for each one, whether it has ever actually been used. Four of the seven have not.

Art. I

The standard is owned by the Board, not by the Executive

The Board of Governors owns the academic standard and the framework beneath it. No member of the Executive may alter either, and no commercial consideration may reach the standard except through the Board.

Unexercised · the Board has never met
Art. II

Academic judgement rests with the Senate alone

Marks, progression and awards are academic decisions. They are not administrative decisions and may not be overturned on administrative or commercial grounds by anyone, at any level of the College.

Unexercised · the Senate has never sat
Art. III

No award under this instrument is conferred without external moderation

An Independent External Examiner reviews assessment and moderates awards made under this instrument, adopted on 14 August 2026. The office exists, its powers are written, and it is empty. Awards conferred before that date — the Level I and Level II awards held by this College’s first two cohorts — were set, marked and second-marked internally, under the College’s own academic authority. The College says which is which rather than letting one word cover both.

Unexercised · the post is unfilled; awards to date are internally moderated
Art. IV

Every decision is dated and attributed

Each mapping, outcome and threshold records the body that decided it and the date it was decided. Nothing is recorded as simply true. Thirty decisions are in force and every one carries both.

In force · 37 decisions
Art. V

Assessment criteria are published before the work

A rubric that appears after a submission is a justification. Every rubric and pass criterion is published to the learner before the assessment it governs, without exception.

In force · enforced in the platform
Art. VI

A credential may be checked by anyone

Verification requires no account and no relationship with the College. A credential nobody outside the institution can check is a credential the institution is asking to be trusted about.

In force · open to the public
Art. VII

The framework is reviewed against what teaching produces

The quality cycle turns annually against real evidence from real cohorts. Eight cohorts have been taught since the College was inaugurated in 2023. The evidence exists; the cycle constituted on 14 August 2026 has not yet completed a turn against it, and the record says that rather than describing a cycle in operation.

Constituted · first annual turn not yet completed
II Leadership

Who holds office today, and the offices that are defined and empty.

Leadership in service of scholarship.

The College is governed through a framework that separates academic judgement, institutional governance, quality assurance, finance and day-to-day administration. That separation is not administrative tidiness. It is what protects every learner, every assessment, every publication and every award the College issues.

The constraint that matters

No individual, including the Founder, may independently determine academic standards, approve their own work, or override established governance procedure. The College is built to outlast the people currently running it.

III Board of Governors

The body that owns the standard. Its powers are written; it has never met.

Strategic oversight, held apart from management.

The Board preserves the College’s mission, safeguards its academic independence, oversees financial sustainability, and appoints senior officers. Its test for any decision is the long-term interest of learners rather than short-term convenience.

IV Academic Senate

Academic authority, separated from executive authority on purpose.

The College’s highest academic authority.

The Senate oversees academic standards, curriculum development, assessment integrity, research quality and academic regulations. It operates independently of operational management and advises the Board on academic matters.

Constituted is not the same as convened. The Senate has 3 appointed members and has not yet met. Until a minuted decision exists, the skill mappings and descriptor thresholds it will approve remain recorded as interim. A membership list is not an approval, and this site will not let one stand in for the other.

What the Senate decides.

The Senate owns the relationship between what a learner is assessed on and what the College then says they can do. Two questions sit inside that: which assessments count as evidence for which of the four language skills, and where the thresholds sit that turn that evidence into a descriptor on a learner’s record.

Skill mapping

Which assessment evidences which skill

A speaking assignment is evidence of speaking; a quiz is not, however many questions it asks about spoken language. The Senate holds that boundary, because it is the one an institution is most tempted to blur when it wants a fuller record.

Descriptor thresholds

How much evidence is enough

A descriptor asserts something about a person. The threshold that triggers it is an academic judgement about sufficiency, not a technical default, and it is the Senate’s to set. Its 3 members were appointed on 2026-08-14; until a minuted meeting exists, thresholds remain the mechanism defaults the software ships, labelled as such.

V College Executive

The people who run the College day to day, and the limits on what they may decide alone.

Daily operation, under policy set by the Board.

The Executive runs the College. It does not set the standards it operates under, and it does not review its own work — those sit with the Senate and with the External Examiner respectively.

VI Independent External Examiner

The office that would check the College from outside. No one holds it.

The post that cannot be filled from inside.

The External Examiner reviews assessments, academic standards, marking consistency and the integrity of awards, and reports directly to the Board of Governors rather than through the Executive. The whole function of the post is to sit outside the College, which is why no appointment made within it can substitute.

Currently vacant

The post is not filled. Until it is, awards are defined and published but carry the College’s authority and nobody else’s, and the College says so on every page where it bears on a decision rather than once in a footnote.

VII The Chain of Authority

Where each power comes from and where it stops.

Two bodies, two different reasons nothing is approved yet.

What the two membership counts mean

BASCE has no members, and a body with no members cannot approve anything. Every competency decision that would properly belong to it is recorded as interim, taken under authority delegated to the Press, and marked in the database with the body it awaits.

The Senate has 3, and has not yet convened. That is a different position from BASCE’s and it produces the same outcome for now: a body that can approve but has not met has not approved anything, so skill mappings and descriptor thresholds also remain interim. They change status when a minuted decision exists, not when an appointment letter does.

IX Coverage

What the standard reaches, and what it does not.

How often each competency is actually assessed.

The table above says what the six competencies are. It cannot say how much weight each one currently carries, and the answer is the most useful thing in this section.

What the shape says

Command and Clarity carry most of Level I. Judgement is assessed four times, Reason once, and Bearing and Reach are not assessed at Level I at all. Against the remit quoted above — each competency assessed multiple times across each level — three of the six clear the bar today. Levels II to VI carry no competency mapping yet.

This is published rather than smoothed for the same reason the mappings are recorded as interim: a framework that reports even coverage it does not have is worth less than one that reports uneven coverage it does. The gap is the work, and naming it is how it gets closed.

Defined

6 competencies

Each with a definition written to be arguable rather than unfalsifiable. “Understood the first time, by the audience actually present” can be disagreed with; “excellent communication skills” cannot.

Mapped

Level I only, so far

Every Level I assessment is mapped to the competencies it bears on, with a weight and a written rationale. Competencies are evidenced where they are genuinely assessed rather than distributed evenly to look complete.

Not approved

0 members appointed

Every mapping is recorded as interim. A board with no members cannot approve, and a test fails the build if any mapping is marked approved while the membership count is zero.

X Quality Assurance

The cycle by which a written standard becomes a checked one.

What is actually checked, and how often.

Quality assurance in a new institution is mostly design. The mechanisms that check internal consistency run on every change; the cycles that depend on a year of teaching have not turned. This section separates the two rather than describing them together.

Curriculum

Programme-wide consistency

Every level, module and lesson is checked for structural completeness — objectives, prerequisites, stage timings, assessment coverage — across all six levels on every change.

Rubrics

Against a published policy

Assignment rubrics are checked against the College’s own rubric policy: criteria count, weightings, and the presence of level-appropriate descriptors.

Claims

Published figures against the record

Figures published on the website are checked against the academic database. A page cannot claim a module count the curriculum does not have.

Terminology

One word per concept

Ambiguous terms are retired and the retirement is enforced automatically, because one word carrying three meanings across the curriculum, the timetable and the platform is how a published figure drifts away from the programme it describes.

XI Not Yet Turned

The parts of the cycle that are specified, and have not yet come round.

The cycles that have not come round.

Each of these is specified — who does it, on what evidence, how often. None has run yet, and the reason is different in each case.

Annual programme review

One turn, not yet completed

The framework is reviewed annually against what teaching produced. Eight cohorts of results exist to review it against; the cycle was constituted on 14 August 2026 and its first annual turn has not been completed.

Lesson observation

No observation on record

Observed teaching is one of five evidence classes in the pedagogical record, and the only one that cannot be reasoned out. Eight cohorts were taught and none of it was recorded under this scheme, so the class is empty and marked empty.

External review

Needs an external reviewer

No External Examiner is appointed. The awards conferred to date were set, marked and second-marked inside the College, and no outside party has confirmed that its levels sit where it says they sit. That is the single largest thing this College cannot yet tell you about its own standard.

XII The Standard Behind It

Where the standard comes from, and why that matters more than what it says.

Never trust a check because it passed.

A published engineering principle governs this work: continuously verify that the checks themselves measure the complete behaviour they claim to guarantee. It is applied by deliberately breaking each assertion and confirming it fails before restoring it — because a test that has never failed has never been shown to work.

How it is applied

Every assertion is broken on purpose

A check earns trust by failing first. The condition it guards is reversed, the failure is confirmed against the exact wording or value it exists to catch, and only then is the check restored. Until that has happened, a passing check is an assumption wearing a green mark.

What it covers

Published claims, not only code

The same rule governs every figure on this site that a test guards — the fee breakdown to the cent, the credit and hours values, the decisions register, the retired claims that may never return. Each guard carries a proof that it catches the wording it was written against.

XIII The Standard

The standard itself, published in full rather than summarised.

What holding a standard actually requires.

Written for the reader who is not a prospective student — a reviewer, a panel, a ministry, an employer checking a credential.

One

Published before it is applied

Outcomes, rubrics and pass criteria are published to learners before assessment, not explained afterwards. A standard revealed after the fact is not a standard; it is a justification.

Two

Marked separately, floored separately

The four language skills are marked apart and an overall mark cannot carry a skill below its floor. Unlimited compensation is how a certificate comes to describe someone who cannot be understood aloud.

Three

Written before the teaching

Each assessment exists before the lesson it tests, so the teaching is built toward the standard rather than the standard assembled afterwards from whatever was taught.

Four

Where a level is pitched, and how that is held

Each of the six levels is mapped to a Common European Framework band, and the mapping is published with the rubric that marks it. The office of Independent External Examiner carries the standing remit to review that pitch from outside the College — assessment frameworks, examination standards, moderation, award classifications — and reports annually to the Board and the Senate.

XIV Enforced, Not Intended

The difference between a rule the College keeps and a rule it means to keep.

The parts a machine checks.

A programme of this size cannot be held consistent by care. Several properties fail the build when broken, which is the only form of consistency that survives a year of revisions.

Sequence

Every prerequisite is taught earlier

A lesson depending on something the programme teaches later, or never, is a defect proofreading does not reliably find.

Rubrics

One published policy across all of them

Criteria, weighting and band descriptors follow a single policy, checked automatically. Sixty rubrics drift apart otherwise.

Claims

Published figures match the record

Every figure on this website is checked against the curriculum it describes. A published number that quietly stopped being true is how an institution comes to misdescribe itself.

Present

A complete, documented programme

Specifications, outcomes, hours, rubrics, competency mappings and an evidence register of 37 items across 23 collections.

Held

What the College exercises in its own right

Teaching, assessment and conferral are within the College’s own academic authority, and it has exercised all three. Eight cohorts have been taught since the College was inaugurated in 2023. More than 22,000 learners in over 60 countries have studied with the College. The instrument adopted on 14 August 2026 binds every award made under it to review by the Independent External Examiner. That remit is published in full at The Quality Architecture.

XV Verification

A credential check open to anyone, requiring no account and no relationship with the College.

Anyone can check a credential. The register is still being loaded.

Anyone holding a WEC-LC credential code can verify it without an account, a relationship with the College or its permission. The route is built and open, and it was built ahead of the register deliberately: retrofitting a signature onto an already-issued credential proves nothing about when it was issued. The awards conferred so far were made before this service existed and their records are still being entered against it, so a name checked today may return nothing — a gap that is the College’s and not its holder’s.

The holder

Controls what is disclosed

A graduate decides what a share exposes and whether they appear in the public register at all. A credential the issuer publishes without consent is a disclosure, not a credential.

The checker

Needs nothing from the College

An employer or an institution verifying a claim should not have to write to WEC-LC and wait. The check resolves for anyone holding the code, immediately.

The issuer

Must be able to be wrong in public

Withdrawal shows as withdrawal rather than deletion. A register that silently loses entries cannot be trusted about the entries it keeps.

The code

On every credential

A verification code, and a scannable code resolving to the same place. Either can be used by anyone.

The signature

Checked, not displayed

Credentials are cryptographically signed and the verification checks the signature. A record that can only be looked up can be forged by anyone able to build a convincing page.

The register

Browsable, and opt-in

A public roll of award-holders exists for anyone wanting to confirm a person rather than a document. Appearing in it is the graduate’s decision, not a consequence of graduating.

XVI Research

What the College studies about its own teaching, and the four questions it has set itself.

No findings. Four answerable questions.

Worldwide English College conducts no research: no papers, no conference contributions, no funded projects, no research staff, no findings. A new institution’s research position is an agenda, not an output — these four questions came out of building the programme, and each is answerable with evidence the College’s own marked work generates.

One

Does declaring stage timings change how a lesson runs?

Every lesson declares minutes per stage. Nobody knows whether real teaching converges on them, exceeds them systematically, or ignores them — and the answer would revise either the plans or the practice.

Two

Do the written “common mistakes” match the mistakes learners make?

The support record names the errors each point provokes, derived from the language rather than from observation. A single term of marked work would test that directly, point by point.

Three

Does keeping recordings change pronunciation outcomes?

The Listening Lab retains recordings so improvement is audible. Whether hearing your own earlier attempt actually improves the later one is an empirical question with an obvious design and no answer here.

Four

Do the per-skill floors change who passes?

The adopted honours scheme refuses unlimited compensation between skills. Whether that materially changes outcomes, or merely feels more rigorous, is measurable the moment there are marks.

If any of these is answered, the answer gets published

Including — particularly — against the College. Each question could return an answer that embarrasses a design decision already published and printed, and those are the answers an institution has every incentive to lose. Until then, nothing here should be read as a finding, a preliminary result or work in progress. They are questions.

How decisions are recorded, and the date each one was taken.

A named authority

A stated rationale

Every competency mapping carries a rationale explaining why that assessment bears on that competency. A mapping without one is an opinion that has acquired the authority of a database row.

XVIII Posts Not Yet Filled

Offices that exist, are defined, and are empty. Published as empty.

What the College does not have.

Listed rather than omitted. Each of these blocks something specific, and naming what it blocks is more useful than a page that simply stops short.

External Examiner

Required before any award is conferred

The independent post whose entire function is to sit outside the College. Until it is filled, every award the College confers is moderated by the College alone — including those already held at Level I and Level II. No internal appointment substitutes for it, which is why filling the Board, the Senate and the Executive has not moved this one.

BASCE members

Required before competencies are approved

The Board of Academic Standards and Curriculum Excellence stands at 0 appointed members. Approval, as opposed to interim adoption, waits on appointment. The Board of Governors has a Governor for Academic Affairs; that is a different body with a different remit, and it is not read as BASCE membership.

A convened Senate

Required before skill mappings are approved

The Senate has 3 appointed members and has not yet met. Constituting a body and convening it are two events, and the record holds them separately so that neither can quietly stand in for the other.

Academic Reviewer

Required before publications are reviewed

Every published volume is authored by the Press and has not been read by a qualified reader who did not write it, and each volume states that on its own imprint page. The reader has to be somebody who did not write the volume, which is why authorship and review are separated by instrument rather than by practice.
